Charles Hermite was a French mathematician whose brilliance shone brightly in the 19th century. Despite a physical disability that made walking difficult, his mind sprinted through complex mathematical landscapes. He's famous for proving that 'e' (the base of natural logarithms) is a transcendental number, a big deal in mathematics, and for his contributions to number theory, quadratic forms, and orthogonal polynomials, which are still cornerstones of advanced math and physics. Imagine proving something is "beyond algebra" – that was Hermite's kind of challenge! He was also a passionate teacher, mentoring future math giants like Henri Poincaré. His work laid foundational stones for entire branches of mathematics, proving that sometimes, the greatest journeys happen within the mind.
