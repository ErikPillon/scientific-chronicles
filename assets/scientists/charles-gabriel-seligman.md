Charles G. Seligman was a British medical doctor who decided that human bodies weren't nearly as interesting as human cultures. He transitioned into ethnology and became a pivotal figure in early 20th-century anthropology. Along with his wife, Brenda Z. Seligman, he embarked on groundbreaking expeditions, documenting the cultures of indigenous peoples in places like Sudan, Sri Lanka, and Melanesia. His work helped shape the understanding of cultural diversity, proving that sometimes, the best way to understand humanity is to pack your bags and go explore. He left a lasting mark on how we perceive and study different societies.
