Before she was an icon, Sally Ride was a brilliant astrophysicist and a serious tennis player. But she traded her racket for rocket science when she joined NASA in 1978, becoming the first American woman to journey into space aboard the Space Shuttle Challenger on June 18, 1983. Forget glass ceilings; Sally smashed through the Kármán line with grace and intelligence, completing two space missions. She wasn't just a passenger; she operated the Shuttle's robotic arm, deployed satellites, and conducted experiments. After her astronaut career, she became a fierce advocate for science education, especially for girls, co-founding Sally Ride Science to inspire young minds. Sally proved that women belonged in space, in science, and wherever their intellect could take them. She truly was out of this world.
