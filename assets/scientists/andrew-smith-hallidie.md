San Francisco's iconic hills? They used to be a pedestrian's nightmare. Then along came Andrew Smith Hallidie, the British-American engineer with a vision (and probably some very tired calves). Born in 1836, Hallidie saw the city's steep inclines and thought, 'There has to be a better way than horses slipping all over the place!' His stroke of genius? The cable car. In 1873, he unveiled the world's first successful cable railway, pulling cars up and down those infamous slopes with a hidden, continuously moving steel rope. Suddenly, San Francisco wasn't just beautiful; it was navigable! Hallidie didn't just build a transportation system; he sculpted a city's very identity and gave tourists a reason to keep coming back. He literally got the city moving in style.
