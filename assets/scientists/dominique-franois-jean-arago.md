François Arago was so much more than a scientist; he was an 19th-century French rockstar. Imagine a brilliant physicist who could also command a room as a politician, and you're getting close. Arago made significant contributions across physics, particularly in optics (working with Fresnel on light's wave theory and discovering that polarized light doesn't interfere) and electromagnetism (showing that a rotating copper disc could induce magnetism). He was also a celebrated astronomer, observing Halley's Comet and even helping to measure the speed of light. But his life wasn't confined to the laboratory or observatory. As a prominent republican and later a government minister, he fiercely advocated for the abolition of slavery and the death penalty, and for universal suffrage. He believed science and liberty were intertwined, embodying the Enlightenment ideal of a scholar deeply engaged with the betterment of society. Arago truly was a man of science, action, and principle.
