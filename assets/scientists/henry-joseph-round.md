Before LEDs were twinkling on every gadget and street corner, there was Henry Joseph Round, a brilliant English electrical engineer. While tinkering with crystal detectors for radio communication in 1907, he noticed something peculiar: certain materials, like silicon carbide, emitted a yellow glow when a voltage was applied. He'd stumbled upon electroluminescence, a phenomenon that underpins modern light-emitting diodes! Though he didn't fully grasp its potential at the time, his careful observations were a crucial early step on the path to today's energy-efficient lighting. Round wasn't just chasing radio waves; he was literally shedding light on what would become a revolutionary technology, long before anyone else saw the sparkle.
