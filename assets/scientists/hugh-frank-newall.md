Hugh Frank Newall was a British astrophysicist who spent his career staring at the sun and stars, deciphering their secrets through spectroscopy. He was instrumental in establishing astrophysical research at the University of Cambridge, building up the Solar Physics Observatory (now part of the Institute of Astronomy). Newall dedicated himself to understanding stellar atmospheres, the chemical composition of stars, and the dynamics of the sun. He wasn't just a theorist; he was a hands-on observer and instrument maker, pushing the boundaries of what could be learned from starlight. His work laid important groundwork for modern astrophysics, helping astronomers understand the physical processes happening light-years away.
