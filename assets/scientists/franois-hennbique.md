Before François Hennébique, building with concrete was a bit like hoping for the best. Concrete is great under compression (pushing down), but terrible under tension (pulling apart). Engineers knew they needed to reinforce it, but how? Hennébique, a French civil engineer and contractor, figured it out in a big way. In 1892, he patented a comprehensive system of reinforced concrete that included steel bars not just at the bottom of beams but also bent upwards to handle shear forces, and a network of stirrups. This wasn't just a simple tweak; it was a game-changer! His system allowed for stronger, more efficient, and more versatile structures, from buildings to bridges. Hennébique's firm rapidly expanded across Europe, constructing thousands of buildings and proving that reinforced concrete was the material of the future. He literally laid the structural foundation for much of the 20th-century urban landscape, making our cities stronger, taller, and much more durable.
