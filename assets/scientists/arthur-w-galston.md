Arthur W. Galston was an American botanist who didn't just grow plants; he understood their inner lives and spoke up for their (and humanity's) ethical treatment. Born in 1920, Galston was a trailblazer in plant physiology, especially renowned for his work on auxins, the plant hormones that regulate growth. He famously studied the effects of light on plant development and was a critical voice in understanding how environmental factors impact crops. But Galston wasn't just a scientist in the lab; he was a giant in bioethics, tirelessly advocating for responsible scientific research and the ethical use of biotechnology. He famously blew the whistle on the use of Agent Orange in Vietnam, highlighting its devastating environmental and health impacts. Galston proved that a scientist could be both a meticulous researcher and a fierce moral compass, blurring the lines between pure science and social responsibility. He showed us that understanding nature also means protecting it, and us.
