Carl Gustav Hempel was the kind of philosopher who asked the big questions about science itself: How do scientists explain things? What makes an explanation good? How do we confirm a scientific theory? This German-American thinker became a leading figure in logical empiricism, meticulously dissecting the structure of scientific reasoning. He's most famous for his Deductive-Nomological (D-N) Model of explanation, which basically said that a good scientific explanation derives a phenomenon from general laws and specific conditions. While his models have been debated, challenged, and refined, Hempel provided the foundational framework for analyzing scientific practice, making the philosophy of science a rigorous academic discipline. He taught us to think critically about how science thinks, which is pretty meta and incredibly important.
