Herbert F. York started his career helping build the atomic bomb (a pretty big scientific splash, eh?). He then became the inaugural director of the super-secret, super-important Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. But here's the twist: this nuclear physicist became one of the loudest, most respected advocates for arms control and peace. He co-founded the UC Institute on Global Conflict and Cooperation and advised multiple presidents on limiting nuclear weapons. From building bombs to trying to banish them, York's journey was a fascinating one, proving scientists can change their minds – and maybe the world – for the better.
