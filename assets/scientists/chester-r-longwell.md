Chester R. Longwell, born in 1887, was a distinguished American geologist whose life's work largely focused on unraveling the complex, folded, and faulted secrets of the American West. A professor at Yale University for many years, Longwell was a master of field geology, meticulously mapping and interpreting the stratigraphy and structural geology of regions like the Basin and Range Province. His research, particularly on the Lake Mead region, provided crucial insights into tectonic processes and the mechanics of large-scale faulting. He was also a prominent educator and author, co-writing a highly influential geology textbook that inspired generations of students. Longwell's dedication to understanding Earth's dramatic past left an indelible mark on how we interpret the dynamic forces shaping our planet. He truly saw the world in layers.
