Gerd Binnig, a sharp German physicist, pretty much gave us X-ray vision for atoms, but way cooler! Along with Heinrich Rohrer, he co-invented the Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM) in 1981, earning them a share of the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1986. Before the STM, imagining individual atoms was like trying to sketch a ghost. Suddenly, we could "see" them, manipulate them, and even spell out words with them (IBM's "atoms" logo, anyone?). This wasn't just a fancy new gadget; it completely revolutionized surface science and paved the way for nanotechnology, letting us tinker with matter on its most fundamental level. Thanks to Gerd, we're building tiny wonders that were once sci-fi dreams.
