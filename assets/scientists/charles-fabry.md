Charles Fabry was a French physicist with a keen eye for light, and not just any light, but the nitty-gritty details of how it behaves. He's most famous for co-inventing the Fabry–Pérot interferometer with Alfred Pérot. This nifty device allows scientists to measure wavelengths of light with incredible precision, becoming a cornerstone for fields ranging from astronomy to spectroscopy. Thanks to Fabry, we could analyze light from distant stars, understand atomic structures better, and even develop technologies like lasers. He spent his career shedding light on the invisible, literally making the world a brighter, more measurable place for physicists. Talk about a bright idea!
