Charles Frederick Cross was a British chemist who, along with his colleague Edward J. Bevan, basically invented a new way to get dressed. In 1892, they developed the 'viscose process,' a revolutionary method for turning cellulose (from wood pulp!) into a versatile regenerated fiber: rayon. Suddenly, elegant fabrics could be produced much more affordably than silk, democratizing fashion and sparking the synthetic fiber industry. This wasn't just some lab experiment; it was an industrial game-changer that impacted textiles, tires, and countless other products. Cross helped turn trees into threads, proving that chemistry can be both practical and surprisingly chic. Quite the thread-setter, wouldn't you say?
