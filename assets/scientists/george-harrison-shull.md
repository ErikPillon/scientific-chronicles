Meet George Harrison Shull, the green-thumbed guru who basically reinvented corn. Back in the early 20th century, this American botanist and geneticist was tinkering with maize, and what he discovered—heterosis, or hybrid vigor—changed farming forever. By crossbreeding different corn lines, he found he could create super-strains that yielded way more crops, resisted diseases better, and generally just performed like agricultural superheroes. His work sparked the hybrid corn revolution, feeding millions and ensuring you have plenty of popcorn for your movie nights. He turned a simple plant into a powerhouse, proving that a little genetic ingenuity can go a long way in feeding the world.
