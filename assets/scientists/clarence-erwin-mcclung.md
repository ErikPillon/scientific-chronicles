Let's hear it for Clarence E. McClung, the American zoologist who basically figured out why we're boys or girls! Back in 1901, while meticulously studying grasshopper chromosomes (yes, grasshoppers!), he noticed something peculiar: one chromosome looked different from the others. He called it the 'accessory chromosome,' which we now know as the X chromosome. McClung proposed that this chromosome was responsible for determining sex. While he initially thought it determined maleness, it was later clarified that the presence or absence of a second sex chromosome (Y) alongside X dictated gender. His groundbreaking observation laid the foundation for our understanding of sex determination and the chromosomal theory of inheritance. He basically opened the door to modern genetics, proving that those tiny structures inside our cells hold the blueprint for who we are. Not bad for a guy who spent his days staring at bugs!
