If you're reading this on a device connected wirelessly, you owe a big thanks to Guglielmo Marconi! Born in Bologna, Italy, in 1874, Marconi was a self-taught electrical engineer with an obsession: harnessing Hertz's electromagnetic waves for practical communication. While others theorized, Marconi tinkered. He was the ultimate innovator, perfecting equipment in his parents' attic, eventually transmitting signals across his garden, then across open fields, and then, dramatically, across the Atlantic Ocean in 1901. His invention of practical radio telegraphy revolutionized communication, making ship-to-shore messages possible and saving countless lives at sea. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1909 for his pioneering work. Marconi didn't just invent a device; he sparked a global revolution in connectivity, laying the foundation for everything from broadcast radio to Wi-Fi. What a legend!
