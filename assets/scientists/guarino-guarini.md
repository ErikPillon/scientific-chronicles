Forget straight lines and simple angles, Guarino Guarini was here to show us that buildings could be mathematical puzzles and optical illusions all at once! Born in Modena, Italy, in 1624, Guarini was a Theatine priest who dabbled (masterfully, we might add) in architecture, mathematics, and philosophy. He was a virtuoso of the High Baroque, known for his incredibly complex and innovative architectural designs, particularly his domes and vaults. His buildings, like the Chapel of the Holy Shroud and the Palazzo Carignano in Turin, feature intricate geometric patterns, intersecting curves, and a playful use of light that creates dizzying and awe-inspiring spaces. Guarini wasn't just drawing plans; he was calculating the interplay of structure, light, and perspective to create dynamic, almost living buildings. He blended his deep understanding of geometry with a flair for the dramatic, making him one of the most original architectural minds of his era.
