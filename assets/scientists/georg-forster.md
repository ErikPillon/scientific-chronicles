Georg Forster was one of those adventurous Enlightenment figures who probably had 'will travel for science' tattooed on his arm. Born in 1754, this German (and Polish-born) naturalist was literally a globetrotter, most famously accompanying Captain James Cook on his second circumnavigation of the world. Imagine being a teenager and sailing around the world, discovering new species and cultures! Forster meticulously documented flora, fauna, and the lives of indigenous peoples, contributing immensely to natural history and ethnology. He was also a political radical, deeply influenced by the French Revolution, which ultimately led to his downfall. From collecting exotic specimens to championing republican ideals, Forster lived a life of intellectual curiosity and daring. He basically saw the world through a scientific lens and wasn't afraid to shake things up.
