Fritz Graebner was a German ethnologist who, at the turn of the 20th century, championed a rather ambitious idea: the 'Culture-circle' theory (Kulturkreislehre). He and his colleagues believed that cultures didn't just pop up randomly; they spread from a few original centers, like ripples in a pond. Graebner spent his career trying to trace these ancient 'culture circles' and their diffusion across the globe. While the theory has since faced heavy criticism and largely fallen out of favor, his meticulous approach to comparing cultural traits left a significant, if contentious, mark on early anthropology. He was definitely one for drawing big lines on the map!
