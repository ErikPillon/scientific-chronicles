Edmund Beecher Wilson was a rockstar in the early days of American biology. This brilliant zoologist and geneticist, often hailed as America's first modern cell biologist, literally wrote the book on the cell – his 'The Cell in Development and Heredity' (1896) was a bible for generations of biologists. But his biggest mic-drop moment? He and Nettie Stevens independently discovered the chromosomal basis of sex determination in 1905, basically figuring out that X and Y chromosomes are what make you male or female. This wasn't just a neat fact; it was a fundamental breakthrough in genetics, showing how heredity works at the chromosomal level. He basically showed us the tiny blueprints that make us who we are.
