Before James Clerk Maxwell famously unified electricity, magnetism, and light, there was Francesco Zantedeschi, an Italian physicist who was already connecting those dots. This curious cleric spent his days experimenting with electricity and optics, making some truly insightful observations in the early 19th century. He's often credited with being among the first to note that electric currents could induce magnetic effects, and perhaps even more remarkably, he suggested that visible light could have an electromagnetic origin way back in the 1830s. While Maxwell formalized the theory decades later, Zantedeschi was clearly on the right track, showing how keen observation and a good hunch can set the stage for monumental scientific breakthroughs. He was basically whispering 'light is electricity' when everyone else was still just playing with magnets.
