Before heart attacks were well understood, and before sickle cell disease had a name, there was James Bryan Herrick, the American physician who put these medical mysteries on the map. In 1910, he published the first comprehensive description of sickle cell anemia, after observing unusually shaped red blood cells in a dental student's blood. Ten years later, he courageously challenged prevailing medical dogma to suggest that coronary thrombosis (a blood clot in the heart's arteries) was the primary cause of myocardial infarction (a heart attack), rather than just a rare event. His insights revolutionized cardiology and hematology, paving the way for countless lives saved and better treatments for complex diseases. Talk about a doctor who saw things differently!
