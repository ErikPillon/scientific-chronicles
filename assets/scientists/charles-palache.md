Charles Palache, born in 1869, was an American mineralogist who really loved rocks – but not just any rocks, he loved knowing exactly *what* kind of rock they were. A long-time professor at Harvard, Palache became a leading authority on mineral classification, meticulously describing and cataloguing countless species. His magnum opus was a major revision of Dana's System of Mineralogy, the bible for mineralogists. Think of him as the ultimate librarian of the mineral kingdom, ensuring every crystalline book was in its proper place and perfectly described. If you ever wondered about the precise geometry or chemical makeup of a shiny specimen, Palache probably had a hand in defining it.
