Henri Becquerel was a French physicist from a family steeped in scientific tradition – and he certainly lived up to the legacy! In 1896, while investigating X-rays and phosphorescence, he had one of those truly serendipitous 'oops, I did it again!' moments. He had left some uranium salts on photographic plates in a dark drawer, expecting nothing unless exposed to sunlight. To his utter amazement, the plates were exposed anyway, proving that uranium itself was emitting some kind of invisible radiation. This accidental discovery of what Marie and Pierre Curie later dubbed 'radioactivity' completely shattered the prevailing view of atoms as immutable, paving the way for nuclear physics, atomic energy, and medical applications. Becquerel shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics for his groundbreaking work, forever etching his name into the annals of scientific discovery (and probably making him thankful for messy lab benches!).
