Charles Benedict Davenport was a pioneering American biologist who made significant contributions to the early development of quantitative genetics, daring to apply statistical methods to heredity. He was instrumental in establishing the Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory and played a key role in making genetics a recognized field of study. However, his legacy is overshadowed by his leadership in the American eugenics movement, where he promoted deeply flawed and harmful ideas about human heredity and 'racial hygiene.' His career highlights the complex and often troubling intersection of scientific inquiry, societal beliefs, and ethical responsibility in the early 20th century. While his early genetic work was innovative, his association with eugenics casts a long, dark shadow.
