If you're into celestial mechanics and mathematical wizardry, Anders Johan Lexell is your man. This Finnish-Swedish polymath was a star student of the legendary Leonhard Euler, and he certainly made his mentor proud. Lexell was a trailblazer in calculating comet orbits, even discovering the first comet whose elliptical orbit was successfully computed (now known as Lexell's Comet, D/1770 L1). This comet came ridiculously close to Earth in 1770, causing a stir among astronomers. He also made significant contributions to trigonometry and polygon geometry, proving that he wasn't just looking up, but also down at the numbers with equal brilliance. A true enlightenment thinker, he left us too soon, but his legacy in predicting the movements of our cosmic visitors lives on.
