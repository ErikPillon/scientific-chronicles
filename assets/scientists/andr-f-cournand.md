Before André F. Cournand, getting a good look inside a living human heart was practically science fiction. But this French-American physician, born in 1895, teamed up with Werner Forssmann and Dickinson Richards to perform what many considered medical heresy: cardiac catheterization. They didn't just poke around; they developed a safe and accurate technique to thread a thin tube directly into the heart, allowing doctors to measure pressures, take samples, and diagnose heart conditions without guessing. This brave, Nobel-winning work in the 1940s revolutionized cardiology, turning life-threatening cardiac procedures into routine diagnostics. Cournand didn't just push boundaries; he gave doctors an unprecedented window into the heart of the matter, literally.
