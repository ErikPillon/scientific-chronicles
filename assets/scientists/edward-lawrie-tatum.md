Edward Lawrie Tatum was a trailblazing geneticist who, alongside George Beadle, rocked the world of biology by showing us exactly how genes work their magic. Using the humble bread mold, *Neurospora crassa*, they demonstrated that individual genes are responsible for directing the synthesis of specific enzymes. This groundbreaking 'one gene–one enzyme' hypothesis (later refined to 'one gene–one polypeptide') earned them a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1958 and fundamentally connected genetics with biochemistry. Tatum basically gave us the blueprint for understanding how our DNA dictates our biological functions. Talk about a mic drop moment in science!
