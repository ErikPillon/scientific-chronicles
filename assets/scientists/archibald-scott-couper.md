Imagine being so brilliantly ahead of your time that your groundbreaking theory gets overshadowed by someone else publishing practically the same thing just weeks before! That was poor Archibald Scott Couper's fate. This Scottish chemist independently developed the radical idea that carbon atoms love to link up in chains and rings, a concept fundamental to understanding organic chemistry. Unfortunately, August Kekulé got his paper out first. Despite this, Couper's insight was profound, laying critical groundwork for how we visualize and comprehend complex organic molecules, even if history didn't give him the immediate, solo applause he deserved.
