If you've ever battled with factorials or grappled with combinations, you've probably encountered the ghost of James Stirling, a Scottish mathematical maestro! This 18th-century genius gave us 'Stirling's approximation,' a handy formula for estimating factorials, and the ever-so-useful 'Stirling numbers,' which pop up in combinatorics and probability like well-behaved, numerical rabbits. He was a master of infinite series and a friend of Isaac Newton, rubbing shoulders with the scientific elite. Stirling's work laid essential groundwork in calculus and discrete mathematics, proving that sometimes, the most elegant solutions come from the most intricate minds.
