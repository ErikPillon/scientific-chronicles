Ferdinand Cohn was a German botanist who looked beyond pretty flowers and peered into the microscopic world, becoming one of the founders of modern bacteriology. Born with hearing impairment, he used his sharp mind to classify bacteria based on their shapes – a system still foundational today. He was also the first to show that bacteria, like Bacillus subtilis, could form spores, which explained how they survived harsh conditions. Imagine trying to categorize these tiny, invisible creatures in the 19th century! His meticulous work helped legitimize microbiology as a scientific discipline and paved the way for future giants like Robert Koch. He was the original 'bacteria-ologist!'
