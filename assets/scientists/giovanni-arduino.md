Long before anyone was talking about plate tectonics, Giovanni Arduino, born in 1714, was getting down and dirty with rocks in the Italian Alps. Often hailed as the "Father of Italian Geology," Arduino was a true pioneer who, in 1759, proposed one of the first comprehensive classifications of Earth's crustal layers based on their composition and relative age. He introduced terms like "Primary," "Secondary," "Tertiary," and "Volcanic" to describe different rock types and their formation sequence, laying crucial groundwork for modern geological periods. While his terms have evolved, his systematic approach to understanding Earth's history from its rocks was revolutionary. He essentially taught us to read the planet's autobiography written in stone, making him a rock star (pun intended!) of early geology.
