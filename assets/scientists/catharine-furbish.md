Kate Furbish was no armchair botanist. This intrepid American scientist spent decades fearlessly tramping through the wilds of Maine, often alone, collecting, drawing, and cataloging every plant she could find. Born in 1834, she wasn't content with just looking; she created an astonishing 14-volume herbarium and over 1,300 detailed watercolors. Her work was so precise and comprehensive that it remains an invaluable record of Maine's historical flora, a true botanical treasure. She even had a plant, Furbish's lousewort (Pedicularis furbishiae), named after her – talk about leaving your mark! Her dedication to fieldwork, at a time when women were rarely seen in such roles, makes her a true unsung hero of natural history.
