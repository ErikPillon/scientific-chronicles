Emilio Segrè was a physicist who loved a good puzzle, especially if it involved matter – or rather, *antimatter*. Born in Italy, he worked under the legendary Enrico Fermi before fleeing fascism for the US. There, he became a key player in the Manhattan Project, helping develop the atomic bomb. But his biggest claim to fame came later: in 1955, working with Owen Chamberlain at Berkeley, he co-discovered the antiproton. This wasn't just a scientific curiosity; it was a monumental achievement, confirming the existence of antimatter particles and earning them the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1959. Essentially, Segrè peered into the fundamental fabric of the universe and found an 'evil twin' to one of its most basic components. Talk about a groundbreaking discovery!
