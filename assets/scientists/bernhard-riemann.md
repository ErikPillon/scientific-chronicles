Bernhard Riemann, born in Germany in 1826, was a mathematical supernova who burned incredibly bright but far too short. Despite his brief life, he completely redefined what mathematics could do. His groundbreaking 1854 lecture, 'On the Hypotheses that Lie at the Bases of Geometry,' introduced non-Euclidean geometries, which seemed abstract at the time but later became the very language Albert Einstein used to describe the curvature of spacetime in his theory of General Relativity. He also tackled complex analysis, defining the Riemann integral and the Riemann surface, and left us the infamous Riemann Hypothesis in number theory, still one of the biggest unsolved problems in math today. Imagine having ideas so far ahead of your time that they literally shape physics a century later! Riemann died tragically young in 1866 at just 39, but his brilliance continues to echo through the halls of mathematics and physics.
