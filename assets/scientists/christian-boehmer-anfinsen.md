Christian Anfinsen, an American biochemist, basically solved a major protein puzzle. Imagine having a long, floppy string (a protein chain) and needing it to fold into a specific, intricate knot to do its job. Anfinsen, in his brilliant experiments with the enzyme ribonuclease, demonstrated that all the instructions needed for this complex 3D folding were *already present* in the protein's linear sequence of amino acids. This wasn't just a neat trick; it was a foundational principle, known as 'Anfinsen's dogma,' that utterly transformed molecular biology and earned him a Nobel Prize in 1972. He showed us that life's most complex machines — proteins — self-assemble with remarkable elegance, based on their inherent chemical code. Mind. Blown.
