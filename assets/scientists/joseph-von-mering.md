Joseph von Mering, the German physician with a knack for groundbreaking discoveries, dramatically altered our understanding of diabetes. In a classic scientific collaboration, he teamed up with Oskar Minkowski to surgically remove the pancreas from dogs, observing that they subsequently developed severe diabetes. This experiment was a lightbulb moment, proving the pancreas's critical role in regulating blood sugar and paving the way for insulin research. But von Mering wasn't a one-trick pony; he also ventured into pharmacology. He co-developed Veronal (barbital), the first clinically useful barbiturate, which was a game-changer for treating insomnia and anxiety, though its addictive properties would become apparent later. He didn't just treat symptoms; he sought out root causes and novel solutions, leaving a dual legacy that impacted both endocrinology and pharmacology, and literally helped millions sleep better – for a while, at least!
