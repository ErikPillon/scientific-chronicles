Before big data, there was James Dunwoody Brownson De Bow, the stats guru of the American South! As the editor and publisher of 'De Bow's Review' from 1846 to 1867, he created the most important statistical and economic journal of the antebellum period. This wasn't just dry numbers; De Bow's publication was a treasure trove of information on Southern commerce, agriculture, demographics, and culture, effectively documenting the region's society in incredible detail. He was a master compiler and disseminator of information, using empirical data to discuss everything from cotton production to public health. While his views often reflected the controversial sentiments of his time, his dedication to gathering and publishing statistical data was pioneering, making him a central figure in early American social science and a historian's goldmine for understanding the era.
