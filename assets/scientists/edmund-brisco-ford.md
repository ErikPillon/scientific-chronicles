Edmund Brisco Ford, or E.B. Ford as he's often known, was a British biologist who pretty much invented the field of ecological genetics. Forget boring lab experiments; Ford took genetics out into the wild, studying how evolution unfolds in real-time within natural populations. He spent decades meticulously observing species like butterflies and moths, tracking genetic changes in response to environmental pressures. His classic work on the Peppered Moth and industrial melanism is legendary, a textbook example of natural selection in action. He wasn't just observing; he was demonstrating how genetics, ecology, and evolution are deeply intertwined, changing how we understand biodiversity and adaptation. If you've ever wondered how animals evolve in their natural habitats, you have Ford to thank for showing us the way.
