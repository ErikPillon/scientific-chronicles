Meet R. Bruce Merrifield, the American biochemist who made building proteins as easy as LEGO (well, almost!). Born in 1921, Merrifield picked up the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1984 for inventing solid-phase peptide synthesis. Before him, synthesizing proteins and peptides in the lab was a slow, messy, and incredibly inefficient process. Merrifield's genius idea was to anchor the growing peptide chain to an insoluble polymer support, allowing for rapid washing and reagent addition without having to purify intermediates. This wasn't just a clever trick; it completely revolutionized biochemistry, making it possible to quickly create custom peptides for drug discovery, biological research, and so much more. He literally built the molecular tools that shaped modern medicine!
