When Henry Ford started putting America on wheels, someone needed to put those wheels on *tires* – and that someone was Harvey S. Firestone. This ingenious American businessman and inventor was a pioneer in the rubber industry. In 1900, he founded Firestone Tire and Rubber Company in Akron, Ohio, right at the dawn of the automobile age. Firestone didn't just sell tires; he innovated. He championed the shift from solid rubber carriage tires to pneumatic tires for automobiles, significantly improving ride comfort and safety. He also famously struck a deal with Henry Ford, becoming a primary supplier for the Ford Motor Company, and later established his own rubber plantations in Liberia to ensure a steady supply. Firestone's relentless pursuit of better and more affordable tires helped make the automobile a practical reality for the average person, literally smoothing the path for modern transportation.
