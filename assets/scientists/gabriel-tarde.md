Before memes and viral trends, there was Gabriel Tarde, the French sociologist, criminologist, and social psychologist who had a seriously interesting take on how society works. While his contemporary Émile Durkheim focused on collective forces, Tarde argued that society was built from the bottom up, through individual interactions based on imitation and innovation. Think of it: one person invents something cool, others imitate it, and then someone else innovates on *that*. It’s a bit like a social ripple effect! He applied this idea to everything from language and law to fashion and crime, suggesting that social change is a continuous process of people copying, modifying, and spreading ideas. Tarde's ideas, though overshadowed for a time, are finding new relevance in our interconnected, viral world, proving that some old ideas never truly go out of style.
