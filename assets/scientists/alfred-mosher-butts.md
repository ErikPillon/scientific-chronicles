Alfred Mosher Butts was an architect by trade, but his real legacy is spelling out fun for generations! Yep, this clever fellow is the mastermind behind Scrabble, the board game that has probably caused more friendly (and not-so-friendly) arguments about obscure two-letter words than any other. Stuck in the Great Depression and out of architecture work, Butts got curious about what makes games tick. He analyzed existing games, realizing they fell into categories: number games (like dice), move games (like chess), and word games. He combined a crossword puzzle with anagrams, even meticulously counting letter frequencies in newspapers like the New York Times to perfectly balance the tile distribution. Who knew lexical statistics could be so entertaining? So, the next time you drop a Q on a triple-word score, give a little nod to Butts, the architect who built a vocabulary empire, one perfectly placed tile at a time.
