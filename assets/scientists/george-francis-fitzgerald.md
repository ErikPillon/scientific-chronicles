Before Einstein became the rockstar of relativity, there was George Francis Fitzgerald, an Irish physicist with some seriously ahead-of-his-time ideas. In 1889, trying to explain the baffling null result of the Michelson-Morley experiment, he proposed that objects moving at high speeds actually *contract* in the direction of their motion. This "Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction" (Hendrik Lorentz published a similar idea later) was a brilliant, if initially strange, solution that paved the way for special relativity. Fitzgerald was a visionary who wrestled with the strange behavior of light and motion, showing that sometimes, the most outlandish ideas are the ones that are actually right.
