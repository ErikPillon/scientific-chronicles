Edwin J. Houston was one of those brilliant minds from the 19th century who didn't just understand electricity but helped wire the world. This American electrical engineer and inventor wasn't content with just theorizing; he co-founded the Thomson-Houston Electric Company, which later merged to become the behemoth we now know as General Electric. From arc lamps to streetcars, Houston's patents lit up cities and got people moving. He also dabbled in education, making sure the next generation of bright sparks could keep the lights on. A true pioneer who electrifyingly shaped modern industry, he truly was a founding father of American electrical power.
