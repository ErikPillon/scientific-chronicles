Before Georg Brandt came along, chemists were mostly stuck with the metals the ancients knew. But this Swedish chemist and mineralogist, born in 1694, changed the game. He wasn't just tinkering in the lab; he was on a mission to figure out what made certain minerals create vibrant blue glass. Turns out, it wasn't bismuth or iron, as many suspected. In 1735, Brandt isolated a completely new element – *cobalt* – thereby earning him the title of discoverer of the first metal unknown to the ancients. Imagine the 'aha!' moment! He proved that this mysterious substance, often mistaken for other metals, was the true pigment for those stunning blue hues. Thanks to Brandt, our world got a little bluer and chemistry got a whole new element to play with.
