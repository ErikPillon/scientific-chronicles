Jean-Marie Lehn helped pioneer supramolecular chemistry, the study of how molecules assemble themselves into larger structures through weak, reversible interactions rather than strong permanent chemical bonds, essentially chemistry that mimics the self-assembling tricks biology uses constantly (think DNA strands recognizing their complementary partner). He designed 'cryptands,' cage-like molecules that can selectively grab and hold specific ions, a foundational tool for the field. This work, alongside Donald Cram and Charles Pedersen, earned the trio the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Lehn's ideas opened the door to designing molecular machines, sensors, and self-healing materials, all built on the principle that sometimes the cleverest chemistry isn't about forcing atoms together, but about designing shapes that fit precisely enough to find each other on their own. 🧩⚗️
