Franklin W. Stahl is a molecular biology rockstar who, along with Matthew Meselson, famously proved how DNA makes copies of itself. In 1958, their elegant Meselson–Stahl experiment (often called 'the most beautiful experiment in biology') provided definitive evidence for semiconservative DNA replication—meaning each new DNA molecule consists of one old strand and one newly synthesized strand. This wasn't just a cool fact; it was a cornerstone discovery that confirmed Watson and Crick's model and underpinned our entire understanding of heredity. Stahl's genius lies in his ability to design experiments that are both profound in their implications and remarkably clean in their execution. He literally showed us how life copies itself, ensuring genetics made sense and paving the way for countless future discoveries.
