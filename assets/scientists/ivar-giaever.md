Ivar Giaever, born in Norway in 1929, is a solid-state physics rockstar who shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1973. His big "aha!" moment came from his experimental work on electron tunneling through thin insulators separating superconductors. Imagine electrons doing a vanishing act and reappearing on the other side! This phenomenon, known as quantum tunneling, was already theorized, but Giaever's meticulous experiments provided crucial empirical evidence and detailed insights, opening new doors for understanding superconductivity. His work has had profound implications for electronics and materials science. This Norwegian-American physicist is still with us, proving that brilliance can have a long shelf life.
