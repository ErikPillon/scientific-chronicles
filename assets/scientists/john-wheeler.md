John Wheeler worked alongside Niels Bohr on early nuclear fission theory and later collaborated closely with Albert Einstein at Princeton, but his most lasting public legacy might be linguistic: he popularized the term 'black hole' in 1967 to describe collapsed stars whose gravity is so strong not even light escapes, and also coined 'wormhole' to describe theoretical shortcuts through spacetime. Beyond the memorable terminology, Wheeler did serious foundational work in general relativity and quantum gravity, and mentored an extraordinary roster of students including Richard Feynman and Kip Thorne. He had a gift for asking deceptively simple, almost childlike questions about the universe ('why does anything exist at all?') that pushed physics into genuinely new conceptual territory. Sometimes the biggest contribution to physics is just naming things well enough that everyone else can talk about them. 🕳️🌌
