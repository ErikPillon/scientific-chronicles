Eric Temple Bell, born in Scotland in 1883, was a fascinating character who led a double life – or perhaps a triple life! By day, he was a serious mathematician contributing to number theory and combinatorics. By night, he was a prolific science fiction writer under the pen name John Taine. But perhaps his most enduring legacy is his captivating popular histories of mathematics, most famously "Men of Mathematics" (1937). With a flair for the dramatic and a knack for storytelling, Bell brought the lives of mathematical giants to life, making their genius (and their quirks) accessible to a wide audience. He showed us that mathematicians weren't just dusty academics, but passionate, often eccentric, individuals who shaped our world with their minds. He made math history, well, *history*.
