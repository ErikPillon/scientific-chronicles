When Allied forces needed to storm the beaches of Normandy on D-Day, they faced a massive problem: how to get endless supplies ashore without a port. Enter Allan Beckett, a British civil engineer born in 1914, who became one of the unsung heroes of World War II. Beckett designed the 'Whale' roadways, the floating sections that connected the 'Phoenix' caissons (massive concrete blocks) to the shore, forming the iconic Mulberry Harbours. His brilliant designs ensured these temporary, pre-fabricated ports could withstand the tumultuous English Channel and tides, allowing millions of tons of equipment and troops to land. It was a feat of engineering ingenuity that played a crucial role in the success of the Normandy landings. Talk about building a path to victory!
