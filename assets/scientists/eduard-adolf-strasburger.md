Eduard Adolf Strasburger was a titan of 19th-century botany, often considered one of the founders of modern plant cytology. This German-Polish scientist was obsessed with what was happening *inside* plant cells. He painstakingly observed and described processes like mitosis (cell division), fertilization, and the movement of sap in plants, even coining terms still used today. His 1875 work 'Ueber Zellbildung und Zelltheilung' (On Cell Formation and Cell Division) was a game-changer, establishing many of the fundamental principles of cell biology. He proved that new cells arise from existing cells and clarified the role of the nucleus in heredity. Basically, if you wanted to know how plants grow, reproduce, and function at a microscopic level, Strasburger was your go-to guy, dissecting the secrets of the botanical world with unmatched precision.
