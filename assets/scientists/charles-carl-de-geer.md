Step aside, Linnaeus! While Carl Linnaeus was busy naming everything under the sun, Charles De Geer, a contemporary Swedish entomologist, was deep-diving into the insect world with unparalleled zeal. Often called the 'Father of Swedish Entomology,' De Geer was a meticulous observer and illustrator, describing hundreds of new insect species. His magnum opus, *Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire des insectes*, published in seven volumes, was a monumental achievement in natural history, filled with exquisite detail and accurate depictions. His work provided an invaluable foundation for future studies of insect life, proving that even the smallest creatures could inspire monumental scientific contributions. He basically wrote the book on creepy crawlies!
