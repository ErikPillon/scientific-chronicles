Edsel Ford, son of the legendary Henry Ford, stepped into some seriously big shoes, becoming president of Ford Motor Company in 1919. While his dad was the genius behind the Model T and the assembly line, Edsel brought a different kind of vision: he pushed for design, style, and engineering excellence beyond mere utility. He was instrumental in creating iconic models like the Lincoln Continental and the Mercury brand, understanding that people wanted more than just basic transportation. He invested in aerodynamics, art and design studios, and generally tried to drag Ford into the modern era, often clashing with his conservative father. Though the car named after him later became a notorious flop, Edsel's legacy is one of forward-thinking leadership and a passion for automotive aesthetics and advanced engineering that shaped the industry.
