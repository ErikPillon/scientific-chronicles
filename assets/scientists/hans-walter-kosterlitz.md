Hans Walter Kosterlitz was a German-born British pharmacologist who, in the mid-1970s, helped us understand why a runner's high feels so good and how acupuncture might work. Along with John Hughes, he co-discovered enkephalins – the brain's very own opioid peptides, soon followed by the broader category of endorphins. Before their groundbreaking work, it was known that opiate drugs like morphine had powerful pain-relieving effects, but the 'why' remained a mystery. Kosterlitz's team found that our brains naturally produce substances that act like internal morphine, binding to the same receptors. This discovery was a massive leap, not only explaining how pain is regulated but also opening new avenues for understanding addiction, mood, and stress. So, the next time you feel a surge of euphoria after a good workout or find relief from pain, send a mental high-five to Hans Kosterlitz – the man who showed us our bodies come with a built-in pharmacy, dispensing nature's very own happy pills.
