Cesare Lombroso was an Italian physician and criminologist who became a rockstar in the late 19th century for his groundbreaking, if utterly bonkers by today's standards, theories on criminal anthropology. He believed criminals weren't made, but *born* – literally identifiable by their physical features like sloping foreheads, long arms, or asymmetrical faces. While his ideas about 'atavism' (criminals being evolutionary throwbacks) are now thoroughly debunked, Lombroso single-handedly kickstarted the scientific study of crime, pulling it out of the realm of pure moral philosophy and into an empirical (if flawed) framework. He might have been wrong about the pointy ears, but he certainly got people thinking about why people commit crimes, laying some bizarre groundwork for modern criminology.
