Helen Sharman wasn't always dreaming of stardust; she was a chemist for Mars, making chocolate flavors just right. But in 1989, a radio advertisement for 'Project Juno' changed everything: 'Astronaut wanted. No experience necessary.' Out of 13,000 applicants, Helen was chosen, undergoing rigorous training in Star City, Russia. On May 18, 1991, she blasted off aboard a Soyuz spacecraft to the Mir space station, making her the first Briton, and the first Western European woman, in space. During her eight-day mission, she conducted medical and agricultural experiments, communicated with British schoolchildren, and proved that ordinary people (who are also highly intelligent scientists, of course!) could reach for the stars. Helen didn't just break a barrier; she ignited a national fascination with space, inspiring a generation to look beyond the mundane and imagine what's possible. She's living proof that sometimes, the most extraordinary journeys begin with a surprising advertisement.
