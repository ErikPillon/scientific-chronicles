Fritz Schaudinn was a brilliant, albeit tragically short-lived, German zoologist and microbiologist who made a discovery that literally changed the course of public health. In 1905, working with Erich Hoffmann, he peered through his microscope and identified *Treponema pallidum* as the spirochete responsible for syphilis. Before this, the causative agent was unknown, making diagnosis and treatment a shot in the dark. Schaudinn’s discovery was a massive leap forward, allowing for targeted therapies and prevention. He might have died young, but his impact on understanding and fighting infectious diseases was huge!
