Garrett Hardin was an American ecologist and microbiologist who really made people think – and sometimes argue! He's most famous for his 1968 essay, "The Tragedy of the Commons." In it, he explored how individuals acting independently and rationally according to their own self-interest can ultimately deplete a shared limited resource, even when it's clear that such actions are not in anyone's long-term interest. Think overfishing, pollution, or even overuse of public parks. This concept became a cornerstone in environmental science, economics, and political science, forcing us to consider how we manage shared resources in a world with finite limits. His ideas were provocative, often controversial, but undeniably shaped conversations about sustainability, population growth, and human impact on the planet, making him a figure whose work is still debated today.
