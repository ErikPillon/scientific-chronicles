Charles Louis Fefferman, born in '49, was a bit of a prodigy, wasn't he? He was doing university-level math at age 12, published his first scientific paper at 15, and snagged his PhD from Princeton at a mere 20. Not content with just being smart, he then became the youngest tenured professor at Princeton at 22, because, why not? His work in harmonic analysis was so revolutionary, it earned him the prestigious Fields Medal in 1978. Basically, he showed up, understood things no one else did, and made it look easy. Your average Tuesday for Charles, a mind-bending revelation for the rest of the mathematical world.
