Before J.J. Thomson "discovered" the electron, someone had to give the theoretical particle a name, right? Enter G. Johnstone Stoney, the brilliant Irish physicist and astronomer who, in 1891, coined the term "electron" for the fundamental unit of electric charge. He didn't just pluck it out of thin air; he actually made remarkably accurate estimations of its value decades earlier in 1874 and 1881. Talk about being ahead of the curve! Stoney was a true polymath, dabbling in everything from gas dynamics to stellar spectroscopy. While Thomson gets the glory for the experimental proof, Stoney was the conceptual godfather, providing the linguistic and theoretical groundwork for one of the most fundamental particles in the universe. Quite a legacy for a word!
