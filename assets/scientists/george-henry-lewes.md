Forget "jack of all trades," George Henry Lewes was a master of many! This Victorian polymath wasn't just a sharp literary critic and the legendary partner of George Eliot; he was also a serious self-taught scientist. He delved deep into physiology and psychology, conducting experiments and writing extensively about the nervous system, sensation, and animal behavior. He explored the links between mind and body with an intellectual rigor that was ahead of his time, contributing to the foundations of modern psychology. Lewes proved that you don't need a fancy degree to make significant scientific contributions—just an insatiable curiosity and a brilliant mind that could tackle everything from Shakespeare to neural pathways.
