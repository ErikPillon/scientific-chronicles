Asaph Hall, an American astronomer with a knack for spotting the cosmic underdog. In 1877, using the then-powerful 26-inch refractor at the U.S. Naval Observatory, he spotted two tiny, irregularly shaped moons orbiting Mars, which he named Phobos (panic) and Deimos (terror) – quite fitting for Mars, the god of war! He nearly gave up the search, but his wife, Angeline, encouraged him to persevere. Good thing she did, or Mars might still be a lonely planet. His discovery was a huge win for observational astronomy and proved that even small celestial bodies can hide in plain sight.
