Donald E. Osterbrock was the kind of astronomer who loved a good cosmic mystery, especially if it involved black holes doing something dramatic. A true trailblazer in studying active galactic nuclei (AGN) – those super bright cores of some galaxies – he spent his career unraveling how supermassive black holes gobble up matter and spit out incredible amounts of energy. He was also a master at dissecting the light from gaseous nebulae, those stunning cosmic clouds where stars are born, telling us what they're made of and how they behave. Beyond his own research, Osterbrock was a brilliant mentor and a historian of astronomy, helping us understand not just the universe, but also how we came to understand it. He literally looked into the abyss and helped us stare back, fearlessly.
