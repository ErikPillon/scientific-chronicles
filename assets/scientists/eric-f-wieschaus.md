Ever wonder how a tiny egg knows how to grow into a complex organism? Eric F. Wieschaus, along with Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard, peered into that very mystery using humble fruit flies (Drosophila melanogaster). Born in 1947, this American developmental biologist embarked on a monumental task: identifying the genes that orchestrate early embryonic development. They systematically mutated fruit flies and observed the resulting defects, pinpointing genes responsible for everything from segment formation to the body plan itself. Their groundbreaking work earned them a share of the 1995 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, revealing fundamental principles of development that apply across the animal kingdom. So, next time you see a fruit fly buzzing around your banana, remember Eric Wieschaus – he helped us understand how we all get our wings (or, well, our segments).
