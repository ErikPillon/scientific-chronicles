Ever notice how the mighty Gulf Stream zips along the East Coast of North America, much faster and narrower than currents on the eastern side of the Atlantic? That's thanks to folks like Henry Melson Stommel, an absolute titan of American oceanography. In the mid-20th century, Stommel cracked the code on "western boundary current intensification," explaining why these powerful currents tend to get squeezed and speed up along the western boundaries of ocean basins. He used elegant theoretical models and observations to reveal the hidden dynamics of global ocean circulation, influencing generations of oceanographers. Stommel wasn't just observing the ocean; he was uncovering its profound, unseen choreography.
