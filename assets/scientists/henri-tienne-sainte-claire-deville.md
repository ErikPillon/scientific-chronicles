Henri-Étienne Sainte-Claire Deville was a French chemist who loved a good challenge, especially if it involved extreme heat. He was a master of high-temperature chemistry, most famously figuring out how to produce aluminum in industrial quantities long before it became a common metal. He also dabbled with platinum group metals, basically making them useful and accessible. This guy wasn't afraid to turn up the heat in the lab, and his efforts paved the way for modern metallurgy, making everything from aircraft to jewelry a bit more shiny and practical.
