Before anyone even dreamed of walking on the moon, Hermann Oberth was already drawing up the blueprints for getting there. This visionary Austro-German physicist and engineer was one of the 'fathers of rocketry,' alongside Tsiolkovsky and Goddard. His 1923 book, 'By Rocket into Interplanetary Space,' laid out the mathematical theory for how rockets could achieve spaceflight, including multi-stage rockets and liquid-fueled engines, concepts that were considered pure science fiction at the time. He not only dreamed big but provided the serious scientific calculations to back it up, inspiring a generation of rocket scientists, including Wernher von Braun. Oberth proved that sometimes, the future is built not with steel, but with audacious equations and an unshakeable belief in the stars.
