In the 19th century, before psychology became a formal science, there were thinkers like Andrew Combe. This Scottish physician was a leading light in phrenology – the once-popular idea that personality traits could be determined by bumps on the skull (yes, really!). While we now know phrenology isn't quite scientific, Combe was a respected medical doctor of his time, writing influential books on physiology and the laws of health, which were widely read and helped shape public understanding of medicine. He focused on hygiene, diet, and lifestyle as crucial for well-being, ideas that were quite progressive for his era. He might have been a bit off on the brain-bumps, but he was certainly advocating for a holistic approach to health long before it was cool.
