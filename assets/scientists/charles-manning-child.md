Charles Manning Child, an American zoologist born in 1869, wasn't just observing creatures; he was trying to figure out how they *became* creatures. He spent his career poking around with worms and other invertebrates, eventually developing his famous 'gradient theory.' This wasn't about hills and valleys, but about physiological gradients within an organism that dictate its development and regeneration. Basically, he suggested that a chemical 'signal strength' across an organism tells its cells what to do and where to grow. Pretty neat stuff for the early 20th century, laying groundwork for future developmental biologists to build upon. He was the guy who made us think twice about how a worm knows which end is up!
