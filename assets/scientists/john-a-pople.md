John Pople developed and refined computational methods that let chemists calculate the properties, structure, and reactivity of molecules using quantum mechanics and a computer, rather than relying purely on lab experiments. He created the widely used Gaussian software package, which became (and largely remains) one of the standard tools computational chemists use worldwide to model everything from simple molecules to complex reactions. This turned theoretical chemistry from a largely abstract pursuit into a genuinely predictive, practical tool used across academic and industrial labs. He shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Walter Kohn for developing these computational methods, a rare Nobel awarded essentially for brilliant software and math rather than a physical lab discovery. 💻⚛️
