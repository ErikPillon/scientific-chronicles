Dr. C. Everett Koop was no ordinary doctor; he was a medical titan with a beard to match. A pioneering pediatric surgeon who achieved major breakthroughs in the treatment of birth defects, he later became the U.S. Surgeon General under Reagan. In this role, he fearlessly took on the AIDS epidemic and the tobacco industry, educating the public with blunt honesty despite political pressures. He transformed the Surgeon General's post from a ceremonial position to a powerful platform for public health advocacy, proving that a doctor's duty extends far beyond the operating room – sometimes, it's about speaking truth to power.
