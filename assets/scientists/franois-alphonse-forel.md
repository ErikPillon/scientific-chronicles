Ever wondered who decided studying lakes should be a serious science? That would be François-Alphonse Forel, a Swiss physician turned natural scientist who practically invented the field of limnology. Back in the late 19th century, while others were gazing at stars or digging up dinosaurs, Forel was knee-deep (sometimes literally) in Lake Geneva, meticulously documenting its currents, temperatures, and biology. His comprehensive "Le Léman" series didn't just tell you about one lake; it laid out the scientific framework for understanding *all* lakes. He even coined the term "seiche" for those lake oscillations. He proved that lakes weren't just pretty scenery, but complex ecosystems deserving of their own scientific spotlight.
