Meet Jacques Boucher de Perthes, the 19th-century French customs official who moonlighted as a serious fossil hunter. While most folks were busy thinking human history was a fairly recent affair, Jacques was out in the Somme Valley, digging up ancient tools alongside bones of long-gone mammoths and rhinos. His groundbreaking work, particularly his book 'Antiquités celtiques et antédiluviennes,' was initially scoffed at by the scientific establishment. Imagine being told humans were around way before the 'Great Flood'! But Boucher de Perthes stuck to his guns, patiently gathering evidence. It took a while, but eventually, the big guns like Lyell and Prestwich came, saw, and were conquered by his compelling proof. He didn't just find old stuff; he fundamentally reshaped our understanding of human deep time, dragging archaeology kicking and screaming into the prehistoric era. A true pioneer who dared to look beyond the accepted narrative, proving that sometimes, the amateur with an open mind can overturn centuries of dogma.
