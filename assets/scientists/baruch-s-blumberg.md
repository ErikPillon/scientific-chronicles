Baruch S. Blumberg was a medical detective who literally saved millions of lives. Back in the 1960s, while studying variations in blood proteins, he stumbled upon an antigen associated with hepatitis B. This wasn't just a quirky observation; it led him directly to the discovery of the hepatitis B virus itself. But Blumberg didn't stop there. He then spearheaded the development of the first vaccine against hepatitis B, turning a deadly global scourge into a preventable disease. For this monumental achievement, he shared the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1976. His work is a shining example of how fundamental research can have a profound, planet-altering impact on public health.
