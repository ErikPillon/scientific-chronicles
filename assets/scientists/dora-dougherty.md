During World War II, the B-29 Superfortress had such a fearsome reputation for engine fires that male pilots were getting cold feet about flying it. So the Army's response was, essentially, 'fine, we'll get women to fly it and see how embarrassed you get.' Dora Dougherty, a Women Airforce Service Pilot (WASP), was hand-picked with a colleague to train on the giant bomber and fly demonstration flights in front of skeptical male crews. It worked exactly as intended: morale and confidence in the aircraft shot up. Dougherty went on to a long career in aviation, later setting helicopter altitude and speed records and earning a doctorate in psychology to boot. Turns out embarrassing people into competence is a legitimate strategy. 🛩️👩‍✈️
