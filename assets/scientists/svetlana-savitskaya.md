Following in the footsteps of Valentina Tereshkova, Svetlana Savitskaya took women's space exploration to the next level. Born in 1948, this accomplished Soviet test pilot and cosmonaut became the second woman in space, but her real claim to fame came on July 25, 1984. During her second mission aboard Salyut 7, she exited the space station, becoming the *first woman ever* to perform a spacewalk. For 3 hours and 35 minutes, she floated outside, cutting and welding metals in the vacuum of space, demonstrating incredible skill and nerve. Her feat was a powerful symbol of capability and courage, smashing another barrier and proving that the final frontier was truly for everyone, no matter their gender. Talk about a cosmic commute!
