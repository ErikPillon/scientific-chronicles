Before there were five kingdoms (or more!), Herbert Copeland was busy adding a crucial one: Monera. Frustrated with the two-kingdom plant-and-animal world, he first proposed Protista, then argued for Monera (bacteria and archaea) in 1956. This wasn't just academic hair-splitting; it fundamentally changed how we categorize and understand Earth's diverse life forms. So, next time you think about bacteria having their own kingdom, give a nod to Copeland – the unsung hero who helped clean up biology's taxonomy act.
