Ignaz Venetz, born in 1788, was a Swiss civil engineer and a keen observer of the Alps who fundamentally changed how we view Earth's history. While others scratched their heads over massive boulders found far from their mountain origins, Venetz looked at the glaciers and put two and two together. In 1821, he boldly suggested that the Alpine glaciers had once been far more extensive, transporting these 'erratic boulders' across valleys. This wasn't a popular idea at first; it seemed too wild! But his meticulous observations and logical deductions laid crucial groundwork for the 'ice age' theory, later championed by scientists like Louis Agassiz. Venetz helped us see that Earth's climate history wasn't static, but a dynamic, icy saga.
