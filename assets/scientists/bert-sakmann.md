Bert Sakmann, born in Stuttgart, Germany, in 1942, is a living legend in neurobiology whose work has literally changed how we understand the brain. Alongside his colleague Erwin Neher, Sakmann developed the groundbreaking 'patch clamp' technique in the 1970s. Imagine being able to listen to the whispers of a single ion channel—tiny pores in cell membranes that control electrical signals. That's what the patch clamp allowed! By suctioning a tiny glass pipette onto a cell membrane, they could isolate and measure the minuscule currents flowing through individual channels. This opened up an entirely new window into how neurons communicate, how muscles contract, and how our senses work. For this revolutionary technological leap, Sakmann shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1991. His work continues to be fundamental to neuroscience research worldwide.
