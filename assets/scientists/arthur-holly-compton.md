Arthur Holly Compton was an American physicist who gave us a whole new way to look at light – not just as a wave, but as a particle, too! In 1923, he performed experiments demonstrating that when X-rays hit electrons, they lose energy and change wavelength, much like billiard balls colliding. This phenomenon, now famously called the Compton effect, provided irrefutable proof of the photon (the particle of light) and earned him a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1927. His work was a cornerstone of quantum mechanics, helping to cement the wave-particle duality concept and proving that even light knows how to play both sides of the fence. He was also a key figure in the Manhattan Project.
