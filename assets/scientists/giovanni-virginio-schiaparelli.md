In 1877, using a modest telescope in Milan, Giovanni Schiaparelli made detailed maps of Mars and described what he called 'canali,' Italian for channels or grooves, referring to what he believed were natural surface features. The problem was translation: 'canali' got rendered into English as 'canals,' implying something built rather than something natural. That single word choice launched decades of public fascination (and plenty of pseudoscience) about intelligent Martian civilizations digging planet-spanning irrigation networks, most famously fueled by American astronomer Percival Lowell. Schiaparelli himself was more cautious about the implications, but he never quite managed to put the genie back in the bottle. He also did serious, less mistranslated work on asteroid orbits and meteor showers, connecting the Perseids to the debris trail of a specific comet. 🔴📡
