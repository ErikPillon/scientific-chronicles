Bernd T. Matthias, born in Frankfurt in 1918, was the Indiana Jones of superconductivity. While others were carefully crafting theories, Matthias was a hands-on experimentalist, mixing and matching elements in his lab like a mad alchemist, always chasing the next high-temperature superconductor. He discovered more superconducting materials than anyone else, pushing the boundaries of what was thought possible for materials to conduct electricity without resistance. He wasn't afraid to trust his gut and his immense chemical intuition, often making discoveries that baffled his more theoretical colleagues. His approach was famously summarized as: 'Superconductivity is found in materials that are on the verge of being unstable.' Matthias was instrumental in the field for decades, from his work at Bell Labs to UCSD, until his death in 1980. He showed that sometimes, the best way to find a treasure is to just keep digging.
