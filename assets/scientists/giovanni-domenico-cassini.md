Giovanni Domenico Cassini, born in 1625 in Italy, was a rockstar astronomer who later became a fixture in French science. Recruited by Louis XIV to help establish the Paris Observatory, he became its first director and an absolute observational powerhouse. Cassini mapped the Moon, discovered four of Saturn's moons (Iapetus, Rhea, Tethys, and Dione), and, most famously, identified the "Cassini Division," the prominent gap in Saturn's rings. He was also the first to observe the Great Red Spot on Jupiter and made surprisingly accurate measurements of planetary distances. His astronomical observations were critical in refining planetary theory and mapmaking. Basically, if you were looking up at the night sky in the late 17th century, chances are Cassini had already charted it, named it, or figured out its secrets!
