In the late 18th century, if you wanted to know what plants grew in the wilds of North America, you basically had to ask André Michaux. This intrepid French botanist and explorer, born in 1746, wasn't content with just collecting pretty flowers; he was on a serious mission for science (and for the French government, looking for useful trees). Michaux spent over a decade trekking across vast stretches of North America, from Canada to Florida, collecting, cataloging, and sending back thousands of plant specimens. He introduced countless American species to European gardens and vice versa, laying essential groundwork for botanical understanding of the continent. Michaux wasn't just a plant collector; he was a botanical Indiana Jones, single-handedly mapping out entire floral kingdoms!
