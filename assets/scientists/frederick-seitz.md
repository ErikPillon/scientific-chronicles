Frederick Seitz was a fascinating, albeit controversial, figure in 20th-century American science. He started his career as a brilliant solid-state physicist, making significant contributions to the understanding of crystal defects and electronic properties of materials. He even co-authored a seminal textbook on the subject. His scientific reputation soared, leading him to become President of the National Academy of Sciences during the Cold War. However, later in life, Seitz pivoted sharply, becoming a vocal skeptic of anthropogenic climate change. He championed the Oregon Petition and publicly questioned the scientific consensus, aligning himself with industries that stood to lose from environmental regulations. This transformation made him a lightning rod for debate, illustrating how a respected scientist's later views can spark intense scientific and political contention. His story is a wild ride from academic stardom to activist notoriety.
