If you've ever worked in a job where you weren't slowly poisoned by your surroundings, you might have Alice Hamilton to thank! Born in 1869, Alice was an American physician who became a fierce champion for industrial safety. She bravely ventured into factories and mines – places most doctors wouldn't dare – to investigate mysterious illnesses plaguing workers. She was a pioneer in toxicology, systematically identifying hazards like lead poisoning, carbon monoxide, and TNT exposure. Her meticulous research and advocacy led to crucial reforms, effectively founding the field of occupational medicine. Alice wasn't just observing; she was battling powerful industries to protect the health of the working class. She even became the first woman appointed to the faculty of Harvard University. Talk about a medical superhero!
