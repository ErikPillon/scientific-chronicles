When humanity first started dreaming of soaring through the skies and beyond, someone had to figure out if our squishy bodies could actually handle it. Enter Harry G. Armstrong, an American physician and flight surgeon who basically wrote the book on aerospace medicine. During the 1930s and beyond, Armstrong was instrumental in understanding how altitude, acceleration, and oxygen deprivation affected pilots. He investigated things like 'Armstrong's Line' (the altitude where atmospheric pressure is so low, water boils at body temperature, requiring a pressure suit) and contributed massively to designing pressure cabins and flight suits. His research laid the groundwork for safe aviation and, crucially, for sending humans into space. Without Armstrong, astronauts might have literally popped! He made sure we could boldly go without exploding.
