Christiaan Eijkman, a Dutch physician and pathologist, stumbled upon one of the biggest 'aha!' moments in nutritional science, almost by accident! While working in the Dutch East Indies, he was trying to figure out what caused beriberi, a debilitating disease. He noticed chickens fed polished rice got sick, while those on unpolished rice didn't. Initially, he thought it was a bacterial infection, but his sharp observations eventually led him to the idea that there was a *deficiency* in the polished rice. This groundbreaking insight, published in 1897, paved the way for the discovery of vitamins (specifically thiamine, or B1) and earned him a share of the Nobel Prize in 1929. He basically taught us that sometimes, it's not what's there, but what's *missing*, that causes trouble.
