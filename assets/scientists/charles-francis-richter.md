Charles Francis Richter, born in 1900, was the American seismologist who gave us a way to measure just how much the Earth was grumbling. Along with Beno Gutenberg, he developed the now-iconic Richter magnitude scale in 1935, providing a standardized numerical measure for the energy released by earthquakes. Before Richter, earthquake reports were mostly descriptive – 'it was a big one!' Now, we had a scientific way to compare them. He spent his career at Caltech, studying earthquakes and making sure the rest of us understood their power. So, the next time you hear about a 6.0 tremor, you're hearing in 'Richter.' He literally gave voice to the Earth's rumbles.
