Heinrich Geissler was a German glassblower and instrument maker with a knack for creating incredibly precise vacuum tubes. His 'Geissler tubes,' invented around 1857, were sealed glass tubes containing rarefied gases that produced stunning colored glows when a high voltage was applied across them. These dazzling devices weren't just pretty light shows; they were crucial for early experiments on electricity in gases, cathode rays, and the nature of light itself. Geissler's meticulous craftsmanship and his pioneering work on vacuum pumps allowed scientists to explore phenomena previously impossible, directly influencing the development of X-ray tubes, neon lights, and even television. He really knew how to put on an electric show!
