Benjamin F. Goodrich was a physician who, rather than diagnosing ailments, chose to tackle the vexing problem of rubber's utility in the rapidly industrializing United States. Born in 1841, he was initially a surgeon during the Civil War, but later pivoted dramatically to industry. In 1870, he founded the B.F. Goodrich Company in Akron, Ohio, recognizing the immense potential of rubber beyond just erasers and galoshes. He was a visionary who understood that better rubber products, especially for tires, hoses, and belting, would be crucial for a burgeoning nation. While not a "scientist" in the pure academic sense, his company invested heavily in chemical research and engineering to improve rubber's durability and versatility. He basically took a sticky, often unreliable material and transformed it into an industrial powerhouse, paving the way for the automotive age and proving that sometimes, the best doctors heal industries.
