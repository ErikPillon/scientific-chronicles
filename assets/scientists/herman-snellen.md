If you've ever squinted at a big letter E in a doctor's office, you have Herman Snellen to thank (or curse). This brilliant Dutch ophthalmologist invented the Snellen chart in 1862, giving us the universal standard for measuring how well we can see. Before Snellen, eye exams were a bit of a free-for-all, with doctors using various, inconsistent methods. His standardized chart, with its famous block letters of decreasing size, made it possible to consistently assess and compare vision, paving the way for better diagnosis and treatment of eye conditions. He basically gave '20/20 vision' its meaning, proving that sometimes, the simplest ideas have the biggest impact, especially when it comes to clarity.
