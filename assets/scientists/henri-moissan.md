Henri Moissan was a French chemist who loved a good challenge, especially if it involved highly reactive and dangerous elements. His greatest triumph, which earned him the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1906, was successfully isolating elemental fluorine in 1886—a feat that had eluded chemists for decades due to fluorine's extreme reactivity. Imagine trying to catch a greased lightning bolt in a glass jar! Moissan also made significant advancements in high-temperature chemistry, developing the electric arc furnace, which allowed him to synthesize diamonds (small ones, mind you!) and study various refractory materials. He was basically the mad scientist of inorganic chemistry, pushing the boundaries of what was possible in the lab and making the world a brighter (and sometimes more explosive) place.
