At the start of the 20th century, some very serious scientists still doubted that atoms actually existed as physical objects rather than useful mathematical fictions. Jean Perrin settled the argument through meticulous experiments on Brownian motion, the random jittering of tiny particles suspended in a fluid, which Einstein had predicted mathematically in 1905 as the result of countless invisible molecules bombarding the particles. Perrin measured this jiggling with painstaking precision and used it to calculate Avogadro's number, the count of molecules in a given amount of substance, with an accuracy that finally convinced the holdouts. He won the 1926 Nobel Prize in Physics for putting the final nail in the anti-atomist coffin. Sometimes the biggest scientific questions get answered by watching something very small wobble around under a microscope. 🔬💧
