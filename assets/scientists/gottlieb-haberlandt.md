Meet Gottlieb Haberlandt, the Austrian botanist who saw plants in a whole new light. Born in 1854, Haberlandt was a trailblazer in plant physiology. He's most famous for being the 'father of plant tissue culture,' boldly proposing in 1902 that individual plant cells could potentially be grown and differentiated into whole plants—a concept known as totipotency. While he couldn't quite achieve it in his lifetime, his theoretical framework laid the groundwork for all future plant tissue culture techniques, revolutionizing agriculture, biotechnology, and our understanding of plant development. He spent decades researching how plant tissues function and communicate, leaving an indelible mark on botany before his passing in 1945.
