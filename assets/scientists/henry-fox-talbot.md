William Henry Fox Talbot (1800-1877) was a polymath who looked at pretty landscapes and thought, "I wish I could *capture* that." While Louis Daguerre was perfecting his direct-positive images in France, Talbot was pioneering a different, ultimately more enduring, path in Britain: the negative-positive photographic process. His "calotype" process, patented in 1841, allowed for multiple prints from a single negative, making photography truly scalable. He basically laid the groundwork for almost all modern photographic techniques, transforming how we record history, art, and ourselves. From math to botany, this guy was brilliant, but photography was his true masterpiece.
