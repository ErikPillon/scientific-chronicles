Ever tried to predict the next word in a sentence, or what the weather might do tomorrow? You're basically dabbling in Markov's territory! Andrey Andreyevich Markov, the brilliant Russian mathematician born in 1856, gave us 'Markov chains' and 'Markov processes.' His big idea? That the future state of a system often depends *only* on its current state, not on the entire history of how it got there. This seemingly simple concept blew open fields from statistics and physics to genetics and language processing (hello, autocorrect!). Markov's work lets us model everything from how gas molecules bounce around to how genes mutate. He literally mapped out the 'memoryless' march of probabilities, making him the quiet architect behind much of modern predictive modeling.
