Alfred Ely Beach was an inventor, patent lawyer, and magazine publisher who really dug innovation – literally! Back in 1870s New York, when horse-drawn carriages reigned supreme and traffic was already a nightmare, Beach had a wild idea: a pneumatic subway. No steam, no smoke, just a giant fan to propel a single passenger car through a tunnel using air pressure. He secretly dug a 312-foot demonstration tunnel under Broadway, complete with plush seats and gaslights, years before the city's official subway system was even conceived. It was a marvel of engineering, a peek into the future, and an absolute sensation! While his full vision never quite took off due to political meddling and funding issues, Beach's inventive spirit and daring subterranean project proved that sometimes, you just need to think outside (or rather, *underneath*) the box to solve urban problems.
