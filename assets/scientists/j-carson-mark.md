J. Carson Mark, born in 1913 in Canada, was a mathematical and physics powerhouse who found himself at the heart of the 20th century's most sensitive scientific endeavors. After earning his Ph.D. in mathematics, he became a key figure at Los Alamos during the Manhattan Project, leading the Theoretical Division from 1947 to 1973. He was essentially the chief theoretician for nuclear weapon design in the US, ensuring the calculations were sound for some of the most powerful and controversial technologies ever conceived. Known for his keen intellect and rigorous approach, Mark navigated the complex world of nuclear physics with calm precision. He passed away in 1997, leaving an indelible, albeit complex, mark on scientific history.
