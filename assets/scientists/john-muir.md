John Muir spent years wandering, often alone and on foot, through the wilderness of California's Sierra Nevada, keeping detailed journals of glaciers, forests, and geology while developing a passionate, almost religious conviction that wild places deserved protection rather than exploitation. His writing, vivid and unapologetically emotional about nature's value, helped convince the American public and politicians (including a famous camping trip with President Theodore Roosevelt in Yosemite) that wilderness was worth preserving for its own sake. Muir co-founded the Sierra Club in 1892 and remains widely credited as the driving force behind the creation of Yosemite and other national parks, effectively founding the modern conservation movement in the United States.
