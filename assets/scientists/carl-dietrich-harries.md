Carl Dietrich Harries, born in Germany in 1866, was the organic chemist who figured out how to 'cut and paste' molecules with ozone! He's most famous for perfecting the technique of ozonolysis, a super-clever method that uses ozone to cleave carbon-carbon double bonds in organic compounds. Why was this such a big deal? Because by breaking molecules at specific points, chemists could then identify the resulting fragments and work backward to deduce the original, often complex, molecular structure. This technique became an indispensable tool for structural elucidation, especially for natural products like rubber, which Harries studied extensively. His work provided crucial insights into the architecture of macromolecules, paving the way for further advances in synthetic chemistry. Harries essentially gave chemists a molecular scalpel, revealing the hidden shapes of the chemical world.
