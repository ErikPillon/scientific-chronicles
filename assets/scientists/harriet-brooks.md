Harriet Brooks was basically a rockstar physicist who worked with some of the biggest names in early radioactivity research, yet often gets less credit than she deserves. This brilliant Canadian was Ernest Rutherford's first graduate student at McGill University and later worked with J.J. Thomson at Cambridge and Marie Curie in Paris. Her experiments were crucial in understanding radioactive recoil and the nature of radioactive emanations, work that paved the way for the concept of atomic transmutation. Despite her immense talent, she faced the societal pressures of her time, almost giving up her career for marriage. Luckily for science, she kept pushing, leaving behind a legacy that shaped modern physics.
