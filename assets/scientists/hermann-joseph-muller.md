Hermann Joseph Muller was the kind of scientist who wasn't afraid to shine a light – specifically, X-rays – on the mysteries of heredity. This American geneticist made a monumental discovery: he showed that X-rays could induce mutations in genes, earning him a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1946. Working with fruit flies (the unsung heroes of genetics research), Muller's work proved that radiation could directly alter the genetic material, forever changing our understanding of evolution, cancer, and genetic risk. His findings were crucial for both understanding natural variation and for developing safety protocols for radiation exposure, proving that sometimes, a little dose of high-energy insight can illuminate a whole new field of biology.
