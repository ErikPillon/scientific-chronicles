Arsène-Jules-Étienne-Juvénal Dupuit sounds like he has enough names for a whole engineering firm, but this French polymath was just one brilliant mind. A civil engineer by trade, he built bridges and designed urban infrastructure, but his analytical genius really shone in economics. He's often credited with being one of the first to articulate the concept of consumer surplus – essentially, the extra satisfaction consumers get beyond what they pay for a good or service. He applied economic theory to public works, arguing for pricing strategies that would maximize social welfare, rather than just covering costs. So, next time you cross a bridge or ponder the cost of public services, remember Dupuit, the engineer who was also a foundational economist.
