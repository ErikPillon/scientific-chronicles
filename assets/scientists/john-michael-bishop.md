J. Michael Bishop is a biological detective who, along with Harold Varmus, cracked a massive case: how normal cells can turn cancerous. These two brilliant minds discovered oncogenes in 1976. Before them, people thought cancer-causing genes were brought into cells by viruses. Bishop and Varmus proved that these "proto-oncogenes" are actually part of our own cellular DNA, lying dormant, ready to be flipped into a cancer-causing state by mutations or other factors. Their groundbreaking work on the genetic origin of cancer in viruses and cells wasn't just a "Eureka!" moment; it revolutionized cancer research and treatment strategies. For this monumental discovery, they shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1989. Talk about a career highlight – helping us understand how our own bodies can betray us, and how we might fight back!
