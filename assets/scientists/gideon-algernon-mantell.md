Gideon Mantell, a country doctor by profession, had a secret passion for fossils that led him to make one of history's most earth-shattering discoveries. In 1822, his wife Mary Ann found peculiar large teeth in a quarry in Sussex. Mantell, recognizing they belonged to a massive herbivore unlike anything known, named it 'Iguanodon' – 'iguana tooth.' This wasn't just finding a cool old bone; it was a crucial step in understanding that colossal, extinct reptiles once roamed the Earth. His work, alongside that of William Buckland, laid the foundation for the concept of 'Dinosauria,' a term later coined by his rival Richard Owen. Mantell’s dedication, despite financial hardship and scientific disputes, cemented his place as a foundational figure in paleontology, proving that sometimes, the biggest discoveries are made by curious minds outside the ivory tower.
