Casimir Funk was the rockstar biochemist who basically invented the concept of vitamins. In 1912, he was puzzling over why some diets caused mysterious diseases like scurvy and beriberi. He brilliantly proposed that certain essential 'vital amines' (hence his coined term 'vitamine') were missing from these diets, sparking a whole new era in nutrition science. While not all vitamins turned out to be amines, his fundamental insight that specific organic compounds, in tiny amounts, are vital for health was a game-changer. Funk's work didn't just give us a word; it launched the global quest to discover, understand, and supplement these tiny but mighty molecules, forever changing how we think about food and our well-being. What a legend!
