Bernard F. Burke, an American radio astronomer born in 1928, had ears for the cosmos that were unmatched. Working with Kenneth Franklin in 1955, he made one of those electrifying, head-scratching discoveries that changed planetary science forever: Jupiter, the gas giant, wasn't just a silent behemoth. It was *talking*! Burke and Franklin detected powerful radio signals emanating from Jupiter, specifically in the decametric wavelength range. Imagine tuning into your radio and suddenly picking up extraterrestrial static – that's essentially what they did! This groundbreaking observation was the first time radio emissions were detected from a planet other than Earth. It not only opened up an entirely new window into understanding planetary magnetospheres and atmospheres but also kicked off the exciting field of planetary radio astronomy. Burke's knack for listening to the universe's unseen symphonies made Jupiter a much noisier, and far more fascinating, neighbor.
