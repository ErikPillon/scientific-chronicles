Bernhard Lauritz Frederik Bang, born in Denmark in 1848, was the kind of veterinary hero who saved countless cattle and, indirectly, people. Imagine a mysterious disease causing abortions in cows, devastating farms, and nobody knew why. Enter Bang! In 1897, this meticulous Danish bacteriologist identified the culprit: a previously unknown bacterium he called *Bacillus abortus* (now *Brucella abortus*). This discovery not only explained 'Bang's disease' (brucellosis) in cattle but also revealed how the infection could spread to humans, causing undulant fever. His work led to essential diagnostic tests and control measures, literally revolutionizing animal health and food safety. Bang's dedication to understanding and combating infectious diseases in livestock made him a pivotal figure in veterinary medicine. He died in 1932, leaving a lasting legacy in both agricultural science and public health.
