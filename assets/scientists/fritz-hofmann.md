Tired of flat tires and rubber shortages? Thank Fritz Hofmann! This German chemist was working for Bayer in the early 20th century when he cracked the code for synthetic rubber. Natural rubber, harvested from trees, was unreliable and expensive. Hofmann’s breakthrough, patented in 1909, created the first commercially viable artificial rubber, 'methyl rubber.' It meant cars could keep rolling, and industrial production wouldn’t grind to a halt when natural supplies dwindled. He basically gave the world a resilient, human-made bounce, forever changing everything from tires to erasers. He certainly rolled with the punches of chemistry!
