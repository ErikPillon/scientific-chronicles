Ever wonder why you keep checking your phone, or why your pet begs for treats? You can thank (or blame!) B.F. Skinner, the godfather of operant conditioning. Born in 1904, this American psychologist practically wrote the book on how consequences shape behavior. He wasn't interested in delving into minds; he wanted to observe and measure actions. Skinner gave us the 'Skinner Box' (officially, an operant conditioning chamber), where he showed how rats or pigeons learned to press levers for food, demonstrating that positive reinforcement increases desired behaviors. His radical behaviorism suggested that free will might be an illusion, and that human behavior could be understood and even shaped through environmental factors. While controversial, his ideas profoundly influenced education, therapy, and even how we train animals. He truly believed we could design better societies by understanding the rules of reinforcement, making him one of the 20th century's most influential, and debated, thinkers.
