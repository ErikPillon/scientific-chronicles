Who needs fancy degrees when you've got a brilliant mind and a knack for digging? That was James Brindley, the unsung hero of the Industrial Revolution who literally reshaped Britain's landscape. A millwright by trade, he taught himself civil engineering and became the nation's premier canal builder. His masterpiece? The Bridgewater Canal, which famously traversed the River Irwell via an aqueduct – a feat that amazed 18th-century onlookers. Brindley dreamt in water and mud, sketching his plans with cheese and dirt when paper wasn't handy. He dug miles of canals, proving that a practical genius could move mountains (or at least, the goods around them) and build the arteries of a new industrial age.
