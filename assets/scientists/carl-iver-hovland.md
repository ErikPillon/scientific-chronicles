Meet Carl I. Hovland, the guy who helped us understand why some messages stick and others flop. As an American psychologist, he spearheaded groundbreaking research on attitude change and persuasion, particularly during World War II when he was tasked by the US Army to study the effectiveness of propaganda films. His work at Yale University, leading the 'Yale Communication and Attitude Change Program,' became legendary. He wasn't just observing; he was systematically breaking down how factors like the source of the message, the content itself, and the audience characteristics all played a role in whether someone's mind would be changed. Hovland's empirical approach provided a scientific foundation for understanding human persuasion, leaving a lasting mark on social psychology, advertising, and political communication. He literally wrote the playbook on how to (or not to) influence people.
