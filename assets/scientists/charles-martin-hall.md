Charles Martin Hall was just 22 years old in 1886 when he basically handed the world aluminum on a silver platter – or rather, on an electric current. While still a student at Oberlin College, Hall became obsessed with finding a cheap way to produce aluminum, which was then a rare and expensive metal. He tinkered in a woodshed laboratory (because, of course, all great discoveries start in a woodshed) and independently discovered the electrolytic process for extracting aluminum from ore. This simultaneous discovery with Paul Héroult in France made aluminum cheap and widely available, transforming everything from cookware to airplanes. Talk about a bright spark! He made a fortune, but more importantly, he made aluminum accessible to everyone.
