Before space telescopes were launching daily, there was Herbert Friedman, an American physicist who truly opened our eyes to the X-ray universe. Working at the Naval Research Laboratory, he pioneered using captured German V-2 rockets (yes, really!) to send instruments high above Earth's atmosphere. In 1949, his team famously detected X-rays from the Sun, a groundbreaking first. This wasn't just a cool trick; it kicked off the entire field of X-ray astronomy, allowing us to see hot, energetic phenomena in space that are invisible to optical telescopes. Basically, he showed us the cosmos has a whole 'nother, fiery side!
