Edwin Joseph Cohn was the kind of scientist who made complicated biology look like a well-organized puzzle. This American biochemist cracked the code of blood plasma, developing a revolutionary process during WWII to separate its components. Think of it: thanks to Cohn's meticulous work, we could isolate things like albumin (for shock), gamma globulin (for immunity), and fibrinogen (for clotting). His plasma fractionation methods literally saved countless lives on battlefields and in hospitals, turning a complex biological soup into life-saving ingredients. He truly put the 'protein' in 'protector of health' and streamlined the messy business of blood.
