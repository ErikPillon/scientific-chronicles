Bernardino Ramazzini, born in Italy in 1633, was the kind of doctor who looked beyond the coughs and fevers to ask, 'What exactly do you *do* for a living?' This seemingly simple question was revolutionary! Before Ramazzini, no one really connected work with sickness systematically. His groundbreaking book, *De Morbis Artificum Diatriba* (Diseases of Workers), published in 1700, meticulously detailed the health hazards faced by people in over 50 different occupations, from miners to weavers to scribes. He didn't just point out the problems; he suggested preventative measures, making him the undisputed father of occupational medicine. Ramazzini basically said, 'Your job might be killing you,' and then showed why, paving the way for safer workplaces centuries later. He passed away in 1714, leaving behind a legacy that still prompts doctors to ask, 'What kind of work do you do?'
