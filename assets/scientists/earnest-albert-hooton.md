Meet Earnest A. Hooton, the American physical anthropologist who certainly knew how to stir the academic pot during the early 20th century. Born in Wisconsin, Hooton made a significant name for himself at Harvard, where he meticulously classified human races and delved into the physical traits of various populations. He even famously (and controversially) tried to link body types to criminal behavior, which, as you can imagine, ruffled more than a few feathers and sparked considerable debate – debates that, let's just say, haven't aged particularly well. While his eugenics-tinged theories are rightly viewed critically today, Hooton was undeniably a prominent figure who shaped discussions around human biological variation for decades, reminding us that even scientific pursuits are deeply influenced by the societal lenses of their time. He was certainly no stranger to controversy, making him a complex character in the annals of anthropology.
