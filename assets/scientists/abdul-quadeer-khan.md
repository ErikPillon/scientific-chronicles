Abdul Qadeer Khan was a figure who truly lived up to the 'mad scientist' stereotype, albeit with geopolitical rather than Frankenstein-esque implications. Born in Bhopal, British India, he trained as a metallurgist in Europe. He returned to Pakistan in the 1970s, bringing crucial centrifuge designs and expertise that were instrumental in Pakistan's journey to developing its nuclear weapons program, earning him the title 'father of Pakistan's atomic bomb.' However, his legacy is deeply tainted by accusations of running an illicit, international nuclear proliferation network, allegedly selling sensitive technology to countries like Iran, Libya, and North Korea. It was quite the scandal, leading to his house arrest and a rather complex national narrative – hailed as a hero by some, condemned as a rogue agent by others.
