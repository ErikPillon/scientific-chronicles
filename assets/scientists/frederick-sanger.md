Freddie Sanger was the ultimate sequence solver, cracking the codes of life not once, but twice, earning him two solo Nobel Prizes in Chemistry – a feat almost unheard of! First, he figured out the amino acid sequence of insulin, proving proteins weren't just random blobs but precisely ordered molecules. Then, he developed methods for DNA sequencing, laying the groundwork for the human genome project and basically opening up modern molecular biology. This humble British biochemist was less about flashy pronouncements and more about meticulous lab work, quietly revolutionizing our understanding of genetics and making it possible for future generations to read the blueprints of life. His methods are still fundamental to almost everything we do in genetics today. Talk about leaving a lasting mark!
