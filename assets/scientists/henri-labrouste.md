Henri Labrouste was a French architect who looked at grand, classical buildings and thought, 'You know what this needs? Iron!' While primarily an architect, his innovative use of new materials like cast iron in visible structural elements was a groundbreaking engineering feat. He dared to expose the skeletal structure of his buildings, most famously in the Sainte-Geneviève Library (now Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève) and the Bibliothèque Nationale de France, thereby influencing generations of architects and engineers. He wasn't just slapping iron into a building; he was celebrating its strength and lightness, creating luminous, airy spaces that redefined the library experience. Labrouste proved that beauty and brute strength could be best friends, marrying classical forms with industrial materials in a truly revolutionary way. He pretty much paved the way for modern architecture.
