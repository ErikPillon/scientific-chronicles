Before Isaac Newton was even thinking about falling apples or calculus, James Gregory was busy revolutionizing telescopes and contemplating advanced mathematics. This Scottish genius not only laid out theoretical foundations for calculus years before Leibniz and Newton (talk about being ahead of your time!), but he also designed the first practical reflecting telescope, known as the Gregorian telescope, in 1663. His innovative optical design made it possible to see the cosmos more clearly, cementing his legacy as a pivotal figure in both mathematics and astronomy. Unfortunately, Gregory's short life prevented him from seeing his telescope built during his lifetime, but his work truly made waves, helping future generations explore the mysteries of the universe. He was practically a human telescope himself, seeing things others couldn't yet imagine!
