George Brayton was an American mechanical engineer who was tinkering with engines long before cars were commonplace. His claim to fame is the 'Ready Motor,' a constant-pressure internal combustion engine patented in 1872. While it wasn't the roaring success of some other engines, it was a significant step in engine development and operated on what we now call the 'Brayton cycle.' This cycle became a fundamental concept for gas turbines, powering everything from jet aircraft to power plants. So, while you might not know his name, his work quietly helped pave the way for some of the most powerful machines on the planet.
