J. Ernest Wilkins Jr., born in Chicago in 1923, was a true intellectual polymath and a quiet trailblazer. He earned his PhD in mathematics from the University of Chicago at just 19 years old – yes, 19! He then went on to contribute significantly to the Manhattan Project, working on the mathematical and computational aspects of nuclear reactor design. Later, his career spanned nuclear engineering, applied mathematics, and even optics, with notable work on gamma ray shielding and the development of mathematical models. As an African American scientist, he navigated racial barriers to achieve incredible academic and professional success. Wilkins's brilliance and versatility made him a remarkable figure in 20th-century science before his death in 2011.
