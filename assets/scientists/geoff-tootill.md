Meet Geoff Tootill, a quiet but mighty pioneer in the early days of computing! Born in 1922, this British genius played a pivotal role at the University of Manchester, where he helped bring the 'Manchester Baby' (SSEM) to life in 1948. This wasn't just any computer; it was one of the *very first* electronic stored-program computers, blazing a trail for every device you're using today. Tootill, alongside Tom Kilburn and Frederic C. Williams, helped design and build the machine that executed its first program, a moment that fundamentally changed the trajectory of technology. He later contributed to the Ferranti Mark 1, the world's first commercially available general-purpose electronic computer. So, next time you power up your laptop, give a nod to Geoff – he helped lay the foundational bits and bytes for the digital world we now navigate!
