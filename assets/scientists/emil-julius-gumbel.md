Emil J. Gumbel wasn't just another mathematician; he was a numbers guy with a heart, and a very brave one at that. Born in Germany, he became a trailblazer in extreme value theory – essentially, figuring out the odds of really rare (and often bad) events happening, like the biggest flood or the longest drought. But Gumbel's courage wasn't limited to probabilities. He was an outspoken pacifist and a vocal critic of German militarism and political assassinations during the Weimar Republic, famously analyzing their statistics and proving a political bias in the justice system. Unsurprisingly, this didn't make him popular with the Nazis, leading him to flee to France and eventually the US, where he continued his brilliant statistical work and his fight for justice. He literally put his life on the line for data and decency.
