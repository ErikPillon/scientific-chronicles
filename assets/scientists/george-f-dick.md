Before penicillin tamed the nastier germs, scarlet fever was a real terror. Enter George F. Dick, an American physician, and bacteriologist, who, working alongside his equally brilliant wife Gladys, became the dynamic duo of disease fighting. In the 1920s, they managed to isolate the streptococcus bacteria responsible for scarlet fever and, even better, developed the "Dick test" to identify susceptibility and a toxin-antitoxin to prevent and treat it. Their work was a major win against a widespread and often deadly childhood illness, proving that sometimes, two minds (especially when married!) are better than one when it comes to medical breakthroughs.
