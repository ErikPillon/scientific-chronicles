Eleanor J. Gibson, born in 1910, was an American psychologist who completely revolutionized our understanding of perceptual learning and development. She's most famous for her ingenious 'visual cliff' experiment in 1960, conducted alongside her husband Richard Walk. This groundbreaking (and slightly nerve-wracking for baby watchers) setup demonstrated that even very young infants could perceive depth, instinctively avoiding what appeared to be a steep drop-off. Her work fundamentally shifted views on infant cognition, showing that perception isn't just a passive reception of sensory input, but an active, developmental process. Gibson's legacy is immense, proving that babies are far smarter, and more aware, than we often give them credit for. She passed away in 2002, leaving behind a profound impact on psychology that still resonates today.
