Navigating America's rivers in the early 19th century was a perilous business, thanks to countless submerged logs and tree trunks, lovingly known as "snags." Enter Henry Miller Shreve, a daring steamboat captain and brilliant inventor, born in 1785. Not content with merely piloting vessels, Shreve engineered the revolutionary "snagboat" in 1829 – essentially a floating saw and crane designed specifically to yank these riverine hazards out of the water. His most famous creation, the *Heliopolis*, was a double-hulled marvel that could clear entire channels, making river travel safer and more efficient. Shreve didn't just sail on rivers; he sculpted them, clearing the way for trade and expansion across the American interior. He truly was the original river cleaner-upper!
