Ever wondered what your brain waves look like? Thank Hans Berger! This German psychiatrist and neurophysiologist was the first human to record electrical activity from the surface of the human brain, inventing the electroencephalogram (EEG) in 1924 (though he didn't publish his findings until 1929). Legend has it, his fascination with the brain's mysterious 'electricity' stemmed from a telepathic experience during a military exercise when he thought he was in danger and his father, miles away, knew something was wrong. Driven by this intriguing personal event, Berger spent years meticulously placing electrodes on people's scalps, detecting tiny electrical signals and categorizing them into alpha and beta waves. His work was initially met with skepticism (imagine telling people you can 'read' their brains!), but it revolutionized neuroscience, providing a non-invasive way to study brain function, diagnose conditions like epilepsy, and even monitor sleep. He literally gave us a peek into the electrical symphony inside our heads. A true pioneer who dared to listen to the whispers of the brain!
