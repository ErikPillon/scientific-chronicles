Godfrey Wilson was a rather bright British social anthropologist who, along with his wife Monica, dived deep into the lives of the Nyakyusa people of East Africa during the 1930s. His work was pivotal in developing social anthropology as a field, pushing for a more rigorous, comparative approach. Sadly, his career was cut short, but his detailed ethnographies and theoretical contributions left a lasting mark, helping us understand how societies function and adapt. He basically made understanding other cultures much more of a science than it used to be.
