J. Georg Bednorz, born in Germany in 1950, is one half of the dynamic duo that completely flipped the world of superconductivity on its head. In 1986, working with K. Alex Müller at IBM, he made the stunning discovery of high-temperature superconductivity in ceramic materials – specifically, a lanthanum-barium-copper oxide. Before this, superconductors needed super-chilly temperatures to work their magic. Bednorz and Müller's finding pushed the temperature threshold higher, sparking a worldwide frenzy of research into new materials and potential applications. Their groundbreaking work earned them the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1987, just a year after their discovery! He's still around, undoubtedly keeping an eye on the next big thing in materials science.
