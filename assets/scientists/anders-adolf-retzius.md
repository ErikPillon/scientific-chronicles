Imagine a guy so into skulls he practically invented a whole new way to measure them! That's Anders Adolf Retzius for you. This Swedish anatomical whiz wasn't just dissecting bodies; he was busy categorizing humanity by head shape. His 'cephalic index' became a cornerstone (and sometimes a controversial one) in physical anthropology, helping classify different human groups based on whether their noggins were long, short, or somewhere in between. Before him, people just looked at heads; Retzius gave them a ruler and a formula. Talk about getting ahead in science! His work, while later facing critical re-evaluation, laid foundational groundwork for understanding human variation, even if his methods were eventually stretched a bit far.
