Arthur Edwin Kennelly was a British-American electrical engineer who had a knack for seeing the invisible. Working independently (but at roughly the same time) as Oliver Heaviside, Kennelly theorized the existence of an electrically conductive layer in Earth's upper atmosphere that could reflect radio waves. This 'Kennelly-Heaviside layer' (now part of what we call the ionosphere) explained why Marconi's transatlantic radio transmissions worked, revolutionizing radio communication and paving the way for global broadcasting. He also worked with Thomas Edison, so you know he was hanging with the electrical big shots. Kennelly's insights literally lifted radio communication to new heights!
