Felix Andries Vening Meinesz was no armchair scientist; he took his gravity measurements seriously – and underwater! This Dutch geophysicist practically invented the art of precision gravity measurements at sea. From the 1920s to the 1940s, he embarked on numerous submarine expeditions, enduring cramped, often queasy conditions, all to measure tiny variations in Earth's gravitational field. Why? Because these anomalies revealed secrets about the planet's interior, particularly where oceanic crust was bending and diving. His meticulous work provided crucial early evidence for the theories of continental drift and, later, plate tectonics, showing areas of 'gravity deficit' that corresponded to oceanic trenches. So, while others might have been sunbathing, Vening Meinesz was deep underwater, providing the gravitational proof that our continents are indeed restless wanderers.
