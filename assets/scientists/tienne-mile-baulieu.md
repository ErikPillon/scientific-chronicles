Étienne-Émile Baulieu is the kind of scientist who makes waves, often quite intentionally. This French endocrinologist is renowned for his groundbreaking work on steroid hormones, delving into how they function at a molecular level and influencing everything from reproduction to aging. But if you know his name, it's likely linked to one particularly significant and controversial invention: mifepristone, better known as RU-486, the 'abortion pill.' His development of this anti-progestin drug in the early 1980s revolutionized reproductive medicine, providing a non-surgical option for abortion and opening up new avenues for hormone-based therapies. A true intellectual provocateur, Baulieu has never shied away from the social and ethical implications of his science, always pushing the boundaries of biological understanding and its impact on human lives.
