Daniel H. Burnham wasn't just an architect; he was a city whisperer, an urban visionary who believed in big ideas for big cities. This American titan of design famously declared, 'Make no little plans; they have no magic to stir men's blood.' He put that philosophy into action, masterminding the iconic 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago and then laying out grand plans for cities like Chicago, Washington D.C., and San Francisco. His blend of aesthetic grandeur and functional urban planning, incorporating principles of civil engineering and logistics, profoundly influenced modern urban development. He literally sculpted skylines and shaped the way millions live, proving that good planning is truly monumental.
