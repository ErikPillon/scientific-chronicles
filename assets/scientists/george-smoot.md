Ever wondered what the universe looked like as a baby? George Smoot, along with John C. Mather, helped us get a peek! This American astrophysicist was a key player in the COBE (Cosmic Background Explorer) satellite mission, which meticulously measured the cosmic microwave background radiation – the leftover heat from the Big Bang. Their incredible findings, announced in 1992, revealed tiny temperature fluctuations (anisotropies) in this background radiation. These 'ripples' were the seeds from which all structures in the universe, from galaxies to galaxy clusters, eventually formed. It was concrete proof for the Big Bang theory and a monumental step in understanding cosmic origins, earning him a Nobel Prize in Physics in 2006. He literally showed us the universe's baby pictures!
