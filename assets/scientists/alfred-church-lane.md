Alfred Church Lane was a prominent American geologist who spent a good chunk of his career peeling back the layers of Earth's history, quite literally. While not as flashy as some, Lane was a serious science sleuth when it came to determining the age of rocks and minerals. He was a champion of using radioactive decay, particularly the lead-uranium ratio, to put hard numbers on geological timescales. Before his work, estimating Earth's age was a bit of a guesstimate party, but Lane helped solidify the methods that made geochronology a rigorous science. He served as the State Geologist of Michigan and was a respected voice in the geological community, pushing for more precise dating techniques. So, next time you marvel at the ancientness of a mountain or a fossil, give a little nod to Alfred Church Lane, who helped figure out just how old 'ancient' really is. He made sure our planet didn't just have a rich history, but a properly dated one too!
