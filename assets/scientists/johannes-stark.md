In 1913, Johannes Stark discovered that a strong external electric field splits atomic spectral lines into multiple distinct components, a phenomenon now called the Stark effect, providing crucial early experimental evidence for the emerging quantum theory of atomic structure. The discovery won him the 1919 Nobel Prize in Physics. His scientific legacy, however, is deeply complicated by what came next: Stark became a fervent supporter of the Nazi regime and a leading proponent of 'Deutsche Physik,' a pseudoscientific movement that rejected Einstein's relativity and quantum mechanics as supposedly corrupt 'Jewish physics.' He used his position to try to purge German physics of Jewish and non-conforming scientists, a campaign that damaged German science for a generation. A stark reminder that genuine scientific talent offers no protection against becoming an instrument of hatred. ⚡🔬
