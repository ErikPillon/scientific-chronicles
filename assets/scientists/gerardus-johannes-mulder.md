Before protein shakes and diet fads, there was Gerardus Johannes Mulder, the Dutch chemist who literally gave 'protein' its name! Born in 1802, Mulder was a medical doctor who became fascinated with the chemistry of living things, especially plants. In the 1830s, he conducted extensive analyses of organic compounds in plants and animals, isolating and studying these nitrogen-rich substances. Recognizing their fundamental importance to life, and after consulting with the great Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius, Mulder introduced the term 'protein' (from the Greek 'proteios,' meaning 'of primary importance') in 1838. He theorized that all these vital substances were based on a common 'protein' molecule, although his exact chemical formula for this 'protein' was later disproven. Still, his work was foundational, marking a crucial step in understanding the building blocks of life. Mulder opened the door to the vast field of biochemistry, making him a true godfather of our understanding of what keeps us going. Eat your protein, folks!
