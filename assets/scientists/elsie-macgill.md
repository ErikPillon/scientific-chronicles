Elsie MacGill truly earned her "Queen of the Hurricanes" moniker, not just for overseeing the production of Hawker Hurricane fighter planes during WWII, but for tackling a storm of expectations as the world's first female aircraft designer. Despite contracting polio and often using crutches, this Canadian powerhouse didn't just break the glass ceiling; she designed a plane that flew right through it. After earning her Master's in aeronautical engineering, she dove into designing and testing aircraft, becoming chief aeronautical engineer at Canadian Car & Foundry. Post-war, she ran her own consulting firm and tirelessly advocated for women's rights, serving on commissions and lending her sharp intellect to public discourse. Elsie proved that intelligence and ingenuity have no gender, leaving a legacy that soars far beyond the airplanes she helped create. Her life was a masterclass in resilience and breaking boundaries, proving that the sky really wasn't the limit.
