Meet Chen Ning Yang, a physics titan who, alongside Tsung-Dao Lee, dropped a bombshell on the physics establishment in 1956. They theorized that 'parity' – the idea that the universe behaves the same way regardless of whether you're looking at it directly or in a mirror – wasn't always conserved in weak nuclear interactions. It was like saying a left-handed glove couldn't just turn into a right-handed one by mirroring it! This mind-bending proposal, soon experimentally confirmed by Chien-Shiung Wu, earned Yang and Lee the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1957, making them the first Chinese-born Nobel laureates. But Yang's genius didn't stop there. He also developed the Yang-Mills theory, a cornerstone of modern particle physics that describes fundamental forces, essentially paving the way for the Standard Model. Still actively contributing to science well into his nineties, this theoretical trailblazer keeps proving that some minds just don't quit.
