Meet Alice Stewart, the British epidemiologist who wasn't afraid to challenge the status quo, even when it meant taking on the nuclear establishment. Born in 1906, Alice was a sharp cookie who, in the 1950s, uncovered a shocking link between prenatal X-rays and childhood cancer. Her groundbreaking Oxford Survey of Childhood Cancers meticulously collected data, showing that even seemingly 'safe' low doses of radiation could be harmful. Her findings were met with skepticism and resistance from those who benefited from the use of radiation, but Stewart stood firm. She continued her research, even warning about the dangers of nuclear fallout long before it was fashionable. Her work reshaped our understanding of radiation risks and saved countless lives, proving that sometimes, being right is more important than being popular.
