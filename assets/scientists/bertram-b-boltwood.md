Bertram B. Boltwood, born in Amherst, Massachusetts, in 1870, was the scientist who practically put a birth certificate on our planet. Working at a time when radioactivity was a brand-new, mind-boggling phenomenon, Boltwood realized that the steady decay of uranium into lead could act as a geological clock. In 1907, he meticulously measured uranium-lead ratios in mineral samples, providing the first truly scientific and robust estimates for the age of the Earth – placing it in the billions of years, a radical departure from previous religious or geological estimates. He basically opened up a whole new field: geochronology. His work was pivotal in shifting scientific understanding of Earth's deep time, laying the foundation for modern geology and planetary science. Boltwood tragically died by suicide in 1927, but his legacy profoundly changed our perception of our planet's ancient history.
