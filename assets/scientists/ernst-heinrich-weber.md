Ever wonder how sensitive your senses really are? Ernst Heinrich Weber, a German physician born in 1795, basically wrote the book on it! Considered a founding father of experimental psychology, Weber meticulously studied human sensory responses, conducting pioneering experiments on touch, weight perception, and the 'just noticeable difference.' His most famous contribution, 'Weber's Law,' states that the change in a stimulus that is just noticeable is a constant ratio of the original stimulus. In simpler terms, to notice a difference, the change needs to be proportionally related to what's already there. He brought scientific rigor to the study of the mind, proving that even our squishy brains can be measured and understood!
