Forget everything you thought you knew about early European farmers – Andrew Sherratt probably shook it up. This sharp British archaeologist, born in 1946, was the mind behind the 'Secondary Products Revolution.' Instead of just seeing early humans as hunters or primary product growers, Sherratt highlighted how they cleverly started exploiting animals for things *other* than just meat – think milk, wool, and pulling power for plows. This wasn't just a quirky idea; it was a massive shift that explained why societies grew, expanded, and developed agriculture in new ways across Eurasia. Sherratt basically said, 'It's not just about dinner; it's about dairy and transportation too!' And just like that, prehistory got a whole lot more complex and fascinating.
