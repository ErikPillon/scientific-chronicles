Before insecticides were a household name, the U.S. government decided it needed an expert on all things creepy-crawly. Enter Townend Glover, appointed in 1854 as the first official U.S. Entomologist within the Patent Office (which handled agricultural matters back then). Born in England but making his significant mark in America, Glover was a true pioneer in agricultural entomology. He meticulously documented insects, their life cycles, and their impact on crops, often using his own intricate and pioneering illustrations. His groundbreaking work laid the groundwork for understanding pest control and protecting American agriculture, proving that even tiny bugs could have a colossal impact on the economy. He essentially spent his career telling farmers, 'Here's what's eating your crops, and here's how to fight back!'
