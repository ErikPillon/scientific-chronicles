As a Harvard undergrad, Donald Griffin borrowed newly invented ultrasonic detection equipment and pointed it at flying bats. What he found rewrote the textbooks: bats navigate and hunt using echolocation, bouncing high-pitched shrieks off objects and reading the echoes, all invisible to human ears. He coined the very term 'echolocation' to describe it. Decades later, Griffin took an even bigger professional risk by arguing that animals might have conscious thoughts and awareness, a position considered borderline heretical in a field trained to avoid anthropomorphism. He more or less founded the discipline of cognitive ethology single-handedly. Not bad for a career that started with a guy in a dark room listening to what bats were actually shouting. 🦇🔊
