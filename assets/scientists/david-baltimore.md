Born in New York in 1938, David Baltimore didn't just study viruses; he revolutionized our understanding of them. In 1970, he dropped a bombshell discovery: the enzyme reverse transcriptase, which allows RNA viruses (like HIV, gulp!) to write their genetic information back into DNA. This completely upended the central dogma of molecular biology, proving that genetic info doesn't just flow one way from DNA to RNA to protein. For this mind-bending feat, he snagged a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1975, sharing it with Howard Temin and Renato Dulbecco. Beyond the lab, he’s been a fierce advocate for science, even navigating controversies. He's still going strong, shaping scientific policy and mentoring the next generation. A true scientific rockstar!
