Felix Wankel was a true maverick – a self-taught German engineer who, despite never earning a driver's license, designed one of the most distinctive engines ever! From an early age, Wankel was obsessed with rotary motion. He started developing his unique engine concept in the 1920s and finally brought it to fruition in 1957. Unlike conventional piston engines, the Wankel engine uses a triangular rotor that spins inside an oval chamber, producing power more smoothly and with fewer parts. While it had its quirks, its compact size and high power-to-weight ratio made it famous in sports cars (think Mazda RX-7) and even aircraft. A testament to thinking outside the piston box!
