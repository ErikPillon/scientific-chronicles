Giuseppe Mercalli, an ordained priest turned dedicated volcano and earthquake researcher, spent his career studying the eruptions of Vesuvius and the seismic hazards of southern Italy up close. In 1902 he devised what became known as the Mercalli intensity scale, a system for classifying earthquakes not by abstract magnitude but by their observed effects on buildings, landscapes, and people, from barely perceptible tremors up to total devastation. It's a wonderfully practical scale precisely because it doesn't require any instruments, just careful observation of the damage, which is why modified versions of it are still used by seismologists today. In a grim twist of fate, Mercalli died in a fire in his own apartment, possibly caused by a knocked-over oil lamp, an oddly domestic end for a man who spent his life studying nature's more dramatic disasters. 🌋📊
