Before steamboats were puffing their way down rivers, there was James Rumsey, an American inventor who dared to dream of a vessel powered by steam. In 1787, his mechanical marvel chugged along the Potomac River, proving that steam power wasn't just for factories anymore. While Robert Fulton often gets all the glory, Rumsey was an unsung hero of the aquatic revolution, battling rival inventors and skeptical onlookers with equal fervor. He laid crucial groundwork for the age of steam navigation, making him a true pioneer who helped shrink the world, one watery journey at a time.
