Before city streets were bathed in the soft glow of LEDs, they were brilliantly, if somewhat flickeringly, lit by the arc lamps of Charles Francis Brush. This American electrical engineer was a true pioneer in the age of electricity. In the 1870s, he developed an improved dynamo (an electric generator) and a reliable arc lamp that could burn for hours, outshining anything before it. Suddenly, cities like New York and Cleveland could light up their streets, turning night into a more productive and safer time. He basically brought the night-life (and night-work) to urban centers, revolutionizing public safety and setting the stage for Edison's incandescent bulb and beyond. Brush was definitely a bright spark in scientific history!
