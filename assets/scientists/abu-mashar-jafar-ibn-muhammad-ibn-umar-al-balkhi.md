Step aside, horoscope apps! Long before Twitter, there was Albumasar, a Persian polymath born around 787. This intellectual giant of the Islamic Golden Age wasn't just an astronomer; he was arguably the most famous astrologer of his time, whose prolific writings profoundly influenced both Islamic and, crucially, European medieval thought. His detailed astrological theories, which connected planetary movements to earthly events and human destiny, were translated into Latin and became core texts in European universities for centuries, blending scientific observation with philosophical inquiry. Albumasar's work showcased the rich interconnections between astronomy and astrology in the pre-modern world, demonstrating how celestial mechanics were used to interpret and predict the world around them. He literally mapped the heavens to understand life on Earth, influencing minds for nearly a millennium!
