Ignaz Semmelweis, born in 1818, was a Hungarian doctor who probably invented the phrase 'wash your hands!' (or at least, he should have). Working in maternity wards in the mid-19th century, he was horrified by the terrifying rates of puerperal fever, a deadly infection plaguing new mothers. He noticed that doctors who performed autopsies and then delivered babies had a much higher rate of infection than midwives. His radical solution in 1847? Get doctors to wash their hands with chlorinated lime solution before examining patients. The death rates plummeted! Despite overwhelming evidence, his peers mocked and resisted his ideas because, well, the concept of invisible 'germs' wasn't mainstream yet. Tragically, Semmelweis died without seeing his life-saving discovery widely accepted. Today, he's hailed as a pioneer of antiseptic procedures and a champion of public health.
