Benjamin Rush wasn't just a doctor; he was a medical rockstar and a Founding Father, signing the Declaration of Independence and all that jazz! Born in Pennsylvania in 1746, Rush was a man of many hats: physician, chemist, educator, and a tireless social reformer. He's often called the 'Father of American Psychiatry' because he was way ahead of his time, arguing that mental illness was a disease, not a punishment, and advocating for humane treatment. Seriously, he was pushing for better conditions for patients when most folks thought 'asylum' meant 'throw away the key.' He also campaigned against slavery, for public education, and for prison reform. His medical theories, particularly his reliance on bloodletting and purging, might make us cringe today (it was the 18th century, after all!), but his progressive ideas on public health and social justice were truly revolutionary. Rush even taught chemistry at the College of Philadelphia, authoring the first American textbook on the subject. So, while his treatments were a bit... intense, his heart was always in the right place, making him one of the most intriguing and influential figures of early American history.
