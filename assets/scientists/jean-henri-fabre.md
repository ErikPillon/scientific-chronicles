Working largely outside formal academic institutions and often in real financial hardship, Jean-Henri Fabre spent an extraordinary career meticulously observing insects in their natural habitat, wasps, beetles, spiders, and above all his beloved dung beetles, recording their behavior with a patience that bordered on the obsessive. His multi-volume 'Souvenirs Entomologiques' turned rigorous field observation into genuinely gripping writing, influencing everyone from Charles Darwin to a young generation of naturalists who'd never before found insects interesting. Darwin himself called him 'that inimitable observer.' Fabre worked well into his eighties, largely self-funded, driven by nothing more than sheer curiosity about what bugs get up to when nobody's watching. Turns out, quite a lot. 🐝🔍
