Elie Metchnikov, a true pioneer in immunology, peered into the microscopic world and revolutionized our understanding of how our bodies fight off disease. Born in Ukraine (then part of the Russian Empire), he observed certain cells 'eating' foreign particles – a process he famously dubbed phagocytosis. This groundbreaking discovery, made in 1882, revealed a cellular immune defense mechanism, earning him a share of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1908. His work laid a crucial foundation for modern immunology, teaching us that our own cells are our first line of defense against unwelcome guests.
