Hermann M. Biggs was the unsung hero who taught New York City how to *not* get sick. This pioneering American physician and public health advocate was a titan in the fight against infectious diseases, especially tuberculosis, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. As the chief medical officer for NYC, he implemented groundbreaking measures like mandatory reporting of infectious diseases, free sputum tests, and public education campaigns – basically inventing modern urban public health. He pushed for legislation to ensure sanitary conditions and safe food, significantly reducing mortality rates. Biggs proved that sometimes, the best medicine isn't a pill, but a well-organized, proactive public health system that protects everyone.
