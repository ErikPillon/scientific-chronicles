Meet George W. Fuller, the unsung hero who cleaned up America's water supply in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Back when typhoid and cholera were regular party crashers, Fuller, a brilliant sanitary engineer, figured out how to make municipal water treatment plants actually work. He wasn't just building sand filters; he was building a healthier future for entire cities. His groundbreaking work on rapid sand filtration and disinfection meant folks could drink from the tap without playing a game of microbial roulette. So next time you enjoy a refreshing glass of H2O, raise a toast to Fuller – he truly made waves in public health!
