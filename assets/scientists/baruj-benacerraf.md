Baruj Benacerraf was an immunological maestro, conducting a symphony of research that revealed how our genes dictate our immune system's every move. Born in Venezuela, he made his monumental discoveries in the US, sharing the 1980 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. His work focused on the major histocompatibility complex (MHC), a group of genes that control the immune system's ability to distinguish between "self" and "non-self." Understanding MHC was a game-changer for organ transplants, autoimmune diseases, and vaccine development. Benacerraf’s insights into this genetic control transformed our understanding of immunity, paving the way for countless medical advancements.
