Edward Charles Pickering, born in 1846, was an American astronomer who basically invented modern photometry and spectrography. Forget just looking at stars; Pickering wanted to measure their light with scientific precision. But here's the best part: he ran the Harvard College Observatory and hired a groundbreaking team of brilliant women, affectionately (and somewhat controversially) known as 'Pickering's Harem.' These 'human computers' – including legends like Annie Jump Cannon and Henrietta Swan Leavitt – processed astronomical data, cataloged thousands of stars, and made discoveries like the period-luminosity relationship for Cepheid variables, giving us a cosmic yardstick. Pickering didn't just observe; he orchestrated a revolution in how we understand the universe, proving that sometimes, the sharpest minds (and eyes!) are the best tech.
