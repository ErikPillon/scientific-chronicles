Howard Martin Temin was a bit of a rebel in the world of molecular biology. Conventional wisdom said DNA makes RNA, which makes protein – a one-way street, the 'central dogma.' But Temin, along with David Baltimore, dared to challenge this fundamental belief. In 1970, he co-discovered reverse transcriptase, an enzyme that allows RNA to be 'reverse transcribed' back into DNA. This mind-blowing discovery, which earned him a share of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1975, was crucial for understanding retroviruses like HIV and how they cause disease. Temin flipped molecular biology on its head, revealing a whole new pathway for genetic information and opening doors to new cancer research and treatments. Sometimes, it pays to think backward!
