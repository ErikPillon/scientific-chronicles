Edwin G. Krebs was a biochemical detective who, along with his partner Edmond H. Fischer, cracked one of life's most fundamental codes. They won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1992 for their groundbreaking discovery of reversible protein phosphorylation. Now, that's a mouthful, but here's why it's a big deal: imagine tiny on-off switches inside every cell that control virtually all biological processes, from muscle contraction to gene expression. Krebs and Fischer found that adding or removing a phosphate group (phosphorylation) acts as these switches, quickly changing a protein's function. It's like finding the master control panel for cellular life! This reversible process is essential for regulating metabolism, cell growth, nerve signals, and pretty much everything else. Their discovery revolutionized biochemistry and medicine, opening doors for understanding diseases like cancer and diabetes, and developing new drugs. Krebs basically showed us a core mechanism that keeps our bodies humming, making him a true maestro of molecular signaling. He found the 'pause' and 'play' buttons of life!
