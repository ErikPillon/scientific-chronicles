Before we had satellites and supercomputers, George Hadley figured out why the winds blow the way they do – a massive feat of deduction! This 18th-century English lawyer-turned-meteorologist basically laid down the law on global air circulation, explaining the trade winds and giving us the "Hadley Cell." He showed how warm air rises at the equator, moves towards the poles, cools, and then sinks, shaping weather patterns across the globe. Pretty smart for a guy whose main gig was probably debating land deeds. His work was fundamental to understanding Earth's climate system, proving that sometimes, the biggest insights come from just looking up and thinking really hard.
