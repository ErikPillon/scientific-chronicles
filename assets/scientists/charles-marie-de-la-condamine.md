Forget armchair philosophy, Charles-Marie de La Condamine was a true 'boots-on-the-ground' Enlightenment figure! This intrepid French explorer, geographer, and mathematician embarked on an epic and utterly perilous expedition to Peru (modern-day Ecuador) in the 1730s. His mission? To measure the length of a degree of latitude at the Equator, a crucial undertaking to finally settle the debate on whether the Earth was flattened at the poles (oblate) or elongated (prolate). Battling treacherous terrain, hostile natives, disease, and internal squabbles, he spent years in the Amazon basin. His findings, alongside those of a parallel expedition to Lapland, definitively proved Isaac Newton's theory that the Earth was indeed oblate. Not only did he measure the Earth, but he also brought back quinine (malaria medicine), introduced rubber to Europe, and made extensive observations on local flora and fauna. La Condamine was the ultimate scientific adventurer, proving that some questions can only be answered by a truly epic journey.
