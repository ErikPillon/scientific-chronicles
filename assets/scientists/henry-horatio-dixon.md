Ever wondered how a giant sequoia sips water from its roots all the way to its crown, without a pump or even a tiny straw? That's where Henry Horatio Dixon steps in! This brilliant Irish botanist, along with his pal J. Joly, conjured up the "cohesion-tension theory" in 1894. Essentially, they figured out that water molecules are super clingy (cohesion) and evaporate from leaves (transpiration), creating a tension that pulls an unbroken column of water up the plant. It's like a microscopic, continuous tug-of-war against gravity, and Dixon was the one who eloquently laid out how these green giants perform such an impressive feat of fluid dynamics daily. He didn't just study plants; he helped us understand their secret plumbing system!
