Jean-Pierre Serre won the Fields Medal in 1954 at age 27, a record for youngest recipient that still stands today, for foundational work connecting algebraic topology and complex analysis. That would be an entire career's worth of achievement for most mathematicians, but Serre kept producing landmark results across algebraic geometry, number theory, and group theory for the following six decades. He's known for an unusually clear, almost minimalist writing style; his textbooks are considered models of mathematical exposition, dense but never a single wasted word. In 2003 he also won the Abel Prize, making him one of the very few mathematicians to hold both of the field's most prestigious honors. Being brilliant at 27 is impressive; staying essential at the top of your field for the following sixty years is another thing entirely. 🏆➗
