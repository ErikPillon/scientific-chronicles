Donald A. Glaser, the guy who gave particle physicists their ultimate magnifying glass, was a true legend. Bored with studying cosmic rays in cloud chambers, he thought, 'Why not bubbles?' And just like that, at only 26, he cooked up the bubble chamber, a device that uses superheated liquid to reveal the paths of invisible subatomic particles. It was an instant hit, quickly becoming *the* essential tool for high-energy physics, allowing scientists to photograph particle interactions. For this ingenious invention, he snagged a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1960. Later in life, he shifted gears to neurobiology and even dabbed in biotech startups, proving that a brilliant mind can chase curiosity wherever it leads, from quarks to consciousness and beyond.
