Before Ian Donald, figuring out what was happening inside a pregnant belly was mostly guesswork (and a stethoscope). This Scottish obstetrician, inspired by industrial ultrasound, bravely pioneered its use in medicine in the 1950s. He developed the first machines to accurately image unborn babies, diagnose gynecological conditions, and truly revolutionize prenatal care. Thanks to Donald, expectant parents could get their first peek at their little ones long before birth, transforming pregnancy from a mystery into a monitored journey. He literally helped us see the unseen, making him a true visionary in modern medicine.
