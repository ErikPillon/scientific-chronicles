George Coyne was a Jesuit priest and a stellar astronomer, who famously headed the Vatican Observatory for decades. He wasn't just observing distant galaxies; he was a passionate advocate for bridging the perceived gap between science and faith. He believed that the pursuit of scientific truth and spiritual understanding weren't at odds but could enrich each other, often engaging in lively discussions about evolution, the Big Bang, and the existence of God. Coyne's work helped modernize the Vatican's approach to science, proving you could wear a cassock and still be utterly fascinated by the cosmos.
