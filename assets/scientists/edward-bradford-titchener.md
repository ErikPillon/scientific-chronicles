Edward Bradford Titchener was the ultimate mental deconstructionist, and if you ever took an intro psychology class, you probably heard his name alongside Wilhelm Wundt, his mentor. Titchener brought Wundt's experimental psychology to America and then put his own spin on it, founding the school of thought called structuralism. His big idea? That the human mind, like a complex chemical compound, could be broken down into its most basic elements – sensations, images, and affections – through highly trained introspection. Forget fuzzy feelings; Titchener wanted to categorize every mental atom! While structuralism eventually gave way to other schools of thought (like functionalism and behaviorism), his rigorous, scientific approach to studying consciousness was groundbreaking and laid crucial groundwork for psychology to become a proper science. He even trained a significant number of women in psychology at a time when that was rare.
