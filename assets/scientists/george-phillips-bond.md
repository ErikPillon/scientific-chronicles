George Phillips Bond was an American astronomer who basically made the cosmos camera-ready. Working at the Harvard College Observatory, he wasn't just observing the stars; he was *photographing* them! In the mid-19th century, this was a massive deal, pushing the boundaries of what telescopes could do. He captured some of the earliest daguerreotypes of celestial objects like the Moon and stars, giving us a permanent record of the night sky. Oh, and he also co-discovered Saturn's eighth moon, Hyperion, and the planet's C-ring. Talk about a multi-talented celestial trailblazer, all before the age of 40!
