Hugo de Vries was a Dutch botanist whose biggest claim to fame was, ironically, rediscovering someone else's brilliant work: Gregor Mendel's laws of heredity! He didn't just stop there, though. In a stroke of genius (or perhaps just observing nature very, very closely), de Vries also introduced the idea of 'mutation' in evolution, suggesting that sudden, heritable changes could drive species diversity. Imagine being the guy who helped bring genetics into the modern age, building on forgotten foundations and adding a crucial new brick. He spent years playing with evening primroses, and thank goodness he did, because his observations shook up the biological world, even if some of his original ideas about mutation needed a little tweaking later on.
