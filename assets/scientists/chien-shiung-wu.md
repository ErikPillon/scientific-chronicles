Chien-Shiung Wu, born in China in 1912, was a formidable experimental physicist whose precision and ingenuity earned her the moniker 'First Lady of Physics.' A key contributor to the Manhattan Project, she later achieved international fame for the 'Wu experiment' in 1956, which definitively demonstrated that parity—a fundamental symmetry principle—is violated during weak nuclear interactions. This stunning discovery, which contradicted deeply held assumptions about the universe, earned her colleagues Tsung-Dao Lee and Chen-Ning Yang the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1957. Wu's omission from the Nobel award is widely considered one of its greatest injustices. Despite this, her meticulous work, groundbreaking insights, and fearless pursuit of scientific truth solidified her legacy as one of the most influential physicists of the 20th century. She truly broke some physics rules!
