Eugen Sänger was an Austrian aerospace engineer who thought way, way beyond the typical airplane. In the 1930s, he, along with his wife Irene Bredt, cooked up revolutionary concepts for 'antipodal bombers' – essentially, sub-orbital, rocket-powered spaceplanes designed to skip off the atmosphere. His 'Silbervogel' (Silverbird) project for Nazi Germany, though never built, was a radical design that would have been capable of bombing New York from Germany. Despite the sinister application, his theoretical work on ramjets, rocket propulsion, and hypersonic flight was incredibly forward-thinking, influencing post-war aerospace research. He explored concepts like reusable spacecraft and even photon propulsion, making him a visionary figure who truly pushed the boundaries of what was imaginable in flight and space travel, long before we had the tech to build it.
