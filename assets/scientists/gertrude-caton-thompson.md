Gertrude Caton-Thompson was a British archaeologist who literally dug up history, proving that the past holds endless secrets if you know where to look! A trailblazer in a male-dominated field, she became renowned for her meticulous scientific methods, particularly her stratigraphic excavations in Egypt and Zimbabwe (then Rhodesia). Unlike some treasure hunters of her era, she was all about careful documentation and understanding context. Her 1929 work at Great Zimbabwe definitively showed that the impressive ruins were built by an indigenous African civilization, challenging prevailing colonialist theories. She also made significant contributions to understanding the prehistoric cultures of the Fayum Depression in Egypt. Caton-Thompson wasn't just unearthing artifacts; she was unearthing truth, helping to rewrite the narrative of ancient African history. She was truly a giant in her field, paving the way for modern archaeological practices with her rigorous approach.
