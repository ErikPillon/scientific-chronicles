Hugh Burgess might not be a household name, but if you've ever read a book or written on paper, you owe him a debt of gratitude! This British chemist was a pivotal figure in the 19th-century revolution of papermaking. He was instrumental in developing and commercializing the chemical pulping process, particularly the sulfite process, which allowed wood to be efficiently converted into high-quality paper pulp. Before Burgess and his contemporaries, paper was often made from rags, a much scarcer resource. His work made paper affordable and abundant, paving the way for mass-produced newspapers, books, and the explosion of information in the modern age.
