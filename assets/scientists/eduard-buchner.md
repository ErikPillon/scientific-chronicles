Before Buchner came along, everyone thought fermentation needed the 'life force' of a whole yeast cell. How quaint! Our man Eduard smashed that idea, literally, by grinding up yeast and showing that the enzymes alone (he called them 'zymase') could get the job done. This wasn't just a party trick; it totally revolutionized biochemistry, revealing that many biological reactions weren't some mystical secret but rather chemical processes driven by specific molecules. He scooped up a Nobel Prize in 1907 for proving that you don't need the whole organism to get the good stuff brewing. His work was a huge leap towards understanding metabolism and enzyme action, paving the way for modern molecular biology. Talk about busting myths with a mortar and pestle!
