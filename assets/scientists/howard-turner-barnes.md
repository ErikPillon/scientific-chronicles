Before climate change was even a blip on the radar, Howard Turner Barnes was diving deep (sometimes literally!) into the mysteries of ice. This Canadian physicist and oceanographer, a professor at McGill University, spent much of his career studying the formation, properties, and behavior of ice, particularly in cold waters like the St. Lawrence River. He was a go-to expert on ice navigation and even proposed early methods for breaking up ice jams using explosives. Barnes's meticulous work laid crucial foundations for understanding polar regions and cold environments, proving that sometimes, the coolest science happens when things get really, really cold. He wasn't just observing ice; he was unlocking its secrets, making vital contributions to glaciology and cryosphere research.
