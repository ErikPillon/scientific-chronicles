Friedrich Gustav Jacob Henle was the kind of doctor who didn't just look at a body; he *really* looked. This German anatomist and pathologist was a master of microscopic detail, famously giving his name to the 'loop of Henle' in the kidney – a tiny, crucial structure that helps us concentrate our pee. Not only did he map out the intricacies of the human body, but he was also way ahead of his time, suggesting that microorganisms were responsible for infectious diseases, long before Pasteur and Koch sealed the deal. His work in anatomy and his forward-thinking ideas made him a giant in 19th-century medicine, proving that small details can lead to massive insights.
