Meet Dirk Coster, the Dutch physicist who, alongside George de Hevesy, pulled off a chemical magic trick in 1923: they found element number 72, which they dubbed hafnium. Imagine sifting through zirconium ores, expecting... well, zirconium, but instead uncovering a brand new element! Coster's expertise in X-ray spectroscopy was absolutely crucial to this discovery, confirming the existence of hafnium and fitting it neatly into Mendeleev's periodic table, right where Niels Bohr had predicted it should be. It was a proper scientific triumph, proving the predictive power of quantum theory and filling a crucial gap in our understanding of matter.
