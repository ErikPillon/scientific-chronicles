Edward Davy, born in 1806, was a British chemist who, despite not always getting top billing, was a seriously sharp cookie in the thrilling early days of electrical communication. He made crucial contributions to the electric telegraph in the 1830s, especially devising a chemical telegraph system and significantly improving the then-nascent electromagnetic telegraph. While Samuel Morse often steals the limelight, Davy's innovations included the vital relay system, which allowed telegraph signals to travel over much longer distances than previously possible. He was a bit unlucky in the patent race, but his ingenious ideas were definitely 'in the air' and undeniably helped pave the way for practical, long-distance telegraphy. A true unsung hero of the information age's dawn.
