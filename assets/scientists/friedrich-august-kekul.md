Ever wonder why chemists draw those hexagonal rings? You can thank Friedrich August Kekulé, the German organic chemistry legend born in 1829. His biggest "Aha!" moment came from a dream – or so the story goes – of a snake biting its own tail, which suddenly illuminated the elusive structure of benzene. Before Kekulé's revelation in 1865, benzene's formula was a mystery, defying the typical linear structures scientists understood. His proposal of a hexagonal ring of carbon atoms, with alternating single and double bonds, was revolutionary and laid the foundation for modern organic chemistry. It wasn't just a cool dream; it was a stroke of genius that unlocked the secrets of countless organic compounds, paving the way for everything from dyes to pharmaceuticals. Kekulé proved that sometimes, the best scientific breakthroughs come not just from hard work, but from letting your mind wander – even into a nap!
