You know his name, or at least the continent named after him, but Amerigo Vespucci was more than just a namesake! This Florentine navigator and explorer was a pretty savvy character who made at least two, possibly four, voyages across the Atlantic. Unlike Columbus, Vespucci was convinced he hadn't landed in Asia but on a "New World." He penned some popular accounts of his travels, vividly describing the lands and their inhabitants. While historians still debate the exact routes and extent of his voyages, his writings definitely captured the imagination of European cartographers. In 1507, German mapmaker Martin Waldseemüller decided to honor Vespucci's insight by labeling the newly charted southern continent "America" on his famous world map. It stuck, much to the chagrin of later historians who argued Columbus deserved more credit for the "discovery." But hey, you can't deny the guy's impact – getting a whole continent named after you is quite the legacy, even if it came with a bit of a historical mix-up!
