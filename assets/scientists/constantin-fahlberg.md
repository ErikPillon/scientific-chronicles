Constantin Fahlberg, a Russian-born German chemist, is famous for arguably one of the tastiest "oops" moments in science history. In 1879, while working at Johns Hopkins University on coal tar derivatives, he forgot to wash his hands after a long day in the lab. Later, at dinner, he noticed his bread tasted sweet. Curious, and probably a bit gross, he tasted his fingers and then rushed back to the lab to trace the source of the sweetness, eventually isolating saccharin. He published his findings with his supervisor, Ira Remsen, but later patented saccharin himself, sparking a bit of a row. While Remsen was reportedly annoyed ("Fahlberg is a scoundrel"), saccharin went on to revolutionize the food industry, offering a sugar alternative that was 300-400 times sweeter than sucrose. So, next time you reach for a sugar-free treat, remember Fahlberg and his rather unhygienic, yet deliciously impactful, discovery!
