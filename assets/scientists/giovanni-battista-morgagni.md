Born in 1682, Giovanni Battista Morgagni was an Italian anatomist whose work totally revamped how doctors understood disease. Before him, illness was often seen as a systemic imbalance, but Morgagni meticulously performed over 700 autopsies, systematically linking specific symptoms observed in patients to corresponding lesions or abnormalities found in their organs after death. His magnum opus, "De Sedibus et Causis Morborum per Anatomen Indagatis" (On the Seats and Causes of Diseases Investigated by Anatomy), published in 1761, was a game-changer. It established pathological anatomy as a foundational medical science, effectively making him the "Father of Modern Pathological Anatomy." Morgagni taught us to look inside the body to find the precise physical seat of disease, transforming medicine from vague theories into a more precise, evidence-based discipline. He was the ultimate medical detective, solving cases from beyond the grave!
