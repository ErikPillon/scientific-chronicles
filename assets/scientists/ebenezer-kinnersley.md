Meet Ebenezer Kinnersley, the 18th-century showman-scientist who probably gave more people their first electric shock (for science!) than anyone else. Born in England but making his mark in colonial America, Kinnersley was a fervent experimenter with electricity, often collaborating with none other than Benjamin Franklin. While Franklin might get the lightning rod fame, Kinnersley was key in demonstrating its principles, even rigging up a system that would ring bells when struck by static electricity. He traveled extensively, giving public lectures and demonstrations that wowed audiences, showing off everything from electric sparks to the rather shocking effects on a human body (all for educational purposes, of course!). He correctly identified that different 'electricities' (positive and negative charges) were not different types of fluid but different states of the same fluid. A true pioneer, Kinnersley helped popularize and advance the understanding of electricity in an era when it still seemed like pure magic.
