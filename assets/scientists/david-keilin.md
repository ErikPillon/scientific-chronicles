David Keilin, a Russian-born British scientist, was a bit of a detective for the microscopic world, but he wasn't just observing tiny creatures; he was getting right down to how they actually lived and breathed. Keilin is most famous for his dramatic rediscovery and naming of the cytochromes – those unsung protein heroes in our cells that are absolutely crucial for cellular respiration, which is basically how we (and almost all life) get energy from food. Imagine finding the tiny, essential cogs that make the entire biological engine run! His work didn't just advance entomology and parasitology; it laid a critical cornerstone in biochemistry, revealing fundamental machinery without which life as we know it simply wouldn't exist.
