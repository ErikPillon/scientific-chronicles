Jean Picard led a landmark 1669-1670 survey to measure the length of one degree of latitude along the meridian through Paris, using rigorous triangulation methods and improved telescopic sighting instruments. From that measurement he calculated the Earth's radius with unprecedented accuracy for the era. That figure turned out to matter well beyond mapmaking: Isaac Newton used Picard's improved Earth-size measurement to correct his own earlier calculations and confirm that his law of universal gravitation actually matched the Moon's observed orbit, a crucial validation for the theory that would define physics for the next two centuries. Picard also helped found the Paris Observatory and trained a generation of French astronomers in precision measurement. Sometimes the guy with the measuring tape ends up making Newton's whole theory work. 🌍📏
