Wilson Markle spent years figuring out how to computationally colorize black-and-white film and video, assigning realistic colors to each shade of gray in a scene using computer processing. His first company, Image Transform, used the technique to add color to black-and-white footage from the Apollo space program for NASA broadcasts. In 1983 he founded Colorization Inc. with Hal Roach Studios, and their process went on to (controversially) colorize classic black-and-white movies, sparking a real debate in Hollywood about whether old films should be "improved" with color at all. Whatever side of that argument you land on, the underlying engineering, teaching a computer to guess plausible colors from shades of gray, was a genuinely clever piece of image processing well ahead of its time. 🎞️🌈
