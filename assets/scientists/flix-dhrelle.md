Meet Félix d’Hérelle, a French-Canadian microbiologist whose scientific journey took him from Guatemala to Paris, and whose biggest discovery was, ironically, about incredibly tiny things that kill even tinier things. In 1917, while working on a dysentery outbreak, d’Hérelle independently observed invisible agents that were destroying bacteria. He called them 'bacteriophages' – 'bacteria eaters.' This wasn't just a cool observation; it revealed a whole new biological weapon against bacterial infections, long before antibiotics became widespread. Though phage therapy fell out of favor in the West with the rise of penicillin, d’Hérelle's pioneering work laid the groundwork for modern virology and genetics. He proved that even bacteria have their own microscopic predators, forever changing our understanding of the microbial world and offering a tantalizing alternative in the fight against disease. He basically found nature's own tiny hitmen!
