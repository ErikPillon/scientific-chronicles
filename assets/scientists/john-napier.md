John Napier, a Scottish landowner with a serious interest in mathematics and theology (and a reputation among locals for dabbling in the occult, partly thanks to a pet black rooster), spent roughly twenty years developing logarithms, a mathematical tool that converts brutal multiplication and division problems into much simpler addition and subtraction. Published in 1614, the idea spread through the scientific world astonishingly fast, becoming essential for astronomers and navigators who previously spent enormous amounts of time on manual calculations. Johannes Kepler was reportedly delighted by the invention, since it dramatically sped up his planetary calculations. Napier also invented 'Napier's bones,' a simple manual calculating tool using rods, an early ancestor of the slide rule and, eventually, the calculator. 🧮📖
