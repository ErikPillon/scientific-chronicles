Georges Cuvier, the French rockstar of bones, basically invented paleontology and comparative anatomy. Before him, people thought weird giant skeletons were mythical beasts. Cuvier, however, looked at those fossils and declared, "Nope, these creatures are *gone*." He introduced the radical (for the time) concept of extinction, proving that species could vanish forever. He was a master at reconstructing entire animals from just a few fragments, like a prehistoric CSI detective, using his deep understanding of how different body parts connect. His work wasn't just about digging up old bones; it was about building a scientific framework for understanding the history of life on Earth, even if he wasn't keen on Darwin's evolutionary ideas. He might have been a bit of a catastrophist, believing in big, sudden events, but his impact on how we study ancient life is still monumental. He taught us that even in death, creatures could reveal the grand story of life.
