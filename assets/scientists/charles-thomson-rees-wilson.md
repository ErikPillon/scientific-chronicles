Charles Thomson Rees Wilson was a Scottish physicist whose fascination with clouds led to a revolutionary invention. While observing atmospheric phenomena on Ben Nevis, he became captivated by the 'glories' and later developed the cloud chamber in 1911. This ingenious device made the invisible world of subatomic particles visible, allowing scientists to track their paths and study their properties. His invention was a game-changer for nuclear physics and earned him a Nobel Prize in 1927, proving that sometimes, looking up at the sky can lead to profound insights into the smallest building blocks of the universe.
