Charles Proteus Steinmetz, born Karl August Rudolf Steinmetz in Germany in 1865, was a true titan of electrical engineering. He immigrated to the US and became a driving force at General Electric, where he tackled the complex math behind alternating current (AC) systems. Back when electricity was a wild, untamed beast, Steinmetz developed mathematical tools (like applying complex numbers to AC circuits) that made it manageable and efficient. This made AC power viable, helping electrify America and beyond. Despite physical disabilities, his intellectual prowess was unmatched, leading to over 200 patents. He was the wizard who tamed lightning, making sure your lights turned on reliably.
