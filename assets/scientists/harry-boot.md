If you've ever eaten microwave popcorn or flown safely thanks to radar, you owe a nod to Harry Boot. This British physicist, alongside John Randall, co-invented the cavity magnetron in 1940. Why was this such a big deal? During World War II, this powerful vacuum tube was the heart of centimeter-wavelength radar, making it possible to detect smaller targets with greater accuracy. It was a game-changer for the Allies, drastically improving defensive capabilities against enemy aircraft and submarines. Post-war, the magnetron found its way into commercial microwave ovens. So, Boot didn't just help win the war; he also helped us heat up leftovers in seconds. Talk about a versatile invention!
