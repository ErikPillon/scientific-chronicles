Bernard de Jussieu, born in 1699, was a French botanist whose green thumb extended far beyond just growing plants; he was intent on bringing order to the botanical chaos of his era. Working at the Royal Garden in Paris (now the Jardin des Plantes), he developed a revolutionary natural system for classifying plants. While Carl Linnaeus was busy with his binomial nomenclature, Jussieu focused on grouping plants based on their natural affinities and shared characteristics, rather than just superficial features like flower parts. He famously arranged the plants in the Trianon gardens at Versailles according to his new system, creating a living botanical encyclopedia. This innovative approach laid the groundwork for modern plant taxonomy and phylogeny, influencing generations of botanists, including his famous nephew, Antoine Laurent de Jussieu. Bernard showed that sometimes, the best way to understand nature is to rethink how we categorize its wonders.
