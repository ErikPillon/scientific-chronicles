Henri-Émile Bazin was a French civil engineer who spent his career watching water flow, but not in a 'staring blankly into a river' kind of way. He was a serious hydraulics expert who meticulously studied how water moves in open channels and over weirs (those little dams that measure flow). His experiments and formulas, particularly the Bazin formula for flow velocity, became fundamental tools for engineers designing canals, irrigation systems, and other water management projects. He basically gave engineers the rulebook for predicting how water would behave, making him the unsung hero of many a smoothly flowing stream.
