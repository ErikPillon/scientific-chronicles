Not to be confused with *the* James Watson of DNA fame, James Watson Cronin was a particle physicist who literally looked for cracks in the laws of physics. Along with Val Fitch, he earned a Nobel Prize in 1980 for discovering CP violation – a tiny asymmetry in the decay of subatomic particles called neutral K-mesons. What's so cool about that? It hinted that matter and antimatter aren't perfect mirror images, which might just explain why there's any matter (like us!) left in the universe at all. Without this slight imbalance, everything would have just annihilated, leaving... well, nothing. So, Cronin helped solve one of the universe's biggest mysteries, all from watching tiny particles misbehave!
