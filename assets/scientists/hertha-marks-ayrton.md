Hertha Marks Ayrton was a force of nature in an era when women were barely allowed into lecture halls, let alone laboratories! This brilliant British engineer and mathematician wasn't just breaking barriers; she was building better electric arcs (literally!) and untangling the mysteries of sand ripples. Her groundbreaking work on the electric arc lamp earned her serious scientific recognition, though, in typical fashion for the time, she was denied a full fellowship to the Royal Society because, well, she was a woman. Unfazed, Ayrton also became a fierce suffragette, proving that a sharp mind and a passion for justice can electrify both science and society. She was definitely more than just a spark.
