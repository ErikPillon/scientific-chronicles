Guillermo Haro was a star among Mexican astronomers, and not just because he studied them! Born in Mexico City, Haro’s journey to the stars was less about rockets and more about sheer grit and a sharp eye. He became a self-taught astronomer, eventually heading the National Astronomical Observatory of Mexico. His big claim to fame? The discovery of what are now known as Herbig-Haro objects – fuzzy patches of nebulosity associated with newly born stars, observed independently by George Herbig. But that's not all; he also found flare stars, T Tauri stars, and even a planetary nebula. Haro was a champion for science in Mexico, passionate about fostering a new generation of astronomers and putting Mexican astronomy on the global map. He truly helped Mexico reach for the stars!
