Before Elisabeth Kübler-Ross, discussions around death were largely taboo, hidden away and often misunderstood. This brave Swiss-American psychiatrist changed all that. Through extensive interviews with terminally ill patients, she developed her groundbreaking 'five stages of grief' model – denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance – outlined in her 1969 book 'On Death and Dying.' Her work wasn't just about the dying; it was about the living, too, offering a framework for understanding and coping with loss. She revolutionized palliative care and helped us all talk more openly and compassionately about life's final chapter.
