If you're reading this online, you owe a big thank you to Donald B. Keck. Along with Robert Maurer and Peter Schultz at Corning Glass Works, he cracked the code for creating low-loss optical fiber in 1970. Before Keck's breakthrough, light signals couldn't travel far without fading, making fiber optics a futuristic dream. But his team's pure silica glass fibers, so clear you could see through miles of it, revolutionized telecommunications, paving the way for the internet, high-speed data, and pretty much every digital convenience we take for granted today. He turned a scientific curiosity into the backbone of our connected world, proving that sometimes, the clearest vision comes from the clearest glass, and that a material scientist can literally connect the globe.
