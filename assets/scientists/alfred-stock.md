Alfred Stock was a German chemist who was equally brilliant with complex compounds and tenacious in battling an insidious poison. He became a world expert on the quirky chemistry of boron hydrides (boranes) and silicon hydrides (silanes), discovering and characterizing a whole slew of new, often highly reactive, compounds. His work laid the groundwork for entire fields of inorganic chemistry that are still active today. But Stock also had a more personal scientific crusade: he suffered from mercury poisoning due to laboratory exposure and dedicated significant effort to understanding its effects and developing safer lab practices. He championed the use of vacuum lines and proper ventilation, effectively making chemistry safer for future generations. So, while his name might not be on a household product, his legacy is literally in the air – cleaner, safer air in laboratories thanks to his diligent work and advocacy. A true hero of both discovery and occupational health!
