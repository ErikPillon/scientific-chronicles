Amelia Blanford Edwards started her career as a popular novelist and journalist, but a life-changing trip to Egypt in 1873 transformed her into a passionate Egyptologist. Horrified by the looting and destruction of ancient sites, she dedicated her life to promoting systematic and responsible archaeological research. She wasn't just observing; she was *doing*. Edwards became a leading figure in the field, giving lectures and writing extensively, most notably 'A Thousand Miles Up the Nile.' Crucially, in 1882, she co-founded the Egypt Exploration Fund (now Society), which funded major excavations and helped establish modern archaeological practices. This formidable Victorian woman proved that a love for ancient history could transform a successful writer into a powerful force for preservation and scientific inquiry, ensuring that Egypt's treasures were studied, not just plundered.
