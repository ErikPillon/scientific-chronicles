In 1960, a young Englishwoman with no university training arrived at Gombe Stream in Tanzania, armed mostly with patience and a notebook, to watch wild chimpanzees. What she found upended decades of assumptions: chimps used and even made tools (stripping twigs to fish for termites), had distinct personalities, formed complex social bonds, and yes, occasionally waged brutal warfare on neighboring troops. Her mentor Louis Leakey had bet that studying chimps would tell us something about early humans, and Goodall's decades of fieldwork proved him spectacularly right. She later became one of the world's most recognizable conservationists, turning her research into a global advocacy career for wildlife and environmental protection. All from just sitting quietly in a forest and actually paying attention. 🐒🌳
