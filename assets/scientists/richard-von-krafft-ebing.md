Before Freud made sex fashionable, there was Baron Richard von Krafft-Ebing, a Victorian-era psychiatrist who dove headfirst into the murky waters of human sexuality. His 1886 magnum opus, *Psychopathia Sexualis*, became an instant classic (and a scandalous bestseller), meticulously documenting everything from fetishism to sadism and masochism (terms he helped popularize). While some of his classifications might raise eyebrows today, Krafft-Ebing was a serious scientific observer attempting to bring clinical understanding to behaviors often dismissed as moral failings. He laid crucial groundwork for future sexologists, even if his detailed case studies were sometimes more titillating than strictly academic.
