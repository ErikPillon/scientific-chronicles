Ferdinand Reich was a German scientist with a keen eye for the unseen – or rather, the spectroscopically visible! In 1863, while hunting for Thallium in zinc ore samples with his assistant Theodor Richter, they stumbled upon something new. Instead of Thallium's green line, their spectroscope showed a brilliant indigo line. This wasn't just a pretty light show; it was a brand-new element! They named it Indium, after the color. Their discovery was a testament to the power of spectroscopy in uncovering the universe's chemical secrets and added another shiny piece to the periodic table puzzle. Not bad for an afternoon's work, eh?
