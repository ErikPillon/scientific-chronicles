Christine Ladd-Franklin was a force of nature in academia, proving that brilliance knows no gender, even when institutions tried to deny it. This American polymath completed her Ph.D. requirements in mathematics at Johns Hopkins in 1882, but they didn't officially grant her the degree until 1926 – forty-four years later! Undeterred, she became a pioneering psychologist, developing a fascinating and influential evolutionary theory of color vision. Her theory proposed that achromatic vision evolved first, followed by blue-yellow, and finally red-green vision, based on observations of how color blindness develops. She didn't just smash through barriers; she helped us understand how we perceive the colorful world around us. Talk about seeing things in a new light!
