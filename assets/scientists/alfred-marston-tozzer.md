Alfred M. Tozzer was a pretty big deal in early 20th-century anthropology, especially if you were into ancient Maya civilization. This American academic wasn't just sitting in an ivory tower; he spent years deep in the jungles of Yucatán, excavating sites and studying the living Maya. Think Indiana Jones, but with more meticulous note-taking and less bullwhip action. He became a leading authority on Mayan languages, religion, and history, publishing foundational works that are still referenced today. Tozzer practically built the modern understanding of Mesoamerican studies at Harvard, nurturing generations of archaeologists. So next time you see a cool documentary about Mayan pyramids, remember Tozzer, the guy who spent his career digging up the secrets of a vanished world, bringing their stories to life long before satellite imagery made it easy.
