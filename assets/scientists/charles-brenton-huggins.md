Before Charles B. Huggins, the idea of treating cancer with hormones seemed like science fiction. But this Canadian-American physician, a brilliant urologist and researcher, proved it was pure, life-saving science. He discovered that by manipulating hormones, he could slow the growth of certain cancers, particularly prostate cancer. This groundbreaking insight, for which he won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1966, was a total game-changer, laying the foundation for modern hormone therapies that continue to save lives today. His work showed that cancer wasn't always a surgical problem; sometimes, it was a chemical conversation that could be interrupted.
