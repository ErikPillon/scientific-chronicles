Ever wondered how we peer into the ultra-tiny realm of viruses and cell structures? Thank James Hillier! This Canadian-American physicist, alongside Albert Prebus, engineered the first successful high-resolution electron microscope at the University of Toronto in the late 1930s. It was a monumental breakthrough, allowing scientists to see things previously invisible and revolutionize fields from biology to materials science. Imagine zooming into cellular intricacies or material defects – that's the kind of revolution Hillier sparked. He spent much of his career at RCA Laboratories, continuing to refine electron microscopy, cementing his legacy as a pioneer of scientific imaging. He basically gave humanity microscopic X-ray vision!
