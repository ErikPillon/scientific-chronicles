Henrietta Swan Leavitt was an American astronomer who, despite being a deaf 'computer' at Harvard, helped unlock the universe's secrets. She painstakingly analyzed photographic plates of stars, eventually discovering that brighter Cepheid variable stars pulsed slower. This seemingly simple observation, the period-luminosity relationship, became a cosmic yardstick! Before Leavitt, estimating distances in space was pure guesswork. Her work allowed Edwin Hubble, and everyone since, to figure out just how vast the universe really is, essentially giving humanity its first real sense of scale in the cosmos. Not bad for someone often overlooked in her time!
