Jack Kilby was the unassuming genius who basically kickstarted the digital age while working at Texas Instruments. In 1958, facing a component crisis, he figured out how to cram all the bits of an electronic circuit onto a single piece of germanium. Presto! The integrated circuit was born, making everything from calculators to smartphones possible. He snagged a Nobel Prize in Physics for this little brainwave in 2000. He wasn't one for the spotlight, but his invention certainly put the 'micro' in microprocessor, making our world a whole lot smarter and smaller.
