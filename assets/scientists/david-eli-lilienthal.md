Born in Morton, Illinois, in 1899, David E. Lilienthal was less a lab-coat scientist and more a big-picture visionary, a lawyer and administrator who shaped how America powered itself. He rose to national prominence as the first chairman of the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA), transforming a poverty-stricken region with ambitious hydroelectric projects and modernizing infrastructure. His success there made him the ideal (and some might say, only) choice to chair the newly formed U.S. Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) in 1946. He faced the immense challenge of transitioning atomic power from wartime weapon to peaceful energy source, navigating the complex ethical and political landscape of the nuclear age. He was the ultimate behind-the-scenes engineering and science facilitator.
