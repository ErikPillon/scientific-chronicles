Before complex numbers were cool and widely understood, they were just… well, complex. Enter Caspar Wessel, a Norwegian-Danish surveyor and cartographer, born in 1745. In 1797, he submitted a paper to the Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters where he brilliantly presented a geometric interpretation of complex numbers. He showed how they could be represented as points or vectors in a plane, and how their operations (like addition and multiplication) corresponded to geometric transformations. His work pre-dated similar discoveries by Gauss and Argand, but was tragically overlooked for nearly a century. This quiet surveyor single-handedly provided the visual foundation for understanding complex numbers, proving that even a cartographer can map out abstract mathematical worlds!
