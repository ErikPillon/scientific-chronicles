Hermann Rorschach, a Swiss psychiatrist, made a splash in psychology (and dinner parties) with his famous inkblot test. Forget boring questionnaires; Rorschach figured our deepest thoughts might just be revealed by how we interpret a splodge of ink. He developed his projective test in 1921, hoping to provide a window into the subconscious mind, especially useful for diagnosing schizophrenia. Sadly, he passed away very young, just a year after publishing his groundbreaking work, "Psychodiagnostics." But his inkblots? They became an enduring, if sometimes controversial, icon of psychology, proving that sometimes, what you see isn't just what you get, it's also a peek into your brain.
