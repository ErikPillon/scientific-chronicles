Born in a rural Pennsylvania farmhouse in 1860, David Hendricks Bergey was a medical doctor who became obsessed with the invisible world of microbes. While many scientists were discovering new bacteria, Bergey realized there was a chaotic mess in terms of classification. So, he took on the monumental task of organizing it. In 1923, he published the first edition of *Bergey's Manual of Determinative Bacteriology*, a meticulously compiled and systematically organized guide that became—and still is—the undisputed authority for identifying and classifying bacteria. This wasn't glamorous, Nobel-Prize-winning discovery, but it was absolutely essential groundwork that enabled countless other discoveries in microbiology and medicine. He was the unsung librarian of the bacterial world, bringing order to microbial chaos.
