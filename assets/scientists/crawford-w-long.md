Before Crawford W. Long, surgery was a screaming match between patient and pain, often requiring a strong drink and a prayer. But in 1842, this quiet country doctor from Georgia decided to experiment with ether, which had been known to cause a bit of a giggle at 'ether frolics.' He successfully used it to remove a tumor from a patient's neck, who apparently felt 'not the slightest pain.' Long didn't rush to publish his findings, which led to some debate over who was truly first, but his pioneering use was undeniably groundbreaking. He essentially offered humanity the gift of painless surgery, a concept almost unimaginable before his brave foray into the world of sleepy gas.
