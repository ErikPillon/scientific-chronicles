Charles Robert Drysdale, born in 1829, was a British physician who wasn't afraid to stir the pot, especially when it came to population and poverty. As a staunch Malthusian, he believed unchecked population growth was a huge problem, making him one of the earliest and most vocal advocates for birth control – a highly controversial stance in Victorian England! He campaigned tirelessly for accessible family planning and social reforms to improve public health and reduce poverty. Essentially, he looked at society's problems and argued that a bit of planning (and some medical intervention) could make things a whole lot better. A trailblazer in public health and a brave advocate for reproductive rights long before it was fashionable.
