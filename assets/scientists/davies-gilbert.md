Davies Gilbert was one of those impressive 18th/19th-century polymaths who seemingly did everything. A British engineer, geologist, politician, and even a Fellow of the Royal Society who eventually became its President (following Sir Humphry Davy). He was a key figure in the scientific establishment, a patron to many budding scientists, and a vital link between the worlds of research and industry. He championed projects like the construction of the Menai Suspension Bridge and tirelessly promoted the application of scientific knowledge for public good, especially in mining and engineering. While not famous for a single groundbreaking discovery of his own, he was the essential facilitator, making sure others' brilliant ideas got the support they needed to change the world. A true scientific godfather!
