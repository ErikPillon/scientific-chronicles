Being the son of Charles Darwin could be a tough act to follow, but Francis Darwin carved out his own impressive scientific legacy as a leading botanist. Following in his father's footsteps (and often collaborating with him), Francis focused on the fascinating world of plants. He meticulously studied plant movements, including phototropism (how plants grow towards light) and geotropism (how they respond to gravity). His work with his father on 'The Power of Movement in Plants' was particularly influential, demonstrating that plants are far more dynamic and responsive than previously thought. Later, he became a strong advocate for Mendelian genetics, even if his father hadn't fully grasped its significance. Francis Darwin showed that scientific curiosity and a keen eye for nature's details ran deep in the family.
