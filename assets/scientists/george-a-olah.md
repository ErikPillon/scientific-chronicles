George A. Olah was the Hungarian-American chemistry maestro who peered into the fleeting, high-energy world of carbocations – those notoriously unstable, positively charged carbon ions. He wasn't afraid to use "superacids" (the fiercest acids known!) to wrangle these elusive intermediates, essentially giving chemists a superpower to understand and manipulate organic reactions better. For his courage and brilliance in taming these chemical wild cards, he earned a Nobel Prize in 1994. Olah proved that even the most fleeting chemical entities can reveal deep secrets.
