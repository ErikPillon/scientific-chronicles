John Venn, a Cambridge lecturer in logic and probability theory, introduced what he called 'Eulerian circles with modification' in an 1880 paper, a diagrammatic method for showing all possible logical relationships between different sets using overlapping circles. The world quickly and permanently renamed them Venn diagrams. What started as a niche tool for formal logic became one of the most widely used visual aids in education, business, and internet memes alike, a rare case of a 19th-century logician's notation surviving essentially unchanged into an era of PowerPoint presentations. Venn also had genuinely serious contributions to probability theory and was, delightfully, involved in building an actual machine for bowling cricket balls in his spare time. Not every mathematician gets a diagram that outlives their entire field of study in public memory. ⭕📊
