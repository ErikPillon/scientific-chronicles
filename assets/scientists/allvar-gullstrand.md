Allvar Gullstrand, the brilliant Swedish ophthalmologist, was the ultimate "eye-guy" long before vision correction became commonplace. He practically rewrote the book on the eye's optics, developing sophisticated mathematical models to describe how light bends and focuses within our peepers. His innovative work on the dioptrics of the eye, particularly his detailed studies of accommodation (how the eye changes focus), was so groundbreaking that he scooped up the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1911. Thanks to Gullstrand, we gained a much clearer picture of how our eyes work, paving the way for better diagnosis and treatment of vision problems. He literally helped us see things more clearly!
