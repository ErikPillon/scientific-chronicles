Before James Lind, long sea voyages were basically a death sentence, thanks to scurvy. This Scottish naval surgeon, a true hero of preventative medicine, decided enough was enough. In 1747, he conducted one of the world's first documented clinical trials aboard HMS Salisbury, dividing sick sailors into groups and giving each a different 'cure.' The result? The group given oranges and lemons made a dramatic recovery! It took a while for his findings to be fully embraced by the Admiralty, but eventually, the Royal Navy adopted citrus, effectively eradicating scurvy and transforming naval welfare and global exploration. Lind's work didn't just save countless lives; it laid crucial groundwork for evidence-based medicine and nutrition, proving that a little bit of vitamin C can indeed conquer a maritime menace.
