Meet Eratosthenes, the ancient Greek superstar who was basically the original "renaissance man" long before the Renaissance. Born in Cyrene around 276 BCE, he wore many hats: chief librarian at the Great Library of Alexandria, mathematician, astronomer, poet, and historian. But his mic-drop moment? He calculated the Earth's circumference with astonishing accuracy, using nothing more than shadows, wells, and some brilliant geometry – all about 2,200 years ago! He also invented the system of latitude and longitude and coined the term "geography." Dubbed "Beta" (the second letter of the Greek alphabet) by some rivals, implying he was second best at everything, Eratosthenes was undoubtedly a first-class mind who charted new territory both literally and intellectually. Talk about a legend!
