Lord Kelvin, born William Thomson, was a force of nature in Victorian science, tackling everything from thermodynamics to transatlantic cables. He's the guy who gave us the Kelvin temperature scale, placing absolute zero firmly on the map. But he wasn't just a theoretician; Kelvin was a brilliant engineer, instrumental in the arduous task of laying the first successful transatlantic telegraph cable, a feat of immense practical importance that truly connected the world. He was prolific, precise, and practical, racking up over 70 patents and leaving an indelible mark on physics and engineering. His scientific legacy is as enduring as his commitment to precise measurement.
