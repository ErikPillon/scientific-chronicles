Friedrich Eduard Beneke was a German philosopher who thought philosophy and psychology should get out of their armchairs and actually look at how the human mind works. Forget all that abstract speculation, he argued; let's observe ourselves, our experiences, and build our understanding from there. It was a refreshing, empirical take that put him at odds with the more traditional German idealists of his day. Sadly, his career ended abruptly and mysteriously when he drowned in a canal in Berlin, leaving us to wonder what more psychological gems he might have unearthed. A true empiricist, even if his life had an unexpected splash ending!
