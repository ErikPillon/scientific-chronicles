Talk about an accidental genius! Christian Friedrich Schönbein, a German-Swiss chemist, had a knack for stumbling upon game-changing discoveries. In 1839, while messing with electrolysis, he noticed a peculiar smell and identified a new gas – ozone, Earth's protective sunscreen! But wait, there's more! In 1846, after a mishap in his kitchen (don't try this at home!), he mixed nitric and sulfuric acids with cotton, creating guncotton. This wasn't just any explosive; it was a smokeless, powerful alternative to black powder, utterly transforming warfare and blasting technology. So, whether you're appreciating a clear sky or marveling at demolition, give a nod to Schönbein, the chemist who literally blew up chemistry… in a good way.
