Ever wonder who first started connecting chemical reactions to criminal investigations? That would be Francesco Selmi, an Italian chemist who, in the 19th century, basically invented forensic toxicology. He was a trailblazer in studying poisons, making significant contributions to detecting substances like morphine and strychnine in biological samples. But his real claim to fame? He did extensive work on 'ptomaines,' a class of putrefactive alkaloids formed during the decomposition of organic matter. These compounds were initially thought to be the cause of food poisoning, and Selmi's research, though later refined, laid critical groundwork for understanding how organic compounds form and react, particularly in a biological context. He was essentially the CSI before DNA evidence was even a glimmer in anyone's eye, helping solve crimes with chemistry!
