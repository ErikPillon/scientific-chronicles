James Ferguson was the ultimate self-made man of science in the 18th century. Starting out as a shepherd boy who taught himself astronomy by watching the stars and using a simple string of beads, he went on to build incredible, elaborate mechanical models of the solar system (known as orreries). But he wasn't just a tinkerer; Ferguson captivated audiences across Britain with his engaging lectures, making complex scientific ideas accessible and exciting to the general public. He proved that you don't need a fancy university degree to have a massive impact on how people see the universe. This Scottish luminary illuminated the cosmos for countless eager minds, cementing his legacy as a true star gazer and knowledge spreader who democratized scientific understanding.
