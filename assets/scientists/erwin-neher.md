Erwin Neher, a German biophysicist, totally revolutionized our understanding of how nerves communicate and muscles contract. Together with Bert Sakmann, he invented the 'patch clamp' technique in the 1970s. Before this, studying the tiny electrical currents through individual ion channels in cell membranes was like trying to hear a whisper in a hurricane. The patch clamp allowed scientists to isolate and measure the minuscule currents from single ion channels – basically, it gave them a super-sensitive microphone for cell membranes. This breakthrough revealed the discrete nature of these channels, showing how they open and close to control electrical signals. It earned Neher and Sakmann the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1991 and became an indispensable tool in neuroscience and cell biology. Talk about making tiny things a big deal!
