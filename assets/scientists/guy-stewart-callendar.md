Guy Stewart Callendar was a steam engineer by trade, but his real passion was digging into weather data from around the world. In the 1930s, when most scientists were scoffing at the idea, Callendar meticulously collected temperature records and atmospheric CO2 measurements from various sources. He was the first to unequivocally suggest that human industrial activity, specifically burning fossil fuels, was leading to a measurable increase in global temperatures – he even coined the term "Callendar Effect." He was basically the lone wolf shouting about climate change decades before it became a mainstream scientific concern, laying crucial groundwork for future climatologists with his detailed, quantitative approach. He was right, even when few listened, making his contributions remarkably prescient.
