César Milstein, born in Argentina in 1927, was a biochemist with a mission: to create incredibly precise biological tools. Working with Georges Köhler, he developed the technique for producing monoclonal antibodies in 1975, a breakthrough that earned them a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Imagine a tiny, perfectly tailored guided missile that can target specific cells or molecules in the body – that's essentially what a monoclonal antibody is. This innovation transformed medicine, leading to new diagnostic tests, highly specific drug delivery systems, and powerful treatments for diseases from cancer to autoimmune disorders. He essentially gave doctors a whole new arsenal, proving that sometimes, the best way to fight disease is with exquisite biological specificity.
