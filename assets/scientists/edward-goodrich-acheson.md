Edward Goodrich Acheson, born in 1856, was an American chemist and inventor extraordinaire whose accidental discovery of carborundum changed industry forever. While trying to cook up artificial diamonds, he instead stumbled upon silicon carbide, a material so incredibly hard that it quickly became essential for abrasives, cutting tools, and eventually, a crucial component in high-performance ceramics. Not content with just one breakthrough, he then figured out how to make synthetic graphite, leading to advancements in lubricants and electrodes. Acheson started his career working for Thomas Edison, but quickly blazed his own trail, founding companies and securing numerous patents. He wasn't just a scientist; he was an industrial alchemist, transforming common elements into materials that built (and still build) the modern world.
