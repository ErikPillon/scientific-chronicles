John Van Vleck applied quantum mechanics to explain the magnetic properties of atoms and molecules, developing much of the theoretical foundation for understanding paramagnetism and how electron spins and orbital motion combine to produce magnetic behavior in materials. His 1932 book on the subject became a standard reference that shaped the entire field of magnetism theory for decades. This foundational, somewhat abstract quantum work later turned out to be essential for understanding and engineering the magnetic materials used in modern data storage, from hard drives to more exotic technologies. Van Vleck shared the 1977 Nobel Prize in Physics for these contributions to understanding the electronic structure of magnetic and disordered systems. Proof that sufficiently deep theoretical physics eventually shows up in your laptop, even if it takes fifty years. 🧲💾
