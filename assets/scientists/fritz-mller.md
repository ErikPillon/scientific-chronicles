While Charles Darwin was busy pondering finches, Fritz Müller, a German zoologist who settled in Brazil, was out in the jungle providing crucial, on-the-ground support for the theory of evolution. He was a meticulous observer, especially of insects, and is best known for describing 'Müllerian mimicry,' where two or more unpalatable species evolve to mimic each other's warning signals, thus sharing the predator-education burden. He was basically Darwin's biggest fan and most diligent data collector in the tropics, showing how evolution played out in the wild. A true unsung hero of natural selection!
