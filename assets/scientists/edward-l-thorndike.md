Before Pavlov's dogs were famous, Edward L. Thorndike was busy figuring out how animals (and people!) learn through trial and error. This American psychologist's experiments with cats escaping puzzle boxes led him to formulate the 'Law of Effect,' stating that responses followed by satisfying consequences are more likely to be repeated. Sounds simple, right? But this idea was revolutionary, forming a bedrock for behaviorism and profoundly shaping educational psychology. He essentially showed us how rewards and punishments really work, influencing everything from classroom teaching to animal training. Thanks, Edward, for helping us understand why we keep doing what works!
