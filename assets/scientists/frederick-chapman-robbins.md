When it comes to conquering terrifying diseases, Frederick C. Robbins was a true hero, even if he worked with tiny viruses instead of capes. This American pediatrician and virologist, alongside Enders and Weller, cracked the code on growing poliovirus in laboratory cell cultures. Before them, studying polio was a messy, animal-intensive business. Their simple yet revolutionary technique transformed the fight against polio, making it possible to produce the massive quantities of virus needed to develop both the Salk and Sabin vaccines. This Nobel Prize-winning work was a pivotal moment in medical history, directly leading to the near eradication of a disease that once instilled widespread fear. So, every time you hear about polio being eradicated in another corner of the world, remember Robbins and his colleagues, who gave us the first crucial step towards kicking that nasty virus to the curb. He helped move science from the realm of the impossible to the utterly achievable.
