Before GPS and laser levels, there was Edward Troughton, the 18th and 19th-century wizard of precision instrument making. This British craftsman wasn't just building tools; he was revolutionizing astronomy and surveying with unprecedented accuracy. His innovative methods for dividing circular scales meant his transit circles, sextants, and other scientific instruments were leagues ahead of their time. Observatories worldwide, from Greenwich to Cape Town, relied on his creations to map the stars and the Earth with astonishing detail. Troughton's legacy? He quite literally helped us get our bearings, both on land and in the cosmos. Talk about a precise talent!
