Before Isaac Newton was, well, Isaac Newton, he had an Isaac who taught him a thing or two: Isaac Barrow. This English polymath was a brilliant mathematician and theologian, holding the first Lucasian Professorship of Mathematics at Cambridge (a chair Newton later inherited!). Barrow's geometric approach to tangents and areas was a significant precursor to the fundamental theorem of calculus, a concept his star pupil would later fully develop. He was a master of geometry, pushing the boundaries of infinitesimal methods. Even though Newton often overshadows him, Barrow's foundational work was crucial, and his influence on one of history's greatest minds is undeniable. He also served as Master of Trinity College, showing he was just as adept at administration as he was at integrals.
