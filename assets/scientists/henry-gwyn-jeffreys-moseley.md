Henry Moseley was a scientific supernova, burning bright but briefly. Born in 1887, this British physicist was a prodigious talent who, before his untimely death in WWI at just 27, fundamentally reshaped our understanding of the elements. Using X-ray spectroscopy, he systematically measured the wavelengths of X-rays emitted by different elements and discovered a precise mathematical relationship between these wavelengths and the atomic number of the element. This wasn't just neat; it proved that the *atomic number* (number of protons), not atomic weight, was the true organizing principle of the periodic table, clearing up ambiguities that plagued Dmitri Mendeleev's original arrangement. He essentially gave chemistry its backbone, cementing our view of the atom's internal structure and helping to predict undiscovered elements. A true tragedy for science that his life was cut short.
