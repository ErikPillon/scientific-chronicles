Move over, Steve Jobs; the true godfather of Silicon Valley might just be Frederick Emmons Terman. This brilliant electrical engineer and Stanford University provost didn't invent microchips, but he did something arguably more important: he created the ecosystem for innovation to explode. Terman famously encouraged his engineering students, like William Hewlett and David Packard, to start their own companies right there in Palo Alto, even letting them use university equipment. He believed in the power of university research coupled with local industry, a radical idea at the time that fundamentally changed the relationship between academia and business. His vision transformed a sleepy agricultural region into the global tech powerhouse we know today. So, next time you marvel at your smartphone, remember Terman – the academic who quietly engineered the future, one brilliant student and start-up at a time.
