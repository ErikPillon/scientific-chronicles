Ever wonder how your eyes perform their daily magic trick, turning light into sight? George Wald, a brilliant biochemist, unraveled much of that mystery. This American scientist earned a Nobel Prize in 1967 for his pioneering work on the visual pigments in the retina. He was the one who figured out the crucial role of Vitamin A in vision – basically, without it, our eyeballs are just expensive marbles. Wald wasn't just about the science; he was also a vocal critic of nuclear weapons and the Vietnam War, proving that some scientists see the big picture far beyond their microscopes. He truly made us 'see' science differently.
