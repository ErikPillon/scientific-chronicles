Geoffrey F. Chew was one of those theoretical physicists who probably saw the universe as a giant puzzle, and he loved trying to fit the pieces together. Born in 1924, he became a central figure in particle physics, famously pushing the 'S-matrix theory' and the 'bootstrap model' in the 1960s. Think of it as his way of saying, 'Particles don't need fundamental building blocks; they just kind of pull themselves up by their bootstraps and create each other.' It was a radical idea that challenged the then-dominant quantum field theory and, while not universally adopted, profoundly influenced generations of physicists at Berkeley and beyond. Chew made you rethink what 'fundamental' even meant in the subatomic world.
