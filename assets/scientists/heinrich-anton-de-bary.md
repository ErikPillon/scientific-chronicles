Heinrich Anton de Bary was a German botanist who basically wrote the book on mycology (the study of fungi) and plant pathology. Forget just naming plants; de Bary was deeply curious about *how* they worked and *why* they got sick. He was the first to demonstrate that fungi weren't just hangers-on but were the direct cause of many plant diseases, like potato blight, which had devastated crops. He coined terms like 'symbiosis' and meticulously described the life cycles of rusts and smuts, showing their complex interactions with host plants. His work transformed botany from mere description to a dynamic field of experimental biology, proving that understanding the tiniest organisms could have massive implications for agriculture and our food supply.
