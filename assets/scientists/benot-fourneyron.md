Meet Benoît Fourneyron, the French engineer (born 1802 in Saint-Étienne) who basically gave us the hydropower we know today. While water wheels had been around for ages, they weren't exactly efficient powerhouses. Fourneyron set out to change that, and oh boy, did he succeed! In 1827, he designed and built the first truly modern hydraulic turbine, a radial flow turbine that dramatically improved efficiency. His 'Fourneyron turbine' wasn't just a slight upgrade; it was a game-changer, capable of harnessing much more energy from water flow, making it ideal for powering factories and later, generating electricity. Imagine the industrial revolution, but with cleaner, more powerful energy! His turbines were showcased at world exhibitions and were adopted globally, marking a significant leap in renewable energy technology. He spent his life improving his designs and advocating for the use of hydropower. So, next time you flip a light switch powered by a hydroelectric dam, send a silent 'merci beaucoup' to Benoît Fourneyron, the guy who made water work harder for us.
