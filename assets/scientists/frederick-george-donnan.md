Ever wondered how cells keep their insides tidy and separated from the outside world, despite all sorts of ions trying to sneak in and out? Thank Frederick George Donnan, the Irish physical chemist who gave us the 'Donnan equilibrium.' His work in the early 20th century explained how the presence of certain charged molecules, unable to pass through a semi-permeable membrane, influences the distribution of other, smaller ions. It sounds a bit technical, but it’s absolutely crucial for understanding biological processes like kidney function and nerve impulses, not to mention industrial applications in things like dialysis. Donnan untangled the complex dance of ions across membranes, providing a fundamental principle that helps explain everything from biological transport to how our bodies maintain balance. A true quiet hero of physical chemistry, he brought order to the chaotic world of molecular movement.
