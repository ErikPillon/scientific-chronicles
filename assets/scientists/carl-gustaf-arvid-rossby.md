Carl-Gustaf Rossby was the Swedish-American meteorologist who finally made sense of why weather patterns aren't just random acts of atmospheric chaos. He figured out that the atmosphere and oceans aren't just swirling aimlessly but are governed by massive, planetary-scale waves now famously known as 'Rossby waves.' These waves are crucial for understanding the jet stream, how high- and low-pressure systems move, and even global climate. He brought a rigorous mathematical approach to meteorology, transforming it from more of an art into a robust science. Rossby established influential meteorology departments at MIT and the University of Chicago, training generations of weather scientists. His insights literally helped us see the big picture of Earth's climate system, allowing us to predict and understand large-scale atmospheric and oceanic phenomena. He basically gave us the blueprints for how the planet breathes.
