Erich Fromm was the kind of intellectual who made you ponder the meaning of life over your morning coffee. Born in Germany in 1900, he was a prominent figure in the Frankfurt School, blending psychoanalysis (hello, Freud!) with sociology and philosophy. He fled Nazi Germany and settled in the US, where he became a leading voice on human nature and society. Fromm wasn't just diagnosing individuals; he was diagnosing civilization itself. His famous works like "Escape from Freedom" (1941) and "The Art of Loving" (1956) explored how modern capitalism and authoritarianism alienate us, and how true freedom and genuine love can be found. He believed in human potential and critiqued the consumer culture, arguing that we often "have" more than we "are." A profound thinker, Fromm nudged us to be more conscious, more loving, and more free.
