When you hear 'Stephenson' and 'railways,' you probably think George Stephenson, the 'Father of Railways.' But don't forget his nephew, George Robert Stephenson, who brilliantly carried on the family business of making tracks across the globe! This civil engineer was responsible for building countless railway lines, bridges, and tunnels, not just in Britain but also in places like Spain, New Zealand, and Brazil. He played a huge role in expanding the global railway network, often working alongside or taking over from his famous uncle. He helped solidify the railway as the dominant mode of transport, proving that engineering genius could indeed run in the family.
