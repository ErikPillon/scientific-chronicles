Isidore Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire was a chip off the old block, following in his famous father Étienne's scientific footsteps. Born in Paris in 1805, this French zoologist became a pioneer in *teratology*, the study of congenital malformations – basically, he was fascinated by how and why things go awry in development. He also helped lay the groundwork for ethology, the study of animal behavior in their natural habitats. So, whether it was peculiar animal development or how critters actually live, Isidore was on it. He served as professor at the Muséum national d'histoire naturelle and later founded the Société impériale zoologique d'acclimatation. A true zoological polymath, he passed away in 1861.
