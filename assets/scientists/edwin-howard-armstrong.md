Meet Edwin Howard Armstrong, the forgotten genius of radio who basically made your favorite music sound good. While Marconi gets credit for inventing radio, Armstrong was the guy who made it *work* for the masses. This electrical engineering wizard developed several foundational technologies that underpinned modern radio and communication. First, there was regenerative circuit feedback, which boosted weak radio signals. Then came the superheterodyne receiver, which is still the basis for most radio receivers today. But his biggest mic drop moment? Inventing Frequency Modulation (FM) radio in the 1930s. Before FM, radio was mostly static-ridden AM, but Armstrong's FM delivered crystal-clear sound, free from interference. He fought tirelessly to get FM adopted, facing fierce resistance from established AM broadcasters, leading to decades of patent battles that tragically took a toll on him. Despite the legal battles, his inventions are ubiquitous, shaping how we listen to music and communicate wirelessly. Armstrong was a true visionary, whose relentless pursuit of better sound transformed the world, even if he didn't always get the credit he deserved in his lifetime. He's the reason your road trip playlists sound so crisp!
