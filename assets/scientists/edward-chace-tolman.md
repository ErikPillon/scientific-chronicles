Edward C. Tolman was the psychologist who dared to suggest that even rats had a brain in their heads, not just a bunch of stimulus-response wires! Back when behaviorism was all the rage (think Pavlov and Skinner), Tolman introduced his "purposive behaviorism," arguing that animals (and, by extension, humans) aren't just reacting mindlessly to their environment. Instead, they form internal "cognitive maps" of their surroundings and act with an *expectation* of outcomes. He proved this with clever maze experiments where rats learned routes even without immediate rewards. This was a radical idea for its time, putting "mind" back into psychology and paving the way for the cognitive revolution. Tolman wasn't afraid to challenge the status quo, reminding us that there's more going on inside than meets the eye (or the twitch of a muscle!).
