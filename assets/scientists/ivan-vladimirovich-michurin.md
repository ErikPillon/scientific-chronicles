Ivan Vladimirovich Michurin, born in 1855, was Russia's green-thumbed guru, a self-taught botanist and horticulturist who spent his life developing new fruit and berry varieties perfectly suited for Russia's harsh climate. He was like the ultimate plant matchmaker, crossing different species to create robust new hybrids. While his practical achievements were significant, his work later became controversially intertwined with Trofim Lysenko's pseudo-scientific theories during the Soviet era, particularly rejecting Mendelian genetics in favor of Lamarckian ideas about acquired characteristics. Despite the later political baggage, Michurin's dedication to plant breeding undeniably expanded the agricultural bounty of his homeland before his death in 1935.
