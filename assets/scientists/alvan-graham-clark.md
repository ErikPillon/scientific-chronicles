Following in his famous father's footsteps, Alvan Graham Clark was a master optician and astronomer, integral to the success of Alvan Clark & Sons. He helped create some of the world's largest and most precise refracting telescope lenses. But he wasn't just a lens-maker; he was a keen observer, too. In 1862, while testing a new 18.5-inch lens (then the largest in the US) for the Dearborn Observatory, he made an astounding discovery: Sirius B, the elusive white dwarf companion to the brightest star in the night sky, Sirius A. This was a monumental find, confirming Bessel's earlier prediction of an unseen companion. Talk about quality control – discovering a whole new star system while checking your equipment! He truly bridged the gap between engineering marvel and astronomical revelation.
