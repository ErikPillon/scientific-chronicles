In an era where both race and gender were immense hurdles, Rebecca Lee Crumpler wasn't just a doctor; she was a revolutionary. Born in 1831, she initially worked as a nurse before pursuing her audacious dream of becoming a physician. In 1864, she shattered stereotypes by becoming the first African American woman in the United States to earn a medical degree from the New England Female Medical College. Her dedication didn't stop there; she later moved to Richmond, Virginia, to provide medical care to freed slaves, often facing intense racism and sexism. Her book, A Book of Medical Discourses, published in 1883, is a testament to her commitment to public health and women's and children's medicine. Dr. Crumpler didn't just practice medicine; she healed communities and opened doors for generations to come.
