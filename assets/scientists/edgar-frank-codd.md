Meet Edgar F. Codd, the British-American computer scientist who single-handedly gave us the relational database. Back in the prehistoric age of computing (the late 1960s/early 70s), data was a chaotic mess. Codd, while at IBM, proposed a brilliant, logical way to structure information using tables and relationships. His 1970 paper, "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks," laid the groundwork for almost every database system we use today, from your online banking to your favorite social media feed. Without Codd, managing information would be, well, a Codd-awful mess. He's why your data isn't a tangled spaghetti junction!
