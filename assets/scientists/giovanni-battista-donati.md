Giovanni Battista Donati, born in 1826, was an Italian astronomer with a flair for the spectacular – especially when it came to comets! He became famous for discovering the magnificent Comet Donati in 1858, one of the brightest and most beautiful comets of the 19th century. But Donati wasn't just a discovery-maker; he was a trailblazer in a new field: astrophysics. He was among the very first to systematically apply spectroscopy to celestial objects. By analyzing the light from stars and comets, he deduced their chemical composition, a revolutionary step that showed astronomers could learn what distant cosmic bodies were made of without ever leaving Earth. He also established the Arcetri Observatory, demonstrating a keen eye for both observation and infrastructure. Sadly, he died young at 46 from cholera, but his legacy in spectral astronomy shines brightly.
