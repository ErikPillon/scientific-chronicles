George Beadle, a Nobel-winning geneticist, was the guy who hammered home the idea that your genes are like tiny master chefs, each one providing the recipe for a specific enzyme. Working with Edward Tatum, he turned the humble bread mold (Neurospora crassa) into a scientific superstar, proving that damaged genes led to missing enzymes. This 'one gene–one enzyme' hypothesis, though later refined, laid the groundwork for understanding how genes dictate biological processes. He essentially pulled back the curtain on the fundamental connection between your DNA and your body's functions, paving the way for modern genetics and biochemistry.
