David Douglas, born in Scone, Scotland, in 1799, wasn't content with just admiring plants; he wanted to find them, catalogue them, and bring them home. This intrepid botanist was practically Indiana Jones with a plant press. Sent by the Horticultural Society of London, he embarked on perilous expeditions across North America, particularly the Pacific Northwest, enduring harsh conditions and dangers from wildlife and native tribes. He introduced over 200 species of plants, shrubs, and trees to European gardens, most famously the towering Pseudotsuga menziesii, now known as the Douglas fir. Sadly, his life ended tragically young in Hawaii, in a pit trap intended for wild cattle. But his botanical legacy stands tall, literally, in forests around the globe.
