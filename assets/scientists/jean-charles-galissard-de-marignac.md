Jean Charles Galissard de Marignac spent much of his career doing exquisitely careful work on atomic weights and rare-earth elements, chemicals notorious for being nearly identical to each other and maddeningly difficult to separate. His patience paid off twice over: he discovered gadolinium in 1880 and ytterbium in 1878, both teased apart from complex mineral mixtures through painstaking fractional crystallization, a process about as glamorous as it sounds (not very, but it works). He was also among the first chemists to correctly determine highly accurate atomic weights for numerous elements, providing crucial data that helped others build and refine the periodic table. Not the flashiest scientific legacy, but the periodic table doesn't organize itself. 🧪⚗️
