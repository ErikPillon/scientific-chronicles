Charles Gerhardt was a French chemist who basically tidied up the messy house of organic chemistry in the mid-19th century. Back then, chemists were discovering new compounds faster than they could name or organize them, leading to a glorious scientific free-for-all. Gerhardt, along with his colleague Auguste Laurent, proposed a radical 'type theory' that systematized organic compounds, laying the groundwork for how we understand molecular structure today. He also famously synthesized acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) in 1853, though it didn't become commercially viable until Felix Hoffmann much later. Gerhardt was a brilliant, if controversial, figure who helped bring order to the bewildering world of carbon chemistry, setting the stage for future breakthroughs. A true chemical clean-up crew!
