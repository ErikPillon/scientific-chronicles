Ever taken a breath? Then you've got Daniel Rutherford to thank, at least partially! This Scottish physician and botanist was tinkering with air in his lab back in 1772 (way before modern chemistry made things easy). He removed oxygen and carbon dioxide from a sample, leaving behind a gas that couldn't support combustion or respiration – what he called 'noxious air' or 'dephlogisticated air.' We now know it as nitrogen, making up a whopping 78% of our atmosphere! While Joseph Priestley and Carl Wilhelm Scheele were also close, Rutherford gets the nod for officially isolating and describing this crucial element. Talk about breathing new life into chemistry!
