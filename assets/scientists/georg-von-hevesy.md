Georg von Hevesy was basically the first detective of the atomic world, using tiny radioactive clues to solve big biological and chemical mysteries. This Hungarian-Swedish radiochemist invented the concept of radioactive tracers, allowing scientists to track atoms through complex processes – think of it as giving molecules a tiny GPS! He also helped discover hafnium. For his genius in making radioactivity useful for exploring life and matter, he scored a Nobel Prize in 1943. Hevesy truly made invisible processes visible.
