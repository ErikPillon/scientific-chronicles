Anselme Payen was a French chemist who basically kickstarted the field of biochemistry by discovering the first enzyme! Back in 1833, he isolated a substance from malt extract that could break down starch, and he named it 'diastase' (which we now know as amylase). This wasn't just a cool party trick; it proved that biological processes were driven by specific chemical agents. As if that wasn't enough, Payen also isolated and named 'cellulose,' the primary component of plant cell walls, which had huge implications for industries like paper and textiles. This guy wasn't just a lab coat wearer; he was an industrialist and a scientific detective, always looking to unlock nature's chemical secrets for practical benefit.
