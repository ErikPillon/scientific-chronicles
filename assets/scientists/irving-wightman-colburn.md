Irving W. Colburn was the unsung hero of everyone who's ever looked through a window (so, everyone!). Before him, making plate glass was a painstaking, stop-and-start affair, resulting in wavy, inconsistent panes. Colburn, bless his inventive heart, figured out a way to pull glass continuously from a tank, through rollers, and onto a conveyor belt, making it smoother, cheaper, and way more consistent. He patented his continuous sheet glass drawing process in 1902, which was a huge leap for architecture and industries everywhere. Unfortunately, he struggled to commercialize it before his death, but his patents were eventually acquired by the Toledo Glass Company, which later became part of Libbey Owens Ford. So, next time you admire a sleek skyscraper, give a little nod to Colburn, who made all that clear vision possible.
