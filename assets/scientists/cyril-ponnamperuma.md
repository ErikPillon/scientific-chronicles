Cyril Ponnamperuma, born in Sri Lanka in 1923, was a chemist who loved a good mystery—especially the ultimate one: how did life begin? He wasn't content just theorizing; he got his hands dirty (figuratively speaking) in the lab, recreating the harsh conditions of early Earth. His experiments, building on the famous Miller-Urey setup, showed how organic molecules, the building blocks of life, could spontaneously form from inorganic matter. He even analyzed lunar samples from the Apollo missions, searching for extraterrestrial life's potential beginnings. Ponnamperuma was a scientific bridge-builder, connecting chemistry with biology and astronomy, constantly pushing the boundaries of what we thought was possible for life's genesis. He essentially turned origin-of-life research into a real, testable science.
