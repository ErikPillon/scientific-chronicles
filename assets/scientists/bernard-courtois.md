Meet Bernard Courtois, the French chemist who accidentally stumbled upon a vibrant purple secret while making gunpowder ingredients. Born in 1777, Courtois worked in his family's saltpeter factory, which also produced sodium carbonate from seaweed ash. One fateful day in 1811, while cleaning his vats, he noticed a strange corrosive vapor rising when he added sulfuric acid to the seaweed ash. This vapor condensed into dark, metallic-looking crystals that, when heated, produced a stunning purple gas. Voilà! He had discovered iodine, a new element! It was a rather serendipitous moment, proving that sometimes, the messiest experiments yield the most beautiful breakthroughs. Courtois' discovery not only added a new player to the periodic table but also paved the way for iodine's future use in medicine, photography, and countless industrial applications. He might not have intended to find it, but his keen observation gave the world a dazzling new element.
