Jean Bourgain had a reputation among mathematicians as someone who simply didn't respect boundaries between subfields. He made fundamental contributions to harmonic analysis, functional analysis, ergodic theory, partial differential equations, combinatorics, and theoretical computer science, often solving problems that had stumped specialists in each of those areas for years. In 1994 he won the Fields Medal, mathematics' highest honor, in large part for resolving deep questions about Banach spaces and nonlinear evolution equations. Colleagues often described his working style as pure relentlessness: throwing immense technical firepower at a problem until it gave way. He kept publishing groundbreaking work right up until his death, a testament to a mind that apparently never got tired of hard problems. ∮📊
