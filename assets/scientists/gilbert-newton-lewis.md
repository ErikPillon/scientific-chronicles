Gilbert Newton Lewis was a rockstar of physical chemistry, responsible for concepts that high school students still grapple with today. As a professor and later Dean of Chemistry at UC Berkeley, Lewis was a powerhouse of ideas. His most famous contribution? The electron-pair bond, which he proposed in 1916, explaining how atoms share electrons to form stable molecules. This led to his iconic 'Lewis dot structures,' making complex chemistry visually understandable. Not content with that, he also redefined acids and bases, introducing the 'Lewis acid' and 'Lewis base' concepts. He was even the first to isolate heavy water! Despite multiple nominations, he never won a Nobel Prize, a fact many considered a major oversight. He was truly a visionary who reshaped our understanding of how matter sticks together.
