Jean Rostand, son of the famous playwright Edmond Rostand, ran his own modest home laboratory studying amphibian genetics and reproduction, including pioneering work on parthenogenesis (reproduction without fertilization) in frogs. But his real cultural footprint came from his second career as a public philosopher of science, writing widely read essays and aphorisms warning about the ethical dangers of nuclear weapons, unchecked biological engineering, and humanity's capacity for self-destruction. He became one of the most trusted scientific voices in postwar France precisely because he refused to separate technical expertise from moral responsibility. Being both a careful lab scientist and a public conscience is a rare combination, but Rostand pulled it off for over four decades. 🐸✍️
