Friedlieb Ferdinand Runge, born in Germany in 1794, was the kind of chemist who literally fueled modern life. Ever had a cup of coffee to kickstart your day? You can thank Runge! In 1820, at the ripe old age of 25 (and reportedly at Goethe's suggestion!), he isolated caffeine from coffee beans, giving us that delightful stimulant we all know and love. But he wasn't a one-hit wonder. Runge also made significant contributions to industrial chemistry, developing a process for dyeing textiles and, crucially, inventing paper chromatography. Imagine a simple piece of paper separating complex mixtures into their colorful components – that's Runge's genius at work, a technique still used today! From coffee to chemical analysis, Runge was a true pioneer.
