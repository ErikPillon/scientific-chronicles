Edward Lorenz was a meteorologist who accidentally discovered that even tiny changes in initial conditions can lead to vastly different outcomes – forever altering our understanding of unpredictability. In 1961, while running weather simulations, he noticed a minute rounding error led to wildly different forecasts, sparking his inquiry into what he termed 'chaos theory.' His famous 'butterfly effect' hypothesis illustrates this sensitivity, explaining why long-range weather forecasting is so darn tricky. Lorenz didn't just predict the weather; he fundamentally changed how we perceive the very nature of complex systems. Talk about a mind-bending revelation!
