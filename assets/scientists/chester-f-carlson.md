Chester F. Carlson, born in 1906, was an American physicist, inventor, and patent attorney who brought us something truly electrifying: the xerographic photocopier. After years of painfully copying patent drawings by hand, Carlson, frustrated with the tedious process, set out to find a better way. His eureka moment came with 'electrophotography' in 1938, a process involving static electricity and dry powder. Big companies initially scoffed at his invention, deeming it too complex or unnecessary. But Carlson persisted, eventually partnering with the Battelle Memorial Institute and then, famously, with the Haloid Company (later Xerox). His invention didn't just save countless hours of manual copying; it spawned an entirely new industry and became the indispensable backbone of every modern office, proving that sometimes, the best ideas come from the most annoying problems. Talk about making a copy for yourself!
