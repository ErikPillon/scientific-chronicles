Alfred Adler was no ordinary shrink; he was one of the big three in early 20th-century psychotherapy, right alongside Freud and Jung, though he famously broke ranks with Freud. He kicked off his own school of thought called "Individual Psychology," which, ironically, put a huge emphasis on *social* factors and our place in the community. Forget Oedipal complexes; Adler was all about "inferiority complexes" and our universal "striving for superiority" – basically, our innate drive to overcome feelings of inadequacy and become our best selves. He believed that our personality develops from how we deal with these feelings and our social environment, highlighting concepts like birth order, lifestyle, and social interest. Adler's fresh perspective brought a more holistic, practical, and optimistic view to understanding the human psyche, shifting focus from unconscious drives to conscious goals and social connections.
