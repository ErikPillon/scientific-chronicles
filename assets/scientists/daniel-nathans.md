Meet Daniel Nathans, one of the brilliant minds who figured out how to 'cut and paste' DNA, a technique that literally unlocked the age of genetic engineering! Along with Werner Arber and Hamilton O. Smith, he snagged a Nobel Prize in 1978 for discovering and applying restriction enzymes, which act like molecular scissors to snip DNA at precise locations. Nathans used these enzymes to map the first viral genome, SV40, showing us how to dissect and understand the building blocks of life. His work wasn't just groundbreaking; it provided the essential toolkit for pretty much every genetic manipulation since, from creating new medicines to understanding genetic diseases. Talk about a scientific game-changer!
