Hugh Miller was no ordinary scientist; he was a working stonemason who became one of Scotland's most celebrated self-taught geologists and writers. Born in humble circumstances, he honed his observational skills in quarries and along the coastline, discovering countless fossils and contributing significantly to paleontology, especially in the Old Red Sandstone formations. His eloquent and poetic writings, like *The Old Red Sandstone* and *Footprints of the Creator*, made geology accessible and fascinating to the public, even managing to bridge the perceived gap between science and religion in an era of intense debate. Miller was a Victorian intellectual powerhouse who proved that you don't need a university degree to make monumental scientific contributions.
