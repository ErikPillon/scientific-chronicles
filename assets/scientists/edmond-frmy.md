Edmond Frémy was a 19th-century French chemist who had a knack for making things in the lab. He's perhaps most famous for 'Frémy's salt,' a super-stable free radical compound (potassium nitrosodisulfonate, if you're feeling fancy) that's still used today as a reagent in organic chemistry. But he didn't stop there! Frémy was also a pioneer in fluorine chemistry, tirelessly working on preparing anhydrous hydrofluoric acid and isolating elemental fluorine, even though Henri Moissan usually gets the gold star for that last one. He also contributed to understanding ozone and its properties. Basically, if it involved a beaker, a fume hood, and some potentially explosive reactions, Frémy was probably tinkering with it, advancing our understanding of how elements play nice (or not so nice) together.
