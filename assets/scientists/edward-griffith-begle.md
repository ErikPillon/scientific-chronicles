Edward Griffith Begle, born in 1914, was an American mathematician who became a central figure in one of the most talked-about educational reforms of the 20th century: the 'New Math' movement. After a solid academic career primarily in topology, Begle shifted his focus dramatically to mathematics education, heading the School Mathematics Study Group (SMSG). His goal? To revamp the K-12 math curriculum, making it more rigorous and concept-driven, moving beyond mere rote memorization. While 'New Math' certainly had its critics and often left parents scratching their heads, Begle's dedication to improving how math was taught was undeniable. He believed that understanding the 'why' behind the numbers was just as important as knowing the 'how,' pushing for a deeper, more logical approach to mathematics for a whole generation of American students.
