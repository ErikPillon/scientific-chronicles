Who needs formal education when you have a brilliant mind and an insatiable curiosity? That was James Croll, a self-taught Scottish scientist who started out as a humble janitor and ended up revolutionizing our understanding of Earth's climate. In the late 19th century, Croll developed groundbreaking theories linking long-term climate cycles, specifically ice ages, to variations in Earth's orbit, tilt, and precession (what we now call Milankovitch cycles). His work was wildly ahead of its time, providing a sophisticated astronomical explanation for geological phenomena long before it was widely accepted. He proved that even without a fancy academic title, a sharp mind can uncover the deepest secrets of our planet's past and future climate.
