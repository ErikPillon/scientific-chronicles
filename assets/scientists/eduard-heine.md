Eduard Heine was one of those brilliant 19th-century mathematicians who helped lay the bedrock for modern analysis. You might not know his name instantly, but if you've ever wrestled with real or complex functions, you've probably encountered his ghost in the machine. He's famous for the Heine-Borel theorem (though Borel gets a chunk of the credit, Heine's work was foundational), which is a big deal in topology and real analysis, dealing with covering compact sets. He also dabbled in spherical functions and the study of special functions, basically giving us tools to describe all sorts of wiggly mathematical phenomena. Heine was a professor at the University of Halle for many years, shaping generations of mathematicians with his rigorous approach. A true unsung hero of the number world!
