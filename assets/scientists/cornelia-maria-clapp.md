Cornelia Maria Clapp was a force of nature in the scientific world, a pioneering American zoologist born in 1859 who didn't just study marine life, she *lived* it! A true trailblazer, she was among the first women to earn a Ph.D. in the U.S. and spent her summers at the iconic Marine Biological Laboratory at Woods Hole, where she became a foundational figure. Her research focused on comparative anatomy and embryology, especially with fish. Beyond her groundbreaking research, Clapp was an incredible educator, inspiring generations of students (many of them women!) at Mount Holyoke College. She proved that curiosity, dedication, and a love for the natural world know no gender.
