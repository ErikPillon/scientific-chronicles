Ever wonder how scientists separate and study complex mixtures like the proteins in your blood? You can thank Arne Tiselius! This Swedish biochemist was a trailblazer in analytical chemistry, specifically for his groundbreaking work on electrophoresis and adsorption analysis. He developed revolutionary methods to separate and purify biological macromolecules, particularly proteins, which was a huge deal for understanding their structure and function. His innovations earned him the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1948 and laid essential groundwork for modern biochemistry and clinical diagnostics. In essence, he gave scientists the ultimate 'sorting hat' for the incredibly intricate world of molecules, making the invisible, visible.
