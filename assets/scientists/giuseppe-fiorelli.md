When Giuseppe Fiorelli took over excavations at Pompeii in 1863, he noticed something previous diggers had missed: hollow cavities in the hardened volcanic ash, left behind where human bodies had decomposed after being buried alive in the 79 AD eruption of Vesuvius. Fiorelli's solution was as simple as it was haunting: pour liquid plaster into the cavities and let it set, then excavate around it. The result was eerily precise casts capturing the exact final poses of Pompeii's victims, an archaeological technique still used today (with resin replacing plaster) and one of the most viscerally moving discoveries in the history of the field. He also brought systematic, stratified excavation methods to Pompeii, replacing the previous free-for-all treasure hunting with something resembling actual science. 🏛️💀
