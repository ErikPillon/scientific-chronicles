Ever wonder why you don't inherit your dad's incredibly well-developed biceps from all his gym time, or your mom's carefully acquired piano skills? Thank August Weismann! This German evolutionary biologist famously challenged Lamarck's idea of inherited acquired characteristics. He argued that changes to the body during an organism's life couldn't be passed down. His big idea, the "germ plasm" theory, proposed a separate, immortal lineage of reproductive cells (the germ plasm) that's distinct from the "soma" (the rest of the body). It was a radical thought in his time, a scientific mic drop that paved the way for modern genetics and solidified Darwinian natural selection. He even did gruesome experiments, like chopping off mouse tails for generations, to prove his point (don't worry, the mice are fine now). Weismann truly shaped our understanding of heredity, making sure we know what's truly passed down.
