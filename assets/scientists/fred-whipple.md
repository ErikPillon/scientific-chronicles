If you've ever imagined a comet as a giant, icy, cosmic dirtball hurtling through space, you're thinking like Fred Whipple. This American astronomer, a true star-gazer and deep thinker, revolutionized our understanding of comets in the mid-20th century. Before him, comets were a bit of a mystery, but Whipple proposed his now-famous "dirty snowball" model, suggesting they're essentially chunks of frozen gases, water ice, and dust. This wasn't just a cool idea; it explained how comets behaved, how their tails formed, and their composition. He essentially gave us the blueprint for what comets truly are, making him a rockstar (or rather, an ice-ball star) in planetary science.
