If you've ever admired a patch of moss or a rare wildflower, you might owe a silent nod to Elizabeth Gertrude Knight Britton. This American botanical powerhouse was absolutely obsessed with plants, especially bryophytes (that's mosses and liverworts to you and me). She built an astounding herbarium collection, discovering new species like it was going out of style. But Britton wasn't just about cataloging; she was a fierce conservationist, passionately advocating for the protection of native flora and fighting against commercial exploitation of wildflowers. She also played a pivotal role in establishing the New York Botanical Garden, helping to create a magnificent green oasis for both study and enjoyment. Essentially, she was a plant-loving superhero, saving flora one specimen and one garden at a time!
