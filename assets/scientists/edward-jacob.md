Before 'scientists' were even a thing, there was Edward Jacob, an 18th-century polymath who dabbled in everything from ancient coins to fossilized sharks' teeth. This English antiquarian and naturalist was particularly keen on the geology and fossils of the Isle of Sheppey, publishing 'Plantae Favershamiensis' (1777), a local flora. He was a keen observer of the natural world, meticulously documenting his findings and contributing to the nascent fields of botany and palaeontology long before they had fancy names. Think of him as an early Sherlock Holmes of the natural world, solving mysteries with a keen eye and a shovel.
