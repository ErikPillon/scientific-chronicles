Charles Robert Richet, a French physiologist born in 1850, was the kind of scientist who poked around to understand how bodies react to... well, *everything*. He's most famous for his groundbreaking work on anaphylaxis, a term he coined himself. Back in 1902, while studying dog reactions to sea anemone toxins, he observed that a second, smaller dose could trigger a far more severe, even deadly, immune response. This wasn't just a curious observation; it explained severe allergic reactions and laid the foundation for understanding allergies, immunology, and even vaccine development. For this 'aha!' moment, he snagged a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1913. He basically taught us why some tiny exposure can make your immune system go absolutely bonkers.
