Before we had fancy thermometers or knew why things eventually stop sliding, there was Guillaume Amontons, a French physicist who was seriously into friction and temperature! Born in Paris in 1663, Amontons was largely self-taught, having lost his hearing early in life, which only sharpened his observational skills. He's famous for formalizing the laws of friction, which describe how much force it takes to get things moving and keep them moving – crucial for everything from building machines to walking without slipping. But that's not all! He also made significant contributions to thermometry, noting that the pressure of a gas is proportional to its temperature, and theorized about an "absolute cold" (what we now call absolute zero) more than a century before Kelvin. Amontons was a practical genius who dug into the everyday mechanics of the world and pulled out fundamental truths, proving that you don't need all your senses to have an incredible sense of scientific inquiry.
