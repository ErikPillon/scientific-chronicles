Georg Simon Ohm, born in 1789, was the kind of guy who didn't just observe electricity; he *quantified* it. This German physicist had a major 'Eureka!' moment that every electrical engineer and physics student still learns today: Ohm's Law (V=IR). Before Ohm, understanding how electricity flowed was a bit of a mystery, but he meticulously experimented with circuits to establish the precise relationship between voltage, current, and resistance. Imagine him carefully setting up wires and batteries, determined to find the mathematical harmony in the flow of charge. His work was initially met with skepticism (because, well, it was German and not French, apparently a big deal back then), but eventually became the cornerstone of electrical circuit analysis. Thanks to Ohm, we can all confidently calculate how much power our gadgets need without blowing them up. Mostly.
