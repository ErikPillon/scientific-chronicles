While researching why blood transfusions sometimes triggered immune reactions, Jean Dausset discovered the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) system in 1958, a set of molecular markers on the surface of cells that the immune system uses to distinguish 'self' from 'foreign.' This turned out to be the single biggest reason organ and bone marrow transplants failed or succeeded: mismatched HLA markers between donor and recipient trigger rejection, while close matches dramatically improve outcomes. His discovery essentially made modern transplant medicine possible, letting doctors screen donors and recipients for compatibility before ever picking up a scalpel. He shared the 1980 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the work. Every successful kidney or bone marrow transplant since owes something to a Frenchman puzzling over a bad transfusion reaction. 🫀🧬
