Charles Richard Van Hise, born in 1857, was a prominent American geologist who spent a lot of time thinking about Earth's structure and then, just as importantly, about how to protect it. He was a distinguished professor of geology at the University of Wisconsin, eventually becoming its president. Van Hise was a leading voice in the early conservation movement, arguing for the wise use of natural resources and emphasizing the interconnectedness of geology, economy, and society. He even advised President Theodore Roosevelt on conservation policies. So, he wasn't just studying rocks; he was making sure we didn't trash the planet while we were at it. A true pioneer for environmental awareness!
