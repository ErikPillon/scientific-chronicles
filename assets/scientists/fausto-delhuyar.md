Fausto D'Elhuyar, a Spanish chemist with a flair for minerals, along with his brother Juan José, put tungsten on the periodic table map. In 1783, these siblings isolated the shiny, strong metal that would later power everything from light bulb filaments to drill bits. Fausto later took his talents to Mexico, where he directed mining operations and helped establish the Royal Mining College, proving his mettle both in the lab and in practical engineering. He wasn't just a discoverer; he was a builder, making sure that scientific advancements had real-world impact.
