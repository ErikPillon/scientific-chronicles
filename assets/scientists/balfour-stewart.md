Balfour Stewart was a Scottish physicist who had a serious thing for invisible forces, particularly Earth's magnetism and the energetic dance of the Sun. Starting his career at the Kew Observatory, he meticulously charted geomagnetic variations, basically giving Earth's magnetic field a scientific health check. He wasn't content just observing; he theorized about how the Sun's energy might be generated, even suggesting gravitational contraction long before nuclear fusion was a twinkle in anyone's eye. While his solar theories eventually got an upgrade, his contributions to terrestrial magnetism and meteorology were solid gold. Stewart was a true Victorian polymath, pushing the boundaries of what we understood about our planet and its star.
