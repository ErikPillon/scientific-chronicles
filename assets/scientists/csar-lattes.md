César Lattes, born in Brazil in 1924, had a knack for spotting the tiniest, most elusive components of the universe. While working with C.F. Powell and Giuseppe Occhialini, he played a crucial role in the discovery of the pion (or pi-meson) in 1947. This tiny particle, predicted by Hideki Yukawa, acts as the 'glue' holding atomic nuclei together. Lattes's work involved analyzing photographic emulsions exposed to cosmic rays at high altitudes, which captured the tracks of these fleeting particles. His experimental brilliance confirmed a major piece of the subatomic puzzle and helped solidify our understanding of the strong nuclear force. He essentially proved that sometimes, the biggest discoveries come from looking at the smallest, fastest things.
