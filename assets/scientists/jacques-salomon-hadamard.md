Jacques-Salomon Hadamard was a titan of French mathematics, a brilliant mind whose career spanned nearly a century, influencing generations of mathematicians. He was a true polymath, making fundamental contributions across several fields. His most celebrated achievement? Proving the Prime Number Theorem in 1896 (independently, at the same time as Charles de la Vallée Poussin), which describes the asymptotic distribution of prime numbers. This was a monumental feat, providing insight into the notoriously quirky behavior of primes. But he didn't stop there. Hadamard also left his mark on complex analysis, differential equations, and functional analysis, developing concepts like Hadamard matrices and Hadamard transforms. Beyond his theorems, he was known for his work on the psychology of invention and his famous quote about the 'shortest path between two truths.' A towering intellect and a survivor of two world wars, Hadamard's elegant solutions and profound insights continue to shape mathematical thought today.
