Ferdinand von Lindemann was a German mathematician who put a definitive end to one of math's oldest head-scratchers: squaring the circle. For millennia, brilliant minds tried to construct a square with the same area as a given circle using only a compass and straightedge. Then, in 1882, Lindemann swooped in and proved that pi (π) is a transcendental number – meaning it's not the root of any non-zero polynomial equation with rational coefficients. This seemingly abstract proof meant that squaring the circle with classical tools is utterly impossible. Mic drop! He basically told centuries of frustrated geometers, 'Nice try, but no.' A true mathematical legend who laid a problem to rest for good.
