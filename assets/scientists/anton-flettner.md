Anton Flettner was the kind of inventor who looked at a ship's mast and thought, "What if it spun?" This German engineering genius is most famous for the Flettner rotor, a peculiar vertical cylinder that harnesses the Magnus effect to propel ships – think of it as a wind turbine for forward motion, way before wind power was cool. He didn't stop there; Flettner also pioneered early helicopter designs, including the Flettner Fl 282 Kolibri, a groundbreaking synchropter that saw limited service in WWII. This guy truly put a new spin on maritime and aviation technology, proving that sometimes, the best way forward is to literally make things rotate!
