Say 'cheese!' because George E. Smith, along with Willard Boyle, basically invented the digital camera (and pretty much all digital imaging) as we know it. In 1969, while working at Bell Labs, they cooked up the charge-coupled device (CCD), a semiconductor device that converts light into digital signals. This wasn't just a neat trick; it was a revolution! From medical imaging to space telescopes, and yes, the camera in your phone, CCDs are everywhere. It earned him a share of the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2009. Smith's invention didn't just capture images; it captured the future, turning light into a digital language. Pretty snappy work, George!
