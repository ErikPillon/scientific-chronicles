Before Carl Reinhold August Wunderlich, a fever was just 'feeling hot.' This pioneering German physician changed all that by making temperature measurement a standard, scientific part of clinical medicine. Back in the mid-19th century, he meticulously took millions of temperature readings from patients, sometimes for hours at a time, eventually establishing that 'normal' human body temperature is around 37°C (98.6°F) and showing how fever patterns correlated with specific diseases. His monumental 1868 work, 'Das Verhalten der Eigenwärme in Krankheiten' (The Course of Temperatures in Diseases), made the thermometer an indispensable diagnostic tool and brought quantitative rigor to clinical practice. Wunderlich literally brought precision to the bedside, making fever not just a symptom, but a crucial clue in solving medical mysteries. He put the 'science' in medical science!
