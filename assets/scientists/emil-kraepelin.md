Ever wondered why mental health conditions have names and categories? You can largely thank Emil Kraepelin, the German psychiatrist who, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, basically organized the messy field of psychiatry. Before him, mental illness was a bit of a free-for-all, with doctors often mixing up symptoms. Kraepelin brought order to the chaos by observing, documenting, and classifying disorders based on their typical course and outcomes, not just their fleeting symptoms. He famously distinguished between what we now largely recognize as schizophrenia (his 'dementia praecox') and bipolar disorder (his 'manic-depressive insanity'). While some of his ideas have been tweaked over time (thank goodness!), his pioneering work in systematic diagnosis remains the bedrock of how we understand and treat mental health today. He was the librarian of the mind.
