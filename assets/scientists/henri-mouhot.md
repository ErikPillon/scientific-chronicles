Henri Mouhot was a French naturalist who, despite his humble beginnings, became a legendary figure in exploration, particularly for 'rediscovering' Angkor Wat for the Western world. He wasn't a professional explorer backed by empires; he was a passionate self-taught scientist driven by an insatiable curiosity for the natural world of Southeast Asia. His meticulous journals and illustrations, published posthumously, captivated audiences with descriptions of exotic flora and fauna, and, of course, the stunning, overgrown temples of Angkor. While local Cambodians never 'lost' Angkor Wat, Mouhot's vivid accounts sparked intense European interest in the site, leading to its eventual study and preservation. Sadly, he succumbed to malaria deep in Laos, but his legacy as a courageous explorer and observer lives on.
