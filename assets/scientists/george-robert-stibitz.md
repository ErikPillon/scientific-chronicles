Before your smartphone, there was George Robert Stibitz, a brilliant mind at Bell Labs who basically cooked up one of the earliest digital computers. In 1937, while messing around with telephone relays in his kitchen, he realized they could perform binary arithmetic. This 'Eureka!' moment led to the 'Complex Number Calculator' in 1940, a machine that could solve complex number problems. But wait, there's more! Stibitz also pulled off one of the first remote computing demonstrations, sending problems to his calculator via teletype from Dartmouth College. He effectively invented remote data entry, making him a true godfather of modern computing. Talk about connecting the dots!
