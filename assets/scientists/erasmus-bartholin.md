Erasmus Bartholin was a Danish jack-of-all-trades in the 17th century – doctor, mathematician, astronomer, and physicist. But his real "aha!" moment came when he was fiddling with a chunk of Iceland spar (calcite, for the scientifically inclined). He noticed something peculiar: when he looked through it, everything appeared double. Yep, he'd stumbled upon double refraction, or birefringence, where light splits into two polarized rays. This wasn't just a cool party trick; it was a fundamental discovery about the nature of light and crystals. His detailed 1669 report, *Experimenta crystalli Islandici disdiaclastici* (Experiments with the doubly refracting Icelandic crystal), laid the groundwork for Christian Huygens' wave theory of light and the study of polarization. So, next time you see polarized sunglasses, give a nod to Erasmus – he was seeing double long before it was cool.
