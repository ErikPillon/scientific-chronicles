Bernhard Bolzano, born in Prague in 1781, was a brilliant polymath whose ideas were often ahead of his time, making him a bit of an unsung hero during his life. As a mathematician, he was laying down rigorous foundations for calculus and analysis decades before others, proving the Intermediate Value Theorem and introducing concepts that would later become crucial in set theory. But he wasn't just about numbers; Bolzano was also a philosopher and a Catholic priest, and his liberal theological and political views often put him at odds with the conservative Austrian establishment. He was suspended from his professorship for daring to speak his mind, which meant much of his groundbreaking mathematical work went unpublished or unappreciated until much later. He died in 1848, but his posthumous recognition cemented his place as a foundational figure in modern mathematics, proving that genius often has to wait for the world to catch up.
