Fritz Haber is one of science's most morally complex figures. This German chemist was a Nobel laureate (1918) for his revolutionary Haber-Bosch process, which synthesizes ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen. This invention essentially saved billions from starvation, enabling mass production of fertilizers and boosting agricultural yields sky-high. He's often called the 'father of chemical warfare,' as he also developed and deployed chlorine gas during WWI, leading to immense suffering. Haber’s life is a stark reminder that scientific brilliance can have both life-saving and devastating applications. Talk about a complicated legacy!
