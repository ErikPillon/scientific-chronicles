Long before anyone had seen a bacterium or virus, Girolamo Fracastoro proposed in his 1546 work 'De Contagione' that diseases spread via tiny, self-replicating 'seminaria' (seeds of disease) that could pass between people directly, through objects, or even through the air over distances. It was a startlingly accurate intuition for germ theory, arrived at almost entirely through careful observation rather than any direct evidence, three hundred years before Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch would actually confirm the existence of microorganisms. Fracastoro is also the reason we call a certain sexually transmitted disease 'syphilis': he coined the name in a 1530 poem about a shepherd suffering from the affliction. A physician, poet, and astronomer all at once, which was a fairly normal career combination in Renaissance Italy. 🦠📜
