Arnold Henry Guyot was a Swiss-American geographer, geologist, and meteorologist who really knew his mountains. He was a protégé of Louis Agassiz and did extensive work studying glaciers in the Alps, proving their movement and their role in shaping landscapes. After moving to the U.S., he continued his scientific endeavors, becoming a professor at Princeton, where he established a meteorological station and improved weather observation techniques. He even wrote a popular series of geography textbooks, bringing the world into American classrooms. So, next time you see a beautifully carved valley or check the weather, give a nod to Guyot for his early insights into Earth's processes.
