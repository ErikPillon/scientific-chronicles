Christian Heinrich Pander, a German-Baltic zoologist, was one of the O.G. embryologists. Imagine looking at an early chicken embryo and trying to figure out how it transforms from a blob into a complex organism. Pander, through painstaking observation in the early 19th century, was the first to identify the three primary germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm) from which all organs and tissues develop. This wasn't just a cool observation; it was a fundamental breakthrough that established a cornerstone of developmental biology. He basically showed us the blueprints for building a body, layer by intricate layer. His work was pivotal for later giants like Karl Ernst von Baer and set the stage for modern embryology.
