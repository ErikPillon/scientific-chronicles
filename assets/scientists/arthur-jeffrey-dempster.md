Arthur Jeffrey Dempster was a Canadian-American physicist who made it his business to weigh atoms – literally! In 1918, he designed and built the first modern mass spectrometer, a groundbreaking device that could separate different isotopes of an element based on their mass. Using this incredible tool, he famously discovered uranium-235 in 1935, an isotope crucial for nuclear fission and the development of atomic energy. His work was pivotal for understanding isotopic composition and laid a fundamental piece of the puzzle for the nuclear age. Dempster's precise measurements truly opened up new frontiers in atomic physics.
