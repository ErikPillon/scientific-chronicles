Forget about just knowing your funny bone – James Douglas made sure you'd know your peritoneal folds! This Scottish physician and anatomist was so incredibly precise in his dissections and descriptions that one particular anatomical landmark still bears his name: the rectouterine pouch, or 'Douglas' pouch,' a cavity in the female pelvis. Published in his 1707 'Description of the Peritoneum,' this detailed work, along with his contributions to midwifery and general anatomy, cemented his place in medical history. He was known for his rigorous approach and sharp observations, proving that sometimes, simply seeing things clearly and naming them accurately can make a lasting mark on science. He definitely knew his insides!
