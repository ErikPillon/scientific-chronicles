Edwin G. Boring (yes, that was his actual, wonderfully ironic, last name) was anything but dull. He was the definitive historian of psychology, painstakingly chronicling its evolution from philosophy to a rigorous science. His magnum opus, *A History of Experimental Psychology* (1929), became the bible for generations of psychology students, setting the standard for how we understand the discipline's past. Boring wasn't just a historian; he was a serious experimental psychologist himself, trained under the legendary E.B. Titchener, and he brought that empirical rigor to his historical work. He believed that understanding the historical context was crucial for appreciating current psychological debates. His dry wit is famously captured in his quote, 'Intelligence is what the intelligence test measures,' a sly jab at the circular definitions often found in the field. Boring literally wrote the book on psychology's past, ensuring that future minds knew exactly how we got to where we are, making him a true foundation builder in the field. He made sure psychology had its footnotes in order!
