If you're into things getting super-cold and acting super-weird, then Fritz Wolfgang London is your guy. This German-American physicist was a pioneer in understanding the mind-bending phenomena of superconductivity and superfluidity in the 1930s. He and his brother Heinz formulated the famous London equations, which describe how superconductors expel magnetic fields (the Meissner effect). He also delved into the subtle, quantum-mechanical 'London dispersion forces' that hold molecules together. Basically, he helped explain why things get slippery and strange when you chill them to near absolute zero. A cool dude with hot ideas!
