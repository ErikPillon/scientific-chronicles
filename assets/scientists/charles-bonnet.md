Charles Bonnet, the 18th-century Swiss naturalist, was basically an intellectual Swiss Army knife – philosopher, biologist, and pioneer in a few quirky fields. He was the first to properly describe parthenogenesis (aphids reproducing without any male involvement – mind blown!). He also dove deep into insect anatomy and regeneration, even pondering the idea of 'germs' containing all future generations. But perhaps his most famous legacy is the 'Charles Bonnet Syndrome,' a condition where visually impaired people experience vivid, silent hallucinations without mental illness. He described it in his own grandfather, who saw intricate patterns and people that weren't there. Talk about seeing things! Bonnet made it cool to be curious about the unseen world.
