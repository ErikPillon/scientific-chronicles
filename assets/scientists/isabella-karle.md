If you wanted to know what molecules *really* looked like, Isabella Karle was your go-to expert. This brilliant American physical chemist, often overshadowed by her Nobel Prize-winning husband, Jerome Karle, was a master of X-ray crystallography. She developed direct methods to interpret the complex diffraction patterns created when X-rays hit a crystal, allowing scientists to figure out the precise three-dimensional atomic structure of molecules. Her work was absolutely crucial for understanding proteins, nucleic acids, and countless other biological and chemical compounds. She basically gave scientists a pair of atomic-level glasses, revealing the secrets of molecular architecture that underpin life itself. Without her contributions, many breakthroughs in drug design and materials science would have been significantly delayed. A true unsung hero of structural biology!
