Audouin Dollfus, a French astronomer with an eagle eye and a knack for spotting things others missed, wasn't just looking at the stars; he was *really* looking at them. Born in 1924, he spent his career at the Meudon Observatory near Paris, becoming a pioneer in the study of planetary atmospheres and surfaces using polarization techniques. But his most famous moment? In 1966, using nothing more than a powerful telescope from Earth, he discovered Janus, one of Saturn's inner moons. Imagine that, finding a tiny moon from millions of miles away without a space probe! He also made detailed observations of Mars, Venus, and Jupiter, helping us understand their cloud layers and surfaces. Dollfus was the kind of scientist who proved you didn't always need a rocket to make groundbreaking discoveries, just an incredible amount of patience and observational skill. A true old-school stargazer with modern insights!
