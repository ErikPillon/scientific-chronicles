Heinrich Olbers, born in 1758, was a German jack-of-all-trades: a busy physician by day, and a passionate astronomer by night. This guy didn't just casually gaze at the stars; he *discovered* them, or rather, chunks of them! He found two asteroids, Pallas and Vesta, and even came up with a neat method for calculating comet orbits. But his most famous contribution is 'Olbers' Paradox,' which asks a seemingly simple question: if the universe is infinite and full of stars, why is the night sky dark? This mind-bending query still sparks debate and helped shape our understanding of the expanding universe. So, while he was healing patients, he was also pondering cosmic mysteries – talk about multitasking!
