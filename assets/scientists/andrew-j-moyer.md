When penicillin was discovered, it was a miracle drug, but a fiddly one to make. Enter Andrew J. Moyer, the unsung American microbiologist who didn't just make penicillin, he made *a lot* of it. Working in a Peoria lab during WWII, Moyer figured out how to supercharge the production of this life-saving antibiotic through deep-tank fermentation. Suddenly, what was once a scarce, precious medicine became widely available, turning the tide against infections and saving countless lives on battlefields and beyond. Moyer's ingenuity scaled a medical breakthrough into a global game-changer. Talk about a glow-up for a moldy brew!
