Bertram Eugene Warren, born in Waltham, Massachusetts, in 1902, was a pioneer in X-ray diffraction who specialized in looking at the 'messy' side of materials. While many crystallographers focused on perfectly ordered crystals, Warren was fascinated by amorphous materials like glass and polymers, which don't have a neat, repeating atomic structure. He developed sophisticated X-ray diffraction techniques to understand the short-range order and structure of these 'disordered' materials, which are crucial for countless industrial applications. His work also extended to studying order-disorder transformations in alloys. His textbook, 'X-Ray Diffraction,' co-authored with his advisor George L. Clark and later his own, became a classic in the field. Warren spent most of his career at MIT, retiring in 1968, and died in 1991, leaving behind a legacy that helped us understand the fundamental properties of a vast array of everyday materials.
