Donald J. Cram was a chemist with a knack for playing molecular matchmaker. He shared the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his pioneering work in 'host-guest chemistry,' where he designed intricate organic molecules (the 'hosts') that could selectively recognize and bind to other molecules (the 'guests') with surprising precision. Think of it as creating tiny, perfectly shaped molecular handcuffs or cozy little apartments for other chemicals. This wasn't just molecular LEGOs; his breakthroughs paved the way for new drugs, sensors, and separation technologies. Cram was also an excellent teacher and a prolific writer, proving that with enough imagination, even atoms can be convinced to play nice together, and that sometimes, the smallest interactions lead to the biggest breakthroughs.
