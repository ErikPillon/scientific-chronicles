If the USSR had a 'Manhattan Project' equivalent, Igor Kurchatov, born in 1903, was its J. Robert Oppenheimer. This brilliant Soviet physicist was the driving force behind the Soviet atomic bomb project, starting from scratch with limited resources and racing against the clock during the Cold War. He oversaw the construction of the first Soviet cyclotron, the first nuclear reactor in Europe, and ultimately, the testing of the first Soviet atomic bomb (RDS-1) in 1949. Kurchatov wasn't just a weapons builder, though; he was also a proponent of peaceful nuclear energy and thermonuclear fusion, pushing for scientific collaboration even in tense political times. A true titan who shaped the nuclear age on the Eastern front.
