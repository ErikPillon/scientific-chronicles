If you've ever gotten lost in a good sci-fi novel, you have Hugo Gernsback to thank! This Luxembourgian-American visionary wasn't just an inventor (he patented a bunch of radio stuff), but he was also the ultimate futurist. He launched Amazing Stories in 1926, the very first magazine dedicated solely to what he called 'scientifiction,' a term that later became 'science fiction.' He practically invented the genre as we know it, inspiring countless authors and readers to dream of rockets, robots, and alien worlds. He might have been a bit of a penny-pincher when it came to paying his writers, earning him a complex legacy, but his impact on popularizing scientific speculation and igniting imaginations is undeniable. The prestigious Hugo Awards? Named after him, of course!
