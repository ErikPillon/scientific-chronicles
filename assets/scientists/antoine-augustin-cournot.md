You might not know his name, but if you've ever taken an economics class, you've probably wrestled with Cournot's work. Antoine-Augustin Cournot, a French mathematical genius born in 1801, was way ahead of his time, basically inventing mathematical economics. In an era when economics was mostly descriptive, Cournot decided to apply rigorous mathematical analysis to concepts like supply, demand, and competition. His most famous contribution, the Cournot duopoly model, still teaches us how two firms duke it out in a market. He also dabbled in philosophy and probability theory, but it's his groundbreaking use of math in economics that truly sets him apart. He proved that numbers aren't just for counting apples; they're essential for understanding the entire orchard of the economy!
