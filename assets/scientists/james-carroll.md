Meet James Carroll, the unsung hero of public health who literally put his body on the line to conquer yellow fever. As a key member of Walter Reed's Yellow Fever Commission in Cuba, Carroll allowed himself to be bitten by infected mosquitoes in 1900 to prove their role in transmitting the deadly disease. He fell gravely ill but survived, providing crucial evidence that revolutionized our understanding of disease transmission. This courageous self-experimentation, along with the work of his colleagues, led directly to strategies that controlled yellow fever, saving countless lives and making the construction of the Panama Canal possible. Talk about scientific dedication!
