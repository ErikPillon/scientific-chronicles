Chester Greenwood, born in 1858 in Farmington, Maine, was a precocious inventor who, at just 15 years old, solved a universally annoying problem: cold ears during winter ice-skating. Tired of itchy wool scarves and frozen lobes, he famously bent some wire, asked his grandmother to sew beaver fur pads onto the ends, and voilà—the first pair of 'ear-protectors' was born in 1873. He later patented his invention in 1877, eventually establishing a factory that produced vast quantities of earmuffs. Chester's genius wasn't just in creating a practical product but in seeing a simple need and fulfilling it with ingenuity, leaving us all to wonder how we ever survived chilly mornings before his brilliant headwear. He didn't just warm ears; he sparked a local industry and cemented his place as a frosty-weather hero!
