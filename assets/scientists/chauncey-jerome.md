Meet Chauncey Jerome, the 19th-century American who said 'tick-tock' to expensive, handmade clocks and ushered in the era of mass-produced, affordable timepieces. Before Jerome, clocks were often crafted by individual artisans, making them luxury items. But Chauncey, a true visionary in industrial manufacturing, perfected the use of stamped brass components instead of pricier wooden ones. His Connecticut-based factories churned out clocks by the thousands, making him one of the wealthiest men in America by the 1840s. He flooded not just the American market but also global markets, particularly England, with his remarkably cheap (and surprisingly accurate) clocks. His genius wasn't just in making clocks, but in creating an entire industrial system for their production, essentially putting a clock in every home. Unfortunately, a bit of financial over-optimism and a dose of bad luck later led to his bankruptcy, but his legacy as a pioneer of industrial production and affordable consumer goods remains firmly timed.
