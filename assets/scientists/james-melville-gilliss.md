James Melville Gilliss was a true man of two worlds: the rough seas and the starry heavens! As an American naval officer, he founded and became the first superintendent of the U.S. Naval Observatory, a pivotal institution for mapping the heavens and providing accurate timekeeping for navigation. But his ambition didn't stop there. He led an epic U.S. Naval Astronomical Expedition to Chile in the early 1850s, spending years meticulously observing the Southern Hemisphere sky, measuring stellar parallaxes, and making crucial contributions to determining the solar parallax (and thus the astronomical unit). This expedition wasn't just about stargazing; it was a feat of international scientific collaboration and logistical prowess, firmly planting American astronomy on the global stage. Gilliss literally navigated his way to astronomical fame!
