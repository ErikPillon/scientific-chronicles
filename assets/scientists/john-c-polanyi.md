John Polanyi pioneered infrared chemiluminescence, a technique that measures the faint infrared light given off by molecules as they form and break bonds during a chemical reaction, essentially letting chemists watch the reaction's energy changes happen in real time rather than just comparing what went in versus what came out. This gave scientists an unprecedented window into 'reaction dynamics,' the actual step-by-step choreography of atoms rearranging themselves during a chemical transformation. The work earned him a share of the 1986 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, alongside Dudley Herschbach and Yuan T. Lee, who developed complementary techniques for studying the same fundamental question. Polanyi has also spent decades as a prominent public voice on science policy, nuclear disarmament, and human rights. 🔬💡
