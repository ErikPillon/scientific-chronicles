Jacob Moleschott was a 19th-century Dutch physiologist and philosopher who stirred up quite a debate with his staunchly materialist views. He argued that all life, including thought and consciousness, could be explained by physical and chemical processes, famously (and perhaps provocatively) stating, 'No thought without phosphorus.' His work pushed back against vitalism and dualism, asserting that the mind was an emergent property of the brain, not some separate spiritual entity. He was a central figure in the scientific materialism movement, influencing fields from physiology to philosophy, and really made people think about what it means to be human in a purely physical world.
