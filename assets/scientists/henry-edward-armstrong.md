Sir Henry Edward Armstrong (1848-1937) was a chemist with a capital C, but also a passionate educator who thought science teaching should be anything but boring. He made significant contributions to organic chemistry, especially regarding the structure of naphthalene, but he’s perhaps best remembered for his outspoken (and often hilarious) critiques of how science was taught. He championed a hands-on, inquiry-based approach, believing students should *do* chemistry, not just read about it. So, while he was unraveling molecular mysteries, he was also trying to unravel the mysteries of getting kids excited about science – a true double threat!
