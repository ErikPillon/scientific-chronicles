Alfredo Niceforo was an Italian scholar who couldn't quite pick just one intellectual playground, so he played in all of them! Born in 1876, he was a physician, a statistician, and a sociologist. Instead of just treating ailments, Niceforo was fascinated by the 'social body' and its illnesses – crime, poverty, and inequality. He was a key figure in early 20th-century criminal anthropology and medical sociology, meticulously collecting data and applying statistical rigor to human behavior. He wasn't afraid to poke at uncomfortable truths about society, making him a central (and sometimes controversial) voice in discussions about social reform. He was essentially a scientific detective for societal problems, always looking for the patterns in human messiness.
