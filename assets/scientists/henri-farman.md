Henri Farman, originally Henry Fournier, was one of those daring souls who saw the sky not as a limit, but as an invitation. Born in Paris to English parents, he started as a champion cyclist and racing car driver before getting bitten by the aviation bug. In 1908, he made history by completing the first 1-kilometer closed-circuit flight in Europe, proving powered flight was more than just a novelty. Not content with just flying, he also became an influential aircraft designer and manufacturer, creating planes that were renowned for their stability and reliability. His Farman III biplane was a true game-changer, influencing aircraft design for years. He essentially helped lift aviation from a quirky experiment to a serious mode of transport and warfare, making sure the world knew that humans were meant to soar.
