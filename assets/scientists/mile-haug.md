Émile Haug was the French geologist who looked at mountains and saw oceans – well, ancient ones anyway. This influential scientist is best known for developing and popularizing the concept of geosynclines in the early 20th century. Essentially, he proposed that huge, elongated basins in the Earth's crust (geosynclines) filled with sediments over vast periods, and these sediments were later folded and uplifted to form mountain ranges. While plate tectonics later provided a more comprehensive framework, Haug's ideas were crucial for understanding how continents grew and mountains formed, bridging earlier ideas about Earth's contraction with later, more dynamic theories. He was also a prolific paleontologist, often integrating fossil evidence into his grand geological narratives. He gave us a peek into Earth's dramatic youth.
