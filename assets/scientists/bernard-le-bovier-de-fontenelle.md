Bernard le Bovier de Fontenelle, born in Rouen, France, in 1657, was like the rock star of popular science before rock stars even existed. He had this incredible knack for explaining complex scientific ideas—think astronomy or Cartesian physics—in a way that made everyone from philosophers to salon socialites go, 'Aha!' For a century, this sharp-witted nephew of Corneille (yes, *that* Corneille) held court at the French Academy, making sure everyone understood that science wasn't just for dusty academics. He wrote dialogues between a philosopher and a lady, making the universe sound like the juiciest gossip. Fontenelle literally lived for almost a century, dying just before his 100th birthday in 1757, making him a walking, talking, writing bridge between the Golden Age of Louis XIV and the Enlightenment. He proved that you could be brilliant, entertaining, and live long enough to see your ideas take root.
