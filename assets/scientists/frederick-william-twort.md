If you're a bacterium, Frederick William Twort was probably your worst nightmare, and if you're a virologist, he's a legend. This British bacteriologist, while investigating a disease affecting cultures of micrococci, stumbled upon a peculiar 'glassy transformation' in his bacterial colonies. What he found in 1915 were tiny agents that could infect and destroy bacteria – what we now call bacteriophages, or simply 'phages.' He published his findings but, unfortunately, his work didn't immediately gain the widespread recognition it deserved, partly due to the First World War. Félix d'Herelle independently discovered them a couple of years later and popularized the term. Twort's discovery was groundbreaking, offering a potential weapon against bacterial infections long before antibiotics became widespread, and providing critical insights into the nature of viruses. His early insights into these bacterial assassins were truly ahead of their time.
