Erwin Wilhelm Müller was a German physicist who had a knack for pushing the boundaries of what we could 'see.' In 1951, he invented the field ion microscope (FIM), an ingenious device that allowed scientists to visualize individual atoms on a material's surface for the very first time. Imagine being able to zoom in so close you can count the atoms in a metal tip – that was Müller's achievement! Before this, atoms were mostly theoretical constructs. The FIM was a monumental leap in materials science and surface physics, providing direct experimental evidence of atomic arrangements. He later went on to invent the atom probe field ion microscope, which could even identify the chemical nature of individual atoms. Müller truly gave us 'atomic vision,' opening up entirely new avenues for understanding matter at its most fundamental level.
