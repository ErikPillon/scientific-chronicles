Imagine trying to analyze a tiny speck of a new substance, but your methods require huge amounts of it. Frustrating, right? Enter Fritz Pregl, an Austrian chemist who revolutionized organic analysis in the early 20th century. Before him, you needed grams of material; Pregl refined techniques to work with mere milligrams (that's a thousand times less!). His method of 'microanalysis' for organic compounds earned him the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1923 and became the bedrock for pharmaceutical development and biochemistry, allowing scientists to characterize rare and precious samples. He taught chemistry how to get a lot from a little!
