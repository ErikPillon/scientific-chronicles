Meet Christian Longomontanus, the diligent Dane who spent a good chunk of his life as Tycho Brahe's right-hand man, crunching celestial numbers and mapping the stars. After Tycho's death, Longomontanus took up the mantle, eventually becoming a professor at the University of Copenhagen. He championed a modified version of Tycho's geo-heliocentric system, where the Earth stayed put, the Moon and Sun orbited the Earth, but the other planets revolved around the Sun. While Copernicus and Kepler were shaking things up, Longomontanus dedicated himself to meticulous observation and calculation, proving that you could be innovative even when sticking to a 'compromise' cosmos. He basically kept the ancient gears of astronomy turning, even as new ideas dawned.
