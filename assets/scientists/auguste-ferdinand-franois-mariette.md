Auguste Mariette wasn't just an archaeologist; he was practically Egypt's protector! This French Egyptologist arrived in Egypt in 1850 and quickly realized that many priceless artifacts were either being damaged, smuggled out, or poorly managed. So, he decided to do something about it. He founded the Egyptian Department of Antiquities (now the Supreme Council of Antiquities) and established the Boulaq Museum (which eventually became the Egyptian Museum in Cairo) to keep Egypt's treasures in Egypt. He conducted numerous excavations, discovering the Serapeum at Saqqara and many other significant sites. Mariette practically invented the scientific approach to Egyptian archaeology, insisting on careful documentation and preservation. He spent his entire career, and indeed, his life, buried in Egyptian soil (he literally requested to be entombed there!), ensuring future generations could marvel at the wonders of ancient Egypt.
