Ilya Frank, born in 1908, was a Soviet physicist who shared the 1958 Nobel Prize in Physics for his crucial contribution to understanding the Cherenkov effect. Along with Igor Tamm (who we just met!), Frank provided the theoretical framework that explained why charged particles moving through a medium faster than the local speed of light emit that distinctive blue glow (Cherenkov radiation). This wasn't just a quirky observation; it became a vital tool in nuclear physics for detecting and measuring high-energy particles. Frank’s work didn’t just illuminate a strange phenomenon; it gave scientists a powerful new way to peer into the subatomic world, proving that even 'faster than light' has its own beautiful, measurable consequences.
