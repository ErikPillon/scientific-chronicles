Charles Frederick Marvin, born in 1858, was the kind of person who loved to watch the weather, then invent gadgets to measure it better. An American meteorologist, he joined the U.S. Signal Corps (which handled weather back then) and later became the long-serving Chief of the U.S. Weather Bureau. He wasn't just a desk jockey; Marvin was a serious innovator in meteorological instrumentation, improving everything from anemometers (to measure wind speed) to sunshine recorders. Basically, if you wanted to know if it was raining or if the wind was blowing, Charles Marvin was making sure you had the best tools to find out. He helped bring weather forecasting out of the 'guess-and-pray' era into something a bit more scientific.
