Before 'eco-friendly' was a buzzword, there was Frederick Gardner Cottrell, quietly working to clean up industrial pollution. This American physical chemist and inventor created the electrostatic precipitator in the early 20th century. What's that, you ask? It's a clever device that uses static electricity to grab soot and dust particles right out of factory smokestacks, preventing them from spewing into our air. Cottrell wasn't just an inventor; he was a visionary humanitarian. He didn't want to get rich off his invention; instead, he founded the Research Corporation, a non-profit that used the profits from his precipitator to fund further scientific research. Talk about putting your money where your scientific ethics are! He was a true pioneer of environmental engineering and scientific philanthropy, proving that some innovations are best shared for the greater good, rather than hoarded for personal gain.
