Gustav Tammann was the guy who made solid stuff exciting. While others were fussing with gases, Tammann was busy figuring out how metals behave under pressure and temperature, essentially writing the guidebook for metallurgy. He dove deep into phase diagrams, showing how different components mix (or don't) to form alloys, and even explored the weirdness of supercooling. He meticulously charted how materials change their properties, becoming a go-to expert for understanding the underlying physics and chemistry of solid states. His work was crucial for industries wanting to make stronger, smarter materials, proving that understanding the microscopic world of atoms and phases could have seriously macroscopic impact. He basically gave metallurgists their secret decoder ring.
