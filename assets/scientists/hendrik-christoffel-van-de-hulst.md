Hendrik Christoffel Van de Hulst was a sharp Dutch astronomer who, as a young student during World War II, made a prediction that utterly revolutionized radio astronomy. In 1944, he theorized that neutral atomic hydrogen atoms in space should emit radiation at a very specific wavelength: 21 centimeters. This might sound obscure, but this '21-cm line' became a cosmic beacon! Its discovery after the war allowed astronomers to map the vast, otherwise invisible clouds of hydrogen gas that make up much of our Milky Way and other galaxies. Suddenly, we could 'see' the spiral arms and dynamics of galaxies in a whole new light (or rather, radio wave!). Van de Hulst essentially gave astronomers x-ray vision for the universe, helping us understand where stars are born and how galaxies evolve.
