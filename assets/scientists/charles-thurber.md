Before computers and even modern keyboards, there was Charles Thurber, an American inventor who helped pave the way for how we put words on paper. While many claim the 'first typewriter,' Thurber patented a 'Chirographer' in 1843, an early writing machine designed to aid the blind. It wasn't quite the speedy office tool we know today, but it was a clever step in the right direction, allowing individual letters to be selected and printed. Beyond typewriters, Thurber was a prolific inventor, dabbling in everything from knitting machines to rail car brakes, demonstrating a broad mechanical genius. He may not be a household name, but his inventive spirit contributed to the slow, steady march toward the automated devices we take for granted today. He basically laid some of the earliest keystrokes for the future.
