Ernst Ruska was a German electrical engineer and physicist who, alongside Max Knoll, developed the first electron microscope in 1931. This wasn't just a fancy new gadget; it blew the doors open on the sub-micron world, letting scientists peer into cells, viruses, and materials with unprecedented detail. Imagine trying to understand biology without seeing a cell's organelles or developing new materials without observing their microstructure! Ruska's invention, which earned him a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1986 (shared with Gerd Binnig and Heinrich Rohrer for their work on the scanning tunneling microscope), finally got its due for a device that utterly transformed fields from biology to materials science. Talk about having an eye for detail, literally!
