Henry Eyring (1901-1981) was a theoretical chemist who made chemical reactions make sense. Before him, understanding how fast reactions happened was a bit like trying to guess the speed of traffic without a speedometer. Eyring swooped in with his "Absolute Rate Theory" (also known as Transition State Theory), which described reactions as molecules climbing an energy hill, passing through a 'transition state' at the peak. This elegant model didn't just explain reaction rates; it allowed chemists to predict them. He was a Nobel Prize near-miss (many times!), but his theory became a cornerstone of modern chemistry, earning him a rockstar status in the field.
