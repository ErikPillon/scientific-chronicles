Auguste Bravais was the kind of guy who saw patterns where others just saw rocks. This French physicist, a true polymath who also dabbed in meteorology and hydrography, is best known for bringing mathematical order to the sparkling world of crystals. In 1848, he published his seminal work, classifying the 14 possible types of unit cells that can fill all space in a perfectly periodic arrangement, now famously known as "Bravais lattices." Before him, crystallographers were trying to make sense of a dazzling array of shapes; Bravais provided the elegant mathematical framework that underpinned all crystal structures. His work was a massive breakthrough, turning crystallography from a descriptive science into a rigorous, predictive field. He basically wrote the rulebook for how atoms arrange themselves in solids – talk about getting to the core of things!
