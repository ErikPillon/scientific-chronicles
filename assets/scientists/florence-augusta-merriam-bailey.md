Forget hunting birds for sport; Florence Merriam Bailey wanted to *watch* them! This pioneering American ornithologist, born in 1863, was a fierce advocate for observing birds in their natural habitats rather than collecting them as specimens. She basically invented modern field ornithology! Her groundbreaking guide, 'Birds Through an Opera-Glass,' published in 1890, championed observation over collection, revolutionizing bird study and conservation. She trekked across the American West, meticulously documenting bird life and tirelessly campaigning against the use of bird feathers in fashion. Bailey's work helped usher in an era of ethical wildlife study and conservation, proving that you could be a serious scientist without harming a feather. She truly put the 'bird' back in 'birdwatching'!
