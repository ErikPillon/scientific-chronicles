Aspirin had been used as a pain reliever and fever reducer since the 1890s, but nobody actually knew the underlying mechanism for over 70 years. In 1971, John Robert Vane discovered that aspirin works by inhibiting the production of prostaglandins, hormone-like compounds involved in inflammation, pain, and blood clotting. This single insight explained not just how aspirin relieves pain and fever, but why it also thins the blood, which led directly to its now-massive use in preventing heart attacks and strokes. It also opened up an entire new field of prostaglandin research that produced many other drugs. Vane won the 1982 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery, proving that sometimes the biggest scientific mysteries are hiding inside the medicine already sitting in your bathroom cabinet. 💊🩸
