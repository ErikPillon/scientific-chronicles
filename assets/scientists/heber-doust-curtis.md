Heber Curtis was an American astronomer best known for his role in the 'Great Debate' of 1920 with Harlow Shapley. While Shapley argued that the Milky Way was the entire universe and spiral nebulae were within it, Curtis championed the idea that these 'nebulae' were in fact separate galaxies, 'island universes,' far outside our own. Though both had elements of truth, Curtis's view on the nature of spiral nebulae proved closer to the cosmic truth later confirmed by Edwin Hubble. He was a keen observer, often using photographs to study these celestial objects, and his contributions helped lay the groundwork for our modern understanding of galactic structure and the vastness of the cosmos.
