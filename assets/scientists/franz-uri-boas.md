Franz Boas was the intellectual earthquake that shook up 19th-century anthropology, replacing outdated, often racist, evolutionary theories with something far more nuanced and respectful. Originally trained in physics and geography in Germany, Boas soon found his true calling among the Inuit of Baffin Island, sparking his lifelong passion for understanding human cultures in their own contexts. He migrated to the US and, from his base at Columbia University, virtually invented modern American anthropology. He championed 'cultural relativism,' arguing that each culture should be understood on its own terms, not judged by external standards. He was a fierce opponent of scientific racism and biological determinism, using meticulous fieldwork to demonstrate that supposed racial differences were largely cultural. His legacy lives on through countless students (like Margaret Mead and Zora Neale Hurston) and his unwavering insistence that diversity is humanity's strength, not a hierarchy.
