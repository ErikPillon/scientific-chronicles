Gustave-Auguste Ferrié was the French military man who was also a total wizard with wireless waves. He wasn't just building radios; he was building a revolution in communication, especially for the armed forces. As a general, he saw the strategic importance of radio early on. During World War I, he developed the first reliable wireless telegraphy systems for the French army, making battlefield coordination a whole lot less chaotic and pretty much changing the face of modern warfare. After the war, he continued to push for the standardization of radio frequencies and international cooperation, basically laying the groundwork for how we use radio today. He made sure that messages could actually get through, whether it was to coordinate troops or broadcast the evening news, earning him the title 'father of French radio'.
