Alfred Henry Sturtevant was a true pioneer in genetics, a wizard of the fruit fly world, and a student of the legendary Thomas Hunt Morgan. As an undergraduate, he cooked up an idea that would forever change our understanding of heredity: genes aren't just floating around randomly; they have specific addresses on chromosomes. In 1913, at the tender age of 22, Sturtevant created the very first genetic map, using recombination frequencies in *Drosophila melanogaster* (that's fruit flies to you and me) to determine the relative distances between genes on a chromosome. This was a monumental feat! Before him, the concept of a linear arrangement of genes was just a theory, but he provided the empirical proof, essentially giving scientists a street map of the genome. His work was foundational for all subsequent genetic research, paving the way for understanding inheritance patterns and eventually, the Human Genome Project. He was the cartographer of the chromosome, making sure every gene knew its place. Without Sturtevant, our genetic landscape would still be a mystery, with no directions at all!
