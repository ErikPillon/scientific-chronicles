Henry Rowe Schoolcraft was basically the Indiana Jones of early American science, but with less whip-cracking and more meticulous note-taking. This American geographer, geologist, and ethnologist spent decades exploring the rugged wilderness of the Great Lakes region in the early 19th century. His biggest 'Aha!' moment? Pinpointing the source of the Mississippi River in 1832 – a quest that stumped many before him. But he wasn't just about maps; Schoolcraft also devoted himself to studying Native American cultures, particularly the Ojibwe, leaving behind a treasure trove of ethnographic data. A true frontiersman of knowledge!
