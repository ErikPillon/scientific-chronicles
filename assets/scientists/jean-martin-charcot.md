Working at the Salpêtrière hospital in Paris, Jean-Martin Charcot turned neurology from a vague, poorly understood branch of medicine into a rigorous clinical science. He meticulously described and classified diseases including multiple sclerosis and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (still sometimes called Charcot's disease), correlating patients' symptoms during life with what autopsies later revealed about their damaged nervous systems. His dramatic public lectures on hysteria and hypnosis, performed almost like theater in front of packed audiences, were hugely influential, if also controversial and later criticized for overreach. One of his most famous students was a young Viennese doctor named Sigmund Freud, who left Charcot's lectures deeply influenced and went on to found psychoanalysis. Not a bad legacy for one hospital ward. 🧠⚕️
