If you've ever marvelled at those stunning images of subatomic particle trails, you can thank Georges Charpak. This Polish-French physicist, a Holocaust survivor, was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1992 for inventing the multiwire proportional chamber. Before Charpak, tracking tiny, fleeting particles was a bit like trying to photograph a ghost in a dimly lit room. His invention provided a much faster and more accurate way to detect and measure these particles, becoming an indispensable tool in high-energy physics labs around the world. He didn't just build a better mousetrap; he built a better particle trap, opening up new windows into the fundamental building blocks of the universe. Truly a visionary in the world of the infinitesimally small.
