Burton Richter was a particle physics rockstar who helped make sense of the subatomic zoo! In 1974, working at the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, he led the team that stumbled upon a new, unexpectedly long-lived particle, which they dubbed the 'ψ' (psi) particle. In a delightful twist of fate, simultaneously, Samuel C.C. Ting's group on the East Coast found the very same particle, calling it 'J.' The 'November Revolution' was born, and the particle became known as the J/ψ meson. Why was this such a big deal? Because it provided definitive proof for the existence of the charm quark, a fundamental building block of matter that had only been theorized before. This discovery cracked open a whole new chapter in understanding the strong force and earned Richter and Ting a well-deserved Nobel Prize in Physics, proving that sometimes, the best things come in tiny, charmed packages.
