Hieronymus Theodor Richter was a German chemist who got seriously spectral. While working at the Freiberg University of Mining and Technology, Richter, alongside his colleague Ferdinand Reich, was analyzing zinc ores. They were looking for thallium using spectroscopy, but instead, they spotted a brilliant, never-before-seen indigo line in the spectrum. "Eureka!" (or rather, "Indium!"), they must have exclaimed. This new spectral line led to the 1863 discovery of a new metallic element, which they named Indium after its distinctive color. Richter's keen analytical eye added another shiny piece to the periodic table, proving that sometimes, you find something even cooler than what you were looking for.
