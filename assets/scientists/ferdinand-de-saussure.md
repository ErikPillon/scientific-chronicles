Ferdinand de Saussure was a Swiss linguist who basically dropped a mic on the traditional study of language. Instead of just tracing words back through history, he proposed we look at language as a system in itself, a structure where every part gets its meaning from its relationship to others – like a giant, intricate puzzle. His ideas, posthumously published in 'Course in General Linguistics,' became the bedrock of structuralism, not just in language but across the humanities. He taught us that 'meaning' isn't inherent but constructed through differences, changing how we understand everything from words to cultural signs. Pretty deep for a guy who mostly taught, right?
