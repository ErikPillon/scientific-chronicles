Herbert C. Brown was the chemist who made boron the belle of the organic chemistry ball! This British-born American chemist, awarded the 1979 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, basically took boron – a rather overlooked element – and turned it into an organic chemist's best friend. He discovered and developed hydroboration and other boron-mediated reactions, providing incredibly versatile and selective ways to synthesize complex organic molecules. Before Brown, many synthetic pathways were clumsy; after him, chemists had powerful new tools to build molecules with precision. He truly proved that sometimes, the simplest elements hold the biggest secrets!
