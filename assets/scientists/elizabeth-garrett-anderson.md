Elizabeth Garrett Anderson wasn't one to quietly accept the status quo. Born into a progressive family, she set her sights on medicine at a time when women were firmly barred from the profession. Undeterred, she found a loophole to qualify as a doctor in Britain in 1865, making her the nation's first qualified female physician and surgeon. But Elizabeth wasn't just about smashing glass ceilings in the operating theatre; she was a fierce advocate for women's rights and education. She co-founded the London School of Medicine for Women and later became the first female mayor in England, proving she could heal bodies, educate minds, and run a city – all with impeccable Victorian grace, we imagine. Talk about a multi-talented trailblazer!
