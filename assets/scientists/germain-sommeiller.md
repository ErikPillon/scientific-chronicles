Germain Sommeiller, an Italian civil engineer with serious mountain-moving ambitions, was the mastermind behind one of the 19th century's most audacious engineering feats: the Frejus Rail Tunnel (or Mont Cenis Tunnel). Imagine trying to bore a 13-kilometer hole straight through the Alps in the 1850s and 60s, a time when "high-tech" meant steam power and grit. Sommeiller didn't just dream big; he invented and refined the pneumatic drills and explosion techniques that made it possible, effectively revolutionizing tunnel construction forever. This wasn't just about connecting two points; it dramatically shortened travel between France and Italy, boosting trade and diplomacy. Sadly, he died shortly after its completion in 1871, not living to see its full impact. But Sommeiller's legacy is literally carved into the mountains, a testament to human ingenuity against formidable natural barriers. He truly bridged nations with rock and rail!
