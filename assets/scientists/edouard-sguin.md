Edouard Séguin was a French physician and educator who revolutionized how we think about and teach individuals with intellectual disabilities. Back in the 19th century, such children were often institutionalized and neglected. Séguin, influenced by Itard, believed in a pedagogical approach, developing a system of sensory, motor, and intellectual training. His methods, detailed in 'Traitement Moral, Hygiène et Éducation des Idiots' (1846), laid the groundwork for modern special education. After moving to the U.S., he continued his work, establishing schools and influencing educators like Maria Montessori. He was a champion for the rights and potential of all children, proving that with the right environment and dedicated teaching, remarkable progress was possible. A true pioneer who brought dignity and hope to an often-forgotten population.
