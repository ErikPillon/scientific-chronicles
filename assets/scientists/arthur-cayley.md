Arthur Cayley was a British mathematician extraordinaire, whose brain seemed to operate on a different plane. He was a lawyer by profession for 14 years, yet somehow still managed to publish over 200 mathematical papers in his 'spare time'! Once he became a professor at Cambridge, he unleashed a torrent of nearly 900 papers, becoming one of the most prolific mathematicians ever. He's best known for inventing matrix theory – yes, the foundation for much of modern computing and physics – and for his work in invariant theory and n-dimensional geometry. If you've ever dealt with matrices, you've benefited from Cayley's profound insights. He basically laid much of the groundwork for 20th-century algebra, making him a true unsung hero of pure mathematics.
