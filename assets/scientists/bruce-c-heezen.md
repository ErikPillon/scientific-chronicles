Bruce C. Heezen was a true deep-sea detective, an oceanographic cartographer who, alongside the brilliant Marie Tharp, literally put the unseen world beneath the waves on the map. Born in 1924, Heezen and Tharp painstakingly analyzed sonar data, revealing the Mid-Atlantic Ridge — a massive underwater mountain range that snakes around the globe. This wasn't just a cool map; it was a crucial piece of evidence that helped confirm the revolutionary theory of plate tectonics, showing how our continents drift and shift. From ocean trenches to abyssal plains, Heezen's work unveiled the dynamic geology of our planet's hidden depths, proving that sometimes, the biggest discoveries are made by looking beneath the surface, far from the light of day.
