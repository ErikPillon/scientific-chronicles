If you've ever been puzzled by how viruses work, Heinz L. Fraenkel-Conrat helped crack a massive part of that code! Born in Germany in 1910 before moving to the US, this biochemist and virologist delivered a mic-drop moment in 1956. He proved, unequivocally, that the genetic material (RNA) of the tobacco mosaic virus was, all by itself, infectious and could direct the creation of new viruses. It was a stunning revelation, showing that a simple nucleic acid could carry all the information needed for life – or, well, *viral* life. This work was fundamental to understanding not just viruses, but also the central role of nucleic acids in all biological systems. He basically showed us viruses are master programmers with incredibly tiny code!
