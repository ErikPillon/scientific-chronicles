Herman Kahn was the kind of guy who didn't shy away from the apocalypse; in fact, he theorized about it. This American futurist and strategic thinker spent the Cold War making people uncomfortable with his incredibly detailed, and often chilling, scenarios of nuclear conflict. He literally encouraged people to 'think the unthinkable' when it came to global annihilation, earning both respect and ire. His work, particularly his book 'On Thermonuclear War,' delved into the grim mechanics of survival and recovery, shaping much of the strategic doctrine of the era. If you wanted to know what happens after the mushroom cloud, Kahn had a spreadsheet for it – proving that sometimes, facing your darkest fears is the first step to avoiding them (or at least, planning for them).
