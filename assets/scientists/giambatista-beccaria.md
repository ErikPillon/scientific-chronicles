Before electricity was a household convenience, it was a captivating mystery, and Giambatista Beccaria was one of its chief unravelers. This Italian priest and physicist, a professor at the University of Turin, spent his days zapping and sparking his way to new discoveries. He was deeply influenced by Benjamin Franklin's work and even translated some of it. Beccaria conducted detailed experiments on electric conduction, insulation, and the effects of pointed conductors, demonstrating that water, seemingly inert, could conduct electricity. His meticulous observations and writings helped solidify early theories of electrical phenomena, making him a crucial figure in the pre-industrial revolution of physics. He literally brought light to the study of electricity.
