Gustave Le Bon was the ultimate armchair observer of human nature, but with a serious scientific bent. This French polymath dabbled in everything from archaeology to physics before finding his true calling in the murky waters of the collective mind. His 1895 classic, *The Crowd: A Study of the Popular Mind*, basically introduced the world to "crowd psychology," arguing that individuals in a group act very differently than they would alone, often becoming irrational, impulsive, and more susceptible to suggestion. He basically wrote the playbook for understanding why people do crazy things in mobs, and his ideas, while sometimes controversial, still echo in discussions about politics, social movements, and mass behavior today. He was a master at dissecting the collective psyche.
