Ewald Hering was the 19th-century physiologist who looked at how we see color and said, 'Hold my beer, Young-Helmholtz!' While the prevailing theory suggested our eyes had three types of color receptors (red, green, blue), Hering proposed his 'opponent process' theory. He argued that we perceive colors in opposing pairs – red-green, blue-yellow, and black-white. Think about it: you can't imagine 'reddish-green' or 'bluish-yellow,' can you? His theory explained things like afterimages and color blindness far better. He basically showed that our brains aren't just passively receiving light; they're actively *interpreting* it in fantastic, contrasting ways. A true visionary of visual perception!
