In the rough-and-tumble world of early 19th-century French medicine, François-Joseph-Victor Broussais was a rockstar – albeit a controversial one. He developed a "physiological medicine" theory arguing that most diseases stemmed from local inflammation, particularly of the gastrointestinal tract. While his ideas led to some rather enthusiastic (and often excessive) use of leeches and bloodletting – his patients must have looked quite drained! – he undeniably pushed for a more systematic, "physiological" approach to understanding illness, moving away from purely symptomatic treatments. He was a force to be reckoned with, inspiring a whole generation of medical students, even if his methods now make us wince.
