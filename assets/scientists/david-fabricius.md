David Fabricius, born in Esens, Germany, in 1564, was a man of God by day and a stargazer by night. This Lutheran pastor had a serious side hustle as a brilliant amateur astronomer, one of the earliest users of the telescope. In 1596, while observing Mercury (and struggling, as one does), he noticed a star in the constellation Cetus that mysteriously appeared and disappeared. He thought it was a nova, but it turned out to be the first *variable* star ever discovered, later named Mira (Latin for "wonderful"). He corresponded with Tycho Brahe and Johannes Kepler, helping to refine planetary orbits and contributing to early cartography. Tragically, he met his end not by a celestial event, but by being bludgeoned to death by a farmer he'd accused of stealing geese. Talk about an untimely demise for a stellar observer!
