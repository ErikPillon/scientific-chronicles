David Hartley, born in Halifax, England, in 1705, was a true polymath of the Enlightenment, dabbling in medicine, philosophy, and psychology before psychology was even "a thing." As a physician, he was fascinated by how the mind worked, and his seminal 1749 work, "Observations on Man, His Frame, His Duty, and His Expectations," laid the foundation for associationism. This theory proposed that sensations, through repeated association, form more complex ideas and emotions. Essentially, he argued that all mental phenomena could be explained by the physical vibrations in the brain, linking the mental with the material in a radical way for his time. He was a trailblazer in trying to create a scientific basis for understanding human thought and behavior. Forget Freud, Hartley was doing mental gymnastics way back when!
