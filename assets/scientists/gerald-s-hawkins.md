Ever wondered if ancient civilizations were secretly master astronomers? Gerald S. Hawkins certainly did! This British-American astrophysicist, born in 1928, was the trailblazer who launched the entire field of 'archaeoastronomy.' Back in the 1960s, while most academics debated the stones of Stonehenge in purely archaeological terms, Hawkins saw something more. Armed with one of the era's nascent computers (imagine the size!), he meticulously calculated the precise positions of the sun and moon at the time Stonehenge was built. His shocking conclusion, published in 'Nature' in 1963 and later in his book 'Stonehenge Decoded,' was that the monumental stone circle was, in fact, a sophisticated astronomical observatory and calendar! He suggested alignments for solstices, equinoxes, and even lunar eclipses. This idea was initially met with skepticism (some even called him a heretic!), but his rigorous scientific approach eventually won over many, fundamentally changing how we view ancient structures and the intellect of prehistoric peoples. Talk about a mind-bending historical detective!
