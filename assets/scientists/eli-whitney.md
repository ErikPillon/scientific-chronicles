Eli Whitney, a true mechanical marvel, gave us two big ideas. First, the cotton gin (1793), which made separating cotton fibers from their seeds shockingly efficient. While it turbo-charged the Southern economy, it also, tragically, entrenched slavery deeper into American society. His second genius move was championing interchangeable parts in manufacturing, which meant producing uniform components for easier assembly and repair. This concept became a cornerstone of modern industrial production, making everything from muskets to machines easier and cheaper to make. A complex legacy, indeed!
