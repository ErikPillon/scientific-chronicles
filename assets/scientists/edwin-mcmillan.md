Edwin McMillan was one of those brilliant minds who loved pushing the boundaries of the periodic table – literally. This American physicist, along with Glenn T. Seaborg, snagged a Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1951 for their work on transuranic elements. McMillan was the first to synthesize a new element heavier than uranium, which he fittingly named Neptunium. Not content with just creating new elements, he also developed the concept of phase stability, a crucial principle for accelerator design, leading to the invention of the synchrotron. He basically made the universe a bit bigger and helped create the tools to explore it. A true heavyweight in nuclear science!
