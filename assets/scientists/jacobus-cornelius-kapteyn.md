Jacobus Cornelius Kapteyn was a Dutch astronomer who basically got us our first good look at the Milky Way's structure, long before fancy telescopes and space missions. He was a master of statistical astronomy, painstakingly analyzing photographic plates to count and measure the movement of millions of stars. His groundbreaking 'Kapteyn Universe' model, though later superseded by observations of galactic rotation, was the first plausible map of our galaxy, placing the Sun near its center. He also discovered the Kapteyn star, one of the closest halo stars. This dude was literally mapping the cosmos one star at a time!
