Edward Calvin Kendall was a biochemist with a knack for isolating crucial substances from the most surprising places. His biggest claim to fame? Isolating cortisone from the adrenal cortex. Sounds like a mouthful, but this was a HUGE deal! Cortisone, it turns out, is a powerful anti-inflammatory, and its discovery and subsequent application (along with Philip S. Hench and Tadeus Reichstein) dramatically changed the treatment of painful diseases like rheumatoid arthritis. Before cortisone, many people were left suffering; after, there was hope. For this game-changing work on hormones of the adrenal cortex, he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1950. Kendall's meticulous laboratory work literally brought relief to millions, proving that sometimes, the most significant breakthroughs come from understanding the tiny chemical factories inside our own bodies.
