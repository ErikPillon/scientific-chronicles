Jean Piaget started out studying mollusks as a teenage biologist, but his real legacy came from watching children, including his own, solve puzzles and answer strange questions. He noticed something adults had mostly overlooked: kids don't just know less than grown-ups, they reason differently at different ages, moving through distinct stages of cognitive development from sensorimotor exploration in infancy to abstract logical thought in adolescence. This became his theory of cognitive development, one of the most influential ideas in 20th-century psychology and education, reshaping how schools think about teaching children at different ages. He published over 60 books across a six-decade career, all built on the deceptively simple act of really listening to what kids said and how they said it. 🧒🧠
