Jacob Volhard was a German organic chemist whose name might not roll off the tongue of every casual science enthusiast, but his contributions are absolutely foundational in the lab. He's famous for two biggies: the Volhard titration, a method for quantitative analysis of silver and halides, which chemists still use today; and the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky halogenation, a reaction that introduces halogen atoms to carboxylic acids. So, while he might not have invented the smartphone, his precise analytical methods and reaction chemistry were crucial tools for countless experiments and discoveries, making him a quiet hero of the chemical world.
