Gabriel-Auguste Daubrée was the French geologist who didn't just study rocks; he made them (or at least, tried to!). A pioneer in experimental geology, he recreated geological processes in the lab, testing theories about mineral formation and the Earth's interior under controlled conditions. But his interests weren't just terrestrial; Daubrée was also a leading figure in the early study of meteoritics. He meticulously analyzed meteorites, discovering similar minerals and structures between these cosmic visitors and Earth's own rocks, which offered fascinating clues about the origins of our solar system. He even suggested that meteorites were fragments of a planetary body – a radical idea for his time! Daubrée showed that you could understand the colossal forces of geology by both looking up at the sky and getting down and dirty in the lab.
