Alfred Nobel was a Swedish chemist, engineer, and industrialist whose life was a fascinating mix of destruction and philanthropy. He's most famous for inventing dynamite in 1867, a much safer and more controllable explosive than nitroglycerin, which revolutionized mining, construction, and... well, warfare. Despite his immense wealth coming from explosives, Nobel was reportedly deeply troubled by the destructive power of his inventions. Legend has it he was motivated to establish the Nobel Prizes after reading his own premature obituary, which mistakenly identified him as "the merchant of death." He wanted to leave a legacy that honored those who "conferred the greatest benefit to humankind." So, this inventor of powerful destruction ended up creating the world's most prestigious awards for peace, literature, and scientific breakthroughs. Talk about a dramatic career pivot!
