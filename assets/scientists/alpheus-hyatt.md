Alpheus Hyatt was an American paleontologist who took a deep dive into the fossil record, particularly of invertebrates like ammonites. He was a prominent figure in the neo-Lamarckian movement, arguing that acquired characteristics could be inherited and that evolution proceeded in predictable, linear paths. While his specific evolutionary theories eventually fell out of favor, his meticulous descriptive work and detailed studies of ontogeny (the development of an individual organism) and phylogeny (the evolutionary history of a species) were incredibly valuable. He was a master at deciphering the life stories etched in ancient shells, even if his evolutionary interpretations took a slightly different path than Darwin's.
