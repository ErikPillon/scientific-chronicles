Henri Cartan was a mathematical powerhouse, a key figure in the mid-20th-century revolution of abstract algebra and topology. If you've ever wrestled with concepts like homological algebra or sheaf theory, you've probably encountered his ghost. Cartan was one of the founding members of the highly secretive and incredibly influential Bourbaki group, a collection of French mathematicians who basically rewrote vast swaths of mathematics with a rigorous, axiomatic approach. He was a master of abstraction, transforming the way we understand complex structures and spaces. His work wasn't just theoretical fluff; it laid crucial foundations for fields from algebraic geometry to theoretical physics. The man lived to 104, so he had plenty of time to shape generations of mathematicians!
