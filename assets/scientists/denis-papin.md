Before Instant Pots and slow cookers, there was Denis Papin, a French physicist and inventor who was basically the culinary genius of the 17th century. In 1679, he invented the "steam digester," which was essentially the world's first pressure cooker, making tough meats tender in a fraction of the time. But his ambitions weren't just about dinner! Papin also worked extensively with steam power, developing the concept of a piston-and-cylinder steam engine, which laid crucial groundwork for later industrial revolutionaries like Thomas Newcomen. Though his own steam engine designs weren't widely adopted, his fundamental insights into using steam to create motion were absolutely vital. He was a pioneer who saw the power in a pot of boiling water.
