Before the 1940s, growing viruses for research or vaccine development generally meant infecting live animals or fertilized eggs, a slow, expensive, and limited method. John Franklin Enders, along with colleagues Thomas Weller and Frederick Robbins, developed reliable techniques to cultivate poliovirus in cultured human tissue cells in a laboratory dish, a breakthrough that won the trio the 1954 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. This single innovation directly enabled Jonas Salk and Albert Sabin to develop their polio vaccines soon after, and became the standard method for producing countless vaccines that followed, including for measles, which Enders' own lab helped develop a vaccine for in the 1960s. An unglamorous lab technique that quietly ended up saving millions of lives. 🧫💉
