Gustav Fechner was a genuine Renaissance man, bouncing between physics, philosophy, and pretty much everything in between. But his real mic drop moment came when he decided to scientifically measure the relationship between physical stimuli and our perception of them. He basically founded psychophysics, proving that you *could* quantify subjective experience. So, the next time you wonder if doubling the sound volume feels twice as loud, you're dabbling in Fechner's territory. He also poked around in aesthetics, figuring out what shapes and proportions humans find most pleasing. He was the first to seriously try and put numbers on how our minds interpret the world, which was a huge deal for psychology, turning it from pure philosophy into an experimental science.
