Ferdinand Braun was a German physicist and a true visionary who basically helped invent your TV! In 1897, he developed the first cathode ray tube (CRT) oscilloscope, which laid the foundation for virtually all television and computer monitor displays for nearly a century. But wait, there's more! He also made crucial improvements to wireless telegraphy, making long-distance radio communication more practical. His innovations were so fundamental that he shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1909 for his contributions to the development of wireless telegraphy. From watching your favorite show to sending a text, you can thank Braun for making it all possible.
