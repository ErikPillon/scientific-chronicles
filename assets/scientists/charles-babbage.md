Long before anyone dreamed of silicon chips, Charles Babbage was already sketching out the future of computing. This brilliant, if perpetually frustrated, English mathematician, inventor, and philosopher is rightfully hailed as the 'Father of the Computer.' His ambitious designs for the Difference Engine and the Analytical Engine, though never fully built in his lifetime, laid down the fundamental principles of modern computing: a programmable, general-purpose machine with memory, a central processing unit, and input/output. He envisioned computers decades ahead of his time, proving that sometimes, the hardest part of innovation is convincing your contemporaries to catch up with your genius.
