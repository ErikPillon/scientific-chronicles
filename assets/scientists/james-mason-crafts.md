James Mason Crafts was an American chemist who, alongside his French colleague Charles Friedel, cooked up one of the most famous reactions in organic chemistry: the Friedel-Crafts reaction. Picture this: it's the 1870s, and these two figured out a way to attach alkyl or acyl groups to aromatic rings using a Lewis acid catalyst. Sounds complicated, but it's a super-powerful tool for synthesizing all sorts of organic compounds, from dyes to pharmaceuticals. This reaction became a cornerstone of organic synthesis and is still taught in chemistry classes worldwide. Crafts also had a distinguished academic career, eventually serving as president of MIT. He literally crafted new molecules, making him a true pioneer in the world of chemical synthesis – without him, our modern material world would look very different!
