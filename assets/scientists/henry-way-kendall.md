Henry Way Kendall was a physicist who went deep... really, *really* deep. Like, into the heart of protons and neutrons deep! This American physicist shared the 1990 Nobel Prize in Physics for his work showing that protons and neutrons weren't just solid blobs, but were actually made of even smaller, fundamental particles: quarks. Working at the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center (SLAC) in the late 1960s, Kendall and his colleagues used high-energy electron scattering to 'see' inside these particles, confirming what had previously been a theoretical hunch. Basically, he helped show us what everything is *really* made of. Pretty cool, right?
