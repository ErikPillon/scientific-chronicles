Esther Boise Van Deman was an absolute trailblazer, shattering glass ceilings in the dusty, male-dominated world of early 20th-century archaeology. An American classical archaeologist, she became the first woman employed as a professional archaeologist by the prestigious Carnegie Institution of Washington. Her specialty? Roman concrete construction and stratigraphy. She developed revolutionary methods for dating ancient Roman structures by meticulously analyzing brick stamps and concrete types – essentially, she was the forensic scientist of ancient Roman buildings! While others might have focused on digging up flashy artifacts, Van Deman was painstakingly piecing together the timeline of Rome itself, from its foundations up. Her meticulous work in Italy and her sharp intellect earned her immense respect, proving that women could not only participate in but lead archaeological science. She quite literally helped us understand Rome from the ground up!
