Ever wonder why generators generate and motors spin? A big chunk of that understanding comes from Heinrich Lenz, a Baltic German physicist born in 1804. His big claim to fame is 'Lenz's Law,' which sounds complicated but basically explains that when you try to change a magnetic field, nature creates an opposing electric current to fight back. It's like the universe saying, 'Nope, not so fast!' to magnetic changes. This seemingly simple rule is absolutely fundamental to how almost all electrical machinery works, from your doorbell to massive power plants. So, next time you flip a light switch, give a little nod to Lenz – he's the reason it actually does something!
