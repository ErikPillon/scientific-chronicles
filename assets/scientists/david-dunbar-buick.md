Born in Scotland in 1854, David Dunbar Buick was a man of many talents, though none seemed to make him rich for long. He started in plumbing, inventing a process for permanently enameling cast iron that made bathtubs (and eventually spark plugs!) much better. But his real claim to fame came when he dipped his toes into the nascent automotive world. In 1903, he founded the Buick Motor Company, pioneering the overhead valve engine design that was pretty cutting-edge for its time. However, despite his engineering brilliance, Buick was a terrible businessman, losing control of his company to William C. Durant. He ended up dying in relative obscurity, a tragic figure who put the "B" in one of America's most iconic car brands but never reaped the financial rewards.
