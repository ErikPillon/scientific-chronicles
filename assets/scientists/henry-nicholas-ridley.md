Henry Nicholas Ridley was a botanist with a mission, and a rather tenacious one at that! Born in England in 1855, he was famously known as "Mad Ridley" in Malaya (modern-day Malaysia) for his relentless, almost obsessive, advocacy for rubber cultivation. While initially serving as the first scientific director of the Singapore Botanic Gardens, he tirelessly promoted the *Hevea brasiliensis* rubber tree. His major breakthrough was popularizing a less destructive method of tapping rubber latex from the trees, which allowed for sustainable and high-yield production. Against skepticism, he distributed rubber seeds and literally rode around on his bicycle convincing planters to adopt his methods. Thanks to Ridley's botanical prowess and sheer stubbornness, Malaya became the world's leading rubber producer, fueling the burgeoning automobile industry and boosting the British economy. He made the jungle profitable, one latex drop at a time!
