Giambattista Vico was a Neapolitan philosopher ahead of his time, whose ideas only truly blossomed long after his death. While the Enlightenment focused on universal reason, Vico championed the study of human history and culture, believing we could only truly understand what we ourselves create. His magnum opus, 'Scienza Nuova' (New Science), laid out a revolutionary cyclical theory of history, asserting that societies progress through distinct stages – divine, heroic, human – before declining and regenerating. He coined the famous phrase 'verum ipsum factum' (the true is precisely what is made), arguing for the creative power of human knowledge. He was largely ignored by his contemporaries, but his insights on language, myth, and the nature of historical knowledge became foundational for modern sociology, anthropology, and the philosophy of history. A quiet rebel who changed how we think about, well, thinking.
