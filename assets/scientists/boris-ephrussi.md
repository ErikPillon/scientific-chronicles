Meet Boris Ephrussi, the Russian-born, French geneticist who peered into the tiny world of cells and helped us understand what makes things tick – or not tick! Born in 1901, Ephrussi was a pioneer in developmental genetics. He wasn't just interested in which genes you inherited, but how those genes actually *worked* to build an organism. He was especially famous for his work with fruit flies, using them to tease apart how different parts of a cell – not just the nucleus – contribute to an organism's traits. He basically showed that your cytoplasm has secrets, too, long before DNA became the ultimate superstar. A true maestro of the microscopic, he reminded us that biology is always more complex, and more fascinating, than we initially think!
