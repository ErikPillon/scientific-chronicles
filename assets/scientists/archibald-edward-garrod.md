Sir Archibald Edward Garrod was a British physician who, long before DNA was a household word, figured out that some diseases are caused by inherited biochemical glitches. In the early 20th century, he studied conditions like alkaptonuria and realized they weren't just random ailments but 'inborn errors of metabolism' — essentially, genetic defects that messed up specific chemical pathways. This groundbreaking idea, published in his 1908 book, laid the foundation for biochemical genetics, connecting genes to enzymes and metabolic processes. He basically gave science a peek into how genetic instructions dictate our internal chemistry. A true visionary, Garrod's work was astonishingly ahead of its time, but its profound impact eventually became huge, transforming our understanding of inherited diseases and paving the way for personalized medicine. He saw the future of individual medical care before anyone else!
