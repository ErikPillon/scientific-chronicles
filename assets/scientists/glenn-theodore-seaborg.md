If the periodic table had a 'most wanted' list, Glenn T. Seaborg was the sheriff! This American chemist was a one-man element-making machine, co-discovering ten—yes, TEN—transuranic elements, including plutonium, americium, and curium. He even got an element, Seaborgium (Sg), named after him while he was still alive, which is basically the scientific equivalent of getting a statue erected in your honor before you've even finished your masterpiece. He didn't just add to the periodic table; he expanded it and even helped propose the actinide concept, revolutionizing how we understand heavy elements.
