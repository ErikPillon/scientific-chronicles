Franz Alexander was a big deal in the world of psychoanalysis, especially if you believe your mind can literally make you sick – or heal you. Born in Budapest, he was one of the earliest students of Sigmund Freud himself, bringing a fresh, dynamic perspective to Freudian theory. He eventually emigrated to the US, where he became the first director of the Chicago Institute for Psychoanalysis, establishing it as a major center for psychoanalytic thought and training. Alexander is widely credited as a founding father of psychosomatic medicine, investigating how emotional conflicts and psychological stress can manifest as physical illnesses. He coined the term 'corrective emotional experience,' emphasizing that therapy wasn't just about insight but also about experiencing new, healthier emotional patterns. So, if you've ever blamed your stress for a stomachache, you're channeling a little bit of Franz Alexander's revolutionary thinking!
