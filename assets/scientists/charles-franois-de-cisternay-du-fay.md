Before Benjamin Franklin's kite experiment, there was Charles Du Fay, a French chemist and superintendent of the Jardin du Roi, who was already getting shocked (for science, of course!). In the 1730s, Du Fay made a brilliant observation about static electricity: there wasn't just one kind, but two! He called them 'vitreous' (like from rubbing glass) and 'resinous' (like from rubbing amber), showing that like charges repel and unlike charges attract. This 'two-fluid theory' was a major step forward in understanding electricity, laying the groundwork for future experiments. He literally sparked our understanding of how charges interact, making him a true electrifying figure of early physics!
