If you've ever wondered why earthquakes happen, you can thank Harry Fielding Reid for a big piece of the puzzle. This American geophysicist was utterly fascinated by the Earth's rumblings. His moment came after the devastating 1906 San Francisco earthquake. Instead of just documenting the destruction, Reid meticulously studied the ground deformation shown by geodetic surveys across the San Andreas Fault. His brilliant deduction? That strain builds up slowly in the Earth's crust (like stretching a rubber band) until it suddenly snaps, releasing energy as an earthquake. This 'elastic rebound theory,' published in 1910, completely transformed our understanding of seismic activity and remains the fundamental model for earthquake generation today. He basically taught us why the Earth throws a fit!
