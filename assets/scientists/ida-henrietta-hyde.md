Born in 1863, Ida Henrietta Hyde didn't just break glass ceilings; she probably electrified them. As the first woman to earn a Ph.D. in physiology from the University of Heidelberg and the first woman to perform research at Harvard Medical School, she was a force to be reckoned with. But her biggest mic-drop moment? Developing the first microelectrode. Picture this: a tiny, almost impossibly fine needle capable of stimulating and recording electrical activity from single cells. This invention revolutionized neurophysiology, letting scientists get up close and personal with neurons in a way previously unimaginable. She basically invented the miniature tools needed for unlocking the brain's secrets, all while fighting for equal pay and opportunities for women in science. Talk about a pioneer!
