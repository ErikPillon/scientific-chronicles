When Paris wowed the world with the Eiffel Tower, Chicago needed something equally epic for its 1893 World's Columbian Exposition. Enter George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., an American civil engineer with a bold idea: a giant rotating wheel that would give riders a bird's-eye view. Skeptics scoffed, but Ferris's 264-foot "pleasure wheel" (complete with 36 opulent cars) was a massive hit, carrying over 2 million passengers and raking in serious cash. He quite literally reinvented the wheel, creating an enduring symbol of fun and engineering prowess that still spins today. Next time you're soaring above the fairgrounds, remember Ferris, the man who dared to make a big wheel spin!
