Meet James Braid, the Scottish surgeon who decided 'mesmerism' sounded a bit too much like magic and not enough like science. So, in 1841, he coined the term 'hypnotism' (from the Greek word for sleep, 'hypnos'), giving the field a much-needed rebrand and a push towards legitimate scientific inquiry. He wasn't just renaming things, though; Braid systematically studied how certain mental states could be induced, moving the practice away from theatrical parlor tricks and into early psychological investigation. He proved that it wasn't about a magnetizer's mystical 'fluid' but a physiological state of the patient. A true pioneer who made us all rethink the power of suggestion!
