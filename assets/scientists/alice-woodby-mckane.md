In an era stacked against both women and African Americans, Alice Woodby McKane didn't just break barriers – she built institutions! Born in 1865, Alice was one of the very first African American women to earn a medical degree in the United States. Not content with just her own success, she and her husband moved to Augusta, Georgia, where they rolled up their sleeves and founded the state's first hospital and nursing training school for African Americans. She was a tireless advocate for public health, especially for underserved communities, tackling everything from hygiene to disease prevention. Her dedication ensured that quality medical care and education were available to those who desperately needed it, leaving a legacy of healing and empowerment.
