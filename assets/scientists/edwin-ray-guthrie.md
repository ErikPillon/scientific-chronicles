Edwin Ray Guthrie was an American psychologist who had a knack for simplifying how we learn – or at least, how he thought we learned. This behaviorist proposed his "contiguous conditioning" theory, which, in a nutshell, suggested that if a stimulus and response occur at the same time, a connection is made, and *boom*! Learning happens, often in a single trial. Forget endless repetition; for Guthrie, it was all about the immediate association. While perhaps a bit too neat for all learning scenarios, his ideas profoundly influenced behavioral psychology and gave us a unique perspective on habits and skill acquisition. He basically said, "You learned that right away, didn't you?"
