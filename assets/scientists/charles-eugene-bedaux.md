Charles Eugene Bedaux was the efficiency guru before efficiency was cool. This French-American industrial engineer was all about making workers work smarter, or at least faster. He developed the 'Bedaux System' in the early 20th century, a pioneering (and sometimes controversial) method of measuring labor efficiency and setting production standards using a unit of work called the 'B.' Think of it as an early ancestor of time-and-motion studies, designed to optimize every second of a worker's day. While his methods were widely adopted by industries looking to squeeze every last drop of productivity, they also sparked plenty of debate about worker conditions. He was a complex figure, to say the least, but his impact on industrial management is undeniable.
