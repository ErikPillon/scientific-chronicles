In 1859, John Tyndall built sensitive apparatus to measure how well different gases absorb and radiate heat, and found that water vapor and carbon dioxide were far more effective at trapping infrared radiation than the main components of air, nitrogen and oxygen. This was the first solid experimental demonstration of what we now call the greenhouse effect, the physical mechanism underlying modern climate change science, discovered over a century before anyone worried about carbon emissions. Tyndall also explained why the sky is blue, through what's now called the Tyndall effect, the scattering of light by tiny particles in the atmosphere. A gifted mountaineer as well as a physicist, he made several notable early ascents in the Alps, apparently finding time between groundbreaking experiments to also nearly fall off a mountain a few times. 🌫️🏔️
