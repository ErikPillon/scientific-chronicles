Charles Goodyear, an American self-taught chemist, struggled for years and faced bankruptcy, prison, and public ridicule before he stumbled upon vulcanization. Legend has it he accidentally dropped a mixture of rubber and sulfur on a hot stove, leading to the discovery that transformed the sticky, unreliable natural rubber into a durable, elastic material. This wasn't just a minor tweak; it was a revolution, paving the way for everything from tires to waterproof clothes and industrial belts. Despite his groundbreaking invention, Goodyear famously died in debt, a classic tale of inventor's struggles. His name, however, lives on, synonymous with the rubber industry he kickstarted. Talk about a resilient legacy!
