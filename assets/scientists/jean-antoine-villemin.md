In the 1860s, most doctors believed tuberculosis, one of history's biggest killers, arose from heredity or bad constitution rather than being transmissible. Jean-Antoine Villemin, a French military physician, ran a decisive experiment: he injected tissue from tuberculosis patients into healthy rabbits, which then developed the disease themselves. It was clear, repeatable proof that TB was an infectious, transmissible illness, years before germ theory was widely accepted and well before anyone could see the actual bacterium responsible. His findings were initially dismissed by much of the French medical establishment, who weren't ready to abandon their existing theories. It took Robert Koch's later discovery of the tuberculosis bacillus in 1882 to fully vindicate Villemin's rabbits. Being right early is often a thankless job. 🐇🫁
