Meet James McKeen Cattell, the American academic who basically said, 'Hey, psychology isn't just for philosophical musings; let's get serious!' Born in 1860, Cattell trained under Wilhelm Wundt, the 'father of psychology,' but brought his own brand of practical, measurement-focused thinking back to the States. In 1888, he earned the prestigious title of the first professor of psychology in the U.S. at the University of Pennsylvania (later moving to Columbia). He wasn't content just theorizing; he was all about quantifying individual differences, pioneering mental tests, and making psychology a rigorous, data-driven science. He also founded *Science* magazine and played a huge role in establishing psychology as a legitimate academic discipline. So, next time you take a personality quiz, give a nod to Cattell, who helped lay the groundwork for understanding what makes us all tick, numerically speaking!
