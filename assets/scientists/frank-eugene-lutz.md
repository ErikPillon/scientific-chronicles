Frank Eugene Lutz was a fascinating entomologist who taught us to appreciate the tiny, buzzing, crawling wonders right in our own backyards. As the curator and later head of entomology at the American Museum of Natural History, he didn't just study exotic insects; he championed the idea of urban natural history, encouraging city dwellers to observe the bugs and wildlife around them. He literally created the 'Insect Zoo' at the museum, making entomology accessible and engaging for the public. Lutz showed that you don't need to trek to a rainforest to discover amazing biology – sometimes, the greatest adventures are happening under a leaf or in a crack in the pavement, if you just know how to look.
