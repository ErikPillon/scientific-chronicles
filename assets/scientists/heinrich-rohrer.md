Ever wondered what atoms actually look like up close? Thanks to Heinrich Rohrer, along with his buddy Gerd Binnig, we stopped just wondering and started *seeing*! Born in Switzerland in 1933, Rohrer co-invented the scanning tunneling microscope (STM) in 1981, a device so revolutionary it won them a Nobel Prize in Physics just five years later. Before the STM, atoms were largely theoretical dots; afterwards, scientists could actually visualize and even manipulate individual atoms. It wasn't just a gadget; it was a magic window into the nanoscale world, kicking off the nanotechnology revolution. Rohrer basically gave science X-ray vision for the tiniest building blocks of everything!
