Meet Hubert Gautier, a French engineer from the late 17th and early 18th centuries who was basically the OG of bridge design. Before him, bridge building was more art than science. But Gautier, with his 1716 opus *Traité des Ponts* (Treatise on Bridges), laid down the mathematical and structural principles for designing sturdy arches and foundations. He wasn't just writing; he was building, contributing to significant projects in Languedoc. He also dabbled in canal and road engineering. So, next time you cross a well-designed bridge, give a silent nod to Gautier – he helped lay the theoretical groundwork for not falling into the water.
