Before Cyrus Hall McCormick came along in 1809, harvesting grain was back-breaking work, a true test of human endurance with scythes and sweat. McCormick, an American inventor with an eye for efficiency, changed all that with his mechanical reaper. Patented in 1834, his machine could do the work of several men, drastically speeding up the harvest. It wasn't the first reaper, but his design was robust and, crucially, he was brilliant at marketing and mass production, making it accessible to farmers. His invention sparked an agricultural revolution, freeing up labor for industrial expansion and making food production more efficient than ever before. He essentially brought the factory floor to the farm, proving that sometimes, the best invention is one that saves your back.
