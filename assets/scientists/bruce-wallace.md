Meet Bruce Wallace, the fly guy who really bugged out over genetics! This American geneticist spent a good chunk of his career meticulously studying Drosophila (fruit flies, for the uninitiated) to unravel the mysteries of population genetics. He was fascinated by how genetic variation is maintained in populations and famously proposed what became known as the 'Wallace effect' – essentially, the idea that natural selection can actually reinforce reproductive isolation between diverging populations, nudging them faster towards becoming distinct species. His research provided critical insights into the forces driving evolution, showing that even seemingly subtle genetic differences could have profound long-term consequences. He was a champion of understanding the complexity of life's blueprints, one tiny fruit fly at a time.
