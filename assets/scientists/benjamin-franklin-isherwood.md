Benjamin Franklin Isherwood, or B.F. as his friends (and enemies, he had a few!) might have called him, was no ordinary naval engineer. Born in 1822, this American innovator became Chief Engineer of the U.S. Navy during the Civil War, a period when steam power was rapidly changing maritime warfare. He was a stickler for scientific principles, pushing for rigorous testing and data-driven design in an era often dominated by guesswork. Isherwood championed compound engines and high-pressure steam, radically improving the efficiency and power of naval vessels. His demanding, sometimes confrontational, approach to engineering reform earned him both respect and friction. Despite the controversies, his dedication to scientific methods laid the groundwork for modern naval engineering, ensuring American warships weren't just big, but also technologically superior. He was truly a driving force behind the 'New Navy,' bringing analytical rigor to an essential field.
