Alfred Wegener was a German polymath whose mind literally moved mountains – or at least, the continents! Primarily a meteorologist and polar explorer, he became fascinated by the uncanny fit of South America and Africa's coastlines. Ignoring the prevailing wisdom of 'fixed' continents, he amassed a mountain of evidence – fossil records, geological formations, paleoclimate data – all pointing to one audacious conclusion: the continents had once been joined in a supercontinent he dubbed Pangaea, and had since drifted apart. His theory of continental drift, first proposed in 1912, was initially met with eye-rolls and outright scorn from the geological establishment. Poor Wegener was dismissed as an outsider, his theory considered too radical. He tragically died during an expedition in Greenland, never seeing his revolutionary idea accepted. It wasn't until decades later, with the advent of plate tectonics, that his brilliant, ridiculed insight was finally vindicated. Talk about being ahead of your time!
