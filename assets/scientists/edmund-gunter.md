Edmund Gunter was a 17th-century English mathematician who was all about making measurements easier and more precise. If you've ever used a ruler, a chain, or even just looked at a graph, you've probably benefited from his work. He invented 'Gunter's chain,' a surveying tool that was the standard for land measurement for centuries, and 'Gunter's scale' (or 'Gunter's line'), an early form of a slide rule. He even gave us the terms 'cosine' and 'cotangent'! This professor of astronomy at Gresham College wasn't just theorizing; he was building practical tools that helped map the world, navigate the seas, and advance mathematical understanding. He brought mathematics out of the abstract and into the hands of navigators and surveyors.
