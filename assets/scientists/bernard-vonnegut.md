Bernard Vonnegut, born in 1914, was an American atmospheric scientist with a fascinating claim to fame beyond being the older brother of celebrated author Kurt Vonnegut. While Kurt penned dystopian novels, Bernard was busy trying to manipulate the weather! In 1946, working with Nobel laureate Irving Langmuir, he made a groundbreaking discovery: silver iodide crystals could effectively act as nuclei for ice crystal formation in clouds, essentially 'seeding' them to produce rain or snow. This was a massive leap for meteorology and the concept of weather modification. Imagine trying to make it rain on demand! While cloud seeding has had its controversies and varying success, Vonnegut's fundamental insight into the microphysics of clouds was pure genius. He truly understood the 'slapstick of the weather' (as his brother might say) and found a way to sprinkle a little scientific magic into it, proving that sometimes, the wildest ideas actually hold water.
