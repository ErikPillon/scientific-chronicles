If you've ever looked at an ambiguous image and told a story about it, you might just be channeling Henry Murray! This American psychologist, born in 1893, was a giant in the field of personality theory. He's most famous for co-developing the Thematic Apperception Test (TAT) in the 1930s, a projective psychological test where individuals are shown evocative pictures and asked to invent narratives about them. Murray believed these stories revealed a person's underlying needs, motivations, and conflicts. He also developed a comprehensive theory of personality, emphasizing "needs" and "presses" that shape our behavior. While some of his later work was controversial, Murray undeniably left an indelible mark on how we try to unravel the complex tapestry of the human psyche. He truly believed every picture told a story, especially about *you*.
