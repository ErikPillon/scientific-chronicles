Charles Richard Drew, born in 1904, was an American surgeon who literally saved countless lives by figuring out how to properly store blood plasma. Before Drew, blood transfusions were a tricky, often immediate affair, making large-scale blood banks nearly impossible. His innovative research proved that plasma could be separated from whole blood and stored for much longer, which was a game-changer, especially during World War II. He directed huge blood collection and storage efforts, perfecting the 'blood mobile' concept. Despite his monumental contributions, Drew faced significant racial discrimination. His work fundamentally transformed modern medicine, making blood transfusions safer and more accessible for everyone, proving that brilliance knows no color.
