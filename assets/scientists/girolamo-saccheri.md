Girolamo Saccheri set out in 1733 to defend Euclid's controversial parallel postulate by assuming its opposite was true and trying to derive a logical contradiction from it, a classic proof-by-contradiction strategy. He worked through page after page of consequences from this 'wrong' assumption, expecting to eventually hit an obvious absurdity. He never quite did, though he convinced himself he had by declaring some results merely 'repugnant to the nature of straight lines' rather than actually contradictory. Ironically, in trying to shore up traditional geometry, Saccheri had unknowingly worked out large chunks of what would later become hyperbolic non-Euclidean geometry, over a century before Lobachevsky and Bolyai got proper credit for founding the field. Sometimes your failed proof is someone else's future breakthrough. 📐♾️
