Before Allan MacLeod Cormack, getting a detailed look inside the human body was a bit of a guessing game. Born in South Africa in 1924, Cormack was a physicist who, in the 1950s and 60s, got thinking about how to reconstruct an image of an object from multiple X-ray projections. Sounds complicated? It was! He developed the mathematical algorithms that made it possible, even though he didn't initially have the technology to put them into practice. His theoretical work, often done on the side of his other physics research, was the crucial, brainy blueprint for what would become the revolutionary Computed Tomography (CT) scan. For his elegant solution to 'seeing inside,' he shared the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1979, proving that sometimes, the biggest medical breakthroughs start with a chalkboard and a brilliant mind.
