When it came to keeping cities clean and people healthy, Charles V. Chapin was the American public health rockstar. For an astonishing 48 years, he served as the Superintendent of Health for Providence, Rhode Island, turning it into a model for urban sanitation and disease control. Chapin was a fierce advocate for evidence-based public health, challenging outdated theories about disease transmission and pushing for practical, scientific methods. He championed better sewage systems, clean water, and rigorous statistical analysis to track outbreaks. He literally wrote the book on municipal sanitation, shaping public health policy across the U.S. Basically, if your city's clean water doesn't give you cholera, you can thank pioneers like Chapin who fought the good fight against germs and ignorance.
