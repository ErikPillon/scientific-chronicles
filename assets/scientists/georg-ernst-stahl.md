Georg Ernst Stahl, born in 1659, was a German physician who tried to explain fire before Lavoisier figured out oxygen. His big idea? The 'phlogiston theory.' Stahl proposed that all combustible materials contained a fire-like element called phlogiston, which was released during burning. If something burned, it lost phlogiston; if it rusted, it lost phlogiston. Simple, right? This theory actually dominated chemistry for nearly a century because it seemed to explain so much, even though it led to some rather odd conclusions (like metals getting *heavier* when they lost phlogiston, which was ignored). While ultimately proven wrong, Stahl's detailed observations and attempts at a systematic chemical theory were crucial steps in the evolution of chemistry from alchemy to modern science. He was wrong, but he was brilliantly wrong!
