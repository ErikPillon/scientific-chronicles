Gene Shoemaker was a scientific pioneer who truly pushed the boundaries of our understanding of Earth and its celestial neighbors. A geologist by training, he championed the then-radical idea that impact craters on Earth, like Arizona's famous Meteor Crater, were indeed formed by extraterrestrial impacts, not volcanic activity. He founded the field of planetary geology, meticulously studying lunar craters and training astronauts for their moonwalks. But his biggest splash came later when, with his wife Carolyn and David Levy, he co-discovered Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, which spectacularly crashed into Jupiter in 1994. Tragically, he never made it to the moon himself due to health issues, but his ashes were sent there, making him the only human whose remains rest on another celestial body. A true cosmic legend.
