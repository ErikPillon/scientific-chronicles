Meet Jacques Piccard, the Swiss oceanographer who inherited a family tradition of extreme exploration from his balloon-pioneering father, Auguste Piccard. Not content with merely floating high above the Earth, Jacques set his sights on the deepest parts of the ocean. He co-designed the bathyscaphe Trieste, a truly revolutionary deep-sea submersible, which was essentially a fancy, pressure-resistant steel ball attached to a gasoline-filled float. In 1960, along with US Navy Lieutenant Don Walsh, Piccard made history by descending into the Mariana Trench, the deepest known point on Earth, reaching an astonishing depth of 10,916 meters (35,814 feet). Imagine the guts! This unprecedented dive proved that complex life could exist in such crushing depths and opened up an entirely new frontier for scientific exploration. Piccard dedicated his life to exploring and protecting the oceans, reminding us that even in the most extreme environments, life finds a way.
