If you've ever imagined mining as just brute force, Henry Livingstone Sulman would have shown you the much more elegant, bubbly side of things. This British metallurgist was a key figure in developing and popularizing the "froth flotation" process in the early 20th century. Instead of just digging and hoping, Sulman and his colleagues figured out how to use bubbles to separate valuable minerals from their less desirable counterparts. By selectively making the good stuff hydrophobic and attaching it to air bubbles, they could skim it off the top of a slurry. It was a game-changer, making mining far more efficient and sustainable, pulling more treasure from less rock. Sulman literally taught rocks how to float!
