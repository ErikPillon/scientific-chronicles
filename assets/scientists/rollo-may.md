Rollo May was a psychological rebel with a cause: understanding the deep, often uncomfortable truths of human existence. Born in 1909, this American psychologist was a key founder of existential psychology in the U.S., a school of thought that pushed back against purely deterministic or mechanistic views of human nature. Instead, May focused on big questions like freedom, responsibility, anxiety, and the search for meaning in a world that can feel pretty chaotic. He believed that facing our existential fears – like death or meaninglessness – was essential for true psychological growth, not something to be avoided. He was a prolific writer, with books like 'Love and Will' and 'Man's Search for Himself' challenging readers to confront their inner lives with courage and authenticity. May wasn't about quick fixes; he was about deep understanding, inspiring generations to think more profoundly about what it means to be human until his death in 1994. Talk about a mind-bending legacy!
