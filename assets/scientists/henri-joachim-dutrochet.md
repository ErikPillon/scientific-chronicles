Henri Dutrochet was a French physician and botanist who probably had more 'aha!' moments than most scientists get in a lifetime. This guy was a polymath before polymaths were cool. He's credited with recognizing and naming osmosis, the fundamental process by which water moves across membranes—a pretty big deal for understanding how cells work. But he didn't stop there; he also figured out that chlorophyll was crucial for photosynthesis and that plants 'breathe' by exchanging gases. He even proposed a rudimentary cell theory before Schleiden and Schwann, recognizing that tissues are made of tiny, organized globular structures. Dutrochet was basically laying the groundwork for much of modern cell biology and plant physiology, all while rocking an early 19th-century powdered wig (probably). A true unsung hero of biological discovery!
