Forget expensive hydrogen! Charles Green, a British aeronaut, was the guy who made ballooning a bit more wallet-friendly and less explosion-prone by substituting costly hydrogen with common coal gas. Talk about a practical innovator! He made his first ascent in 1821 and went on to complete over 500 flights, including a record-breaking journey from London to Weilburg, Germany, in 1836. He also pioneered the use of a guide rope, which helped control altitude, basically inventing the precursor to modern flight controls. Green wasn't just a daring adventurer; he was a serious experimentalist who pushed the boundaries of early aviation, proving that humans could indeed master the skies, one gas-filled balloon at a time.
