Antoine-Laurent Lavoisier, the ultimate chemistry rockstar of the 18th century, basically dragged chemistry out of the alchemical shadows and into the scientific spotlight. Born in 1743, this French genius isn't just famous for giving oxygen and hydrogen their cool names, but for being the first to precisely state the Law of Conservation of Mass. He proved that matter isn't just poofing in and out of existence; it simply changes forms. Oh, and he also debunked the phlogiston theory, showing that combustion involves combining with oxygen, not releasing a mythical substance. Tragically, his brilliant career was cut short by the French Revolution, leading to his execution in 1794. Talk about a raw deal for someone who literally created the metric system and improved gunpowder! "The Republic has no need of scientists," they supposedly said. History, however, emphatically disagreed.
