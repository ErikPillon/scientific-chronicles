Benjamin Banneker was an extraordinary self-taught astronomer, surveyor, and almanac author, proving that genius knows no bounds, even in the face of incredible societal obstacles. Born free in Maryland in 1731, Banneker, an African American, overcame the systemic racism of his era to become a celebrated intellectual. He accurately predicted a solar eclipse, published a series of popular almanacs (containing astronomical calculations, tides, and medical information), and, most famously, assisted in surveying the original boundaries for Washington, D.C. After Pierre L'Enfant left the project in a huff, Banneker, from memory and with his own calculations, helped reconstruct the city's plans. He even corresponded with Thomas Jefferson, eloquently arguing against slavery and for racial equality. Banneker wasn't just brilliant; he was a living testament to the power of intellect and perseverance against immense odds, reminding us that knowledge is truly for everyone.
