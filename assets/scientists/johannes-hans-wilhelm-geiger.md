Ever heard a click-click-click in a movie when someone's near something radioactive? That's the sound of the Geiger counter, and you have Hans Geiger to thank for it! This German physicist, working alongside Ernest Rutherford, was instrumental in the famous gold foil experiment, which revealed the atomic nucleus. But Geiger's most famous brainchild, developed with Walther Müller in 1928, was the Geiger-Müller tube, the heart of the Geiger counter. This ingenious device allowed scientists (and later, anyone with a bit of curiosity and fear of radiation) to detect and measure individual ionizing particles. Before this, measuring radioactivity was a much more cumbersome affair. The Geiger counter democratized radiation detection, making it accessible for everything from geological surveys to medical applications to, well, those dramatic movie scenes. He literally gave us the ears to hear the invisible whispers of the atomic world. A true pioneer in instrumentation!
