Edward Emerson Barnard, born in 1857, was the ultimate 'rags-to-riches' story in astronomy. Starting life in poverty, he taught himself photography and astronomy, eventually becoming one of the most prolific observers of his time. This American astronomer had an eagle eye for celestial wonders, discovering 16 comets and spotting Jupiter's fifth moon (Amalthea) – the last visual discovery of a planetary moon. He was a pioneer in astrophotography, capturing stunning images of the Milky Way, nebulae, and dark dust clouds (now famously called Barnard dark nebulae). Barnard proved that you don't need fancy degrees to make groundbreaking scientific contributions; sometimes, all you need is relentless dedication, a keen eye, and a lot of late nights under the stars, even if your first telescope was made from a tin can.
