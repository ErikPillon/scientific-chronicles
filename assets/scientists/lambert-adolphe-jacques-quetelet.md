Adolphe Quetelet was a Belgian jack-of-all-sciences, but he's most famous for being the original "data guy" for humanity. A brilliant mathematician and astronomer, he founded the Brussels Observatory. However, his real splash came from applying statistical methods, normally reserved for physical sciences, to social phenomena. He analyzed everything from crime rates to birth weights, trying to find underlying patterns. He coined the term "l'homme moyen" or "the average man," arguing that statistical regularities in human behavior pointed to underlying social laws, much like physical laws. He even developed an early version of the Body Mass Index (BMI), originally called the Quetelet Index! His work was foundational for modern statistics, demography, and sociology, proving that numbers could tell us fascinating stories about ourselves and society.
