George Wallace Kidder was that guy who spent his career getting to know the tiny, often overlooked residents of the microscopic world: protozoa. This American biologist, born in the early 20th century, became a leading authority on the biochemistry and nutrition of these single-celled organisms. He was less about discovering new species and more about figuring out what made them tick – what they ate, how they metabolized, and how their internal chemistry powered their minuscule lives. His meticulous research laid foundational knowledge for understanding cellular processes that are surprisingly relevant even to larger, more complex life forms. He proved that even the smallest creatures have big scientific stories to tell.
