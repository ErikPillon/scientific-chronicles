Alphonse Beau de Rochas, a French engineer, might not have built the first four-stroke engine, but he certainly drew up the blueprints for success! In 1862, he penned a pamphlet outlining the theoretical principles and ideal cycle for an internal combustion engine that we now call the four-stroke cycle: intake, compression, power, and exhaust. While others like Nikolaus Otto later developed practical engines based on these principles, Beau de Rochas was the one who brilliantly laid out the thermodynamic roadmap. So, every time you hear a car engine purring along, remember it's fundamentally following the elegant theoretical cycle described by this insightful French engineer over a century and a half ago.
