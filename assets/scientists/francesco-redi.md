Imagine a world where people thought maggots just spontaneously appeared from rotting meat. Gross, right? Well, that was the prevailing belief of 'spontaneous generation' until Francesco Redi, an Italian physician and naturalist, decided to put it to the test. In 1668, he conducted one of the most elegant experiments in science history: he placed meat in three jars – one open, one sealed, one covered with gauze. Maggots only appeared in the open jar and on the gauze of the second, proving they came from flies, not the meat itself. Redi didn't just disprove an old myth; he championed experimental design and observation, paving the way for modern parasitology. He's basically the guy who told everyone, 'No, your dirty laundry isn't making mice; actual mice are making mice.' A true pioneer!
