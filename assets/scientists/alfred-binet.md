Alfred Binet was the French psychologist who basically wrote the playbook for understanding intelligence. Back in the early 1900s, the French government needed a way to identify students who required special educational support. Binet, along with his colleague Théodore Simon, developed the first practical intelligence test, which aimed to measure a child's "mental age" compared to their chronological age. Unlike later, more rigid IQ tests, Binet's original scale wasn't meant to brand kids as smart or dumb; it was a tool to help. He emphasized that intelligence was complex and could be improved, not a fixed, single score. His pioneering work laid the foundation for modern psychometrics and revolutionized how we assess cognitive abilities, even if subsequent interpretations sometimes strayed from his original, more nuanced intentions. He gave us the yardstick, even if others later used it to measure the wrong things!
