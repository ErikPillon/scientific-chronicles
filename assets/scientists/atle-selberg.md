Atle Selberg was the kind of brilliant mathematician who seemed to pull fundamental truths out of thin air. This Norwegian prodigy, who later settled in the US, was a giant in analytic number theory, most famously known for his groundbreaking work on the distribution of prime numbers. In 1947, he independently proved the Prime Number Theorem, a monumental achievement that baffled mathematicians for decades. He also invented the 'Selberg sieve,' a powerful tool for counting prime numbers that still has applications today. Selberg was known for his intense focus, his quiet brilliance, and his tendency to work in splendid isolation, often arriving at profound results that left his peers in awe. He snagged a Fields Medal (math's Nobel equivalent) in 1950, solidifying his status as one of the 20th century's most original mathematical minds.
