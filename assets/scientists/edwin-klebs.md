Edwin Klebs was a 19th-century German pathologist who peered into the microscopic world and saw trouble brewing – specifically, the pesky bacteria causing diphtheria! Not only did he identify the sneaky bug (now known as *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*, often called Klebs-Loeffler bacillus after him and Friedrich Loeffler), but he also made significant contributions to the burgeoning field of germ theory. Before antibiotics, understanding what caused diseases was half the battle, and Klebs was at the forefront, helping lay the groundwork for modern microbiology and pathology. He was definitely a germ detective before it was cool, paving the way for countless medical breakthroughs.
