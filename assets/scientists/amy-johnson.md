Meet Amy Johnson, the British "Queen of the Air" who didn't just fly planes; she built them, fixed them, and then broke records in them! In an era when aviation was dominated by men, Amy, a certified aircraft engineer, became the first woman to fly solo from Britain to Australia in 1930. Talk about smashing barriers! She followed that up with an impressive string of long-distance flights, often with her husband Jim Mollison, setting new records and becoming a global celebrity. Her daring adventures and mechanical prowess made her a true inspiration, proving that women could be just as courageous and capable in the male-dominated world of engineering and aviation. Tragically, she died during a ferry flight for the Air Transport Auxiliary during World War II, but her legacy as a pioneering aviator who embodied courage and technical skill continues to soar.
