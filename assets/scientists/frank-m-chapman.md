Frank M. Chapman was a true bird whisperer and an early champion for conservation. As the curator of birds at the American Museum of Natural History, he didn't just collect specimens; he inspired a generation to watch, study, and protect wild birds. He famously founded the Christmas Bird Count, turning a traditional bird hunt into a citizen science effort to count birds, which is still a major annual event today. Chapman's innovative field guides and popular writings made ornithology accessible to the public, helping shift attitudes from collecting to conserving. He taught us that birds aren't just pretty fliers; they're vital parts of our ecosystem worth understanding and saving.
