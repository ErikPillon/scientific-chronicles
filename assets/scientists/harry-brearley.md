Imagine trying to make gun barrels last longer and accidentally inventing something that would revolutionize kitchens and surgical tools worldwide! That's Harry Brearley for you. In 1913, while tinkering in his lab in Sheffield, England, he was experimenting with different steel alloys to resist erosion in rifle barrels. He noticed one particular sample, a steel with 12.8% chromium and 0.24% carbon, resisted rust like a champ. 'Bingo!' he probably didn't say, but he definitely realized he had stumbled upon something big: 'rustless steel,' which later became 'stainless steel.' From cutlery to skyscrapers, Harry's happy accident made life shinier and more hygienic for everyone. Not bad for a bloke just trying to make a better bang-bang tube!
