Before selfies, there were dissections, and Bartolomeo Eustachi was a master of the latter. This 16th-century Italian anatomist was a trailblazer in understanding the human body, particularly the intricate workings of the ear. He's immortalized in medical textbooks for describing the "Eustachian tube," the tiny passage connecting the middle ear to the nasopharynx – crucial for equalizing pressure and avoiding that annoying popping feeling on planes. But he didn't stop there; Eustachi also made significant contributions to our knowledge of the kidneys, teeth, and muscles, constantly challenging ancient beliefs with his meticulous, hands-on observations. He basically gave modern anatomy an earful of new information.
