Antony Hewish was a British radio astronomer who had a knack for spotting cosmic anomalies. He, along with his PhD student Jocelyn Bell Burnell, discovered the first pulsars in 1967 – rapidly rotating neutron stars that emit beams of radiation, causing them to "pulse" like cosmic lighthouses. While the Nobel Prize for this groundbreaking discovery went to Hewish and Martin Ryle in 1974 (a decision that still sparks debate about Bell Burnell's omission), Hewish's leadership and instrumental design were crucial. His work opened up an entirely new window into understanding the extreme physics of the universe. He wasn't just listening to the stars; he was picking up their secret Morse code messages!
