Alois Senefelder, a bit of a struggling playwright, accidentally became a printing revolutionary. Fed up with the cost and complexity of traditional printing methods for his plays, he started experimenting. Legend has it he jotted down his laundry list on a piece of limestone with a greasy crayon, and *voila*! He discovered that oil and water don't mix, leading to his invention of lithography in 1798. This process allowed for much cheaper and faster reproduction of text and images, sparking a graphic arts revolution. From posters to sheet music, Senefelder's "chemical printing" changed the game, making him the accidental godfather of modern printing and a hero to artists and publishers alike.
