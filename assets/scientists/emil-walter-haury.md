When it came to the ancient Southwest, Emil W. Haury practically wrote the book – and then dug up the evidence for it. This American archaeologist spent a lifetime piecing together the vibrant and complex prehistory of Arizona and its surrounding areas, particularly focusing on the Hohokam people. He was a master of stratigraphy (understanding layers of dirt and time) and was instrumental in applying dendrochronology (tree-ring dating) to Southwestern archaeology, giving precise dates to long-lost settlements. From uncovering ancient ballcourts to explaining irrigation systems that predated modern engineering, Haury's methodical excavations and insightful interpretations reshaped our understanding of early North American cultures. He wasn't just digging holes; he was unearthing stories that had been buried for centuries.
