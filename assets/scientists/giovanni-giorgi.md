Born in 1871, Giovanni Giorgi was an Italian electrical engineer and physicist who, frankly, made the world's measurements make sense. Tired of the muddled mess of different unit systems, in 1901, Giorgi proposed his groundbreaking "Giorgi system," which used the meter, kilogram, and second (MKS) as base units, but crucially, added a fourth electrical unit (like the ampere or ohm) to unify mechanical and electromagnetic units. This elegant solution provided a coherent and practical system that was eventually adopted internationally and became the foundation for the modern International System of Units (SI). So, every time you talk about volts, amps, or watts in a consistent way, you're essentially tipping your hat to Giorgi's ingenious work. He was the unsung hero of coherent scientific measurement, bringing order to the universe of units!
