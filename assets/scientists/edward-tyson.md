Meet Edward Tyson, the 17th-century rockstar of comparative anatomy who basically kickstarted the field. Before DNA tests, Tyson was busy dissecting everything from porpoises to chimpanzees, laying the groundwork for understanding how different species connect. His most famous feat? A detailed anatomical study of a young chimpanzee (which he called a 'pygmie'), published in 1699. He meticulously compared its body to humans and monkeys, arguing it was closer to the latter, helping to clarify the evolutionary pecking order long before Darwin even thought about a beard. Tyson's work was revolutionary, challenging existing notions about the great chain of being and nudging science toward a more evidence-based understanding of life. He wasn't just poking around; he was profoundly changing how we see our place in the animal kingdom, one dissection at a time. A true OG in understanding biological diversity!
