Before Eugène Freyssinet came along, concrete was a bit of a slouch – great for bulk, but not so good if you wanted something slender and strong. This French civil engineer changed the game entirely with his invention of prestressed concrete. Instead of just pouring concrete and hoping for the best, he figured out how to put it under *tension* before it even had a load to bear, making it incredibly resilient. Suddenly, engineers could design bridges, hangars, and buildings with far less material and much more grace. He basically gave concrete a spine, and structures worldwide have been thanking him ever since.
