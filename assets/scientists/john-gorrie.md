Working as a physician in humid Apalachicola, Florida, John Gorrie treated patients suffering from malaria and yellow fever and became convinced that cooling their rooms could ease their suffering, since natural ice was scarce and expensive in the Deep South. Unable to rely on shipped ice, he designed and built a mechanical refrigeration and ice-making machine using compressed air, patenting it in 1851, decades before mechanical cooling became commercially widespread. His invention worked, but it was a commercial flop in his own lifetime, partly torpedoed by the entrenched natural ice industry (whose most prominent backer publicly mocked Gorrie's invention). He died in debt and relative obscurity, but is now recognized as the father of modern refrigeration and air conditioning, technologies that quietly reshaped where and how humans could comfortably live. 🧊🌡️
