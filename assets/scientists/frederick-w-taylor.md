Frederick Winslow Taylor was the original efficiency guru, a mechanical engineer who took factory floors and said, 'We can do this better!' His big idea, dubbed 'scientific management,' aimed to optimize work processes by meticulously studying and standardizing tasks, timing workers with stopwatches, and even redesigning tools. Think of it as applying scientific method to manual labor. While his methods sometimes earned him a reputation as an authoritarian taskmaster, especially with his controversial 'pig iron experiments,' Taylor genuinely believed he was creating a system that benefited both employers (through increased productivity) and employees (through higher wages and less arbitrary demands). His principles profoundly influenced industrial production worldwide, from Ford's assembly lines to modern business process re-engineering. Love him or loathe him, Taylor's ghost still whispers about efficiency in boardrooms everywhere.
