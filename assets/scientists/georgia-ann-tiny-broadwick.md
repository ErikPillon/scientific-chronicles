Don't let the nickname "Tiny" fool you; Georgia Ann Broadwick had a giant appetite for adventure! Born in 1893, she started her career as a performer, joining a carnival act. But it was in 1913, while working with aviation pioneer Glenn L. Martin, that she truly made history. On June 21, 1913, over Griffith Park in Los Angeles, Tiny became the first woman to parachute from an airplane. Not only that, but she also invented the rip-cord mechanism during a later demonstration when her parachute lines snagged, allowing her to pull the chute manually – a design still in use today! Her daring jumps and contributions to parachute safety cemented her legacy as a fearless innovator who quite literally showed everyone how to take a leap of faith. Talk about a sky-high career!
