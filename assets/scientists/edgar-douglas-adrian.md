Edgar Douglas Adrian, a British physiologist with an ear for nerves, literally listened in on individual nerve fibers. Before him, how nerves transmitted signals was a bit of a mystery. Adrian, using incredibly sensitive instruments (for his time), showed that nerve impulses are "all-or-none"—meaning a nerve either fires completely or not at all, like a light switch, not a dimmer. This wasn't just a cool party trick; it was fundamental to understanding how our brains and bodies communicate. His precise measurements, often conducted with fellow Nobelist Charles Sherrington, unveiled the basic electrical language of the nervous system. For this monumental insight, which cracked open the secrets of sensory organs and neural mechanisms, he snagged a Nobel Prize. He basically decoded the silent conversations happening inside you right now, proving that sometimes, the smallest signals have the biggest stories to tell.
