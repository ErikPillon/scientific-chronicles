Axel Fredrik Cronstedt was a Swedish mineralogist with a knack for discovering shiny new things – literally! In 1751, he pulled off a scientific coup by isolating a new metal from a mineral he playfully called 'kupfernickel' (meaning 'devil's copper'), which we now know as the element nickel! Not only did he discover a valuable new element, but he also became a champion of using the blowpipe for mineral analysis, making on-the-spot identification way easier and more practical for prospectors and scientists alike. He was a truly practical scientist who loved getting his hands dirty (or fiery, with the blowpipe), shaping the early days of modern mineralogy and proving that even 'devil's copper' can hold a scientific treasure.
