Detlev W. Bronk was a man who didn't just study science; he built institutions to *enable* science. A distinguished biophysicist and neurophysiologist, he was a trailblazer in understanding the nervous system's electrical activity. But his true legacy arguably lies in his leadership: he became the first president of Johns Hopkins University, later president of the National Academy of Sciences, and president of Rockefeller University. He was a master networker and visionary who tirelessly championed federal funding for scientific research, playing a pivotal role in shaping American science policy in the post-WWII era. He helped create the fertile ground where countless discoveries could bloom, proving that sometimes, the greatest scientific contribution is creating the environment for others to shine.
