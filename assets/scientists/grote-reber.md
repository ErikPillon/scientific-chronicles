Meet Grote Reber, the ultimate DIY scientist who literally built his own observatory in his backyard and changed our understanding of the cosmos! Born in Chicago in 1911, Reber started as an electrical engineer with a passion for amateur radio. When Karl Jansky discovered cosmic radio waves in the 1930s but didn't pursue it, Reber stepped in. He quit his day job, built the world's first parabolic radio telescope in his backyard in Wheaton, Illinois, and spent years meticulously scanning the skies. From 1937 to 1943, he produced the first radio maps of the sky, identifying radio sources in the Milky Way, including Sagittarius A, the supermassive black hole at our galaxy's center. His groundbreaking work laid the foundations for modern radio astronomy, proving that sometimes, the biggest discoveries come from the most unlikely (and homemade!) places. He was truly a one-man cosmic detective agency.
