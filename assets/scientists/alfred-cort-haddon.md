Meet Alfred Cort Haddon, a British academic who kicked off his career studying marine zoology but made a radical pivot that changed the course of British anthropology forever. Initially, he went to the Torres Strait for a zoological expedition, but he ended up getting utterly captivated by the local cultures, art, and customs. Instead of collecting sea slugs, he started collecting cultural data! Haddon was instrumental in moving anthropology away from armchair speculation and towards rigorous, empirical fieldwork. He led the groundbreaking Cambridge Anthropological Expedition to Torres Straits in 1898, a multidisciplinary venture that is often considered the first modern anthropological field trip. This wasn't just a holiday with notes; it was a systematic effort to record languages, customs, music, and physical characteristics of indigenous populations before they were lost to colonial influence. He trained a generation of anthropologists and basically said, 'If you want to understand people, you actually have to go talk to them.' What a concept! He made anthropology cool by getting his hands dirty.
