J. Norman Collie was a man who lived life on multiple peaks – both literally and scientifically. As a chemist, he made significant contributions to our understanding of nitrogen and was one of the first to produce neon in a discharge tube in 1895, albeit not discovering it (that was Ramsay and Travers). He also worked on liquid crystals and X-rays. But wait, there's more! Collie was also an utterly legendary mountaineer, making numerous first ascents in the Canadian Rockies and the Isle of Skye. Imagine spending your days synthesizing compounds in a lab, then packing your bags to conquer unclimbed mountains! He even designed a new type of rope for climbing. Talk about a polymath! His blend of rigorous scientific inquiry and adventurous spirit makes him one of the most interesting figures in late 19th/early 20th-century British science. A true pioneer in more ways than one.
