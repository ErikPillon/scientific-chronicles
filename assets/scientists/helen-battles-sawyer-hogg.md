Helen Hogg, born Helen Battles Sawyer in 1905, was a true star-gazer in every sense! This American-Canadian astronomer dedicated her life to unlocking the secrets of globular clusters – ancient, dense groupings of stars. Known for her tireless work, she extensively cataloged variable stars within these clusters, using them as cosmic measuring sticks to determine distances in the universe. She was also a fantastic science communicator, famously writing a weekly column 'With the Stars' for the Toronto Star for decades. Hogg wasn't just observing the universe; she was mapping it out and making sure everyone else could appreciate its grandeur. She showed us that the best way to understand the vast cosmos is often found in the tiny, twinkling details!
