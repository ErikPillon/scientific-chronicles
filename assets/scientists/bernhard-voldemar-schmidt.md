Bernhard Voldemar Schmidt, born in Estonia in 1879, was a brilliant, eccentric optical genius who transformed how we look at the night sky. Losing his right arm in a childhood accident didn't stop him; he became a master lens grinder and mirror maker, renowned for his uncanny ability to craft perfect optics by hand. His true stroke of genius came in 1930 with the invention of the 'Schmidt corrector plate,' a specially shaped aspheric lens that corrected spherical aberration in wide-field telescopes. This innovation led to the 'Schmidt telescope' or 'Schmidt camera,' which could capture vast swaths of the sky with incredibly sharp images, a game-changer for astronomical surveys and photography. Imagine creating an instrument that helps map the universe! Schmidt died in 1935, leaving behind a legacy that fundamentally changed observational astronomy and paved the way for massive sky surveys.
