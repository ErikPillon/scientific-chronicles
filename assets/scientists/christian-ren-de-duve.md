Christian René de Duve was a Belgian biochemist who made some seriously neat discoveries about the inner workings of our cells. In 1974, he nabbed a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his groundbreaking identification of lysosomes and peroxisomes. Lysosomes? Think of them as the cell's tiny recycling and waste disposal units, breaking down unwanted materials. Peroxisomes? They're the detox specialists, handling harmful substances. De Duve's meticulous use of cell fractionation techniques allowed him to literally spin cells apart and isolate these previously unknown organelles, revealing just how organized and complex our cellular world really is. He basically found the miniature organs that keep our cells tidy and poison-free – pretty crucial stuff, right?
