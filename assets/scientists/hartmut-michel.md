Plants convert sunlight into energy, but how exactly do they pull off this magic trick? Hartmut Michel, a brilliant German biochemist, gave us a dazzlingly clear picture. In the early 1980s, Michel, along with Johann Deisenhofer and Robert Huber, successfully crystallized and then determined the three-dimensional structure of a photosynthetic reaction center from a purple bacterium. This was a massive achievement because it was the first time a membrane protein, notoriously difficult to crystallize, had its atomic structure revealed. Their detailed atomic model showed precisely how light energy is captured and converted into chemical energy at the molecular level. For this monumental feat, which unlocked secrets of life's fundamental processes, the trio shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1988. Michel truly shed light on how life harnesses the sun!
