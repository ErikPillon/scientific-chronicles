Imagine proving that something everyone thought was a tiny particle actually behaves like a wave. That's exactly what Clinton Joseph Davisson did! Working at Bell Labs, this American physicist, alongside Lester Germer, conducted a groundbreaking experiment in 1927. They bounced electrons off a nickel crystal and observed a diffraction pattern – a phenomenon previously only associated with waves (like light). This Davisson-Germer experiment was a huge deal, providing the first experimental confirmation of Louis de Broglie's hypothesis of wave-particle duality and earning Davisson a share of the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1937. He literally pulled back the curtain on the bizarre, wave-like behavior of electrons, solidifying a cornerstone of quantum mechanics!
