Edward Forbes, born in 1815, was a Manx naturalist with an insatiable curiosity for the natural world, especially what lurked beneath the waves. Despite his tragically short life (he died at 39!), he became a foundational figure in marine biology and biogeography. He basically invented the concept of 'faunal provinces' and was an early advocate for studying life in the deep sea, even if his famous 'azoic theory' (that no life existed below certain depths) was later disproven – it still spurred crucial research! Forbes's work on the distribution of species across geological time and space had a significant impact on Charles Darwin's thinking about evolution. He was a whirlwind of activity, publishing prolifically and inspiring a generation of naturalists before his untimely death. A true pioneer who peered into the oceans' depths and our planet's past.
