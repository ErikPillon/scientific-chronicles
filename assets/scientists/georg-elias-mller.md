Georg Elias Müller, born in 1850, was one of those meticulous German psychologists who liked to put human thought under the microscope. He was a big deal in the early days of experimental psychology, often overshadowed by Wundt, but no less influential. Müller wasn't just theorizing; he was building serious lab setups to study memory, sensation, and perception with scientific precision. Ever heard of 'retroactive interference' or 'Müller-Lyer illusion'? That's his kind of work! He meticulously investigated how we forget things and how our perception can be tricked. His systematic approach to psychology, emphasizing quantitative methods and controlled experiments, cemented the field as a rigorous science rather than just armchair philosophy. He basically taught memory how to behave in a lab.
