Before there was hypnotism, there was 'Mesmerism,' all thanks to this flamboyant Austrian physician, Franz Anton Mesmer. In the late 18th century, Mesmer captivated high society (and scandalized the medical establishment) with his theory of 'animal magnetism.' He believed that an invisible natural force, like a fluid, flowed between living things and that blockages in this flow caused illness. His 'cures' involved patients sitting around tubs filled with iron filings, holding rods, while Mesmer, often in a lilac robe, would pass his hands over them, supposedly realigning their magnetic fields. While modern science largely debunked his theories (a French commission including Benjamin Franklin and Antoine Lavoisier wasn't impressed), Mesmer's theatrical sessions undoubtedly triggered powerful placebo effects and laid some very shaky groundwork for later studies of the subconscious and therapeutic suggestion. He might have been a quack to some, but he sure knew how to put on a show and, in doing so, left us with a fantastic verb: to be mesmerized!
