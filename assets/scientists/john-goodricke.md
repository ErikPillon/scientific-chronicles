John Goodricke, who was profoundly deaf from childhood after a bout of scarlet fever, spent his tragically short life carefully observing the star Algol, nicknamed the 'Demon Star,' and noticed it regularly dimmed and brightened on a precise cycle. In 1783, at just 19 years old, he correctly proposed that this was because a fainter companion star was periodically passing in front of it, blocking some of its light, essentially describing what we now call an eclipsing binary star system. He also discovered the periodic variability of other stars, including Delta Cephei, whose behavior later became crucial for measuring cosmic distances. The Royal Society awarded him its prestigious Copley Medal for the Algol discovery, just weeks before he died of pneumonia at only 21. An extraordinary scientific career compressed into a tragically brief life. ⭐👂
