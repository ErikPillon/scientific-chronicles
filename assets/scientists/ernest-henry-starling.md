Ernest Henry Starling was the physiologist who taught us that our bodies are basically a super-efficient messaging service. Along with his brother-in-law William Bayliss, he co-discovered secretin, the first hormone ever identified, and even coined the term 'hormone' itself. Before them, scientists knew things happened, but not always *how* organs talked to each other. Starling figured out that these chemical messengers travel through the bloodstream, coordinating complex bodily functions. He also gave us 'Starling's Law of the Heart,' which explains how the heart's pumping strength is directly related to how much blood fills it. So, whether your heart is working hard or your pancreas is getting a message, you can thank Starling for uncovering the elegant internal communication network that keeps us ticking.
