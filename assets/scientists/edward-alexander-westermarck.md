Edward Westermarck was a Finnish polymath who practically invented the anthropology of marriage and morality. This guy spent years doing extensive fieldwork, especially in Morocco, digging deep into the social structures and ethical codes of various cultures. His monumental work, *The History of Human Marriage* (1891), challenged prevailing evolutionary theories of the time by arguing that monogamy was a very old form of marriage. But his most enduring legacy is the 'Westermarck effect' – the fascinating idea that people raised in close proximity from early childhood tend to develop a sexual aversion to each other. Think of it as nature's way of preventing incest without needing awkward family therapy sessions. This isn't just a theory; it's a well-observed phenomenon, influencing everything from kibbutz studies to understanding human pair-bonding. Westermarck was a true intellectual pioneer, blending philosophy, sociology, and anthropology to give us profound insights into the very human foundations of love, family, and morality. He basically made sense of why your childhood best friend probably isn't your soulmate.
