Imagine stumbling upon a new element while studying seawater! That's exactly what Antoine Jérôme Balard did in 1826. This self-taught genius from Montpellier, France, was busy extracting salts from brine when he noticed a peculiar reddish-brown liquid. Turns out, it was bromine, a volatile halogen with a pungent odor (hence its name, from the Greek "bromos" for stench). Balard's discovery was a significant addition to the periodic table, right between chlorine and iodine. His work not only expanded our understanding of elements but also laid the groundwork for bromine's later uses in photography, disinfectants, and flame retardants. Not bad for someone who started out as a humble pharmacy assistant!
