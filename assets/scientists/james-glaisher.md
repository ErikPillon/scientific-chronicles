Forget expensive satellites and weather balloons launched from the ground; James Glaisher literally went to the edge of space in a hot-air balloon, all in the name of science! This intrepid British meteorologist made daring ascents in the 1860s, reaching dizzying (and freezing) heights to measure temperature, humidity, and air currents. He nearly froze to death and even passed out multiple times during his record-breaking flights, but his groundbreaking data helped us understand Earth's atmosphere long before anyone dreamed of airplanes. Glaisher truly embodied the spirit of 'getting up there' to get the data, pushing the boundaries of both human endurance and scientific exploration to unlock the secrets of our skies. His adventures sound like something out of a Jules Verne novel, but they were very real and very important!
