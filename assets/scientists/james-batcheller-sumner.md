Talk about a scientific mic drop! James B. Sumner, an American biochemist, was the first person to crystallize an enzyme (urease, in case you were wondering). Before him, scientists weren't even sure if enzymes were proteins or some other mysterious goo. Many thought it was impossible to isolate them. Despite skepticism and a lab that was a bit... rustic (he was basically working with second-hand equipment), Sumner's persistence paid off. His proof that enzymes were, in fact, proteins was a game-changer, earning him a Nobel Prize and fundamentally altering our understanding of biological catalysts. He literally brought clarity to the murky world of molecular biology, paving the way for countless future discoveries. Bravo, Dr. Sumner!
