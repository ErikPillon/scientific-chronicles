In the 1880s, Giulio Bizzozero used meticulous microscope work to identify platelets as a distinct type of blood cell fragment, separate from red and white blood cells, and to demonstrate their crucial role in blood clotting and stopping bleeding. Before his work, these tiny structures were poorly understood and often dismissed as debris. Bizzozero also made major contributions to understanding bone marrow's role in producing blood cells and helped found modern experimental pathology in Italy, setting up rigorous laboratory standards at a time when much of medicine still ran on guesswork and tradition. He became one of the youngest full professors in Italian history, appointed at just 24, presumably because it's hard to argue with someone who just showed you what your own blood is actually made of. 🩸🔬
