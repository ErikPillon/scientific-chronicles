Gerard de Geer was the Swedish geologist who basically taught us how to read Earth's annual rings, but for glaciers! He developed "varve chronology," a brilliant method of dating events in the Quaternary period by studying the layered sediments (varves) left behind by melting glaciers. Each layer, like tree rings, represents a year, giving scientists a ridiculously precise geological calendar. Imagine being able to count back thousands of years just by looking at mud! His work was crucial for understanding climate change, glacial cycles, and the timeline of the last Ice Age. De Geer didn't just look at rocks; he made them tell a story, providing a revolutionary tool for piecing together Earth's recent tumultuous past. He literally gave us a stopwatch for ancient ice, proving that sometimes, the quietest layers of sediment hold the loudest stories.
