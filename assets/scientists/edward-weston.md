Edward Weston emigrated from England to the US as a young chemist and quickly found his calling in the booming electroplating and electrical industries. His most enduring contribution was the Weston standard cell, a beautifully stable mercury-cadmium battery so precise and unchanging in its voltage output that it became the international reference standard for the volt for decades. If you wanted to know your voltmeter was actually telling the truth, you checked it against a Weston cell. He also built one of the earliest successful electrical instrument companies, churning out ammeters and voltmeters that made accurate measurement possible for the entire electrical industry. Not flashy work, but nothing else in electrical engineering works without someone first nailing down what a volt actually is. 🔋⚡
