Charles Hubbard Judd was an American psychologist and educator who wasn't content with just traditional teaching methods; he wanted to bring science into the classroom! As a prominent figure at the University of Chicago, Judd advocated for applying psychological principles to understand and improve educational practices. He was a pioneer in developing standardized tests and studying the learning process, particularly focusing on how children learn to read and write. His work helped shape modern educational psychology and curriculum design, emphasizing empirical research over intuition. Essentially, he was one of the brains behind making schools smarter about how they teach, ensuring that education wasn't just about rote learning but about understanding the mind of the learner.
