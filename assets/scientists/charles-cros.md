Before Edison got all the glory for the phonograph, there was Charles Cros, a French poet and inventor with a seriously ahead-of-his-time mind. In 1877, he published a detailed paper outlining a method for recording and reproducing sound using a vibrating membrane and a stylus – basically, he *invented* the phonograph on paper, complete with diagrams! He just didn't get around to building it before others did. But Cros wasn't just a sound visionary; he also made significant contributions to color photography, dreaming up processes for capturing the full spectrum. A true polymath, he dabbled in everything from medicine to astronomy, proving that sometimes, genius is just a little too fast for its own era.
