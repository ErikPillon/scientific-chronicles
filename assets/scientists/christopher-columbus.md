Christopher Columbus, the Genoese navigator sailing under Spanish colors, was the ultimate risk-taker who redefined the known world – even if by accident. Convinced he could reach Asia by sailing west, he embarked on his famous 1492 voyage, accidentally stumbling upon the Americas. While his motivations were mercantile and his actions had devastating consequences for indigenous populations, his audacious navigation skills and determination were undeniable feats of early scientific exploration. His expeditions fundamentally reshaped global geography, initiated the 'Columbian Exchange' of goods, ideas, and unfortunately, diseases, and forever linked two hemispheres. He didn't just sail the ocean blue; he opened up a whole new world, for better or for worse, irrevocably altering the course of human history and our understanding of Earth's true size.
