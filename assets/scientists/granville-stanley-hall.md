If you've ever had a rebellious teen phase, you can thank (or blame) G. Stanley Hall for giving it a scientific name! This American psychological pioneer practically invented the study of adolescence and childhood development. Not content with just observing, Hall was a true institution builder: he founded the first psychology laboratory in the US at Johns Hopkins, started the first American psychology journal, and was the inaugural president of the American Psychological Association (APA). He also had the distinct honor of being Sigmund Freud's only American host during his famous lecture series. Hall was a tireless researcher and advocate, pushing psychology from philosophical musings into empirical science, proving that the journey from childhood to adulthood is far more complex (and interesting!) than we ever imagined.
