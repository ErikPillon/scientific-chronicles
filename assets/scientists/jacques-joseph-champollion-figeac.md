Jacques-Joseph Champollion-Figeac might be in the shadow of his incredibly famous younger brother, Jean-François (the guy who deciphered hieroglyphs), but don't underestimate his own substantial contributions! As an accomplished archaeologist, paleographer, and librarian, Jacques-Joseph was a brilliant scholar in his own right. Crucially, he was his brother's biggest champion and intellectual confidant, meticulously organizing, editing, and publishing Jean-François's groundbreaking work on the Rosetta Stone and Egyptian hieroglyphs after his premature death. Without Jacques-Joseph's dedication, much of that invaluable research might have been lost or delayed. He also made significant contributions to the understanding of ancient Gaul and medieval French history. So, while Jean-François was the superstar, Jacques-Joseph was the indispensable backbone, ensuring that the world could fully grasp and build upon one of the greatest linguistic breakthroughs in history. A true unsung hero of Egyptology!
