David Gregory, born in Aberdeen, Scotland, in 1659, was a key figure in spreading the gospel of Newtonian physics. While not an inventor of grand new theories himself, his genius lay in understanding, promoting, and refining the work of his famous contemporary, Isaac Newton. He was the first to openly lecture on Newton's *Principia Mathematica* at the University of Edinburgh and later, as Savilian Professor of Astronomy at Oxford, he edited and expanded on Newton's work. He also had his own contributions to mathematics, particularly on geometry and optics, and explored the use of reflecting telescopes. Think of him as Newton's brilliant hype-man and academic interpreter, making sure the world understood the seismic shift in scientific thought that was happening.
