Jacques Étienne Montgolfier, along with his brother Joseph-Michel, literally took human ambition to new heights. These French paper manufacturers were fascinated by the idea of flight, and their experiments with hot air led to one of history's most breathtaking 'aha!' moments. In 1783, after seeing laundry dry over a fire billow upwards, Étienne realized that hot air could lift a large enough envelope. They launched their first public demonstration of a hot air balloon, carrying a sheep, a duck, and a rooster (because, why not?) before eventually sending humans into the skies over Paris. Étienne was the more technically minded of the duo, focusing on the engineering and design of these early flying machines. Their groundbreaking work didn't just impress royalty; it ignited the world's imagination and ushered in the era of aviation, proving that humans could indeed defy gravity, all thanks to a bit of fire and a lot of fabric. Talk about a glow-up!
