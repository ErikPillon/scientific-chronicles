Before airplanes were buzzing around, there was Griffith Brewer, a true pioneer of flight who preferred the serene silence of a hot air balloon! Born in London in 1867, Brewer was a passionate British balloonist and an early enthusiast of all things aviation. He famously made the first aerial crossing of the English Channel by balloon in 1908, a full year *before* Louis Blériot's iconic airplane flight. It was a risky adventure, relying on the whims of the wind, but it showcased the burgeoning possibilities of air travel. Brewer also served as the first Chairman of the Royal Aero Club and was a staunch supporter of the Wright brothers, even testifying in their patent disputes. He was a crucial figure in the early days of aviation, championing the cause of human flight when it was still a daring dream, pushing the boundaries of what was thought possible through the air.
