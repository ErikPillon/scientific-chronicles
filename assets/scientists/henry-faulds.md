Dr. Henry Faulds (1843-1930) was a Scottish missionary doctor in Japan when he had a Eureka! moment that changed forensic science forever. While sifting through ancient pottery shards, he noticed fingerprints left by artisans. A keen observer, he then experimented on himself and others, confirming that fingerprints are unique and persistent. In 1880, he published a letter suggesting fingerprints could be used to identify criminals and even wrote to Charles Darwin about it! Though credit was later debated, Faulds was undeniably one of the first to propose and demonstrate the practical application of fingerprints in forensic investigations. Talk about leaving your mark!
