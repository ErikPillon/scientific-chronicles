Conrad Hal Waddington was one of those brilliant minds who just couldn't stick to one scientific box. Born in 1905, this British polymath was a geneticist, an embryologist, a paleontologist, and even dabbled in philosophy of science and art criticism! His most enduring legacy, however, is his pioneering work in developmental biology, where he explored how genes and environment interact to shape an organism. He coined the term 'epigenetics' in the 1940s, essentially kickstarting an entire field dedicated to understanding how gene expression changes without altering the underlying DNA sequence. Waddington challenged conventional thinking, showing us that life's instructions are far more dynamic than we ever imagined.
