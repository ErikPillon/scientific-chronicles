Einstein famously distrusted quantum mechanics' implication that particles could be mysteriously 'entangled,' instantly correlated no matter how far apart, calling it 'spooky action at a distance' and suspecting some hidden, unmeasured variables must be secretly determining the outcomes all along. In 1964, John Stewart Bell devised a mathematical inequality, now called Bell's theorem, that could actually be tested experimentally to settle the debate: if certain measurable correlations exceeded a specific limit, no theory based on local hidden variables could explain it, and quantum mechanics' inherent weirdness would have to be genuinely real. Decades of increasingly precise experiments (eventually earning their own Nobel Prize in 2022) have consistently violated Bell's inequality, confirming that quantum entanglement really is as strange as the theory says. Bell essentially figured out how to put Einstein's philosophical objection on trial, and quantum mechanics won. 🔗⚛️
