Clifford G. Shull was a physicist who made matter reveal its secrets, not by smashing it, but by gently tickling it with neutrons! Sharing the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1994, Shull, alongside Bertram Brockhouse, developed the groundbreaking technique of neutron diffraction. Think of it like using special X-rays, but with neutrons, to peek inside materials and see exactly where all the atoms are hanging out and how they're behaving. This wasn't just a cool parlor trick; it became an indispensable tool for understanding the structure of everything from magnets to biological molecules. Shull literally gave scientists x-ray vision, but for neutrons, opening up a whole new world of discovery in solid-state physics and materials science.
