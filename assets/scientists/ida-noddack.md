Ida Noddack, born in 1896, was a German chemist who didn't just observe science; she challenged it. Most famously, alongside her husband Walter Noddack, she co-discovered the element Rhenium (number 75 on the periodic table). But her real claim to fame for modern physics buffs? In 1934, she dared to suggest that when bombarded with neutrons, a heavy nucleus *might* break into smaller fragments. Everyone, including big names like Enrico Fermi, essentially scoffed. It was considered impossible! Five years later, Lise Meitner, Otto Hahn, and Fritz Strassmann confirmed it, calling it 'nuclear fission.' Noddack's bold, if initially ignored, hypothesis showed an incredible scientific intuition that was years ahead of its time. She was a true visionary, even if the world took a while to catch up.
