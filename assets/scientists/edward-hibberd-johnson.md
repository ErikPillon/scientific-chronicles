Edward Hibberd Johnson, born in 1846, was an American electrical engineer and a key figure in Thomas Edison's early ventures. While Edison often gets the spotlight for the lightbulb, it was Johnson who, in 1882, had the brilliant (and festive) idea to hand-wire 80 red, white, and blue incandescent bulbs and string them on a Christmas tree in his New York City home. This wasn't just a cozy family tradition; it was the world's first electrically illuminated Christmas tree, a dazzling display that amazed reporters and sparked a brand new tradition. Johnson wasn't just about holiday cheer, though; he was instrumental in establishing early electrical power companies and expanding the practical use of Edison's groundbreaking inventions. He truly helped light up both homes and the future of electricity.
