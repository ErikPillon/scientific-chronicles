Gustav Hertz was a German physicist who, alongside James Franck, performed an experiment that really shook up the world of atomic physics – in a good way! In 1914, their famous Franck-Hertz experiment provided crucial experimental evidence for Niels Bohr's quantum hypothesis, which stated that atoms absorb energy only in specific, discrete amounts. They basically fired electrons at mercury atoms and observed that the electrons only lost specific amounts of energy after colliding, confirming that atomic energy levels are quantized. This wasn't just a neat trick; it was a cornerstone discovery that helped cement quantum mechanics and earned them the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1925. He continued to be a significant figure in physics, even working in the Soviet Union after WWII, helping develop isotope separation techniques.
