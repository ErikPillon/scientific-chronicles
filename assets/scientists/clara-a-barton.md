Forget what you thought about "Barton" and "firsts" – this isn't the Red Cross founder! Clara A. Barton (a different one!) was a groundbreaking figure in her own right. Born in 1846, she made history in 1874 when she became the first woman to be appointed a U.S. Patent Examiner. Imagine walking into that very male-dominated office in the late 19th century! She was a pioneer, meticulously reviewing inventions and innovations, proving that women were more than capable of handling complex technical and legal work. Her appointment wasn't just a personal achievement; it was a quiet but powerful statement for women's professional capabilities, helping to chip away at the patriarchal barriers in science, technology, and government service. She was basically the OG of "girl power" in patent law.
