Charles Thomas Jackson was an American polymath who seemingly dabbled in everything — medicine, chemistry, geology, and even inventing. The problem? He had a bit of a knack for getting into heated arguments over who invented what. He famously claimed credit for the discovery of ether anesthesia (which was ultimately credited to William T.G. Morton), and also put his hat in the ring for the telegraph (Samuel Morse won that one). He even had a priority dispute over vulcanized rubber with Charles Goodyear! While his genuine contributions to geology and mineralogy were notable, his relentless pursuit of credit in other fields made him one of the most controversial figures of his time. It seems Jackson was truly brilliant, but perhaps also had a chronic case of 'I thought of it first!'
