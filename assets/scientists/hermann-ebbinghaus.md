Ever cram for an exam and then forget everything a week later? You can blame, or rather, thank Hermann Ebbinghaus for formally explaining why. This pioneering German psychologist was the first to systematically study memory using experimental methods. He became his own test subject, memorizing lists of nonsense syllables (because who wants prior associations?) to map out how our brains retain and lose information. His groundbreaking work led to the discovery of the 'forgetting curve,' showing how quickly memory decays over time, and the 'spacing effect,' which suggests that spreading out learning sessions is more effective than cramming. He basically wrote the instruction manual for your brain's delete button, proving that sometimes, you have to memorize a lot of 'DAX' and 'BOK' to understand how we remember... or don't.
