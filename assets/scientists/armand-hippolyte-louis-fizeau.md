Before Armand Fizeau, measuring the speed of light on Earth was like trying to catch a greased lightning bolt – seemingly impossible! But this brilliant French physicist, in 1849, devised an ingenious experiment using a rapidly rotating toothed wheel and a mirror. He shone a light through the teeth, let it travel several kilometers, bounce off a mirror, and return. By precisely timing how fast the wheel needed to spin for the light to pass through one gap and return through the *next* gap, he calculated the speed of light with remarkable accuracy. It was a clever, elegant, and highly accurate terrestrial measurement, proving that light, though mind-bogglingly fast, wasn't infinitely so. Take that, universe, Fizeau clocked you!
