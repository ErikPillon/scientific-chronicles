Before there were fancy pest control services, there was Benjamin Dann Walsh, a proper English gentleman who decided America needed someone to properly scrutinize its creepy crawlies. After a career switch from classics at Cambridge to farming in Illinois, Walsh found his true calling: insects! He became an outspoken advocate for scientific pest management and, in 1867, Illinois, recognizing his genius (and perhaps tired of crop-munching invaders), appointed him as the state's first State Entomologist (a few years after New York's Asa Fitch became the very first in the nation). He even mentored the legendary C.V. Riley. Walsh battled agricultural pests with wit and rigor, proving that even the tiniest creatures could have huge economic impacts. He literally wrote the book on dealing with bugs, and his legacy swarms on!
