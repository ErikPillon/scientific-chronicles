Clyde Cowan, a brilliant American physicist, is best known for his collaborative work with Frederick Reines, which led to the experimental detection of the neutrino. Predicted by Wolfgang Pauli in 1930 to explain energy conservation in beta decay, this ghostly particle was thought to be nearly undetectable. But in 1956, Cowan and Reines, using a nuclear reactor as an intense source of antineutrinos, set up their detector and, boom! They confirmed its existence, a feat of experimental ingenuity that forever changed particle physics. Though Reines later received the Nobel Prize (Cowan had passed away), their "neutrino experiment" stands as a monumental achievement, proving that even the most ethereal particles can't hide forever from a determined scientist.
