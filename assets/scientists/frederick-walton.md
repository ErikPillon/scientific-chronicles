Before vinyl and laminate, there was linoleum, and the man who made it all happen was Frederick Walton. This British inventor was a true practical chemist. In the 1860s, he noticed that a skin of oxidized linseed oil formed on paint. Instead of just scraping it off, his inventive mind saw potential. After some serious tinkering, he developed a method to combine oxidized linseed oil with cork dust, wood flour, and resins, pressing it onto a jute backing. The result? Linoleum! It was durable, waterproof, relatively inexpensive, and pretty easy to clean, quickly becoming the go-to floor covering for homes, hospitals, and public buildings worldwide. Walton's invention wasn't just a flooring solution; it was a revolution in hygiene and domestic comfort, paving the way for modern resilient flooring materials. He also invented Lincrusta, a popular embossed wall covering. Talk about a solid foundation for innovation!
