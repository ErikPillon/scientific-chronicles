Bernard Forest de Bélidor, born in 1698, was the kind of polymath who makes you wonder if he had extra hours in his day. This French engineer and mathematician wasn't content with just building; he wanted to codify it all. He became one of the most influential writers on engineering and architecture in the 18th century, penning definitive works that shaped a generation of builders and military strategists. His masterpiece, 'Architecture Hydraulique,' wasn't just a book; it was *the* bible for hydraulic engineering, covering everything from watermills to fountains with unprecedented detail and mathematical rigor. He also taught at the French artillery school, molding minds that would build fortifications and design weaponry. Bélidor didn't just understand mechanics; he explained them with such clarity that his work became indispensable, proving that knowing how to build is great, but knowing how to *teach* others to build is revolutionary.
