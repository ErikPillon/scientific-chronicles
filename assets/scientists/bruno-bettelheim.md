Bruno Bettelheim was the kind of psychologist who made waves – and often, controversy. An Austrian-born psychoanalyst who fled the Nazis, he made his mark in the US with his radical (for the time) approach to emotionally disturbed children, particularly those with autism, at the Orthogenic School in Chicago. He famously, and now controversially, posited that autism stemmed from 'refrigerator mothers' – a cold, unloving maternal environment – a theory long since debunked. Yet, his interpretations of fairy tales, as detailed in 'The Uses of Enchantment,' captivated many, exploring their psychological resonance for children. Despite later revelations about his authoritarian methods and embellished credentials, Bettelheim undeniably provoked rethinking in child psychology, even if some of his ideas proved more fascinating than factual.
