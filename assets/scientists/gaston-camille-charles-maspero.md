Gaston Maspero was the rockstar of Egyptology in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, a brilliant French scholar who wasn't just about dusty texts. He was the real-life Indiana Jones, but with more bureaucracy and less whip-cracking. As Director-General of Antiquities in Egypt, he battled tomb raiders, established strict conservation laws, and managed a staggering number of excavations, often against overwhelming odds. He discovered the royal mummies cache at Deir el-Bahari, where many pharaohs, including Ramses II, were found, and was instrumental in establishing the grand Egyptian Museum in Cairo. Maspero wasn't just studying history; he was actively saving it, ensuring that ancient Egypt's treasures could be appreciated for generations, not just looted and sold. A true guardian of heritage.
