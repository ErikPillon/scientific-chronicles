Bengt Edlén, a Swedish physicist, was the Sherlock Holmes of spectroscopy, particularly when it came to the Sun. For decades, astronomers were stumped by strange spectral lines in the Sun's corona, the outermost atmosphere, which didn't match any known elements. Some even suggested an unknown element, "coronium." But Edlén, with a brilliant flash of insight in the 1940s, proved that these lines were actually from highly ionized known elements like iron and calcium. His deduction? For these ions to exist, the corona had to be insanely hot — millions of degrees Celsius — a radical idea at the time. This wasn't just a clever parlor trick; his discovery revolutionized solar physics, explaining how the Sun's ethereal crown could be so incandescent. He basically peered into the Sun's fiery halo and told everyone, "It's not aliens, it's just really, really hot iron!"
