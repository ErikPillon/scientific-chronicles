If you've ever enjoyed a truly cold beer, you owe a little thanks to Heike Kamerlingh Onnes. This Dutch physicist was the undisputed 'gentleman of absolute zero,' obsessed with achieving the coldest temperatures possible. In 1908, he spectacularly succeeded in liquefying helium, dropping the temperature to an astonishing 4.2 Kelvin (that's -269°C or -452°F!). But he wasn't done yet. In 1911, while experimenting with mercury's electrical resistance at these super-low temperatures, he stumbled upon superconductivity – the mind-blowing phenomenon where electricity flows with absolutely no resistance. His meticulous work not only earned him a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1913 but also opened up entirely new fields of quantum physics and low-temperature research, forever changing our understanding of matter.
