Picture this: it's the 1850s, and a sharp American scientist named Eunice Foote is busy experimenting with air and sunlight. While everyone else was perhaps debating bonnets, she was sticking thermometers in glass cylinders filled with different gases. Her big 'aha!' moment? That carbon dioxide traps heat far more effectively than regular air, warming up significantly more under the sun. She published her findings in 1856, suggesting that changes in atmospheric CO2 'would result in an increase of temperature.' Sound familiar? She was essentially predicting the greenhouse effect decades before most of her male counterparts even considered it. A true unsung hero of climate science!
