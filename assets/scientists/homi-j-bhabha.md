Meet Homi J. Bhabha, the brilliant mind who put India on the nuclear map! Born in Bombay in 1909, Bhabha was a theoretical physicist who studied at Cambridge before returning to India with a grand vision: to harness atomic energy for his newly independent nation. He wasn't just a scientist; he was a strategic thinker and institution builder, establishing the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Atomic Energy Establishment, Trombay (now named the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre in his honor). He championed India's three-stage nuclear power program, aiming for self-reliance in energy. Sadly, his life was cut short in a mysterious plane crash in 1966. His legacy, however, is monumental: he laid the scientific and institutional groundwork for India's nuclear capabilities, making him a true pioneer who propelled his country into the atomic age.
