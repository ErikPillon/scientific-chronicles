Guillaume Le Gentil was an 18th-century French astronomer whose tale is a masterclass in cosmic bad luck. Tasked with observing the extremely rare 1761 transit of Venus from Pondicherry, India, he set sail... and missed it because his ship was delayed by war and couldn't reach his destination in time. 'No problem,' he thought, 'I'll just wait for the next one in 1769!' So he stayed in the Indian Ocean, patiently waiting for eight years. When 1769 rolled around, he finally got to Pondicherry with clear skies. But just as Venus began its journey across the Sun, a cloud, a single, perfectly timed cloud, appeared and obscured his view. He made it home after 11 years, only to find he'd been declared legally dead and his estate was being divided. Talk about a cosmic joke! Despite the missed transits, his meticulous observations during his long journey still contributed to cartography and meteorology.
