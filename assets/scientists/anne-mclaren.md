Anne McLaren was a quiet powerhouse who played a huge, often unsung, role in bringing babies into the world – specifically, via IVF! This brilliant British developmental biologist spent her career unraveling the mysteries of early mammalian development. Working with John Biggers in the 1950s, she demonstrated the first successful development of mouse embryos outside the uterus and their transfer back into a surrogate mother. This groundbreaking work was absolutely crucial for developing human in vitro fertilization (IVF) techniques. Beyond the lab, she was a fierce advocate for ethical considerations in science and a respected voice on reproductive technologies. Anne McLaren wasn't just a scientist; she was a pioneer who helped shape the future of human reproduction.
