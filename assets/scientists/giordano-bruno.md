Giordano Bruno, born in 1548, was the kind of guy who didn't just think outside the box; he thought the box itself was probably just one of infinite boxes in an infinite, inhabited universe. A Dominican friar turned rogue philosopher, Bruno championed Nicolaus Copernicus's heliocentric model, but then took it light-years further, proposing an infinite cosmos filled with countless stars, each potentially with its own planets and life. This was way too radical for the 16th-century Catholic Church, who preferred their universe neat, tidy, and Earth-centric. Despite years of fleeing and imprisonment, Bruno refused to recant his "heretical" ideas, leading to his spectacular (and tragic) execution by burning in Rome's Campo de' Fiori on February 17, 1600. Talk about standing your ground! His courage and cosmological visions still spark inspiration and debate today.
