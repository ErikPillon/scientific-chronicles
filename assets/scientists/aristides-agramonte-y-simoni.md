Aristides Agramonte was a medical hero, often overshadowed but no less crucial to one of medicine's biggest breakthroughs. As a Cuban physician and pathologist on the U.S. Army Yellow Fever Commission in the early 1900s, he helped conduct the dangerous experiments (yes, self-experimentation was a thing!) that unequivocally proved mosquitoes were the carriers of yellow fever. This discovery wasn't just interesting trivia; it saved countless lives, especially in tropical regions, and made monumental engineering feats like the Panama Canal possible. Talk about a guy who literally changed the world by understanding tiny, disease-carrying bugs!
