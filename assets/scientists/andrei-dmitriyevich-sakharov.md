Picture a genius who helped build the world's most terrifying weapon, then spent the rest of his life trying to save humanity from itself. That's Andrei Sakharov in a nutshell. This brilliant Soviet theoretical physicist was a key architect of the Soviet hydrogen bomb. But witnessing the destructive power he helped unleash transformed him into a passionate advocate for peace, disarmament, and human rights. He bravely spoke out against his own government's abuses, becoming a prominent dissident, which earned him internal exile and constant harassment. Despite the immense pressure, he never wavered, ultimately winning the Nobel Peace Prize in 1975 for his tireless efforts. Sakharov was a living testament that even the most formidable scientific minds can (and should) engage with the ethical implications of their work, proving that courage can be as powerful as any bomb.
