Bruce Ames is that rare scientist who made a fundamental discovery that's still a household name (well, in science households, anyway!). Back in the 1970s, he cooked up the 'Ames test,' a super-clever bacterial assay that could tell if a chemical was a sneaky mutagen, potentially causing cancer, long before it could wreak havoc on actual people. It became an indispensable tool in toxicology and chemical safety testing, helping to flag dangerous compounds. Later in his career, he pivoted to championing the role of micronutrients and advocating for a more nuanced understanding of carcinogens, often sparking lively debates. This guy wasn't afraid to stir the pot, both in the lab and in public health discussions, leaving a lasting mark on how we think about environmental health and dietary science.
