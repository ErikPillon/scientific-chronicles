Arthur Kornberg was an American biochemist who basically figured out how life copies its instruction manual. In 1956, he isolated and characterized DNA polymerase, the enzyme responsible for replicating DNA. This wasn't just a neat trick; it was a monumental discovery that unlocked the secrets of genetic inheritance and earned him a Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1959 (shared with Severo Ochoa). His work provided the foundation for much of modern molecular biology, from genetic engineering to understanding diseases. Kornberg showed us how those tiny double helices make more of themselves, proving that some of life's biggest mysteries can be solved by getting down to the nitty-gritty of biochemistry.
