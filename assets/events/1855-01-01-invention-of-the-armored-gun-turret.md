Before the gun turret, warships were essentially floating fortresses with cannons fixed to fire broadsides – meaning the entire ship had to turn to aim! That all changed with the invention of the armored gun turret. While several inventors toyed with the idea, Captain Cowper Phipps Coles of the British Royal Navy is widely credited with developing and championing the modern rotating gun turret in the mid-19th century. His designs, starting in the 1850s, offered unprecedented flexibility, allowing heavy guns to traverse and elevate independently of the ship's orientation. The idea truly revolutionized naval combat. Soon after, the American engineer John Ericsson independently developed a similar design for the USS Monitor, famously used in the Battle of Hampton Roads during the American Civil War. This innovation marked a massive leap in naval engineering, enabling ships to become more compact, powerful, and adaptable, forever changing how battles were fought at sea.
