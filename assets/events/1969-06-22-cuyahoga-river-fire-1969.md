The Cuyahoga River in Cleveland, Ohio, was so polluted from industrial waste that it literally caught fire on multiple occasions, most famously on June 22, 1969. While not the first time the river had ignited (it had a fiery history!), the 1969 blaze captured national attention thanks to vivid media coverage. Images of flames dancing on the water became a powerful symbol of environmental neglect, shocking Americans and sparking outrage. This event, alongside others like Rachel Carson's "Silent Spring," fueled the nascent environmental movement, directly contributing to the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the passage of the Clean Water Act. It was a literal environmental wake-up call that truly changed the course of policy!
