Before electric store lighting, shopping after dark was a dim, often sooty affair under gas lamps. But with the opening of Thomas Edison's Pearl Street Station on September 4, 1882, central station electricity became available for commercial use. This meant businesses, from small shops to burgeoning department stores, could ditch the flickering flames for steady, bright electric light. It wasn't just about visibility; electric lights allowed stores to operate longer, showcase products better, and create more inviting, safer environments, revolutionizing retail and ushering in an era of brightly lit consumerism. Suddenly, window shopping at night became a thing!
