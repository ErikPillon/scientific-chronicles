Back in 1903, German engineer Alfred Maul wasn't content with just taking pictures from the ground. He had a vision: to send a camera soaring into the sky on a rocket! And he didn't just dream it; he patented it. His 'rocket with a camera and parachute' patent described an apparatus that would launch, take a photo at its apex, and then gracefully descend with the parachute deploying to protect the precious camera and film. This wasn't some futuristic fantasy; Maul actually built and demonstrated it successfully. His patent was a critical step in formalizing the concept of rocket-borne photography, laying the legal and technical groundwork for everything from spy satellites to weather observation and, eventually, cosmic selfies. He literally paved the way for cameras to reach for the stars!
