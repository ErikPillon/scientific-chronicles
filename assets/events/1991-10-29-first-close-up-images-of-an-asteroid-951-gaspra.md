Before 1991, asteroids were just fuzzy dots, intriguing but distant. Then came NASA's Galileo spacecraft, on its way to Jupiter, with a bonus pit stop. On October 29, 1991, Galileo made history, soaring past asteroid 951 Gaspra and sending back the first-ever close-up images of one of these cosmic pebbles. Suddenly, Gaspra, with its cratered, irregular potato-like shape, was real. This wasn't just a pretty picture; it was a game-changer, revealing details about asteroid geology, composition, and helping scientists understand the building blocks of our solar system. It showed us asteroids weren't just big rocks, but worlds with their own stories, paving the way for future asteroid missions and a whole new era of planetary exploration.
