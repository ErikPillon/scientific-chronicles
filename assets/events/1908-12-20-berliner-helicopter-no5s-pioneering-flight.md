In the thrilling early days of aviation, when getting off the ground vertically was still a wild dream, Emile Berliner and his son Henry were busy tinkering. Their persistence paid off handsomely on December 20, 1908, with the impressive flight of their 'No. 5' helicopter. Imagine the scene: a contraption of rotors and a buzzing 50 hp Adams-Farwell rotary engine, weighing in at 600 pounds, daring to defy gravity. And it did! The No. 5 didn't just hop; it majestically lifted up to 12 feet, proving that stable, controlled helicopter flight was not just possible, but practically within reach. This wasn't just a flight; it was a triumphant pirouette in the history of aviation, showcasing the ingenuity of a father-son duo determined to make the sky a playground for new types of machines.
