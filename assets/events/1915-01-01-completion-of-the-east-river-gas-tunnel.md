Imagine heating and lighting an entire metropolis like New York City back in the early 1900s! That's where the East River Gas Tunnel comes in, a truly ambitious engineering project designed to transport gas from the company's Astoria plant in Queens all the way to Manhattan and the Bronx. Construction, which kicked off in 1904, involved digging two parallel, concrete-lined tunnels almost a mile long, reaching depths of over 200 feet beneath the East River. It was a Herculean task, battling shifting sands, water incursions, and immense pressure. When it was finally completed in 1915, this underground marvel wasn't just a tunnel; it was a vital artery for New York's burgeoning energy needs, ensuring homes stayed warm and streets remained lit. It stands as a testament to the colossal infrastructure projects that powered the rise of modern cities, often hidden, but always essential.
