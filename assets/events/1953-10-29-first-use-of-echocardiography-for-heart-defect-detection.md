Imagine trying to understand what's going on inside a beating heart without actually opening someone up! For a long time, it was a huge challenge. But in the 1950s, Swedish physician Inge Edler and physicist Hellmuth Hertz came up with a brilliant idea: use ultrasound waves! They adapted industrial sonar technology to 'see' inside the heart, essentially creating the first echocardiogram. This non-invasive breakthrough allowed doctors to visualize heart chambers, valves, and detect defects like holes or blockages without a single incision. It was like giving doctors X-ray vision for the heart, forever changing how we diagnose and treat cardiac conditions.
