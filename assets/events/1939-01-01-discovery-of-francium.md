Drumroll please for Francium! This extremely rare and highly radioactive chemical element (atomic number 87) holds the distinction of being the last naturally occurring element to be discovered. The credit goes to the brilliant French physicist Marguerite Perey in 1939 at the Curie Institute in Paris. She spotted it as a decay product of actinium-227, basically finding a fleeting new 'child' in a radioactive family tree. Given its extreme instability and scarcity, Francium is more of a scientific curiosity than an industrial workhorse. It decays so rapidly that you'd be lucky to ever hold more than a few atoms at once! Its discovery filled a gap in the periodic table, proving that even nature's most elusive secrets can be unearthed by keen scientific eyes and meticulous radiochemical analysis.
