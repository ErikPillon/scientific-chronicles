Imagine your heart deciding to take a slow-motion break, putting your life in danger. Before 1958, options were limited, and external pacemakers were clumsy at best. Then, Swedish engineer Rune Elmqvist and surgeon Åke Senning changed everything. Elmqvist, famously working in his kitchen (yes, really!), developed the first fully implantable pacemaker. Senning then surgically implanted it into patient Arne Larsson on October 8, 1958. Though this early device had a short battery life, requiring replacements, it proved the concept: a tiny electronic device could regulate a failing heart. It was a true medical marvel, jump-starting millions of lives and becoming one of the most successful biomedical inventions in history.
