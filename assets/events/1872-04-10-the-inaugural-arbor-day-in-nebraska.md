In the mid-19th century, Nebraska was a vast, treeless prairie, and one man, J. Sterling Morton, thought that was a problem. A journalist and politician, Morton proposed a day dedicated to planting trees – and so, on April 10, 1872, the first-ever Arbor Day was born! Nebraskans were so enthusiastic that they planted an estimated one million trees across the state, transforming the landscape and setting a precedent for environmental action. It was a clever blend of practical agriculture and civic pride, recognizing that trees weren't just pretty; they provided shade, improved soil, and even helped break the wind. This simple idea caught on like wildfire (the good kind, of course), inspiring states, and eventually countries, to designate their own days for tree planting. Talk about a grassroots movement that really grew!
