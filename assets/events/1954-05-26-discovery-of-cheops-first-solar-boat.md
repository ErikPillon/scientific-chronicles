Imagine the thrill! On May 26, 1954, Egyptian archaeologist Kamal el-Mallakh made a discovery that would make any Indiana Jones fan swoon. While clearing debris on the south side of the Great Pyramid of Giza, his team unearthed a massive, sealed pit. Inside, packed like a giant, ancient LEGO set, lay over 1,200 pieces of a perfectly preserved cedarwood ship – Cheops' (or Khufu's) 'solar boat.' This wasn't some dinghy; it was a majestic 43.4-meter-long vessel, disassembled and buried over 4,500 years ago, intended to carry the pharaoh's resurrected soul on its daily journey with the sun god Ra. It took years for Egyptian restorers to painstakingly piece the entire ship back together, using only ancient Egyptian tools and techniques. The discovery offered an unparalleled glimpse into ancient Egyptian boat-building technology and their complex beliefs about the afterlife, proving that even pharaohs needed a sweet ride for eternity.
