Long before Robert Fulton hogged the glory, John Fitch was already making waves – quite literally! In 1787, his pioneering steamboat, powered by a set of paddles and a whole lot of ambition, made its debut on the Delaware River. It might not have been a sleek, commercially successful vessel, but it was a monumental moment: it proved beyond a doubt that steam power could propel a boat. Fitch, a visionary who often ran short on funds but never on ideas, orchestrated this public trial, demonstrating a technology that would soon revolutionize transport. This wasn't just a clever invention; it was the genesis of the steamboat era, laying the groundwork for a future where rivers and oceans would be conquered by the puffing power of steam.
