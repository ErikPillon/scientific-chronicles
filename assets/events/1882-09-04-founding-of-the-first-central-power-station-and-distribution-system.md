In 1882, Thomas Edison didn't just invent a light bulb; he invented the infrastructure to power it. On September 4th, he flipped the switch on the world's first commercial central power station at Pearl Street in Lower Manhattan. This wasn't merely about lighting a few fancy lamps; it was about creating an entire electrical ecosystem. The Edison Electric Illuminating Company then distributed this power, turning electricity from a laboratory curiosity into a scalable, everyday utility for homes and businesses. Imagine the buzz (pun absolutely intended!) as buildings suddenly had reliable, safe light without the fumes of gas lamps or the fire risk of candles. This wasn't just a company; it was the birth of the modern power grid, literally powering the future of urban life and industry, setting the standard for how we receive electricity even today.
