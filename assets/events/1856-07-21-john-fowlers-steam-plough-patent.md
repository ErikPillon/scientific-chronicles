Before tractors were a twinkle in anyone's eye, farming was back-breaking work. Enter John Fowler, a clever engineer who saw the future in steam. In 1856, he patented his groundbreaking steam plough, which wasn't just a fancy toy but a serious game-changer. His system used a portable steam engine to pull ploughs across fields, dramatically increasing efficiency and reducing the sheer muscle power needed. It might sound like a clunky contraption now, but back then, it was like landing a robot on Mars – a massive leap for productivity and a pivotal moment in the mechanization of agriculture. Farmers' backs breathed a collective sigh of relief, even if they had to learn how to wrangle a steam engine.
