For centuries, printing was largely a single-color affair, usually black ink on paper. But as the industrial revolution gained steam, so did the ambition to reproduce images and text in full color. The groundwork for modern color printing presses was laid with innovations like the steam-powered press by Friedrich Koenig and Andreas Friedrich Bauer, which was first used to print *The Times* newspaper in 1814. While this wasn't *yet* a multi-color press, it dramatically increased printing speed and capacity, creating the industrial basis. Over the following decades, engineers developed ways to register multiple plates, each applying a different color, to create full-color images. This complex process, perfected through the 19th century, revolutionized publishing, allowing for vibrant illustrations in books, magazines, and advertisements, fundamentally changing how information and art were disseminated to the masses. It was a truly transformative leap from simple impressions to chromatic masterpieces.
