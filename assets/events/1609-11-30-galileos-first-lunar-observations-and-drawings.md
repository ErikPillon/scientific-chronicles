When Galileo first pointed his improved telescope at the Moon in late 1609, he wasn't just casually stargazing; he was rewriting astronomy textbooks! His meticulous drawings, published in his groundbreaking 1610 work *Sidereus Nuncius*, revealed a lunar surface far from the pristine, perfectly smooth orb imagined by ancient philosophers. Instead, he saw mountains and craters, just like Earth! This wasn't just a pretty picture; it was revolutionary evidence that celestial bodies weren't ethereal, flawless spheres but physical worlds, challenging long-held Aristotelian dogma and proving the power of observational science. Take that, ancient Greeks! Suddenly, the heavens were a lot more rugged and relatable.
