It was a seemingly ordinary Monday in August 1896, when Bridget Driscoll, a 44-year-old mother of two, was struck by an Anglo-French Motor Carriage Company car in the grounds of Crystal Palace, London. The driver, Arthur Edsall, was reportedly going at a blistering 4 mph. An eyewitness described the car "wobbling about like a drunken man." The coroner's verdict? "Accidental death." But he also famously declared, "This must never happen again." Sadly, it did, many, many times over. Driscoll's death stands as a grim marker, highlighting the nascent dangers of a new technology and sparking early debates about road safety and speed limits. A stark reminder that progress often comes with unforeseen costs.
