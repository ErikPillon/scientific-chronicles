For centuries, London Bridge has been doing its best impression of a busy beaver dam, constantly being rebuilt. In 1967, the old 19th-century bridge by John Rennie (which was literally falling apart) was famously sold to an American entrepreneur, Robert P. McCulloch, for a cool $2.46 million. Piece by piece, it was shipped across the pond and reassembled in Lake Havasu City, Arizona, where it still stands, perhaps a little confused by the desert heat. Back in London, a brand-spanking-new, rather less ornate concrete structure was built in its place, opening officially by Queen Elizabeth II in March 1973. It's a testament to modern engineering, though perhaps lacking the romantic charm of its predecessor. Still, it gets millions of commuters across the Thames daily without a splash – a rather important job!
