When it comes to scientific papers with flair, the Alpher–Bethe–Gamow (αβγ) paper is in a league of its own. Published on April Fool's Day in 1948, this seminal work by Ralph Alpher and George Gamow, with a humorous addition of Hans Bethe's name (making the Greek alpha-beta-gamma pun), laid out the groundbreaking theory of Big Bang nucleosynthesis. It explained how, in the scorching hot moments after the Big Bang, light elements like hydrogen and helium were forged. This wasn't just a theory; it provided concrete predictions for the cosmic abundances of these elements, which were later confirmed by observation, solidifying the Big Bang model as the dominant theory of the universe's origin. A paper whose title alone became a legendary scientific pun!
