Before luxury liners, there was the SS Savannah, a hybrid marvel that made waves (literally!) when it set sail on May 24, 1819. Part sailing ship, part steamboat, she embarked from Savannah, Georgia, bound for Liverpool, England. Though she used her steam engine for only about 80 hours of the 29-day journey (coal was heavy, and the engine was, well, experimental!), it proved that steam could power ships across vast oceans. This trailblazing voyage, captained by Moses Rogers, didn't immediately kill off sailing ships, but it planted the seed for a future where predictable, faster sea travel would shrink the world and kickstart the age of steam-powered global trade and travel.
