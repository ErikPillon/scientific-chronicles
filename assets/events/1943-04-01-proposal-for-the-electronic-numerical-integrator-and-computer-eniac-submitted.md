Back in April 1943, with World War II raging and the need for faster ballistic calculations becoming critical, John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert submitted a groundbreaking proposal to the U.S. Army. Their idea? An "Electronic Diff. Analyzer" (which would become ENIAC) – an all-electronic, general-purpose digital computer, something truly unheard of at the time. This wasn't just a casual chat; it was a detailed plan that promised to accelerate complex calculations that were then done laboriously by hand (often by "computers" who were actually women!). The Army, recognizing its potential, greenlit the project, setting the stage for one of history's most pivotal technological leaps. War, it turns out, often births invention!
