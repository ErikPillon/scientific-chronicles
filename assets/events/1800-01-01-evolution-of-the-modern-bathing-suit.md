The "bathing suit" as we know it has had a wild ride, reflecting centuries of changing social norms and technological leaps. In the 1800s, 'swimwear' for women meant elaborate, heavy wool dresses with weights sewn into the hems to prevent them from floating up. Men's suits were equally modest. The 20th century saw radical shifts: the flapper era introduced shorter, less restrictive designs, followed by the daring two-piece in the 1930s. Then came the bombshell: the bikini, unveiled in 1946, was so shocking it was initially modeled by a stripper because no "respectable" model would wear it. Fabric innovations like nylon and spandex also played a huge role, allowing for tighter, lighter, and faster designs. It's a story of liberation, comfort, and sometimes, a little bit of shock value.
