For centuries, making coffee often involved messy grounds and inconsistent brews. Enter the coffee percolator! While various designs popped up, the practical stovetop percolator was significantly refined and patented by folks like James H. Nason in 1865. The genius of the percolator lies in its simple yet effective cycle: boiling water is forced up a central tube, flows over a basket of coffee grounds, and drips back down, extracting flavor with each pass. This iterative process ensured a stronger, more consistent brew, revolutionizing home coffee making. It meant no more guesswork, just a reliable, aromatic cup every time. Hanson Goodrich later patented an improved version in 1889.
