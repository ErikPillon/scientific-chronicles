The idea of a Channel Tunnel wasn't a sudden flash of genius in the 20th century. Oh no, the notion of an undersea connection between Britain and France has a surprisingly long pedigree. The earliest formal proposal dates back to 1802, when French engineer Albert Mathieu-Favier pitched a sub-sea tunnel for horse-drawn carriages, complete with artificial islands for ventilation and gas lamps to light the way. Napoleon Bonaparte himself reportedly expressed interest. While it took nearly two more centuries for the dream to become reality, Mathieu-Favier's visionary plan marked the initial conceptual leap for one of the world's most iconic infrastructure projects.
