In a significant leap for public health, the FDA approved Recombivax HB, the first vaccine produced using recombinant DNA technology, on October 23, 1986. This wasn't just another shot in the arm; it was a game-changer for combating Hepatitis B. Unlike previous vaccines derived from human plasma (which carried inherent risks), this new vaccine was produced by engineering yeast cells to make a key viral protein. This made it safer, more scalable, and ethically less complex. It ushered in a new era of vaccine development, proving that genetic engineering could be harnessed to create powerful, safer tools against infectious diseases, protecting millions globally from a serious liver infection.
