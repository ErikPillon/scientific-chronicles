While America had its NTSC color TV standard in the 1950s, Europe, particularly West Germany, developed its own system: Phase Alternating Line (PAL). The UK adopted PAL, and a significant milestone arrived on July 1, 1967, when BBC Two began regular color broadcasts. Suddenly, British living rooms could experience their favorite shows, news, and sporting events in glorious, vibrant colour, a stark contrast to the familiar black-and-white. This wasn't just a simple upgrade; it was a complex engineering feat that offered better colour fidelity and stability compared to NTSC, becoming the dominant colour TV standard across much of Europe and beyond. It truly transformed the viewing experience, making television an even more immersive and engaging medium.
