For centuries, scientists pondered how traits pass from one generation to the next. The answer lay hidden in the microscopic world of molecules. In 1953, with a dash of collaboration, a bit of competition, and some vital X-ray data (thanks, Rosalind Franklin!), James Watson and Francis Crick presented their groundbreaking model of the DNA structure. It wasn't just any structure; it was a double helix, an elegant spiral staircase of genetic information that explained replication and heredity, igniting a biological revolution.
