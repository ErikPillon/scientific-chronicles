Back in the 15th century, intellectual property wasn't exactly a hot topic, but innovation still needed protecting! While early forms of royal privileges existed, a significant milestone occurred in England around 1449. John of Utynam received a 20-year monopoly from King Henry VI for a new method of making stained glass. This wasn't just any grant; it's often cited as the first documented patent for a specific machine or mechanical process, rather than just a general privilege. It showed that even then, governments understood the value of giving inventors exclusive rights to profit from their clever contraptions. Without this early concept, who knows how long it would've taken to get to Apple patents? It was a truly visionary step towards encouraging creativity and industrial progress!
