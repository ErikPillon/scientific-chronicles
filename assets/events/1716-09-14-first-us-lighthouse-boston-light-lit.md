Boston Light, perched on Little Brewster Island, first flickered to life on September 14, 1716, marking a significant milestone in American maritime history. Before this beacon, navigating the tricky waters around Boston Harbor was a gamble, especially for ships laden with colonial goods. Imagine trying to find your way home in the dark, only to crash into a rocky shore! John Hayes led its construction, and George Worthylake became its first keeper – a job that, sadly, saw him and his family perish in a shipwreck just a few years later. This wasn't just a building; it was a symbol of growing maritime trade and the nascent nation's commitment to safety at sea. Even after being destroyed by the British in 1776, it was rebuilt and continues to shine today, a true old-timer of the coast.
