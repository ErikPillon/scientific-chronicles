Long before tech giants battled over intellectual property, there was Filippo Brunelleschi, the Renaissance master behind Florence's iconic Duomo. In 1421, he wasn't just building domes; he was inventing new ways to transport marble using a specially designed river barge with hoisting gear. Recognizing his genius and the need to protect his investment, the city of Florence granted him a 3-year monopoly on his invention – essentially, the world's first known patent! This groundbreaking legal move meant that if anyone wanted to copy his fantastic freight-mover, they had to go through him. It was a clear signal that innovative ideas had value and deserved protection, laying the very foundation for modern patent systems that still fuel innovation (and sometimes, lawsuits) today. Genius, pure genius!
