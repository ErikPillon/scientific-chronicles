On March 25, 1867, Chicago made history by opening its revolutionary Lake Michigan water supply tunnel. This engineering marvel was the world's first successful tunnel built under a large body of water for municipal water. Designed by chief engineer Ellis S. Chesbrough, it stretched two miles out under Lake Michigan to an intake crib, drawing clean water from beyond the city's polluted shoreline. This ambitious project dramatically improved public health and sanitation in a rapidly growing Chicago, which had previously relied on contaminated near-shore sources. It wasn't just a tunnel; it was a testament to urban ingenuity and a critical step in providing safe drinking water for millions. Finally, clean water that didn't taste like the river!
