When you think 'firsts,' Robert Fulton's Clermont often steams into mind. This groundbreaking vessel, which made its famous journey up the Hudson in 1807, wasn't just a marvel of engineering; it was a symbol of America's burgeoning technological prowess. So, it's only fitting that in 1909, the U.S. Post Office decided to honor this marvel by featuring it on a postage stamp. Issued as part of the Hudson-Fulton Celebration, the two-cent carmine stamp proudly displayed the Clermont, reminding everyone that this wasn't just a boat, but a pivotal moment in transportation history. It cemented the Clermont's place not just in textbooks, but in the everyday mail, a tiny paper testament to a mighty innovation that changed how people and goods moved across the water.
