Imagine a world where lighting up your Christmas tree meant carefully balancing flickering candles – charming, yes, but also a massive fire hazard! That all changed on December 22, 1882, when Edward Johnson, a vice president at the Edison Electric Light Company, unveiled the world's first electrically lit Christmas tree in his New York City home. The tree, adorned with 80 hand-blown, hand-painted red, white, and blue light bulbs, caused quite a sensation, attracting reporters and passersby. This dazzling display was a far cry from today's ubiquitous LED strands, but it was the spark that ignited a global tradition. Johnson's innovation wasn't just about pretty lights; it was about bringing the wonders of electricity into the home in a safe, celebratory way, paving the path for the sparkling, fire-safe festive trees we know and love today. No more singed branches, just pure, electric cheer!
