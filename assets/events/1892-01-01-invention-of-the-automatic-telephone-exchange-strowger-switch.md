Before the automatic telephone exchange, making a call meant talking to a human operator who would manually connect your line. Almon Brown Strowger, an undertaker who, legend has it, suspected operators were rerouting his calls (perhaps to a rival undertaker!), invented a brilliant, if a bit revenge-driven, solution: the Strowger switch. Patented in 1891, his automatic exchange meant callers could directly dial their party, revolutionizing telecommunications. The first commercial exchange opened in La Porte, Indiana, in 1892. It was a massive leap from manual connections to automated efficiency, paving the way for the vast global telephone network we know today – all thanks to a man who really didn't like talking to operators. Talk about a dial-a-revolution!
