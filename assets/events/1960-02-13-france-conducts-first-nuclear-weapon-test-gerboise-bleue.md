On February 13, 1960, deep in the Algerian Sahara, France detonated "Gerboise Bleue" (Blue Jerboa), its very first atomic bomb. With a yield of 70 kilotons, this wasn't just any old bang; it was a thunderous declaration of France's independent defense policy, championed by President Charles de Gaulle. The world watched as France joined the U.S., Soviet Union, and Britain in the exclusive "nuclear club," a clear statement that it would not rely solely on its allies for security. While controversial and environmentally impactful, this event marked a major milestone in French scientific and military ambition, solidifying its role as a significant global player in the Cold War era and beyond.
