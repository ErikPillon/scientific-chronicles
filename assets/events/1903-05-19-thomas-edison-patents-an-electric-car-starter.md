Before push-button ignition, starting a car meant a dangerous, arm-breaking crank. Thomas Edison, ever the problem-solver, sought to electrify the process. On May 19, 1903, he filed a patent for an electric car starter. While his specific design wasn't the one that ultimately dominated the market (that honor often goes to Charles Kettering and Cadillac), Edison's patent shows his foresight into making cars more user-friendly and highlights his wide-ranging inventive interests. He was clearly revved up about getting rid of the crank!
