Talk about an explosive debut! The element Fermium (atomic number 100) wasn't found chilling in a rock; it literally emerged from the mushroom cloud of a thermonuclear explosion. Discovered in 1952 by a team of scientists led by Albert Ghiorso at the University of California, Berkeley, Fermium was identified in the debris from the 'Ivy Mike' hydrogen bomb test. It was the first element discovered after a nuclear weapon test, a testament to the extreme conditions produced. Named after the legendary physicist Enrico Fermi, this synthetic transuranic element pushed the boundaries of our understanding of heavy elements and nuclear reactions. Who knew devastation could lead to such a cool scientific revelation?
