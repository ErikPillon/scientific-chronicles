Before the electric range, cooking meant wrestling with wood-fired stoves or gas flames. In 1882, Canadian inventor Thomas Ahearn decided it was time to electrify the culinary world, patenting one of the earliest electric cooking ranges. While it might have seemed like a futuristic gadget at the time, particularly when Ahearn used it to cook a meal for a local hotel during an electricity exhibition in Ottawa, it laid the groundwork for a kitchen revolution. Imagine, no more chopping wood or dealing with soot! Although widespread adoption took decades as electricity became more common and affordable, Ahearn's invention sparked the beginning of the journey towards the sleek, push-button cooking systems we know and love today. It truly cooked up a storm of innovation!
