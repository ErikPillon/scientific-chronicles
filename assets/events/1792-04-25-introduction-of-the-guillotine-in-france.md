Ah, the Guillotine! This isn't a person, but an infamous device synonymous with the French Revolution. While Dr. Joseph-Ignace Guillotin advocated for a more 'humane' and efficient method of execution, he didn't actually invent the contraption. That credit usually goes to Antoine Louis, a surgeon, and a German engineer named Tobias Schmidt, who built the first prototype. The machine, designed for swift, supposedly painless decapitation, debuted on April 25, 1792, with the execution of a highwayman. It was quickly adopted as the standard method, becoming a chilling symbol of revolutionary justice – and terror. Its chilling efficiency meant that executions became a public spectacle, and it was used for decades, only officially retired in France in 1981. Talk about a device that left a lasting, if gruesome, mark on history!
