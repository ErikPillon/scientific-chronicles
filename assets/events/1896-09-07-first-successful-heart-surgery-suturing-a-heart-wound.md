Before 1896, the heart was largely considered untouchable by surgeons – operating on it was practically a death sentence. Doctors were terrified to even touch it! Then came Ludwig Rehn, a courageous German surgeon who wasn't afraid to challenge the medical taboos. Faced with a patient whose heart had been pierced by a knife, Rehn, with steady hands and incredible nerve, carefully opened the chest and sutured the wound. The patient not only survived but thrived! This monumental success proved that the human heart *could* be operated on, opening the floodgates for future advancements in cardiac surgery and saving countless lives from previously fatal injuries.
