After years of audacious design, epic construction, and battling both the elements and skepticism, the Golden Gate Bridge finally threw open its majestic gates to vehicular traffic on May 28, 1937. Following a day of pedestrian revelry, President Franklin D. Roosevelt himself announced the opening from Washington D.C. with a telegraph signal. Cars streamed across, paying a 50-cent toll, instantly connecting San Francisco to Marin County and beyond. This wasn't just about a quicker commute; it was a symbol of American ingenuity and perseverance during the Great Depression, an orange art deco giant standing proud against the Pacific. What a spectacle!
