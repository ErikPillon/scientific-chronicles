While adding machines existed in various forms, William Seward Burroughs’s invention took the concept to a new level of practicality and widespread adoption. On August 21, 1888, Burroughs secured a patent for his 'calculating machine,' which was essentially the first commercially successful adding machine capable of printing results. This was a game-changer for businesses, banks, and any organization drowning in ledgers and manual calculations. His machine didn't just add; it recorded the calculations on paper, dramatically reducing errors and speeding up accounting processes. Burroughs went on to found the American Arithmometer Company (later Burroughs Corporation), transforming how businesses handled their numbers and paving the way for modern office automation. It was a clear leap from manual drudgery to mechanical efficiency.
