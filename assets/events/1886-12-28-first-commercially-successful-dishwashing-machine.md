If you've ever dreaded washing a mountain of dishes, you have Josephine Cochrane to thank. Frustrated with her servants chipping her prized china and the sheer drudgery of handwashing, this socialite from Shelbyville, Illinois, decided to invent a better way. She famously declared, "If nobody else is going to invent a dishwashing machine, I'll do it myself!" And she did! On December 28, 1886, she received a patent for her groundbreaking design, which used water pressure to clean dishes. Though initially targeted at hotels and restaurants, her Garis-Cochrane Dish-Washer Co. eventually laid the groundwork for the home appliance that saves countless hours of scrubbing today. A true hero for clean dishes everywhere!
