Back when radio was all crackly AM, a genius named Edwin Howard Armstrong was busy perfecting something much better: Frequency Modulation, or FM. On July 18, 1939, Armstrong pulled off a jaw-dropping demonstration, relaying a signal over five FM stations from Alpine, New Jersey, all the way up to New Hampshire. This wasn't just a technical stunt; it was proof that FM could deliver crystal-clear audio over long distances, free from the static and interference that plagued AM. It highlighted FM's potential to revolutionize broadcasting, offering superior sound quality that AM just couldn't match. Despite fierce opposition and a brutal patent battle, Armstrong's tenacious work and this pivotal relay test laid the essential groundwork for the high-fidelity radio experience we enjoy today. He truly was the unsung hero of your favorite tunes.
