On May 18, 1974, India conducted its first nuclear weapons test, code-named "Smiling Buddha," in the Pokhran Test Range, Rajasthan. This wasn't just a bang; it was a seismic shift in global geopolitics. India maintained it was a "peaceful nuclear explosion" for scientific research and not a weapon, but the world knew better. The test, a proud moment for many Indians, immediately drew international condemnation and led to severe sanctions, sparking concerns about nuclear proliferation. Regardless of the controversy, it firmly established India as a significant player on the world stage, a nation capable of developing advanced scientific and military technology independently.
