Imagine a world where emergency blood transfusions were practically impossible because blood clotted too fast outside the body. Yikes! The ability to store blood was a massive hurdle, but thanks to some clever scientists, it became a reality. A pivotal moment came in 1914 when Dr. Richard Lewisohn discovered that adding sodium citrate prevented blood from clotting without being toxic to recipients. This chemical magic meant blood could be drawn and kept for later use. Building on this, Dr. Oswald Robertson, a U.S. Army doctor, used "banked" blood (stored in ice chests with citrate) for transfusions on the Western Front in 1916, making him the first to successfully use stored blood in human patients. This innovation completely transformed battlefield medicine and paved the way for modern blood banks, turning a desperate, last-minute procedure into a reliable, life-saving staple in hospitals worldwide. Cheers to citrate!
