Before 1956, American roads were a patchwork quilt of local routes, often congested and crumbling. Enter President Dwight D. Eisenhower, a big fan of German autobahns (which he'd seen during WWII) and a believer in robust infrastructure for national defense and economic growth. On June 29, 1956, he signed the Federal-Aid Highway Act, commonly known as the Interstate Highway Act. This wasn't just about paving roads; it was about creating a nationwide network of high-speed, controlled-access superhighways. The project was monumental, creating jobs, boosting industries, and fundamentally reshaping American travel, suburban growth, and even how fast you could get to Disneyland. It also gave rise to the great American road trip, complete with roadside diners and quirky attractions. Talk about a game-changer!
