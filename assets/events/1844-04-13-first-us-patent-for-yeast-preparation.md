Before pre-packaged packets of active dry yeast made baking a breeze, getting your dough to rise was a bit more… rustic. But in 1844, Robert Hunter stepped up to the plate (or rather, the kneading trough) and secured the very first U.S. patent for a "Process for preparing yeast." This wasn't about discovering yeast (those microscopic munchkins were already doing their thing for millennia), but about standardizing its preparation for reliable baking. Imagine the joy of bakers finally having a consistent, predictable way to get that perfect rise, without relying on finicky wild yeasts or sourdough starters! Hunter's patent was a quiet but crucial step in the industrialization of food, helping to transform home baking and commercial bread production. It proved that even the tiniest organisms, when properly managed, could spark an agricultural and culinary revolution.
