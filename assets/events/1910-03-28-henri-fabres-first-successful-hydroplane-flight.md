In the early days of aviation, getting a plane airborne often meant needing a long, smooth runway. But what if you could take off from water? That's precisely what French inventor Henri Fabre achieved on March 28, 1910, when his aptly named 'Le Canard' (The Duck) successfully took off from the water near Martigues, France. This wasn't just a plane with floats; Fabre's design was purpose-built, featuring three floats rather than wheels, and it worked beautifully. His pioneering flight demonstrated the feasibility of seaplanes, opening up new possibilities for aviation where land-based airstrips were impractical or impossible. Soon, others like Glenn Curtiss in the U.S. built upon Fabre's work, leading to the development of vital naval aviation and showcasing that sometimes, the best way to conquer the air is to start with the water.
