Talk about a cosmic coincidence! In November 1974, physicists hit the jackpot, almost simultaneously, with the discovery of the charmed quark. One team at Brookhaven National Laboratory, led by Samuel C.C. Ting, found it and named their new particle the 'J' particle. Meanwhile, at the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center (SLAC), Burton Richter's group stumbled upon the very same particle, calling it the 'psi' (ψ). This mind-boggling, near-simultaneous revelation, dubbed the 'November Revolution,' confirmed the existence of a fourth quark predicted by theoretical models. It wasn't just a tiny new particle; it was a giant leap for the Standard Model of particle physics, proving that quarks came in more 'flavors' than anyone had previously tasted. Richter and Ting snagged a Nobel Prize in Physics for their excellent timing and groundbreaking work in 1976.
