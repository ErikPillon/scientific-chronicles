Before there was a museum, there was an idea – a big, bold, ambitious idea hatched by professor Albert S. Bickmore and championed by influential New Yorkers, including Theodore Roosevelt Sr. (yes, the president's dad!). Around early 1869, Bickmore presented his vision to the New York State Legislature: a magnificent museum dedicated to natural history, open for public education and scientific research. He had traveled far and wide, studying European museums, and came back convinced New York needed its own grand temple of nature. This wasn't just a casual chat; it was a formal proposal and petition that galvanized support from the city's elite and ultimately led to the museum's incorporation. It just goes to show, every world-class institution starts with someone saying, "Hey, what if we built something amazing right here?"
