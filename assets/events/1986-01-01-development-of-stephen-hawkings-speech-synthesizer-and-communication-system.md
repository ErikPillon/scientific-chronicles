Stephen Hawking, despite being one of the greatest minds of our time, faced a devastating challenge: ALS gradually robbed him of his ability to move and speak. But giving up wasn't in his vocabulary! The 'Hawking wheelchair' wasn't just a chair; it was his lifeline. In 1986, after losing his voice due to a tracheotomy, he began using a speech synthesizer. Initially, he'd twitch a finger to select words; later, a a single cheek muscle became his only control point. This remarkable piece of adaptive engineering, constantly updated, allowed him to continue writing books, giving lectures, and sharing his cosmic insights, proving that the mind's reach is limitless, even when the body is not.
