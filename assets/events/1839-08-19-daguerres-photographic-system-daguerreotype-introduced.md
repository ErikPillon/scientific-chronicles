Before selfies and Instagram, there was the daguerreotype. Louis Daguerre, building on the earlier work of Nicéphore Niépce, perfected a photographic process that captured stunningly detailed images on a silver-plated copper sheet. In 1839, when France announced the 'free gift to the world' of this invention, it sparked a global sensation. Suddenly, anyone could 'fix' reality, creating portraits that offered an uncanny, almost magical resemblance. It was slow, expensive, and fragile, but it ushered in the era of photography, forever changing how we perceive and record the world.
