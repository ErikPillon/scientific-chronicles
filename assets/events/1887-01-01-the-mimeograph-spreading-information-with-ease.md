Before photocopiers or laser printers, if you needed more than one copy of something, you were either a master calligrapher or you needed a duplicating machine! While Thomas Edison had a hand in early stencil duplicating, it was Albert Blake Dick who truly popularized the 'Mimeograph' from 1887, even licensing the name directly from Edison. This clever contraption worked by pressing ink through a stencil onto paper, creating cheap and relatively fast copies. It became the unsung hero of countless offices, schools, and grassroots organizations, allowing information to be spread far and wide without the need for expensive printing presses. The distinct smell of mimeograph copies even became a nostalgic scent for generations of students and office workers – a true revolution in information sharing, one inky sheet at a time!
