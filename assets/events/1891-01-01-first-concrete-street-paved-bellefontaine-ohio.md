Before everyone complained about potholes, someone had to invent the smooth ride! In 1891, the town of Bellefontaine, Ohio, decided they were tired of muddy roads and dusty streets. Under the guidance of local engineer George W. Bartholomew, they paved a section of their downtown with concrete, creating America's (and arguably the world's) first concrete street. This wasn't just a local upgrade; it was a groundbreaking experiment that proved concrete was a durable, long-lasting surface for urban traffic, paving the way for the smooth, grey highways we all drive on today. Take that, horse-and-buggy ruts!
