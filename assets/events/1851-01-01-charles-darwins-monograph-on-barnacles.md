Before he blew everyone's minds with evolution, Charles Darwin spent a grueling, almost obsessive, eight years studying barnacles. Yep, those little crusty critters you find on rocks and boats! From 1846 to 1854, Darwin became the world's leading (and arguably only) expert on these sessile wonders. He dissected, classified, and described every known species, alive and fossilized, in two massive monographs. This wasn't just a quirky pastime; it was his deep dive into taxonomy and comparative anatomy, honing the observational and analytical skills he'd later use to explain natural selection. It might sound like a weird detour, but his barnacle obsession was crucial in proving he was a rigorous scientist, not just some guy with a theory.
