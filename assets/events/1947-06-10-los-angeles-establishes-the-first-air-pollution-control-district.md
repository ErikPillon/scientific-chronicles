By the mid-20th century, cities like Los Angeles were choking on their own progress. The infamous "smog" was no longer just an aesthetic nuisance but a serious public health crisis. In a landmark move, on June 10, 1947, California passed legislation establishing the Los Angeles County Air Pollution Control District (APCD). This wasn't just a committee; it was the world's first independent governmental agency specifically created to regulate and combat air pollution. It marked a pivotal shift from merely noticing dirty air to actively legislating against it, setting a precedent for environmental protection and public health initiatives worldwide. It was the first official step in a long, ongoing battle for cleaner air.
