Sick of horse-drawn traffic jams? So were 19th-century New Yorkers! On July 3, 1868, the city inaugurated its first permanent elevated railway line, a marvel of engineering that promised to revolutionize urban transit. While earlier prototypes existed, this steam-powered 'el' line, conceived by Charles T. Harvey, was built along Greenwich Street and proved that moving commuters *above* the street chaos was possible. It was noisy, smoky, and rattled teeth, but it was fast! This bold experiment laid the tracks for cities worldwide to adopt elevated rail, fundamentally reshaping urban landscapes and paving the way for modern rapid transit systems.
