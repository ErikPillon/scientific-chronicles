Forget drag racing; let's talk about human-powered speed! The quest to go as fast as possible on human muscle alone has pushed the boundaries of engineering and human physiology. The current mind-boggling record belongs to Canadian engineer and athlete Todd Reichert, who, on September 17, 2016, hit a blistering 144.17 km/h (89.59 mph) on a perfectly flat stretch of Nevada highway. His vehicle, the AeroVelo Eta Prime, is less a bike and more a sleek, carbon-fiber bullet, designed for minimal aerodynamic drag. It's an incredible synergy of human power and hyper-efficient design, demonstrating what happens when top-tier athletes team up with brilliant engineers. It's not just a race; it's a testament to the relentless pursuit of efficiency and speed, proving that with enough pedal power and smart design, humans can fly low to the ground!
