Before Dandy, Beano, or 2000 AD, there was 'Ally Sloper's Half Holiday.' On May 3, 1884, this groundbreaking publication hit the newsstands, widely considered Britain's first proper comic paper. It starred Ally Sloper, a lovable rogue, lazy, indebted, and perpetually scheming, who had previously appeared in the magazine 'Judy.' But 'Half Holiday' was different: it was solely dedicated to comic strips, blending illustrations with speech bubbles and captions in a format recognizable as a modern comic. It was a smash hit, captivating readers with its humour and vibrant visuals. More than just entertainment, it reflected working-class life and aspirations, making it a significant cultural phenomenon. This paper wasn't just a collection of jokes; it was a revolutionary step in publishing, laying the foundation for an entire industry and proving that sequential art could be a powerful, popular, and incredibly funny medium. Ally Sloper truly had the last laugh!
