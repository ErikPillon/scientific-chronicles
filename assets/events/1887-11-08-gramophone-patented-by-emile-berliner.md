Before iPods and streaming, there was the gramophone! On November 8, 1887, the brilliant German-American inventor Emile Berliner filed the patent for his groundbreaking 'gramophone,' forever changing how we listen to music. While Edison had his phonograph with fragile cylinders, Berliner's genius was in using flat, disc-shaped records. These discs were not only more durable and easier to mass-produce, but they also offered superior sound quality, making music more accessible to the masses. Suddenly, entire orchestras and opera singers could be brought into your living room! This invention was a monumental leap in acoustics and mechanical engineering, kickstarting the modern music industry and solidifying Berliner's place as a true pioneer in sound recording.
