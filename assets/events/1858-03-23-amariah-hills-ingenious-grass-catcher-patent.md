Before the grass catcher, mowing the lawn meant a rather messy aftermath, leaving clippings everywhere. Enter Amariah Hills, who, in 1858, patented an improvement to lawnmowers that included a clever attachment to collect those pesky clippings. While simple, this invention was a small but mighty step forward in domestic convenience. It tidied up the chore of lawn mowing, saving countless hours of raking and sweeping. Hills might not have been a rockstar scientist, but his practical innovation made suburban life just a little bit cleaner and a lot less annoying for generations of homeowners!
