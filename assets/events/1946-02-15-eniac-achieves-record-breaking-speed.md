Imagine a machine so massive it filled an entire room, weighed 30 tons, and guzzled enough electricity to power a small town. That was ENIAC, the Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer, unveiled in 1946. This beast wasn't just big; it was blisteringly fast for its time, capable of performing approximately 10,000 additions per second! That's right, while your grandpa was still doing long division by hand, ENIAC was crunching numbers for ballistic trajectories at speeds previously unimaginable. Developed by J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly for the U.S. Army, it revolutionized scientific computation and kicked off the race for faster, more powerful machines. It proved that electronic computing was the way forward, setting a new benchmark for speed and demonstrating the immense potential of digital calculation. ENIAC wasn't just fast; it was a vision of the future, powered by 18,000 vacuum tubes and pure scientific ambition!
