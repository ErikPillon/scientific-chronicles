In the wild west of early motoring, setting a 'speed record' was often just bragging rights. But that changed on December 18, 1898, near Paris. French aristocrat Count Gaston de Chasseloup-Laubat, driving an electric Jeantaud Duc, became the first person to set an officially recognized land speed record. His blazing speed? A whopping 39.24 mph (63.15 km/h)! It might sound slow now, but this was a groundbreaking achievement, showing that cars weren't just for puttering around anymore. It kicked off a century-long obsession with speed, pushing engineers and drivers to go ever faster and redefining what 'fast' even meant.
