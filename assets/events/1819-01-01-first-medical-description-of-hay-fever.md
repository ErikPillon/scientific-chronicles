Sneezing, itching, runny nose – sound familiar? For centuries, people just thought these seasonal miseries were a particularly annoying summer cold. But in 1819, a clever London physician named John Bostock decided to get scientific about his own annual suffering. He meticulously described his symptoms, initially calling it 'summer catarrh' before settling on 'hay fever' (he originally thought it was triggered by hay, though he later realized it was pollen). His detailed observations marked the first formal medical description of this pesky allergic reaction, finally giving a name to the dreaded pollen party that still plagues millions every year.
