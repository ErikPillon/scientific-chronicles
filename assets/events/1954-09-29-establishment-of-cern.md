Imagine a bunch of scientists and politicians across Europe, just after a devastating war, deciding to team up to build the world's most powerful particle accelerators. That's essentially the origin story of CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research. Officially established on September 29, 1954, it was envisioned as a beacon of international scientific collaboration, preventing brain drain and fostering peace through shared pursuit of knowledge. Located on the Franco-Swiss border near Geneva, CERN has since become the largest particle physics laboratory in the world, responsible for monumental discoveries like the W and Z bosons, the Higgs boson, and even the invention of the World Wide Web. It’s a testament to what humans can achieve when they work together, aiming for the stars (or, well, the quantum foam).
