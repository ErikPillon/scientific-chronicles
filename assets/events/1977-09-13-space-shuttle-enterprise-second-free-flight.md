Just a month after its maiden solo glide, the Space Shuttle Enterprise was back in the air on September 13, 1977, for its second free flight test. This time, the pilot's seats were occupied by astronauts Joe Engle and Richard Truly. Dropped once again from its Boeing 747 carrier, the Enterprise executed another flawless unpowered descent and landing at Edwards Air Force Base. These crucial approach and landing tests (ALT) were all about pushing the envelope, gathering vital data on the shuttle's aerodynamics, control systems, and landing characteristics. Each successful glide brought NASA closer to certifying the orbiter for orbital missions, cementing the Enterprise's legacy as the prototype that made spaceplane dreams a reality – even without ever seeing space itself!
