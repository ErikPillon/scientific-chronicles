Forget flat, shadowy X-rays; imagine seeing inside the human body in glorious, detailed 3D! That's what 3D X-rays, better known as computed tomography (CT) scans, brought to medicine. Before CT, doctors relied on standard X-rays, which gave a 2D projection, often making it hard to pinpoint problems. But in the early 1970s, Sir Godfrey Hounsfield developed the first practical CT scanner. The machine takes multiple X-ray images from different angles around the body and then uses powerful computers to reconstruct a cross-sectional, three-dimensional view. It was nothing short of a revolution! Suddenly, doctors could see tumours, blood clots, and fractures with unprecedented clarity, leading to faster, more accurate diagnoses and treatments. Hounsfield and Allan Cormack later shared a Nobel Prize for this incredible leap, literally changing how we look at internal medicine and saving countless lives in the process.
