Before the internet, before smartphones, there was Ceefax! Launched by the BBC on September 23, 1974, Ceefax was the world's first public teletext service, a pioneering digital information system that delivered pages of text and simple graphics directly to your television screen. Imagine a world where you had to wait for the evening news or buy a newspaper for headlines, weather, or sports scores. Ceefax was revolutionary, offering near real-time updates accessible via a remote control, simply by switching to a specific 'page.' While rudimentary by today's standards – blocky graphics and clunky navigation – it was a marvel of its time, providing instant access to information in homes across the UK. Ceefax paved the way for modern digital information delivery and truly represented a giant leap in broadcasting, proving that your TV could be more than just a box for watching shows. It finally switched off in 2012, but its legacy lives on.
