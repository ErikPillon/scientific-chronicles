In March 1969, Apollo 9 zipped into Earth orbit, not to fly to the moon, but to play a critical game of 'space tag' with the Lunar Module (LM) – the flimsy-looking craft designed to land on the moon. Astronauts James McDivitt, David Scott, and Rusty Schweickart spent 10 days thoroughly testing the LM, performing the first manned flight of the moon lander and even practicing the crucial rendezvous and docking maneuvers. Schweickart even went on a spacewalk to test the new Apollo Extravehicular Mobility Unit, making sure it was ready for its moon-bound counterparts. It was a massive success, proving the LM was safe and capable, and giving NASA the green light for future lunar missions. Without this vital 'dress rehearsal,' getting to the moon would have been a much riskier business, if not impossible.
