Lighthouses are cool, but what's cooler? Lighthouses powered by atoms! In the mid-1960s, the Soviet Union, ever keen on pushing technological boundaries (and often, safety boundaries), started deploying lighthouses powered by Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs). These aren't miniature nuclear reactors, but rather devices that use the heat from radioactive decay to generate electricity. They were perfect for remote, unmanned locations, providing a reliable, long-lasting power source. The first such lighthouse was reportedly deployed in the Baltic Sea around 1965. It was a fascinating (and somewhat controversial, due to potential radiation exposure) application of nuclear science, proving that the atom could light the way for ships, not just launch rockets or create mushroom clouds. Talk about a bright idea!
