Picture this: no waiters, just shiny coin slots and tiny glass doors revealing your lunch. The automat, a self-service restaurant concept, was a marvel of early 20th-century convenience and automation. The very first opened in Berlin in 1895, but it was popularized in the U.S. by Horn & Hardart, starting in Philadelphia in 1902. These establishments offered quick, affordable, and surprisingly good food through gleaming vending machines. It was efficient, futuristic, and became a beloved part of city life, especially in New York. The automat proved that sometimes, you just want to grab a slice of pie from a machine without any fuss, making dining an intriguing blend of technology and gastronomy.
