Before Lewis Edson Waterman's stroke of genius, fountain pens were notorious for their leaky, blotchy habits – a real mess for anyone trying to write a legible letter. But on February 12, 1884, Waterman, a New York insurance broker, secured a patent for his groundbreaking capillary feed system. Legend says his frustration stemmed from a leaky pen ruining a crucial contract! His design used tiny grooves to control ink and air flow, preventing gluts and ensuring a smooth, consistent line. This innovation transformed the fountain pen from a fussy novelty into a reliable, everyday writing instrument, paving the way for the sophisticated pens we know today. It was a triumph of engineering in a tiny package!
