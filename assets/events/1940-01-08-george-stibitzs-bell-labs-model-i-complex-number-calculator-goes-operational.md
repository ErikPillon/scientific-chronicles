Long before anyone dreamed of laptops or smartphones, a brilliant Bell Labs scientist named George Stibitz was busy making history in computing. In the late 1930s, fueled by a desire to automate complex electrical engineering calculations, Stibitz tinkered with telephone relays in his kitchen. His initial 'Model K' (for Kitchen) proved the concept, but the real star arrived on January 8, 1940: the Bell Labs Model I, officially dubbed the 'Complex Number Calculator.' This wasn't just any clunky machine; it was one of the first relay-based digital computers, purpose-built to handle complex numbers, which are crucial for electrical circuit design. But here's the kicker: Stibitz also demonstrated its remote operation during a conference in September 1940, using a teletype machine to send commands over telephone lines from Dartmouth College to the calculator in New York. This was arguably the very first instance of remote computing! Stibitz’s ingenious use of binary arithmetic and electromechanical relays laid foundational groundwork for modern digital computing, proving that sometimes, the most complex problems can be solved with a clever, humble switch. He effectively gave computers their first long-distance call!
