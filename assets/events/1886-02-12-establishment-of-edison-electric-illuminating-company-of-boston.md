In 1886, Boston joined the growing list of cities ditching gas lamps for the bright promise of electricity. The Edison Electric Illuminating Company of Boston was incorporated, bringing Edison's innovative systems — from central power stations to reliable light bulbs — to homes and businesses. This wasn't just about fancy new lights; it was about building the infrastructure for a modern city, fueling growth, and literally illuminating daily life. It was a crucial step in the widespread adoption of electricity, proving that Edison's vision wasn't just for a few pioneering cities, but for everyone.
