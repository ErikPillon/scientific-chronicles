After the dramatic explosions of 1945, Uncle Sam realized he needed a proper organization to handle all this newfangled atomic power. Enter the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) in 1946, a civilian agency designed to manage nuclear weapons and promote peaceful nuclear energy. Basically, they took the atomic genie out of the military's bottle and tried to keep it from causing too much trouble, while also figuring out how to make it light up homes instead of cities. It was a massive undertaking, symbolizing the new era of nuclear oversight and the hope (and fear) wrapped up in the atom. From bomb-building to power plants, the AEC was the atomic boss for decades.
