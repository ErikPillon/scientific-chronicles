On a cold Florida morning, January 28, 1986, the Space Shuttle Challenger launched, carrying seven astronauts, including Christa McAuliffe, the first teacher in space. Just 73 seconds later, a catastrophic failure in an O-ring seal on one of its solid rocket boosters led to the orbiter's disintegration, killing all aboard. The disaster was a shocking public tragedy, broadcast live globally, and halted the U.S. shuttle program for over two years. The subsequent investigation exposed serious issues with NASA's management, decision-making, and safety culture. It remains a stark reminder of the immense risks and unforgiving nature of space exploration, and a turning point in how NASA approached safety and public trust.
