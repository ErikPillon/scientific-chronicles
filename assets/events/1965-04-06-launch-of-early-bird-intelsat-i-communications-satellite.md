On April 6, 1965, a little satellite named Early Bird (officially Intelsat I) soared into orbit, and suddenly, the world got a lot smaller. This revolutionary bird, the first commercial communications satellite to be placed in geostationary orbit, made it possible for Europeans and North Americans to actually see each other live on TV. Forget grainy newsreels; this was instantaneous, transatlantic communication! While it could only handle 240 telephone calls or one TV channel at a time, Early Bird was the grand opening act for global telecommunications, proving that distance was no match for a really smart satellite.
