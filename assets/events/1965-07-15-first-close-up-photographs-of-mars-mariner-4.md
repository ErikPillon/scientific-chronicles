For centuries, Mars was a planet of mystery, canals, and potential alien civilizations (or so some hoped!). That all changed on July 15, 1965, when NASA's Mariner 4 spacecraft executed a flawless flyby, snapping 21 detailed close-up photographs. These black-and-white images revealed a desolate, crater-scarred surface, surprisingly similar to the Moon, and shattered many romantic notions about the Red Planet. This wasn't just a stunning visual; it was a scientific game-changer, providing the first real data about Mars's geology and atmosphere, setting the stage for all future missions and proving that sometimes, the truth is more fascinating than fiction (even if it means no Martians).
