Imagine a squash court under the stands of a football stadium, filled with graphite bricks and uranium. Sounds like a weird art installation, right? But on December 2, 1942, this makeshift lab at the University of Chicago became the birthplace of the atomic age. Under the watchful eye of Enrico Fermi and his team, the Chicago Pile-1 achieved the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction. It was a monumental achievement, proving that we could control the atom's power. Leo Szilard had conceived the idea years before, but seeing it work was the real 'Eureka!' moment. This quiet, tense experiment paved the way for atomic bombs and, eventually, nuclear power plants. It was literally a slow-burn revolution.
