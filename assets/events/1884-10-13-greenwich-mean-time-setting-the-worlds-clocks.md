Before everyone's phone synced automatically, the world needed a time lord, and that role was largely taken up by Greenwich Mean Time (GMT). Established as the global standard at the 1884 International Meridian Conference in Washington D.C., GMT set the prime meridian at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London. This wasn't just about convenience; it was crucial for accurate navigation at sea and coordinating international communication, especially with the rise of railways and telegraphs. It meant that, for the first time, everyone could set their watches to a universally agreed-upon zero point, bringing order to the chaotic world of local times. Sure, UTC is the modern successor, but GMT paved the way for our synchronized global village.
