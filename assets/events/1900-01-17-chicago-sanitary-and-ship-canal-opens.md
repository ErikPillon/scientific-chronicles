On January 17, 1900, after years of monumental effort, the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal officially opened. This colossal engineering feat wasn't just about digging a ditch; it famously reversed the flow of the Chicago River, diverting sewage away from Lake Michigan (the city's primary drinking water source) and sending it down the Des Plaines River into the Mississippi. Designed by chief engineer Isham Randolph, the canal spanned 28 miles and integrated with earlier canal projects, creating a navigable waterway from Lake Michigan to the Gulf of Mexico. It was a groundbreaking solution to a severe public health crisis and a bold declaration of human power over natural topography, though not without its own environmental controversies downstream. Talk about making a big splash!
