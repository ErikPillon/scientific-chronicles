What's a computer without memory? Just a really fast calculator! The ability to store and retrieve information electronically was a monumental leap, making programmable computers a reality. Early efforts included delay lines (using sound waves in mercury!) and magnetic drums, but 1947 saw the invention of the Williams-Kilburn tube, which used a cathode-ray tube to store bits. Then came the real game-changer: magnetic core memory, developed independently by An Wang and Jay Forrester around the early 1950s. This tiny, donut-shaped ferrite core technology was reliable, relatively fast, and non-volatile, meaning it held data even when the power was off. Core memory reigned supreme for decades, storing everything from missile trajectories to lunar landing instructions. These pioneering memory systems transformed computers from single-task machines into versatile data-processing powerhouses, making complex software and operating systems possible. Without them, your smartphone would be a fancy paperweight!
