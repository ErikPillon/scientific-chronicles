Before 1474, if you wanted a book in England, someone had to write it out by hand – a slow, expensive business! Enter William Caxton, a savvy merchant and diplomat who learned the printing trade in Bruges. He set up England's first printing press near Westminster Abbey, and sometime in 1474 (the exact date is a bit fuzzy!), he printed 'The Recuyell of the Historyes of Troye,' a translation of a French romance. This wasn't just a book; it was a revolution! Caxton's press ushered in an era of mass communication, making books more accessible and fundamentally shaping the English language and its literary landscape forever. Take that, quill and parchment!
