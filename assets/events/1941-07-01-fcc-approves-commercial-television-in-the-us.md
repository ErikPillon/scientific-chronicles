Before July 1, 1941, TV was mostly experimental, a curious novelty in a few lucky homes. But on that day, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) gave the official 'all clear' for commercial television broadcasting to begin in the United States. This wasn't just some bureaucratic rubber stamp; it opened the floodgates for a media revolution. Suddenly, stations could broadcast programs *with* advertisements, transforming TV from a niche hobby into a potential money-making machine. While World War II soon put a temporary damper on rapid expansion, this FCC decision laid the essential groundwork for the vast, ad-fueled television landscape we know today. It was the moment TV stopped being a parlor trick and started eyeing its future as a global phenomenon.
