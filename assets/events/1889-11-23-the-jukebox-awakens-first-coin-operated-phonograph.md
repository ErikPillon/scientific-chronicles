Imagine a world without Spotify or even radio. Then, in 1889, Louis Glass and William S. Arnold dropped a bombshell (or rather, a coin) on the music scene. They installed their 'nickel-in-the-slot phonograph' at the Palais Royale Saloon in San Francisco, grafting a coin-op mechanism onto an Edison Class M Electric Phonograph. For a single nickel, patrons could listen to a tune through one of four listening tubes. It wasn't quite the flashing, chrome behemoth we picture today, but it was the ancestor of every jukebox, proving that people would pay good money to pick their own soundtrack. This little contraption revolutionized public entertainment and planted the seeds for a century of coin-operated musical magic. Talk about a sound investment!
