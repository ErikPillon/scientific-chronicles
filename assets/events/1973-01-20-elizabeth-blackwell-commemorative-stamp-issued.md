In 1973, the U.S. Postal Service finally got around to giving Elizabeth Blackwell her due, issuing a commemorative stamp featuring her likeness. It was a well-deserved tribute to a woman who, more than a century earlier, had to fight tooth and nail just to be allowed to study medicine, let alone practice it. This wasn't just any old stamp; it was a tiny, sticky acknowledgment of Blackwell's monumental achievement as the first woman to earn a medical degree in the United States and her tireless advocacy for women in science and public health. Every letter mailed with her face on it was a little reminder that breaking barriers often starts with one incredibly determined individual. Take that, patriarchy!
