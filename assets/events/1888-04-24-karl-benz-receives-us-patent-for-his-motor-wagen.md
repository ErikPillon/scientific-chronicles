While Karl Benz had already secured the German patent (DRP 37435) in January 1886 for his 'vehicle with gas engine operation' – essentially the birth certificate of the automobile – ensuring international intellectual property protection was key to its future. On April 24, 1888, Benz received U.S. Patent No. 381,648 for his 'Motor-Wagen.' This wasn't just a bureaucratic formality; it was a crucial step in recognizing and protecting his revolutionary invention on a global stage, particularly in the burgeoning American market. This patent officially cemented the intellectual ownership of his three-wheeled, internal-combustion-powered vehicle, which had first publicly driven in 1886. The U.S. patent helped prevent others from easily copying his design in America and set the stage for the global adoption and development of the automobile. It signaled that the 'horseless carriage' was not just a European curiosity but a serious contender for transforming transportation worldwide. So, a few years after he built it, Benz made sure his gas-powered auto was officially recognized across the pond, paving the way for endless road trips and traffic jams.
