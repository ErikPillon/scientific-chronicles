Back in the mid-19th century, medicine in America was a bit of a free-for-all, with varying standards and training. Enter the American Medical Association (AMA), formally established on May 5, 1847, after a national medical convention in Philadelphia. This wasn't just a friendly gathering; it was a serious effort by leading physicians, notably Nathan Smith Davis, to bring order, ethics, and rigorous scientific principles to the practice of medicine. The AMA quickly became the nation's largest and most influential physicians' organization, setting benchmarks for medical education, developing a code of ethics, and advocating for public health. They were, and still are, basically the big sibling of American medicine, ensuring doctors are properly trained and patients receive quality care. Thanks to them, "trust me, I'm a doctor" started to actually mean something.
