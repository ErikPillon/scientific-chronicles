Imagine stumbling upon a 3,000-year-old clay tablet detailing a catastrophic global flood, complete with a hero building an ark, saving animals, and releasing birds. That's exactly what Assyriologist George Smith did in 1872 while translating tablets from the ruins of Nineveh, unearthed by Layard and Rassam. Known as the 'Chaldean Account of the Deluge,' this discovery, part of the Epic of Gilgamesh, caused an absolute sensation. It was a pre-biblical flood narrative, striking a profound chord in a religiously conservative Victorian society, and dramatically expanded our understanding of ancient Mesopotamian mythology and its influence on later cultures. Suddenly, the Bible wasn't the only game in town for ancient flood stories!
