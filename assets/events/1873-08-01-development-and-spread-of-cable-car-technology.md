“Cable cars” as a general concept represent a fascinating chapter in urban engineering and transportation history. While San Francisco's Andrew Smith Hallidie gets credit for the first successful commercial system in 1873, the technology quickly spread. Imagine cities suddenly able to build upwards and outwards, with their toughest hills now traversable by public transport! These ingenious systems, with cars gripping onto moving subterranean cables, revolutionized cityscapes in places like Chicago, New York, and Edinburgh. They were quieter and cleaner than steam trams and far more efficient than horse-drawn carriages on steep inclines. Though largely replaced by electric streetcars and buses by the early 20th century, cable cars stand as a testament to human ingenuity in solving urban challenges, and in some cities, like San Francisco, they remain beloved, rumbling relics of a clever past.
