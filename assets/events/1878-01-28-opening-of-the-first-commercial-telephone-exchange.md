Remember when calling someone involved shouting across the street? Okay, maybe not that primitive, but before telephone exchanges, direct lines were the only way to communicate. Then, on January 28, 1878, New Haven, Connecticut, changed everything by opening the world's first commercial telephone exchange. Run by George W. Coy and staffed by a handful of operators, this innovative system allowed subscribers (initially 21 of them!) to connect to *any* other subscriber through a central switchboard. No more running a dedicated wire to every friend or business! This was the birth of the telephone network as we know it, making the phone a truly practical and indispensable tool, paving the way for the global communication web that now connects us all. It was literally the switch that flipped global conversation.
