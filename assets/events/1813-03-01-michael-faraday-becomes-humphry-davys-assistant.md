Imagine getting your dream job after sending a meticulously bound copy of a famous scientist's lectures! That's exactly how Michael Faraday, then a humble bookbinder's apprentice, landed a gig as an assistant to the rock star chemist Humphry Davy in March 1813. This wasn't just an entry-level position; it was Faraday's golden ticket into the scientific elite. He assisted Davy on experiments, traveled Europe, and soaked up knowledge like a sponge, setting the stage for his own revolutionary discoveries in electromagnetism. It was truly the start of something big for both of them, even if Davy later famously downplayed Faraday's genius.
