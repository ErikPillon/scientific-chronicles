Imagine a city lighting up for the very first time with reliable, widespread electricity. That's exactly what happened on September 4, 1882, when Thomas Edison's Pearl Street Station in lower Manhattan whirred to life. This wasn't just a building; it was the world's first central power station providing electricity to customers. With six massive 'Jumbo' dynamos, it began supplying power to 85 customers, including the New York Times building, illuminating their lives with Edison's incandescent bulbs. It was a monumental achievement, proving that direct current (DC) could be efficiently generated and distributed to an urban area, forever changing the face of cities and laying the foundation for modern electrical grids. A truly electrifying moment in history!
