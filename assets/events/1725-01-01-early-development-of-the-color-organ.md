Long before light shows at concerts were cool, there was the "color organ," an ambitious device designed to project colored light in sync with musical notes. The concept truly gained traction in the 18th century, notably with Jesuit priest Louis-Bertrand Castel. Around 1725, he proposed his "clavecin oculaire" (ocular harpsichord), envisioning a keyboard instrument that would illuminate different colored glass strips for each note played, based on a presumed natural correspondence between colors and tones. While Castel's original device was more of a grand concept than a widely successful invention, it ignited a fascinating artistic and scientific pursuit. The idea sparked centuries of experimentation, from Wallace Rimington's "Colour-Organ" in the early 20th century to Thomas Wilfred's "Clavilux," aiming to create a truly immersive, synesthetic experience. It's a testament to the human desire to blend art and science in innovative ways!
