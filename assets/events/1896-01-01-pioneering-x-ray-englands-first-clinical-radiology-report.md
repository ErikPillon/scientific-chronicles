Hot on the heels of Wilhelm Röntgen's mind-blowing discovery of X-rays in late 1895, the medical world wasted no time putting them to use. In January 1896, Dr. David Walsh of London published what's often cited as one of England's very first clinical radiology reports in *The Lancet*. He detailed using X-rays to locate a needle embedded in a patient's hand—a seemingly small feat, but a monumental one for diagnostic medicine! This marked the beginning of doctors peering inside the human body without a scalpel, forever changing how injuries and illnesses were diagnosed. Suddenly, medicine had a whole new way of seeing things.
