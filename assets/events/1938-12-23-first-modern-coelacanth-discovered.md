Imagine thinking a creature went extinct 65 million years ago, only for it to pop up in a fisherman's net! That's exactly what happened on December 23, 1938, near the Chalumna River in South Africa. Museum curator Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer was alerted to a bizarre, deep-blue fish among a trawler's catch. Recognizing it as something extraordinary, she sent a sketch to ichthyologist J.L.B. Smith, who immediately identified it as a coelacanth, a fish previously known only from fossils. This incredible "living fossil" discovery was akin to finding a dinosaur walking around, challenging evolutionary theories and thrilling the scientific community with a glimpse into ancient marine life.
