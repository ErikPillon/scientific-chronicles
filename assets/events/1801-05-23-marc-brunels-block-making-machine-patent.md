Before the age of gadgets, ships relied on countless wooden pulley blocks, each painstakingly carved by hand. Enter Marc Brunel, the brilliant engineer (and father of *that* Brunel), who had a lightbulb moment. In 1801, he secured a patent for a series of 44 automated machines designed to manufacture these blocks. This wasn't just a slight improvement; it was a total game-changer. Installed at Portsmouth Dockyard, these steam-powered marvels, with skilled machinist Henry Maudslay's help, could produce 130,000 blocks a year with only ten men, a feat that previously required 100 manual laborers! It was one of the earliest examples of true mass production, standardized parts, and assembly line methods, propelling naval power and setting the stage for the Industrial Revolution. Talk about a patent that actually earned its stripes!
