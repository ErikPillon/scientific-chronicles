Before cars jammed the streets and underground tubes whisked folks around, cities were getting cramped. Enter the London and Greenwich Railway, opening its first section on February 8, 1836. This wasn't just any railway; it was specifically designed to ferry commuters from the burgeoning Greenwich area into the heart of London. It was elevated, a true engineering feat of its time, giving early passengers a unique view over the rooftops. This railway wasn't just about moving people; it pioneered the idea of the 'commuter,' allowing folks to live a little further out and still work in the city. In essence, it invented the modern suburban dream (or perhaps, the modern suburban dilemma of the daily commute!). It changed how cities grew and how people lived, one train ticket at a time.
