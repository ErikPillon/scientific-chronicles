Before 1875, a trip to the dentist was less about 'open wide' and more about 'brace yourself,' with hand-cranked tools that made every procedure a marathon of agony. Enter George F. Green, who in January 1875, secured a patent for his groundbreaking electric dental drill. This wasn't just a fancy new gadget; it was a revelation. Suddenly, dentists could work faster, with greater precision, and significantly reduce the torture patients endured. Green's invention, powered by a small electric motor, drastically cut down chair time and pain, transforming the landscape of oral care. It set the stage for modern dentistry, making dental health accessible and far less dreadful for everyone, proving that a little electrical ingenuity could truly make the world (or at least your mouth) a better place.
