Back in the good old days of colonial America, anyone could pretty much declare themselves a doctor and start prescribing questionable remedies. Not exactly reassuring, right? That began to change around 1760 when Dr. John Bard, a prominent physician in New York, became the first person to receive a formal medical license issued by a colonial government. This wasn't just about giving one doctor a piece of paper; it was a crucial step towards regulating the medical profession, ensuring a baseline of competence, and protecting public health from quacks and snake oil salesmen. It laid the groundwork for medical standards we (mostly) trust today.
