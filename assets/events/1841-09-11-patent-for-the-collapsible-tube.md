Before handy tubes, paint (and later, toothpaste) often came in tricky, messy pouches or jars. Enter John Goffe Rand, an American portrait painter with a knack for problem-solving. Tired of his paints drying out, he came up with a brilliant idea: a collapsible metal tube, much like the ones we use today. He filed his patent on September 11, 1841, for "improvement in the construction of tubes for paint." This wasn't just about paint; it was a stroke of genius that revolutionized packaging, making countless products, from toothpaste to salves, vastly more convenient and hygienic. Rand's simple yet transformative invention showed that sometimes, the biggest breakthroughs come from fixing everyday annoyances.
