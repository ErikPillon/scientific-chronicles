Before laptops, even before electric calculators, there was Herman Hollerith and his magical machine! Faced with the daunting task of processing the 1890 US Census (which was taking years manually), Hollerith, inspired by railroad conductors punching tickets, invented an electromechanical tabulating machine that read information from punch cards. Patented in 1889, this wasn't just a clever gadget; it was a revolution in data processing! What used to take a decade could now be done in months, saving millions. His invention didn't just count people; it laid the fundamental groundwork for automated data handling, statistics, and eventually, the entire computer industry. Hollerith's company eventually evolved into International Business Machines (IBM), proving that sometimes, a simple hole in a card can spark a massive technological empire. Truly a game-changer!
