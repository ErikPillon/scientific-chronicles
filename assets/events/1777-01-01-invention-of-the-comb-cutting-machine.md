For centuries, making combs was a tedious, manual process, often involving individual teeth being cut by hand. This made combs relatively expensive and slow to produce. Enter the comb-cutting machine, an innovation that brought a touch of industrial efficiency to personal grooming! While various attempts were made, one of the earliest successful and widely recognized machines was developed by Obadiah Wyatt in the late 18th century, with a patent granted around 1777 in England. These machines used a series of reciprocating saws or cutters to rapidly produce multiple comb teeth simultaneously, vastly speeding up production and making combs more affordable and accessible. It was a small but significant step in the broader industrial revolution, showing how even everyday items could be transformed by clever mechanical engineering.
