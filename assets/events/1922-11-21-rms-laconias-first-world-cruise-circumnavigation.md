Before cruises were the norm, sailing around the entire globe was a serious undertaking, mostly for explorers or traders. But on November 21, 1922, the grand British ocean liner RMS Laconia changed the game by setting off from New York for the first-ever continuous passenger cruise around the world. Over 130 days, she carried curious travelers to exotic locales, from Havana to Bombay, proving that circumnavigation wasn't just for adventurers anymore; it could be a luxurious vacation! This wasn't just a pleasure trip; it marked a significant moment in maritime travel and the burgeoning tourism industry, shrinking the world for the well-heeled and setting a new standard for global exploration and leisure travel.
