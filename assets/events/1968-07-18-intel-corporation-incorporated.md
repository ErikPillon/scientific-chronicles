While 'founded' often gets the spotlight, 'incorporates' is where things get official, a legal handshake sealing the deal for future tech dominance. On July 18, 1968, Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore, with the crucial addition of Andrew Grove, officially incorporated Intel. This wasn't just paperwork; it was the formal birth of a company destined to become synonymous with microprocessors. Fresh from their groundbreaking work at Fairchild Semiconductor, these visionaries weren't content to rest on their laurels. They knew the future of electronics was small, fast, and silicon-based. Their incorporation wasn't just an administrative step; it was the bureaucratic equivalent of firing the starting gun on a race to build the brains of the digital age. And boy, did they run!
