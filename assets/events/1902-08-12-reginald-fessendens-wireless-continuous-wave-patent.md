While Guglielmo Marconi famously pioneered wireless telegraphy (dots and dashes), Reginald Fessenden was the maverick determined to transmit actual *voices* over the airwaves. His work involved developing high-frequency alternators to create continuous waves, a massive leap from the spark-gap transmitters of the day. One of his key patents, granted in 1902, protected aspects of his method for generating these continuous electromagnetic waves, which were essential for carrying audio signals. This patent was a foundational step toward the world's first true radio broadcast on Christmas Eve, 1906, where Fessenden himself played the violin and read from the Bible. Without his patented ingenuity, our airwaves might have remained silent save for the incessant beeping of Morse code!
