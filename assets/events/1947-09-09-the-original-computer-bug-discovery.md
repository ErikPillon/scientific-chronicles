Ever wonder why we say "bug" when software acts up? Thank a moth! On September 9, 1947, a team working on the Harvard Mark II computer was scratching their heads over a glitch. Upon investigation, they found a real, actual moth trapped in a relay, interrupting the circuit. Admiral Grace Hopper, a pioneering computer scientist, famously taped the deceased insect into the logbook with the note, "First actual case of bug being found." While the term "bug" for a mechanical defect existed long before this (Edison even used it!), this incident cemented its place in computer lore, providing a charmingly literal origin story for every programmer's nightmare. It’s a delightful reminder that even the most cutting-edge technology can be foiled by the smallest, flappiest of creatures!
