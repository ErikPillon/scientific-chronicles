Imagine pushing a button and the lights come on, pretty standard, right? Now imagine flicking a switch, and the lights illuminate *because of light that left a star 40 years ago*! That's exactly what happened at the 1933 Chicago World's Fair. The fair, themed 'A Century of Progress,' aimed for futuristic spectacle. To symbolize this, starlight from Arcturus (believed to have begun its journey around the time of the 1893 fair) was captured by photo-electric cells at astronomical observatories, converted into electrical impulses, and then used to remotely trigger the fair's dazzling illumination. It was a spectacular piece of scientific showmanship, blending astronomy with engineering to create a truly unforgettable moment from the past that felt utterly space-age.
