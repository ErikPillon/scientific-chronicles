Who knew concrete could float? While it sounds like a bad architectural joke, it was actually a stroke of genius, especially during times of material scarcity. Back in 1897, Italian engineer Carlo Gabellini built the Pioneer, often cited as the first concrete barge. This wasn't some flimsy float; it was a tough vessel demonstrating that ferro-cement (a concrete reinforced with wire mesh) could be a viable, cost-effective shipbuilding material. It might not have had the sleek lines of a wooden yacht, but it got the job done, proving that ingenuity often looks beyond traditional methods.
