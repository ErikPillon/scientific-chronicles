On December 2, 1980, U.S. Patent 4,237,224 was issued to Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer for their revolutionary method of gene splicing. This wasn't just any patent; it was the birth certificate of the biotechnology industry, establishing the fundamental technique for creating recombinant DNA. Imagine: taking a gene from one organism and inserting it into another to create new biological functions. This breakthrough made it possible to produce human insulin in bacteria, develop new vaccines, and engineer crops. The patent sparked both immense innovation and heated debates over ownership of life, but there's no denying it was a legal and scientific watershed that unleashed the full commercial potential of genetic engineering.
