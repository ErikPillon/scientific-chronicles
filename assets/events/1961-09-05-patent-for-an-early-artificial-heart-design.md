Before a medical device can save lives, it often needs to secure its intellectual property, and the artificial heart was no exception. One notable early patent for an artificial heart design, US Patent 3,001,202, was filed by none other than ventriloquist Paul Winchell (yes, *that* Paul Winchell!) and physician Henry Heimlich (yes, *that* Heimlich of the maneuver fame!) on September 5, 1961. Winchell, a mechanical tinkerer and medical enthusiast, actually developed several designs for artificial hearts in collaboration with doctors. This patent wasn't for the Jarvik-7, but it represented a crucial step in formalizing the conceptual and engineering groundwork for such devices. Patents are often the unsung heroes of scientific progress, documenting the ingenuity and protecting the ideas that eventually lead to revolutionary breakthroughs. This particular patent highlights the diverse minds drawn to the challenge of creating an artificial heart, proving that sometimes, even a ventriloquist can have a brilliant idea for keeping the blood pumping!
