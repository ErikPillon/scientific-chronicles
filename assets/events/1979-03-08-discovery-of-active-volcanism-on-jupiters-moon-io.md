When NASA's Voyager 1 spacecraft zipped past Jupiter's moon Io in March 1979, scientists expected a quiet, cratered world. What they got was a mind-blowing surprise! Linda Morabito, a navigation engineer at JPL, was processing images when she spotted a plume erupting from the moon's limb, a tell-tale sign of active volcanism. The discovery, announced on March 8, 1979, was revolutionary. Before Io, scientists thought Earth was the only body in the inner solar system with active volcanoes. Io, it turned out, was a geological hot mess, constantly resurfacing itself with lava flows and plumes, powered by the immense tidal forces from Jupiter. It completely redefined our understanding of planetary geology and the diverse dynamics within our solar system. Talk about a fiery personality!
