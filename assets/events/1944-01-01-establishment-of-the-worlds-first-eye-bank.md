Before the mid-20th century, if you suffered from corneal blindness, your chances of regaining sight were slim to none. That all changed in 1944, thanks to the humanitarian vision of Dr. R. Townley Paton, who established the world's first eye bank in New York City. The idea was simple but revolutionary: create an organized system for collecting, preserving, and distributing donated corneas for transplantation. This wasn't just a medical facility; it was a logistics marvel and a testament to collective human generosity. The eye bank standardized procedures, ensuring that delicate corneal tissue could be safely stored and transported, ready to be surgically implanted. Paton's pioneering effort immediately boosted the availability and success rates of corneal transplants, dramatically improving the lives of countless individuals and setting the global standard for similar institutions dedicated to restoring sight.
