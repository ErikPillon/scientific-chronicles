Before 1908, roads were mostly horse-and-buggy affairs, often muddy and bumpy. Motors were a newfangled nuisance sharing space with livestock. But William Kissam Vanderbilt II, speed demon and millionaire, had a better idea: the Long Island Motor Parkway. Opening on October 10, 1908, this isn't just a road; it was the world's first automobile-only highway, complete with concrete surfaces, grade separations (no pesky horse-drawn carriages to dodge!), and even innovative toll booths. It was a 48-mile ribbon of asphalt paradise for early motorists, designed for speed and efficiency, bypassing congested villages. While it eventually faded into history, it was a groundbreaking blueprint for modern expressways and freeways, forever changing how we commute and envision road travel. Vanderbilt truly put the "motor" in "motorway," paving the way for our car-centric world.
