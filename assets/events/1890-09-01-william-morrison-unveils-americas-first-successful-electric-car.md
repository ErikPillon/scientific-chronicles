In 1890, while gasoline cars were still sputtering into existence, chemist William Morrison in Des Moines, Iowa, quietly rolled out America’s very first successful electric automobile. This six-passenger contraption, looking a bit like a motorized carriage, was powered by 24 storage battery cells and could hit a top speed of 14 miles per hour – which was pretty zippy for the era! It caused quite a sensation, especially when showcased at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, demonstrating a clean, silent alternative to the noisy, smelly internal combustion engines. For a brief period in the early 20th century, electric cars actually outsold gasoline models, proving that silent power had an early, electrifying appeal that was almost forgotten for decades.
