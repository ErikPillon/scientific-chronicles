Before Fulton, many tried and failed to make steamboats practical. But Robert Fulton, an American engineer with a knack for innovation, pulled it off. After a stint in Europe where he experimented with submarines and canals, he returned to the U.S. and, with financial backing, designed what would become the first commercially successful steamboat. While not the *first* steamboat ever, his vessel, launched in 1807 and often called the *Clermont*, proved that steam power could reliably carry passengers and cargo upriver against the current. This wasn't just a quirky invention; it was a game-changer for commerce, connecting distant towns and making river travel far more efficient and predictable. Fulton didn't just build a steamboat; he launched an industry, charting a course for the future of transportation.
