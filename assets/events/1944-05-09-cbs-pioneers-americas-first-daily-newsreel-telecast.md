Before smartphones delivered news to your pocket, and even before nightly news anchors became household names, getting your daily dose of current events was a bit more... cinematic. On May 9, 1944, CBS took a bold leap, launching what's recognized as the first daily newsreel telecast in the United States. While television was still in its infancy, largely experimental, and confined to a handful of privileged homes, this broadcast was a monumental step. It took the familiar format of cinema newsreels—short film clips of current events—and brought it to the small screen, offering a glimpse into what broadcast journalism would become. Imagine tuning in and seeing moving images of wartime efforts or political developments for the very first time, right in your home! This pioneering effort, though limited in reach, paved the way for the continuous, visual news cycle we take for granted today, proving that even in black and white, television was destined to change how we consume the world.
