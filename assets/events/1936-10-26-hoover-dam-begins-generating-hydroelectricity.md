While the Hoover Dam itself was dedicated in 1935, its true electrifying purpose came online on October 26, 1936, when its first commercial generator, N-1, officially began producing hydroelectricity. This wasn't just a flip of a switch; it was the culmination of decades of dreams to harness the raw power of the Colorado River. The massive turbines, driven by the colossal head of water, began spinning, sending clean, renewable energy across the Southwest. This power fueled the growth of cities like Los Angeles, Phoenix, and Las Vegas, transforming arid regions into booming metropolitan areas. The dam didn't just manage water; it generated opportunity, powering industries, homes, and an entire economic expansion. It was the moment the "Power Plant of the West" truly earned its title, lighting up a new era for millions.
