Imagine a world where valuable minerals were just… hanging out in rocks, too sparse to be worth the trouble. Enter the froth flotation process, a clever bit of alchemy patented in 1906 by Minerals Separation Ltd. (with a trio of engineers — Sulman, Picard, and Ballot — doing the heavy lifting). Instead of just crushing and hoping, this method involves grinding ore into a fine powder, mixing it with water, oil, and frothing agents, then blowing air through it. The desired minerals cling to the oil, float to the surface in a bubbly foam (hence 'froth'), and are skimmed off. It sounds simple, but it was nothing short of miraculous for the mining industry! Suddenly, mountains of previously uneconomical low-grade ores became profitable, massively increasing the global supply of copper, lead, zinc, and more. This wasn't just a patent; it was a game-changer that sparked a resource boom and literally lifted fortunes from the dust, proving that sometimes, the best way to get something out of a rock is to give it a little bubble bath.
