Before 1921, a diagnosis of Type 1 diabetes was a grim prognosis, often leading to a slow, agonizing death. Then, in a true medical marvel, a team at the University of Toronto – led by Frederick Banting and Charles Best, under the supervision of John Macleod, with vital biochemical help from James Collip – isolated a hormone from the pancreas: insulin. This wasn't just a discovery; it was a revelation! Within months, it was successfully used to treat diabetic patients, literally bringing people back from the brink of death. The isolation of insulin single-handedly transformed diabetes care, turning a fatal disease into a manageable condition and earning Banting and Macleod a Nobel Prize (which they shared with Best and Collip, respectively). A genuine lifesaver!
