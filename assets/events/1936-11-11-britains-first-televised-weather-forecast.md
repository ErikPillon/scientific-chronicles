Ever complained about the weather? Imagine doing it without a helpful map on your screen! On November 11, 1936, the BBC aired the UK's very first televised weather forecast. While today's forecasts are slick, animated affairs, this pioneering broadcast was a charmingly primitive affair. Meteorologist George Cowling, a true trailblazer, stood in front of a simple chalkboard, pointing to hand-drawn weather symbols. It might sound quaint now, but it was a colossal leap in how information, especially about something as universal as the weather, could be shared with a mass audience, setting the stage for every raincloud graphic we now take for granted.
