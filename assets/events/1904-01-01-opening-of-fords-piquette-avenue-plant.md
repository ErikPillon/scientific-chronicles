Every empire starts somewhere, and for Henry Ford's automotive dominion, a significant chapter began with his 'first new factory' – the Piquette Avenue Plant in Detroit, which opened its doors around 1904. After outgrowing his initial small workshop, Ford needed a dedicated space to ramp up production and experiment with his revolutionary ideas. This three-story brick factory, while modest by later Ford standards, was where the magic really started to happen. It was here that many of the early Ford models, including the Models B, C, F, K, N, R, and S, were meticulously crafted. Crucially, Piquette was also the secret lab where Henry Ford and his brilliant team clandestinely designed and developed the world-changing Model T. It wasn't yet the moving assembly line of Highland Park, but Piquette was where the ideas of interchangeable parts and streamlined production were truly honed. This factory wasn't just a building; it was a crucible of innovation, a place where Ford's vision of affordable cars for the masses took tangible form, proving that even monumental industrial revolutions start with a well-placed brick.
