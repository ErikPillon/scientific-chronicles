Remember the days when every minor cut meant a trip to get stitches, followed by the annoying tug and eventual removal? Enter Dermabond, a medical skin adhesive (specifically 2-octyl cyanoacrylate) that gained FDA approval around 1998. Instead of needles and thread, doctors could simply paint on this liquid plastic, which quickly hardened to close minor wounds. It offered a quicker, less painful, and often more cosmetically appealing alternative to traditional suturing, especially for children who weren't exactly thrilled by needles. Dermabond became a staple in emergency rooms and clinics, proving that sometimes, the best solution isn't to tie things up, but to stick them together with a touch of chemical magic.
