For decades, Jacques Cousteau's research vessel, the Calypso, was practically a household name, synonymous with underwater adventure and marine conservation. This former British Royal Navy minesweeper, converted into a floating laboratory, took Cousteau and his team to the deepest, most mysterious parts of the ocean, unveiling its secrets to millions through groundbreaking documentaries. But even legends can meet a sad fate. On January 11, 1996, while docked in Singapore, the Calypso was accidentally rammed and sunk by a barge. Though eventually salvaged, the damage was immense, marking the end of its active career. It was a somber day for ocean enthusiasts, as the ship that once sailed the world for discovery now faced its own battle for preservation, becoming a poignant symbol of both exploration's triumphs and its fragility.
