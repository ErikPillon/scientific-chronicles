In the late 19th century, before homes were awash in Edison's incandescent glow, arc lights were the big thing for illuminating streets and factories. The problem? Dynamos, the generators of the day, were a bit fussy. Enter Charles F. Brush, who in 1876, perfected his self-regulating dynamo. What made it special? It could maintain a constant current even with a varying load of arc lamps, making it incredibly reliable and efficient. This wasn't just a technical tweak; it was the backbone for the rapid adoption of arc lighting systems across major cities like New York and London. Suddenly, public spaces could be brightly lit, boosting safety and commerce after dark. Brush's dynamo was truly a bright idea, pushing the world into the electric age, one powerful spark at a time.
