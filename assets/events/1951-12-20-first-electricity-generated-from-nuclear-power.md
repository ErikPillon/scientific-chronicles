After splitting the atom and blowing things up, smart folks realized, 'Hey, what if we used this energy for something... less explosive?' Enter the Experimental Breeder Reactor I (EBR-I) in Idaho. On December 20, 1951, this pioneering reactor became the first to generate electricity from nuclear fission. It was a tiny amount, just enough to light four light bulbs, but it was a massive leap. It proved that controlled nuclear power wasn't just a weapon, but a potential source of clean, abundant energy. Walter Zinn and his team had basically flipped the atomic light switch for the first time, showing the world that atoms could power homes, not just end wars. The future, for better or worse, just got a whole lot more nuclear.
