Before the assembly line, building a car was a bespoke, slow, and expensive process. Then came Henry Ford, who, though not inventing the concept of the assembly line (Ransom E. Olds had earlier versions), perfected and popularized the *moving* assembly line for the mass production of his Model T in 1913. Imagine parts moving to workers, rather than workers moving to parts! This innovation slashed production time from 12 hours to just 93 minutes per car, making automobiles affordable for the average American. It wasn't just about cars; it revolutionized manufacturing across countless industries, making mass production the norm and changing the face of global commerce forever.
