Named after astronomer George Ellery Hale, who championed the project but died over a decade before its completion, the 200-inch Hale Telescope at Palomar Observatory in California was dedicated on June 3, 1948, after roughly 20 years of design, glass-casting, grinding, and construction delayed further by the Second World War. Its enormous mirror, a single piece of specially formulated Pyrex glass ground to within millionths of an inch of a perfect curve, made it the largest and most powerful optical telescope in the world for the next 45 years. Astronomers used it to measure the true scale and expansion rate of the universe more precisely than ever before, discover quasars, and refine our understanding of galactic structure. Building the biggest telescope on Earth turned out to take nearly as long as Hale's entire working life. 🔭🌌
