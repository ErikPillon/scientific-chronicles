August 12, 1981: a date that shook the world of computing. Before this, personal computers were often seen as hobbyist toys. Then, Big Blue—IBM—dropped its own Personal Computer (the IBM PC model 5150) and changed everything. It wasn't the most innovative machine, famously using an 'open architecture' with parts from other companies (including Microsoft's DOS), but its very existence gave the burgeoning PC market a massive shot of credibility. Suddenly, businesses and serious users took notice, paving the way for the home computer revolution and countless 'IBM PC compatible' clones that defined the next two decades of computing. Talk about making a grand entrance!
