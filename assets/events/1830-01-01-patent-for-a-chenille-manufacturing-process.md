Long before chenille became a go-to for cozy sweaters and bathrobes, its production was a laborious affair. Enter Robert Buchanan, a sharp Scottish weaver from Glasgow. Around 1830, Buchanan patented a groundbreaking method for manufacturing chenille. His innovation involved weaving a 'leno' fabric (a type of open weave), cutting it into strips, and then folding and twisting these strips into those distinctive fuzzy yarns we know as chenille. While it wasn't a single 'machine' in the modern sense, his patented process laid the industrial foundation for mass-producing this luxurious material, making it more accessible. Later, James Templeton would perfect the use of chenille for carpets, transforming the interiors of homes everywhere. Buchanan's work was a pivotal step in mechanizing complex textile production, demonstrating how a clever trick with threads could lead to a whole new industry of fluffy goodness.
