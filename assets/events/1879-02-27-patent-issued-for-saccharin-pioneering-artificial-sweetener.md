The moment a patent is officially 'issued' is a huge deal, transforming a discovery into a protected invention. For saccharin, the very first artificial sweetener, that pivotal moment arrived on February 27, 1879, when Constantin Fahlberg was granted the U.S. patent. This legal stamp of approval solidified Fahlberg's (albeit disputed) claim to the discovery and, more importantly, paved the way for saccharin's commercialization. It signaled the dawn of an era where sweetness could be enjoyed without calories, a concept that would revolutionize diet foods, impact public health, and forever change how industries approached food formulation and consumer tastes.
