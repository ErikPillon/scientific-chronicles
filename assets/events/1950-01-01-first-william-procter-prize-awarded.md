The William Procter Prize for Scientific Achievement is a big deal, awarded by Sigma Xi, The Scientific Research Honor Society. Its first recipient in 1950 was none other than Charles Franklin Kettering, a legendary inventor and head of research at General Motors. The prize honors scientists who have not only made significant research contributions but also demonstrated an ability to communicate their work effectively to a broad audience. So, while Procter was the namesake (and a titan in science himself), Kettering was the trailblazer who first walked away with the award, setting the standard for scientific excellence and accessibility.
