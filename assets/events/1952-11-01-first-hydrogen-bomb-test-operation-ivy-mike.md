On November 1, 1952, on Enewetak Atoll in the Pacific, the United States conducted "Operation Ivy Mike," detonating the world's first hydrogen bomb. This wasn't just a bigger atomic bomb; it was a thermonucelar device, far more powerful, yielding an explosion equivalent to 10.4 megatons of TNT – 700 times more powerful than the Hiroshima bomb. The resulting fireball stretched three miles wide, and the mushroom cloud soared 27 miles high, vaporizing the island of Elugelab. This terrifying demonstration of destructive power dramatically escalated the Cold War arms race, forever changing the global geopolitical landscape and introducing humanity to a new, chilling era of self-annihilation potential.
