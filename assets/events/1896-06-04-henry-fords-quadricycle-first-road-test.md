Imagine the scene: a starry June night in 1896, Detroit, Michigan. Henry Ford, bursting with excitement, hammered through the brick wall of his workshop to get his contraption, the Quadricycle, onto the street. Why the wall? Because the vehicle was wider than the doorframe! This wasn't some grand exhibition; it was a gritty, late-night solo test run. The Quadricycle, essentially a light frame powered by a two-cylinder engine, puttered through the streets at a blistering (for the time) 20 mph. This clandestine test was Ford's first step from theoretical tinkerer to practical pioneer, proving his vision for personal motorized transport wasn't just a pipe dream, but a noisy, smelly reality.
