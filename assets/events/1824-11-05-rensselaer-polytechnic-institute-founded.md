While military academies dabbled in engineering, the true birth of specialized engineering education for the American public happened on November 5, 1824, with the founding of Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) in Troy, New York. Stephen Van Rensselaer, a visionary patroon and politician, established it specifically 'for the application of science to the common purposes of life.' RPI quickly became the go-to place for aspiring civil engineers, setting the standard for hands-on, practical scientific and technical training. It churned out the brilliant minds that built the canals, bridges, and factories of a rapidly industrializing nation, less about military strategy and more about shaping the physical world around us.
