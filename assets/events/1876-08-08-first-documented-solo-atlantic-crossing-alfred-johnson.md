Before 1876, sailing across the Atlantic solo in anything less than a sturdy yacht was practically unheard of. Enter Alfred Johnson, a humble fisherman from Gloucester, Massachusetts, who decided to challenge the impossible. On June 15, 1876, he set sail in his tiny 20-foot dory, named 'Centennial,' embarking on a journey that would forever etch his name in the annals of maritime history. Forty-six days of relentless waves, storms, and solitude later, Johnson reached Abercastle, Wales, on August 8, becoming the first person to successfully complete a solo crossing of the Atlantic Ocean in a small boat. His incredible feat, driven by raw determination and skilled seamanship, shattered perceptions of what was possible and inspired countless future adventurers to test their limits against the vastness of the sea. It was a victory for the underdog and a masterclass in human perseverance.
