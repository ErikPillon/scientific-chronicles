On a quiet Detroit evening, Henry Ford, then a chief engineer at Edison Illuminating Company, fired up his creation: a gasoline engine driving a "Quadricycle." This isn't the sleek machine you imagine; it was essentially two bicycle frames welded together, powered by an ethanol-fueled engine. It vibrated, it clattered, and it ran! This wasn't just an engine; it was the spark that would ignite an automotive revolution, though Ford still had a fair bit of tinkering (and a couple more prototypes) before he'd change the world. Still, on this night, the future of personal transport sputtered into existence.
