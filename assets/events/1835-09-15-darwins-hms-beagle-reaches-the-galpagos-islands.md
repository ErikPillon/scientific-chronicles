On September 15, 1835, HMS Beagle arrived at the remote volcanic Galápagos Islands, roughly 600 miles off the coast of Ecuador. Over the following five weeks, Darwin explored several islands, collecting specimens of the finches, mockingbirds, and giant tortoises he found there, initially without realizing how significant the subtle differences between islands would turn out to be. It was only after returning to England, when ornithologist John Gould examined his bird specimens, that Darwin learned the various finches he'd assumed were unrelated species were actually closely related birds that had adapted differently on each island. Combined with his tortoise and mockingbird observations, this became key evidence in developing his theory of evolution by natural selection, published over two decades later in 'On the Origin of Species.' Five weeks that quietly rewired biology forever. 🐢🦜
