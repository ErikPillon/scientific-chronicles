Imagine a world where every single envelope was folded by hand – slow, laborious, and costly! Enter Warren De La Rue, a true Victorian visionary who wasn't just interested in stationery; he wanted to automate it. On January 1, 1845 (the patent was sealed then), he secured a crucial British patent (No. 10,477) for his envelope folding machine. This wasn't the *very first* attempt, but De La Rue's design was remarkably practical and efficient, moving the industry from tedious manual labor to industrial-scale production. This patent marked a significant step in the mechanization of paper goods, making envelopes cheaper and more widely available, thus quietly fueling the boom in letter writing and global communication. He folded the future into his invention!
