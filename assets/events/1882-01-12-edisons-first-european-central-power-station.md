Before everyone had electricity zapping into their homes, someone had to build the infrastructure. Enter Thomas Edison, who in 1882, fired up the Holborn Viaduct power station in London, marking the very first permanent central power station in Europe. This wasn't just a fancy light switch; it was a massive step towards electrifying cities. Imagine the buzz (pun intended!) as businesses and public spaces got their glow on, ditching gas lamps for Edison's brilliant bulbs. It showcased how a centralized system could power a whole district, laying the groundwork for the modern electrical grids we often take for granted. Edison wasn't just inventing; he was electrifying the future, one city at a time.
