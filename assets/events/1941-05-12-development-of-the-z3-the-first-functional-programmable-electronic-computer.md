While many names pop up when we talk 'early computer,' Konrad Zuse, a German civil engineer, was quietly building wonders in his parents' living room. His Z3, completed on May 12, 1941, wasn't just a calculator; it was the world's first functional, programmable, fully automatic digital computer. Running on electromechanical relays and using binary floating-point numbers, it was a beast of a machine designed to solve complex engineering problems for aircraft design. Sadly, the original Z3 was destroyed in a WWII bombing raid, but its design was decades ahead of its time, laying the conceptual groundwork for the digital age we live in now. Talk about a silent revolution!
