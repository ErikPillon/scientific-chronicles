It's not just peanuts and turbulence you might encounter at 30,000 feet; sometimes, a medical emergency pops up. Recognizing the critical need for immediate response to cardiac arrest, the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a rule, effective April 1, 2001, requiring all large commercial aircraft to carry automated external defibrillators (AEDs). This wasn't some April Fool's joke; it was a serious step to enhance passenger safety. Before this, passengers experiencing sudden cardiac arrest mid-flight had grim odds. With AEDs on board and crew trained to use them, the chances of survival soared, turning routine flights into potential lifesavers.
