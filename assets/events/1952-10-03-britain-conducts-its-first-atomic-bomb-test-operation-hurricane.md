Post-World War II, with the Cold War chilling relations and the US guarding its nuclear secrets, Britain felt a pressing need to develop its own independent atomic deterrent. Operation Hurricane was the code name for this momentous endeavour. On October 3, 1952, a specially constructed frigate, HMS Plym, containing a British-made atomic bomb, was detonated in the Montebello Islands off the coast of Western Australia. The idea was to simulate a ship-borne attack on a British port, proving the bomb's effectiveness and the country's capability. Led by the brilliant physicist William Penney, often dubbed 'the father of the British bomb,' the successful test made Britain the third nation to possess nuclear weapons. It was a clear statement on the global stage: Britain was still a force to be reckoned with, even if it meant a rather big bang in a very remote location. This test forever changed Britain's strategic standing.
