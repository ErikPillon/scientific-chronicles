Imagine a world lit by gas and oil lamps – charming, perhaps, but a fire hazard and a chore. Then, on September 4, 1882, Thomas Edison flipped the switch on his Pearl Street Station in New York City, launching the era of centralized household electricity. Initially powering only a few dozen homes and businesses in lower Manhattan, this wasn't just about lights; it was about transforming daily life. No more smoky lamps, no more carrying coal for heating, eventually paving the way for refrigerators, radios, and all the gadgets we can't live without. It kicked off a monumental engineering race, a 'War of the Currents' between Edison's DC and Westinghouse's AC, ultimately shaping the modern electrified world we take for granted. Life, quite literally, got brighter.
