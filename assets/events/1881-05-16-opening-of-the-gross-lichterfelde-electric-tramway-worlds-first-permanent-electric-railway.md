After wowing crowds with his electric locomotive in 1879, Werner von Siemens doubled down on electrification. On May 16, 1881, he inaugurated the Gross-Lichterfelde tramway near Berlin, marking the world's first *permanent* electric street tram line. This wasn't just a demonstration; it was a regular passenger service, initially operating over 2.5 kilometers. It proved that electric traction was not only feasible but also a superior, quieter, and cleaner alternative to horse-drawn or steam trams. Siemens’ audacious project laid the tracks for urban electric transport systems across the globe, transforming how cities moved and connecting communities in a whole new, electric way.
