Antarctica remained a forbidding frontier long after many other parts of the world had been explored, and aviation was key to truly unlocking it. On October 31, 1956, during the U.S. Navy's Operation Deep Freeze I, a Lockheed R4D Skytrain (a military version of the DC-3), commanded by Rear Admiral George J. Dufek, made the historic first airplane landing at the Geographic South Pole. This wasn't just a joyride; it was a crucial step in establishing the Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station, a permanent research outpost. This incredible feat demonstrated the viability of air support for polar exploration and research, revolutionizing access to the Earth's most remote continent and making scientific endeavors in the extreme south far more feasible.
