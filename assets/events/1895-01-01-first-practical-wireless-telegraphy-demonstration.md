Before your Wi-Fi router, there was Guglielmo Marconi. Building on the theoretical work of James Clerk Maxwell and the experimental proofs of Heinrich Hertz, Marconi took the leap from laboratory curiosity to world-changing technology. Starting around 1895, he successfully sent and received radio signals over increasing distances, famously transmitting signals across the English Channel in 1899 and the Atlantic Ocean in 1901. His relentless practical application of electromagnetic waves meant messages could zip across vast expanses without a single cable, forever shrinking the globe and paving the way for everything from radio broadcasts to cell phones. Suddenly, talking without shouting across the ocean wasn't just a dream, it was a scientific reality!
