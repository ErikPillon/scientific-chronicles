On September 15, 1835, the HMS Beagle dropped anchor in the Galápagos Islands, and little did the crew know, they were about to unleash a scientific revolution. For five intense weeks, Charles Darwin meticulously explored these volcanic outposts, observing their bizarre and fearless inhabitants: giant tortoises, marine iguanas, and those famous finches. What struck him wasn't just the weirdness, but how similar yet distinct the species were from island to island. He noticed how bill shapes varied among finches, perfectly suited to local food sources. This visit wasn't an instant 'eureka!' moment, but the Galápagos observations became a crucial puzzle piece, simmering in his mind until he eventually cooked up the theory of natural selection. It was the ultimate field trip!
