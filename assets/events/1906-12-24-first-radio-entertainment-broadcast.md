Before podcasts and streaming, there was Reginald Fessenden. On Christmas Eve, 1906, from Brant Rock, Massachusetts, Fessenden didn't just transmit dots and dashes; he broadcasted his voice, played a violin, and even aired a phonograph record of Handel's "Largo." Operators on ships off the coast, used to hearing only Morse code, suddenly heard human voices and music – reportedly some thought it was angels! This wasn't just a scientific experiment; it was the world's first public radio broadcast for entertainment, demonstrating that radio could be more than just a telegraph without wires. It transformed radio from a purely military and commercial tool into a potential source of mass entertainment, paving the way for news, dramas, and music to fill homes worldwide. Fessenden didn't just invent a technology; he accidentally invented an industry, one caroling note at a time.
