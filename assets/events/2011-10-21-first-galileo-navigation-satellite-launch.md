Not to be confused with the guy who looked through a telescope! The 'Galileo' in this case refers to Europe's very own global satellite navigation system, designed to be a civilian-controlled alternative to the US's GPS and Russia's GLONASS. On October 21, 2011, the first two operational satellites, 'Thijs' and 'Natalia' (named after kids who won a drawing contest!), blasted off from French Guiana aboard a Soyuz rocket. This launch was a huge step for European independence in satellite technology, promising super-accurate positioning and timing services for everyone from truckers to hikers. It's a testament to international collaboration and a symbol of Europe's commitment to space, proving that getting lost is now an optional activity.
