On March 10, 1876, Alexander Graham Bell wasn't just experimenting; he was making history. While working on his telephone apparatus, he accidentally spilled battery acid on himself. In a moment of urgency (and perhaps pain!), he called out to his assistant in an adjoining room: 'Mr. Watson, come here, I want to see you.' And to his utter amazement, and Watson's, the words crackled distinctly through the receiver! This wasn't just a 'twang'; it was the first successful transmission of intelligible human speech over a telephone. This accidental moment, born from a mishap, proved Bell's invention worked, unlocking an era of instant long-distance communication and forever shrinking the world. It was the original 'Can you hear me now?' moment, but with far higher stakes.
