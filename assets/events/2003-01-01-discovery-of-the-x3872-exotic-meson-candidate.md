For decades, physicists thought mesons were simple pairs of a quark and an antiquark. Then came 2003, and the Belle and CDF collaborations spotted something peculiar: the X(3872). This particle didn't quite fit the standard two-quark picture, earning it the 'exotic' label. Its discovery suggested that mesons could be more complex, perhaps four-quark 'tetraquarks' or even 'molecular' states of two mesons. This tiny particle kicked off a whole new hunting season for 'exotic' matter, pushing the boundaries of quantum chromodynamics and making physicists rethink how quarks can hang out. It's like finding a new kind of LEGO brick you didn't know existed, and suddenly all your models could be way cooler – or way more complicated!
