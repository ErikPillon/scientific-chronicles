Talk about a fixer-upper! Skylab, America's first space station, launched with a few… structural issues. But on June 22, 1973, after 28 days of orbital repairs and groundbreaking scientific work, the intrepid Skylab 2 crew — Charles 'Pete' Conrad Jr., Paul J. Weitz, and Joseph P. Kerwin — safely splashed down. They didn't just conduct experiments; they became cosmic handymen, deploying a makeshift sunshield and freeing a stuck solar panel, saving the station from overheating and making future long-duration missions possible. Their return marked the successful completion of a mission that proved humans could live and work effectively in space for extended periods, paving the way for the International Space Station and beyond.
