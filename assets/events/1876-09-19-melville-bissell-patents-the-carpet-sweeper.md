Before the age of the vacuum, cleaning carpets was a truly dismal affair, often involving brooms and a lot of backache. Enter Melville R. Bissell, a crockery store owner from Michigan, who, in 1876, decided he'd had enough of the dusty mess in his own shop. He put his clever mind to work and invented a contraption with rotating brushes and a dustpan that actually *worked* – the carpet sweeper! He secured a patent for his ingenious device, and soon, homes across America were a little cleaner and lives a little easier. This simple yet revolutionary invention not only transformed domestic chores but also laid the groundwork for future cleaning technology. Thanks, Melville, for saving us from crumb-filled rugs!
