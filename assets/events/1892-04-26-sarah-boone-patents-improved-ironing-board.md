Tired of awkwardly ironing sleeves and the curved seams of women's garments on a clumsy, flat board? Sarah Boone felt your pain! A resourceful dressmaker and inventor, Boone decided there had to be a better way. On April 26, 1892, she secured US Patent No. 473,653 for her brilliantly improved ironing board. Her design was narrower and curved, specifically crafted to make ironing sleeves, bodices, and other fitted clothing much easier and more efficient. Boone's invention wasn't just a minor tweak; it was a significant practical improvement that streamlined a common household chore, saving countless hours and ensuring crisp, well-pressed clothes. This incredible innovation came from a Black woman born enslaved, highlighting the immense ingenuity and determination that thrived despite immense systemic challenges. Her work smoothed out not just fabrics, but also the path for future domestic innovations.
