This isn't just any old telescope; it's the Airy Transit Circle, a beefy instrument designed by Astronomer Royal George Biddell Airy and installed at the Royal Observatory, Greenwich, in 1850. Its job? To precisely measure the exact moment stars crossed the meridian, which meant incredibly accurate timekeeping and mapping the heavens. It quite literally defined the Prime Meridian (0° longitude) – the very line from which all other longitudes are measured, putting Greenwich firmly at the center of the cartographic world. So, every time you glance at a world map or check global time zones, remember this marvel of Victorian engineering that made it all possible. It was retired in 1953, but its legacy as a foundational tool for global navigation and time remains undisputed.
