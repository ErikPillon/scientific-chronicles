In a world where ladies were generally expected to swoon, not extract molars, Lucy Hobbs Taylor was a true trailblazer. After being rejected from dental schools *multiple* times (they weren't exactly lining up to admit women back then), she finally gained acceptance to the Ohio College of Dental Surgery. On February 21, 1866, she wasn't just graduating; she was smashing through a very thick, very sexist ceiling to become the first woman in the U.S. to earn a Doctor of Dental Surgery degree. Imagine the gumption! She had already been practicing for years, effectively self-taught, but this official parchment made it undeniable. Her achievement wasn't just a personal victory; it opened the door for countless women to pursue dentistry, proving that a woman's place was indeed in the dental chair... with a drill in hand, fixing smiles.
