Before George Westinghouse Jr. came along, stopping a train was a clumsy, dangerous affair, often requiring multiple brakemen to manually crank brakes car by car. In 1869, Westinghouse changed the game with his brilliant invention: the railway air brake. This system used compressed air to apply brakes simultaneously on all cars from the locomotive, making braking far more effective, reliable, and safe. It dramatically reduced accidents and allowed trains to travel faster and carry heavier loads. This wasn't just a gadget; it was a foundational safety innovation that propelled the growth of rail transport and solidified Westinghouse's place as an industrial titan.
