While its grand dedication was in 1946, ENIAC actually first flexed its electronic muscles in front of observers on December 10, 1945. This was its initial public demonstration, performing calculations for the Army's Ballistic Research Laboratory. Imagine the scene: a room full of blinky lights and whirring fans, and this colossal machine spitting out answers faster than any human or mechanical calculator ever could. It might not have been fully perfected, but this early operation was proof-of-concept for John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert's vision, a sneak peek at the future where electronics would conquer computation. The digital revolution's pilot light just flickered on!
