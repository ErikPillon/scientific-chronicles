Before 1880, prematurity was often a death sentence. But then, French obstetrician Stéphane Tarnier, inspired by the incubators used for hatching chickens, rolled out a surprisingly simple wooden box designed to keep fragile newborns warm. First demonstrated in Paris, Tarnier's rudimentary incubator, sometimes called a 'couveuse', was a revolutionary step that dramatically improved the survival rates of premature babies. It laid the groundwork for the high-tech, life-saving incubators we know today, proving that sometimes, the simplest ideas have the most profound impact on humanity and offer a vital lifeline for the smallest among us.
