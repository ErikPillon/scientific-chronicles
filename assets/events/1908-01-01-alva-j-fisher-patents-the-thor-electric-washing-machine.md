Before 1908, doing laundry was a grueling, back-breaking chore involving scrub boards and lots of elbow grease. Then came Alva J. Fisher, who reportedly invented the first electric-powered washing machine, known as the 'Thor,' for the Hurley Machine Company of Chicago. Imagine the sighs of relief! This contraption used an electric motor to rotate a galvanized tub, cutting down on manual labor significantly. While hand-cranked machines existed, the 'Thor' brought the power of electricity to the laundry room, freeing up countless hours for homemakers and kickstarting a domestic revolution that promised more leisure time and cleaner clothes with far less effort. It spun tedious chores into modern convenience.
