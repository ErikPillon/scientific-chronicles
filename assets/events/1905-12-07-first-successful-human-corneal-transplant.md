When we talk about 'eye transplants,' we're almost always referring to corneal transplants – the replacement of a damaged cornea (the transparent outer layer of the eye) with a healthy one from a donor. A full 'whole eye' transplant that restores vision remains a distant dream of science fiction, largely due to the immense challenge of reattaching the optic nerve. But the game-changer happened on December 7, 1905, when Austrian ophthalmologist Eduard Zirm performed the world's first truly successful human corneal transplant. His patient, a laborer blinded by lime burns, regained enough sight to return to work. This pioneering surgery wasn't just a technical marvel; it sparked hope and paved the way for modern ophthalmology, turning what was once considered an impossible feat into a routine, sight-saving procedure performed hundreds of thousands of times globally each year. It truly was a moment that brought light back into the lives of many.
