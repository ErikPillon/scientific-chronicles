In the Cold War era, anything that offered a technological edge was a huge deal, especially when it came to power! On January 16, 1959, a truly groundbreaking moment occurred: the U.S. Navy successfully sent a radio message using power from a tiny atomic battery, specifically the SNAP-3 radioisotope thermoelectric generator (RTG). Developed by the Martin Company for the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission, this compact device converted the heat from decaying polonium-210 directly into electricity. This wasn't just a cool gadget; it demonstrated the potential for incredibly long-lasting, reliable power sources for satellites, remote weather stations, and even pacemakers. It was a silent but mighty step towards powering the future, proving that good things really do come in small, radioactive packages!
