The future looked a lot more vibrant on January 1, 1954, when the Rose Bowl Parade became the first coast-to-coast color television broadcast in the United States. While only a handful of lucky (and wealthy) viewers with those super-expensive, early color sets could actually witness the spectacle in its full chromatic glory, it was a massive technological leap. This event, broadcast by NBC, signaled the slow but inevitable march towards a full-color TV experience for everyone. No more black and white limitations for those dazzling floats and marching bands! It truly was a colorful turning point for American broadcasting.
