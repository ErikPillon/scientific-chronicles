In the summer of 1855, London's River Thames was less a river and more a 'fetid ditch' of raw sewage, causing a truly *great stink*. Michael Faraday, usually found pondering electromagnetism, was so appalled by the pollution that he famously wrote to The Times newspaper, describing how he dropped pieces of white paper into the river and watched them vanish from sight within inches. His vivid, disgusted account drew public attention to the crisis, helping to galvanize efforts towards building London's modern sewer system. Faraday proved that even the greatest scientific minds aren't afraid to get their hands (or at least their nose) dirty for public good!
