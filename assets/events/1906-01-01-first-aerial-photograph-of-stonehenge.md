Before drones made aerial photography commonplace, getting a snapshot from above was a serious undertaking. In 1906, Captain B.F.S. Baden-Powell (yes, related to the founder of Scouting!) took to the skies in a balloon and captured the first-ever aerial photograph of Stonehenge. This wasn't just a pretty picture; it was a groundbreaking moment for archaeology. Seeing the ancient monument from above offered a completely new perspective on its layout and relationship to the surrounding landscape, providing insights that ground-level observation simply couldn't. It proved that sometimes, you need to step back (or float up) to really see the bigger picture.
