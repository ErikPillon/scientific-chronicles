Think your QWERTY keyboard is the only game in town? Think again! In the 1930s, psychology professor August Dvorak (and his brother-in-law William Dealey) looked at the typewriter and thought, 'We can do better!' The QWERTY layout, designed to prevent mechanical typewriters from jamming, wasn't exactly optimized for speed or comfort. So, in 1936, they unleashed the Dvorak Simplified Keyboard. This ergonomic marvel placed the most frequently used letters on the home row, allowing for faster typing with less finger movement. While it never fully dethroned QWERTY (habit is a powerful thing!), the Dvorak layout remains a favorite among speed typists and those seeking relief from repetitive strain. It's a testament to the idea that sometimes, even well-entrenched designs can be improved upon – if you're willing to learn a new trick (or fifty-two keys)!
