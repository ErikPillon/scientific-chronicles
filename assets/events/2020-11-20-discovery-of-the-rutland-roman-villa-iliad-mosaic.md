In 2020, archaeologists digging at a Roman villa in Rutland, UK, struck gold – or rather, millions of colorful tesserae! They uncovered an incredibly well-preserved mosaic, measuring an impressive 11 by 7 meters, depicting scenes from Homer's *Iliad*, specifically the duel between Achilles and Hector at the climax of the Trojan War. This wasn't just any old Roman floor art; it was the first mosaic of its kind to be found in Britain, providing a rare glimpse into how wealthy Romans living in Britannia connected with classical mythology. The find was a huge deal, not only for its artistic beauty and scale but also for challenging our understanding of Roman cultural sophistication in the furthest reaches of their empire. It really showed that even in ancient Britain, you could get your epic poem fix in glorious mosaic form.
