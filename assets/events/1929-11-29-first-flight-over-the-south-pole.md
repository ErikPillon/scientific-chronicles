Antarctic exploration was already a big deal, but in 1929, Admiral Richard E. Byrd decided it was time to take to the skies! On November 29, 1929, Byrd, along with pilot Bernt Balchen, co-pilot Harold June, and radio operator Ashley McKinley, embarked on a perilous flight in their Ford Trimotor plane, "Floyd Bennett," from their Little America base. Battling treacherous weather and high altitudes, they successfully navigated over the geographic South Pole, a truly groundbreaking feat of exploration and aviation. They even dropped an American flag from the plane as a symbolic gesture. This daring aerial expedition cemented Byrd's legendary status and demonstrated the incredible potential of aviation for polar exploration, shrinking yet another vast, untamed frontier.
