In July 1957, the USS Norton Sound (AVM-1), a former seaplane tender converted into a guided missile ship, made history by conducting the first U.S. launch of a long-range ballistic missile from a naval vessel. This wasn't just a cool fireworks show; it was a crucial step in the development of America's sea-based strategic deterrence capabilities. Imagine a giant rocket blasting off from a ship in the middle of the ocean! While the specifics of the missile (a Redstone) and the launch were highly classified at the time, this event laid the groundwork for future submarine-launched ballistic missiles, fundamentally changing naval warfare and global power dynamics. It meant the U.S. could project power and deliver a formidable response from virtually anywhere on the planet, a pretty big deal in the early Cold War arms race.
