In an era before cell phones and GPS, how did trains communicate? Thanks to brilliant minds like Granville T. Woods! On October 11, 1887, Woods was granted a patent for his 'Induction Telegraph,' a revolutionary system that allowed for real-time communication between moving trains and train stations. This wasn't just a clever gadget; it dramatically improved railway safety and efficiency, helping to prevent collisions and streamline operations across the burgeoning American rail network. Woods, a prolific African-American inventor, beat out even Thomas Edison in patent disputes over similar technology, cementing his legacy as a true innovator in electrical engineering and telecommunications. This patent was a testament to his ingenuity and impact on modern transportation.
