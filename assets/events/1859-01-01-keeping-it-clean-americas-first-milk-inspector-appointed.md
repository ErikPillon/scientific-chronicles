In the mid-19th century, milk was a wild west of dubious quality. Adulteration with water, chalk, and even plaster of Paris was rampant, leading to widespread illness, especially among children. Enter Boston, a city determined to clean up its act! In 1859, a significant stride in public health was made with the appointment of Henry Clifford as the first milk inspector in the United States. His job was to ensure that the milk sold to the public was actually, well, *milk*. This pioneering role was crucial in establishing standards, combating fraud, and dramatically improving urban health by making a basic staple safe to consume. Clifford's appointment kicked off the regulatory journey that gave us the safe, wholesome dairy products we largely take for granted today. He was literally the original 'milk man' of public safety!
