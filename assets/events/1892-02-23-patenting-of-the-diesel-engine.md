Before electric cars dominated dinner table conversations, there was a different kind of engine revolution. Enter Rudolf Diesel, a German engineer with a brilliant idea: an internal combustion engine that ignites fuel by compressing air to a high temperature, rather than using a spark plug. He filed his first patent application on February 23, 1892, setting the stage for one of the most impactful inventions in industrial history. The diesel engine, efficient and robust, quickly became the workhorse of the world, powering everything from trains and ships to heavy machinery and later, cars. It transformed transportation, agriculture, and industry, proving that sometimes, skipping the spark can lead to some seriously powerful ideas.
