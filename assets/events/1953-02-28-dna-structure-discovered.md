Before 1953, the instruction manual for life was locked away. Then came the revelation: DNA's structure was a double helix, a spiral ladder described by Watson and Crick, heavily informed by Rosalind Franklin's sharp X-ray images. This wasn't just a pretty picture; it was the fundamental architecture explaining how genes work, how traits are passed down, and how life itself perpetuates. It flipped biology on its head, setting the stage for everything from genetic engineering to personalized medicine.
