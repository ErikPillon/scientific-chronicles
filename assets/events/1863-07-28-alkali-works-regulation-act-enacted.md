In the smog-choked, industrial heartlands of Victorian Britain, chemical factories (especially those producing soda ash via the LeBlanc process) were spewing out hydrochloric acid gas, destroying crops, sickening people, and generally making life miserable. Enough was enough! On July 28, 1863, the Alkali Works Regulation Act was passed, becoming the first effective piece of air pollution control legislation in the world. It mandated that 95% of the noxious gas had to be condensed, and it appointed inspectors (like the brilliant chemist Robert Angus Smith) to enforce the rules. This wasn't just about cleaner air; it was a watershed moment that recognized industry's responsibility for its environmental impact and laid the groundwork for modern environmental law. Take that, toxic clouds!
