On March 17, 1921, Dr. Marie Stopes, a bold and controversial pioneer, opened the first birth control clinic in Britain: The Mothers' Clinic for Constructive Birth Control in Holloway, North London. This wasn't just a medical facility; it was a bombshell dropped into conservative society, offering free advice on contraception to married women. Stopes believed that birth control was essential for women's health and societal well-being, challenging deeply ingrained social and religious norms. The clinic quickly became a beacon for thousands seeking control over their family size, but it also faced fierce opposition, igniting a national debate on sex, morality, and women's rights that would echo for decades. Talk about changing the game!
