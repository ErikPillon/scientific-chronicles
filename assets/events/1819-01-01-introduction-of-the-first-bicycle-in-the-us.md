Before Peloton, there was the velocipede! While the 'running machine' was invented in Germany by Karl Drais in 1817, it made its way across the pond soon after. The first reported public exhibition of a velocipede, sometimes called a 'dandy horse,' in the United States was in New York City in 1819. These early bikes had no pedals; riders propelled themselves by pushing their feet on the ground, making for a rather awkward, though innovative, ride. It wasn't exactly practical, but it sparked curiosity and laid the groundwork for the pedal-powered bicycles that would eventually revolutionize personal transportation in America, proving that getting around could be a lot more fun than walking.
