For decades, blocked arteries meant invasive, often risky, open-heart surgery. But in 1977, Swiss physician Andreas Gruentzig changed all that. On September 16, he performed the world's first percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty (PTCA) in Zurich, using a tiny balloon-tipped catheter to unblock a patient's coronary artery. This minimally invasive procedure was a monumental breakthrough, offering a less traumatic and often life-saving alternative to bypass surgery. Gruentzig literally paved the way for millions of people to have their arteries cleared, proving that sometimes, the smallest innovations can have the biggest impact on human health.
