The dream of a national highway system had just received a massive green light with President Eisenhower's signing of the Federal-Aid Highway Act in June 1956. Less than two months later, on August 2, 1956, Missouri made history by awarding the very first contract for a segment of brand-new Interstate construction. The honor went to a stretch of what would become I-70 near St. Charles. It might not sound glamorous – a government contract for roadwork – but this seemingly mundane event marked the official start of one of the largest public works projects in human history. It was the bureaucratic equivalent of firing the starting gun on a marathon that would transform American travel, commerce, and culture. No more bumpy two-lane roads; the future was paved, literally.
