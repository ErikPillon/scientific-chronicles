Imagine a clogged highway in your heart, stopping vital blood flow. Before 1967, options were grim for many with severe coronary artery disease. Enter Dr. René Favaloro, the Argentine surgical maestro at the Cleveland Clinic. On May 9, 1967, he performed the first successful coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery, using a saphenous vein from the patient's leg to create a new path for blood to flow around a blocked coronary artery. It was a revolutionary moment, effectively building a 'detour' around the heart's traffic jams. This pioneering procedure transformed cardiac surgery, saving countless lives and becoming one of the most common and critical operations in modern medicine. Talk about a heart-stopping innovation!
