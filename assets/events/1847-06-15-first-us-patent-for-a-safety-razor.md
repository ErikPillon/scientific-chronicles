Before King C. Gillette made disposable blades famous, someone had to figure out how to shave without looking like you'd wrestled a badger. Enter William S. Henson, who in 1847, secured the first U.S. patent for a "Guard for Razor Blades." This wasn't the sleek, multi-bladed wonder you see today, but rather a clever attachment designed to prevent those nasty nicks and cuts that were a daily hazard with traditional straight razors. Imagine the relief! No more fearing your morning ritual would end in a bloodbath. Henson's innovation was a crucial step towards making shaving a less perilous affair, paving the way for the personal grooming revolution. It showed that even the most mundane daily tasks could be improved with a bit of ingenuity, proving that sometimes, the greatest inventions are the ones that save your face (literally).
