In 1878, just two years after Alexander Graham Bell unveiled his talky-box, the good folks in New Haven, Connecticut, realized something: with more than 50 telephones in town, people needed a way to find each other! Thus, the very first U.S. telephone directory was born. This wasn't a hefty tome like today's phone books (remember those?). It was a single, glorious sheet of cardboard, listing 50 residential and 11 commercial subscribers. No numbers, though – you just told the operator who you wanted to talk to! It was a marvel of organization, transforming the burgeoning telephone network from a chaotic tangle of wires into a slightly more manageable system. This humble sheet was the grandparent of every contact list, search engine, and social network, proving that even revolutionary tech needs a good index to make sense of it all.
