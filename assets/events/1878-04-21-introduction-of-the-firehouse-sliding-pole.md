Before the iconic sliding pole, firefighters often had to race down stairs to get to their apparatus on lower floors – wasting precious seconds. Legend has it that in 1878, at Engine Company 21 in Chicago, Captain David B. Kenyon noticed a smooth wooden pole used to store hay for the horses. An astute firefighter named George Reid, who had a sprained ankle, found it faster to slide down. The idea stuck! It was quickly adopted, radically speeding up response times and becoming an instant symbol of heroism and rapid action. This simple, yet brilliant, innovation became a quintessential part of fire stations worldwide, proving that sometimes the best solutions are the most straightforward. Gravity's never been so useful!
