Before the Wright brothers mastered crewed flight, Samuel Pierpont Langley, a prominent astronomer and secretary of the Smithsonian Institution, was making waves with his "Aerodromes." On May 6, 1896, his steam-powered, uncrewed Aerodrome No. 5 successfully flew over the Potomac River near Quantico, Virginia, covering nearly 3/4 of a mile (about 1,200 meters) in 90 seconds. This wasn't just a lucky toss; it was the first sustained flight of a powered, heavier-than-air machine of significant size. While his later, larger crewed versions famously failed, the success of Aerodrome No. 5 proved that powered flight *was* possible. It was a crucial, if often overlooked, stepping stone in the quest to conquer the skies, proving that even a fancy professor could get something to fly before the bike guys got their turn.
