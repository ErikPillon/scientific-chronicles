Imagine a world where books were painstakingly copied by hand, taking years to produce and costing a fortune. Then, along came Johannes Gutenberg, a German goldsmith with a brilliant idea: movable type. Around 1455, his masterpiece, the Gutenberg Bible, became the first major work printed using this revolutionary technology. This wasn't just about making copies; it was about democratizing knowledge. Suddenly, books could be produced faster, cheaper, and in greater numbers, spreading ideas and literacy like wildfire across Europe. It kickstarted the printing revolution, paving the way for the Renaissance, the Reformation, and ultimately, our modern information age. Without Gutenberg's ingenious press, who knows how long we'd still be waiting for our favorite novels? Thanks, Johannes, for making reading a mass-market phenomenon!
