Before widespread electricity, life was pretty dim. Then, on September 30, 1882, a literal spark of genius in Appleton, Wisconsin, forever changed how we power our world. The Appleton Paper & Pulp Company, with H.F. Rogers and J.F. Merrill leading the charge, inaugurated the world's first commercial hydroelectric power plant, aptly named the 'Vulcan Street Plant.' Using a water wheel to drive a generator, it began supplying electricity to a paper mill and two local buildings, including Rogers' own house. It wasn't the biggest plant, but it demonstrated the immense potential of converting the kinetic energy of flowing water into clean, reliable power. This pioneering effort kicked off the hydroelectric boom, proving that rivers aren't just for sailing – they're for lighting up our lives, too!
