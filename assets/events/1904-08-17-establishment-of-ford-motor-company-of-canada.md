Henry Ford, ever the visionary, understood that the 'horseless carriage' wasn't just an American fad. Barely a year after launching his namesake company in the U.S., he set his sights north, and on August 17, 1904, Ford Motor Company of Canada was officially established. This wasn't just a casual offshoot; it was a shrewd business move that positioned Ford to tap into markets across the British Empire. The initial setup was modest, assembling cars in a small factory in Walkerville, Ontario. But from these humble beginnings, Ford Canada quickly grew, becoming a major player in the country's industrial landscape. It provided jobs, boosted local economies, and, most importantly, started mass-producing affordable vehicles that helped Canadians hit the open road. This establishment wasn't just about making cars; it was about building an industrial presence that would shape Canada's manufacturing identity for generations, all thanks to Henry Ford's relentless drive to put the world on wheels.
