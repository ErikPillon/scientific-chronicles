Before 1970, pollution was pretty much just the cost of doing business, and environmental concerns rarely made headlines. Enter Wisconsin Senator Gaylord Nelson, who, inspired by the anti-war teach-ins, dreamed up a 'national teach-in on the environment.' Enlisting the bright and energetic Denis Hayes to organize it, April 22nd, 1970, saw an astonishing 20 million Americans pour into the streets, parks, and auditoriums across the country. From coast to coast, people rallied, protested, and educated themselves on everything from oil spills to dwindling wildlife. It was a grassroots tsunami that suddenly made 'environmental protection' a household phrase and pushed it onto the political agenda with undeniable force. Earth Day didn't just happen; it roared into existence, kickstarting modern environmentalism and proving that when millions speak up for the planet, people listen.
