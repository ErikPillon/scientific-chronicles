For centuries, Britain proudly clung to its imperial units: pounds, ounces, feet, and miles. But as the world globalized and trade became increasingly international, the pressure to conform to the metric system grew. On May 24, 1965, the then Minister of Technology, Frank Cousins, announced that Britain would officially begin its transition to metric units. This wasn't an overnight flip; it was a gradual, often contentious, process that involved everything from road signs to grocery scales. While industries largely adopted metric, everyday life proved a bit more stubborn. Pints of beer, miles on the road, and the occasional 'stone' for weighing oneself still linger, making Britain a charmingly muddled mix of both systems. It was a massive societal shift, driven by scientific and economic logic, proving that even a nation famous for its traditions can (eventually) measure up to global standards.
