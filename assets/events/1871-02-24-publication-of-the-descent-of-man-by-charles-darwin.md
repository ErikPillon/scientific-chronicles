A dozen years after rocking the world with 'On the Origin of Species,' Charles Darwin finally tackled the big one: us. In 1871, 'The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex' hit bookshelves, directly addressing human evolution and sexual selection—a topic he had famously (and strategically) avoided in his previous work. This book solidified the idea that humans share a common ancestor with other primates, sending fresh shockwaves through society and science. It wasn't just about monkeys and men; it was a deep dive into human characteristics, morality, and the powerful role of sexual selection in shaping our species. Essentially, Darwin completed his grand theory, explaining our place in the natural world with compelling (and controversial) evidence.
