On December 21, 1872, the converted Royal Navy corvette HMS Challenger departed Portsmouth, England, on a nearly four-year scientific expedition covering roughly 68,890 nautical miles across the Atlantic, Pacific, and Antarctic oceans. Led by naturalist Charles Wyville Thomson, the crew systematically measured ocean depths, temperatures, currents, and seafloor composition, and collected thousands of new marine species, including many from the deep sea, previously thought incapable of supporting life at all. The expedition's staggering fifty-volume final report, largely compiled by scientist John Murray after Thomson's death, took nearly two decades to publish and essentially created oceanography as a rigorous scientific discipline from scratch. The mission's name lives on today in NASA's Challenger space shuttle and the Challenger Deep, the deepest known point in the ocean, first measured by the very same voyage. 🚢🌊
