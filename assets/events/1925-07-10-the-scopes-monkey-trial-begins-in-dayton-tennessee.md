On July 10, 1925, substitute teacher John T. Scopes went on trial in Dayton, Tennessee, for violating a new state law banning the teaching of human evolution in public schools. The case was deliberately engineered as a test case by local boosters hoping to draw publicity to their small town, and it succeeded spectacularly, drawing legendary defense attorney Clarence Darrow and three-time presidential candidate William Jennings Bryan, who argued for the prosecution, into a fierce, widely broadcast public showdown over evolution, science, and biblical literalism. Scopes was ultimately found guilty and fined a modest $100 (later overturned on a technicality), but the trial's real impact was cultural: it became a defining early flashpoint in the long-running American debate over how, and whether, evolution should be taught in schools, an argument that in various forms continues a century later. 🐒⚖️
