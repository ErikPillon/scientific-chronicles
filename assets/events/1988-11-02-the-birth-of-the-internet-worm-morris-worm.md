Picture this: it's 1988, the internet (or ARPANET, as it was then) is a cozy club of academics and researchers. Then, *bam*! A mischievous program, later dubbed the Morris Worm, escapes into the wild. Created by Cornell grad student Robert Tappan Morris, it wasn't supposed to be malicious, just a cheeky experiment to gauge the size of the internet. But a tiny coding error (oops!) turned it into a self-replicating digital beast that clogged thousands of computers, slowing them to a crawl and causing an estimated $10-100 million in damages. It was the internet's first wake-up call, a dramatic demonstration of how interconnected — and vulnerable — our digital world was becoming. Morris became the first person prosecuted under the then-new Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. Talk about making history the hard way! The incident spawned the first Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT) and forever changed how we think about network security.
