Imagine a world where TV broadcasts were strictly local. That was the reality until the early 1940s! In 1940, AT&T successfully conducted an experimental intercity television transmission between New York and Philadelphia using their shiny new coaxial cable infrastructure. This wasn't just a technical demo; it was a watershed moment. It proved that television signals could travel long distances without significant degradation, making the dream of national television networks a tangible possibility. This pioneering use of coaxial cable meant that a single broadcast could soon reach multiple cities, fundamentally transforming how Americans would consume news, entertainment, and sports. Get ready for national TV, folks!
