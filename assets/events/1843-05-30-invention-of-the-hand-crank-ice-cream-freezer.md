Before 1843, making ice cream at home was a laborious, arm-aching affair, often involving a bucket of ice and salt and lots of stirring. Then came Nancy Johnson, who, on May 30, 1843, patented her ingenious hand-cranked ice cream freezer. This simple but brilliant device, featuring a removable inner bucket and a crank mechanism, made the process infinitely easier and more efficient. Though she later sold the patent, her design became the foundation for virtually all subsequent home ice cream makers, cementing her place as a true unsung hero of dessert history. Thanks to Nancy, countless summer days have been filled with delicious, homemade scoops!
