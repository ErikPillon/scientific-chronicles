The City of Lights truly earned its nickname in 1878 when Paris began installing Paul Jablochkoff's ingenious "electric candles" (arc lamps) for street lighting, especially around the Place de l'Opéra and other prominent areas. These weren't subtle; they produced an intense, dazzling light, far brighter and more reliable than gas lamps. While earlier experimental electric lights existed, Paris's widespread adoption of Jablochkoff's arc lamps marked a significant milestone, demonstrating the practical application of electricity for public illumination. It literally brightened the urban nightscape, making streets safer and more vibrant, and setting a brilliant precedent for cities worldwide.
