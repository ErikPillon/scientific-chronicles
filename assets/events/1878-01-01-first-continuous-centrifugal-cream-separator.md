Before de Laval stepped in, separating cream from milk was a slow, back-breaking chore using gravity. But in 1878, this Swedish genius unveiled his continuous centrifugal cream separator. Suddenly, dairies could rapidly spin milk, flinging the heavier skim milk outwards and leaving the lighter, fatty cream in the middle. It was a game-changer, making butter and cream production super efficient and hygienic, paving the way for modern dairy farming and ensuring your morning coffee had that perfect creamy touch, all thanks to a bit of clever physics and engineering.
