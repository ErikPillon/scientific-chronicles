While the Duryea brothers were busy making noise with America's first functional gasoline cars, they also made sure to protect their inventive spirit! On February 9, 1897, Charles E. Duryea was granted U.S. Patent 576,827 for his 'Motor-vehicle.' This patent detailed several improvements and designs for his innovative automobile, which had already made headlines by winning America's first car race. Unlike the broader, often litigious patents of the era, Duryea's patent focused on the practical advancements of his specific vehicle. It highlighted the detailed engineering that went into their early automobiles, showing that the brothers weren't just tinkers but serious inventors laying the groundwork for the burgeoning auto industry. It was a tangible testament to their pioneering role, far beyond just being the first across the finish line.
