In a world still reeling from WWII and in the thick of the Cold War, having your own atomic deterrent was the ultimate status symbol. On October 3, 1952, Britain joined the exclusive nuclear club with Operation Hurricane. This test, conducted off the Montebello Islands in Western Australia, involved detonating a plutonium bomb inside the hull of a frigate to assess its effect on ships in a harbour. The blast, equivalent to 25 kilotons of TNT, was a formidable display of scientific might and political resolve, signaling Britain's firm intention to remain a major global player, no matter how much radiation it might involve.
