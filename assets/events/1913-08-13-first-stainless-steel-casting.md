Imagine a world where everything metal eventually rusts into oblivion. Not pretty, right? Enter Harry Brearley, a British metallurgist with a knack for tinkering. In 1913, while trying to find a corrosion-resistant metal for gun barrels, he accidentally cooked up what we now know as stainless steel. His crucible yielded a sample that stubbornly refused to rust, even when faced with acid. On August 13, 1913, the first commercial casting was made in Sheffield, England, at the Firth Brown steelworks. This wasn't just a shiny new alloy; it was a revolution for everything from surgical instruments to skyscrapers. Thanks to Brearley's happy accident, our world is a whole lot more durable, hygienic, and, well, stainless.
