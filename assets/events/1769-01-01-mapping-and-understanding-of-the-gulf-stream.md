The Gulf Stream isn't just a warm current; it's a colossal oceanic conveyor belt that hugely influences global climate and shipping. While mariners knew something was 'up' with currents, it was Benjamin Franklin who, with the help of his cousin Timothy Folger (a Nantucket whaling captain), truly mapped and understood its significance. In the late 1760s, Franklin, then Postmaster General, wondered why mail ships took so long to cross the Atlantic compared to merchant ships. Folger explained the powerful, swift current flowing from the Gulf of Mexico up the East Coast and across to Europe. Franklin published the first accurate map of the Gulf Stream around 1769-1770, advising captains to use its flow for faster eastward journeys and avoid it when traveling west. This wasn't just a map; it was a fundamental discovery in oceanography, revealing a hidden 'river in the ocean' that shaped trade, travel, and even weather patterns for centuries to come.
