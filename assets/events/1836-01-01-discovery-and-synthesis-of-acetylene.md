Acetylene, that wonderfully reactive gas, has a bit of a split personality when it comes to its discovery. It was first accidentally produced in 1836 by Edmund Davy, a cousin of Humphry Davy, while trying to isolate potassium metal. He named it 'bi-carburet of hydrogen.' Fast forward to 1859, and French chemist Marcelin Berthelot independently synthesized it from carbon and hydrogen at high temperatures and, crucially, gave it its enduring name: 'acétylène.' Berthelot also explored its chemical properties, realizing its potential. This highly combustible gas became famous for acetylene lamps, providing brilliant illumination before the widespread adoption of electricity, and later as a key component in oxy-acetylene torches for welding and cutting metal. A true workhorse molecule!
