Imagine trying to manually sort and count mountains of data, like an entire country's census! It used to take years. Enter Herman Hollerith, who in the late 1880s, revolutionized the process with his brilliant electromechanical tabulating machine. Using punch cards (the OG data storage!), this ingenious device could 'read' holes with electrical contacts, tallying vast amounts of information in a fraction of the time. First deployed for the 1890 U.S. Census, it slashed processing time from seven years to just two and a half. Hollerith's system wasn't just a statistical marvel; it laid the fundamental groundwork for modern data processing, inspiring future computing pioneers and eventually leading to the formation of IBM. It was the original 'big data' solution, long before 'big data' was even a twinkle in anyone's eye.
