Imagine a world where a diabetes diagnosis was a death sentence. Not a 'manageable chronic condition,' but a guaranteed, often agonizing, end. That was life before 1922. Then came the 'eureka!' moment (or rather, the sustained, grubby lab work) of Banting and Best, under Macleod's supervision, with Collip purifying the magic potion. On January 11, 1922, a 14-year-old boy named Leonard Thompson, wasting away from Type 1 diabetes, received the first successful insulin injection. He was literally brought back from the brink. The announcement wasn't just a medical paper; it was a global sigh of relief and a promise of life to millions. Suddenly, diabetes wasn't a death knell, but a manageable condition, all thanks to a pancreatic extract that regulated blood sugar. Talk about a game-changer!
