Who needs roads when you have water? On January 1, 1914, the St. Petersburg-Tampa Airboat Line made history by launching the world's (and therefore U.S.'s) first scheduled commercial airline service. Piloted by the fearless Tony Jannus, a Benoist XIV flying boat carried its first passenger, former St. Petersburg mayor Abram C. Pheil, across Tampa Bay in a mere 23 minutes – a journey that would take hours by boat or train. This wasn't just a scenic ride; it was a groundbreaking moment for aviation, proving that air travel could be a viable, time-saving form of commercial transportation. Though it lasted only a few months, it proved the potential of 'flying taxi' services and ushered in an exciting new era for both travel and commerce.
