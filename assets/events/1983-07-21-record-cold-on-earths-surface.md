On July 21, 1983, scientists at the Soviet Vostok Station in Antarctica bundled up (or rather, stayed inside!) as they recorded the coldest natural temperature ever measured on Earth's surface: a staggering -89.2°C (-128.6°F). Tucked away on the high, frigid East Antarctic ice sheet, Vostok is basically a natural freezer. This wasn't just a chilly day; it was a landmark in climatology, reminding us just how extreme our planet can get. It still stands as the official ground-level record, proving that some places truly are colder than your ex's heart.
