Imagine a world before widespread electricity, where bright, clean light was a luxury. Enter calcium carbide! While calcium carbide itself wasn't 'invented' in a single flash, its industrial production became feasible in the early 1890s thanks to breakthroughs by chemists like Henri Moissan and engineers like Thomas Willson. They perfected using electric arc furnaces to react lime and coke, producing this amazing compound. Why was it a big deal? Drop calcium carbide in water, and you get acetylene gas – a brilliant, intensely bright fuel that lit up mines, bicycles, and even early automobiles. It was also a crucial ingredient for making plastics and other chemicals, kickstarting a whole new era of industrial possibility.
