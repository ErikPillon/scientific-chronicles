Before July 1, 1941, television was largely a novelty, a scientific marvel tinkering in labs and making sporadic, experimental appearances. But on that pivotal day, the world tuned into something entirely new: commercial television! In New York City, NBC's WNBT (now WNBC) and CBS's WCBW (now WCBS-TV) began transmitting regular, sponsored programming. WNBT famously broadcast the first-ever TV commercial, a delightful little ad for Bulova watches, showing a watch face over a map of the United States, ticking away for a grand total of $9.00 for 10 seconds. Imagine that! This wasn't just about selling watches; it was about opening the floodgates for an entirely new industry. Suddenly, television wasn't just a curiosity; it was a powerful medium for communication, entertainment, and, yes, commerce. While World War II soon put a temporary damper on its expansion, the seed was planted. Commercial television transformed living rooms into mini-cinemas and news hubs, fundamentally changing how societies entertained, informed, and sold to themselves. From that humble Bulova ad, an entire global media empire would eventually blossom. Who knew a tiny ticking watch could spark such a revolution?
