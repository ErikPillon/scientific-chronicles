Think the folding chair is a modern marvel? Think again! This ingenious piece of furniture has been collapsing and expanding through history for millennia. We've seen evidence of folding chairs in ancient Egypt (fancy enough for pharaohs!), Rome, and medieval Europe – they were the ultimate portable power seat for military commanders and VIPs. Fast forward to more recent times, and the folding chair got a serious upgrade for the masses. While various forms existed, the concept truly hit its stride with industrialization. In the U.S., a significant patent for a 'folding chair' was granted to John D. Johnson in 1855, marking a step towards mass-produced, more familiar designs. These weren't just convenient; they were democratic, making extra seating accessible for picnics, gatherings, and impromptu parties. From ancient battlefields to backyard barbecues, the folding chair has consistently delivered on its promise: a seat when you need it, and out of sight when you don't. It's a design classic that proves good ideas, much like a well-designed chair, can endure for ages.
