For over half a century, the Arecibo Observatory's massive 305-meter dish, nestled in the Puerto Rican hills, was a titan of radio astronomy. It peered into the cosmos, discovered the first binary pulsar, and even sent messages to potential alien neighbors! But time, weather, and structural fatigue caught up. After two major cable breaks in late 2020, the entire 900-ton receiver platform tragically crashed into the dish below on December 1st, 2020, bringing an end to an era. It was a heartbreaking moment for scientists and space enthusiasts worldwide, like losing an old, wise friend who constantly brought new, exciting stories from the universe.
