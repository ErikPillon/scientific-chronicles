Who knew the Sun was such a hungry giant? While it might seem like a dramatic movie plot, comets actually do strike the Sun, and these incredible 'sungrazer' events are a goldmine for astronomers. Though such collisions happen regularly, it was with the launch of the Solar and Heliospheric Observatory (SOHO) in 1995 that humanity got a front-row seat to these cosmic suicide dives. SOHO, a joint ESA/NASA mission, recorded its first sungrazer in October 1996, and since then, it has spotted thousands! These aren't just pretty light shows; as a comet gets too close to our star, it's torn apart by intense gravity and vaporized by the searing heat. The dust and gas released briefly become part of the Sun's atmosphere, giving scientists a fleeting chance to study the comet's composition and the solar environment in ways that would otherwise be impossible. It’s like the universe's most extreme science experiment, with our own Sun playing both the destroyer and the ultimate laboratory. Talk about going out with a bang!
