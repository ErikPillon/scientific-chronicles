For decades, television was a monochrome affair. But behind the scenes, brilliant engineers were battling it out to bring color to the small screen. While various systems were developed, it was the National Television System Committee (NTSC) standard, adopted by the FCC, that officially launched commercial color TV in the United States. On December 17, 1953, the first NTSC compatible color broadcast hit the airwaves, a truly momentous occasion. Though early color sets were pricey and programming limited, this marked the beginning of a vibrant new era for entertainment and information. It wasn't just a technical upgrade; it was a cultural revolution, turning living rooms into colorful windows on the world and paving the way for the dazzling screens we enjoy today.
