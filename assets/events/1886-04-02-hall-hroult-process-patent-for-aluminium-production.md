Imagine two young inventors, thousands of miles apart, simultaneously cracking one of the biggest industrial challenges of their time! That's exactly what happened with Charles Martin Hall in the US and Paul Héroult in France in 1886. Both, independently, discovered the electrolytic process to extract aluminium cheaply from its ore, bauxite, using molten cryolite. Hall filed his US patent on April 2, 1886, with Héroult following shortly after with a French patent. Before this, aluminium was more expensive than silver! Their separate patents revolutionized metallurgy and industry. Suddenly, airplanes, cooking foil, and even mundane things like window frames could be made from this light, strong, and now affordable metal. It's a classic tale of parallel discovery, proving great minds often think alike, especially when there's a huge problem to solve.
