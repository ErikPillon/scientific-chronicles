For decades, a definitive diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease could only happen after death, through brain autopsy. That’s a bit late for intervention, isn't it? The quest for a reliable 'Alzheimer's disease test' has been a scientific marathon. While early diagnosis relied heavily on clinical assessments, the real game-changers started appearing in the 21st century with the development of biomarkers. Think cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) tests that detect amyloid and tau proteins, or PET scans that visualize amyloid plaques in the living brain. Amyloid PET imaging, for instance, became more widely accepted and used in the mid-2000s. More recently, blood tests are emerging, promising simpler, less invasive screening. These tests are crucial, not just for confirming diagnosis, but for enabling earlier treatment and research into prevention, offering a much-needed glimmer of hope in the battle against this devastating disease.
