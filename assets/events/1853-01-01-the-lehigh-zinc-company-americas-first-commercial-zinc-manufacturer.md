Before 'zinc' was just a supplement in your medicine cabinet, it was a crucial industrial metal, vital for everything from galvanizing steel to creating brass alloys. While Europe had been smelting zinc for centuries, the United States was a late bloomer. That all changed in 1853, when the Lehigh Zinc Company (which later became part of the storied New Jersey Zinc Company) kicked off commercial production in Friedensville, Pennsylvania. This wasn't an easy feat; early zinc smelting was a notoriously tricky business, requiring high temperatures and specialized techniques to extract the metal from its ore. Lehigh Zinc's success marked a turning point, establishing a domestic source for this essential metal and reducing America's reliance on imports. It spurred industrial growth, enabled new manufacturing processes, and showcased American ingenuity in mastering complex metallurgical challenges. Who knew something so common had such a pioneering past?
