On November 15, 2004, the European Space Agency (ESA) officially joined the lunar club when its SMART-1 probe successfully entered orbit around the Moon. Launched in 2003, this compact spacecraft wasn't just a trip for sightseeing; it was a tech demonstrator, primarily testing an innovative solar-electric (ion) propulsion system and miniaturized scientific instruments. SMART-1 meticulously mapped the lunar surface, searched for water ice, and provided valuable data before its planned impact in 2006. It was a clever, efficient way for Europe to show off its space chops and quietly explore Earth's celestial neighbor.
