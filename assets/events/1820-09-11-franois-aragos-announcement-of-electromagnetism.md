In 1820, the scientific world was buzzing, and much of that buzz was about Hans Christian Ørsted's incredible discovery: electricity could produce magnetism! On September 11, 1820, French physicist and astronomer François Arago brought this electrifying news to the French Academy of Sciences. He didn't just present Ørsted's findings; he also immediately began his own experiments, demonstrating the magnetization of iron and steel by an electric current. Arago's energetic presentation and swift follow-up experiments quickly spread the word about electromagnetism across France and beyond, spurring other scientists like Ampère to dive into the field. This moment was crucial, as it linked two previously distinct forces, opening up a whole new realm of physics and eventually leading to technologies we can't live without, like motors and generators. Magnets and electricity, living in harmony!
