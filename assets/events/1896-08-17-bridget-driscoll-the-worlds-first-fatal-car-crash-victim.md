On August 17, 1896, in Crystal Palace, London, a grim milestone was reached. Bridget Driscoll, a 44-year-old mother of two, was crossing the grounds when she was struck by an Anglo-French Motor Carriage, becoming the world's first pedestrian to die in a petrol-fueled car accident. The car, driven by Arthur Edsell, an employee of the Anglo-French Motor Car Company, was reportedly traveling at just 4 mph. At the inquest, the coroner famously declared, "This must never happen again." Sadly, it did, many times over, highlighting the nascent dangers of a revolutionary new technology and prompting early discussions about road safety.
