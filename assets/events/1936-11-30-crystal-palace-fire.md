The Crystal Palace, originally built for the Great Exhibition of 1851 in Hyde Park and later relocated to Sydenham Hill, was a magnificent marvel of glass and cast iron, embodying Victorian ingenuity. For decades, it hosted countless events and exhibitions, dazzling millions. But on the evening of November 30, 1936, a small office fire rapidly escalated, consuming the entire structure in a spectacular and devastating inferno. Thousands watched as the iconic landmark burned, transforming from a glittering symbol of progress into a smoldering ruin. A true architectural tragedy, its loss was felt deeply, marking the end of an era.
