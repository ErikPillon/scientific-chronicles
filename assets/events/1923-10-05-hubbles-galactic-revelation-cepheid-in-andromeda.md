Imagine thinking our galaxy was the *entire* universe. That was the prevailing view until Edwin Hubble aimed his telescope at the 'Andromeda Nebula.' On October 5, 1923, he captured a photographic plate that, after careful analysis, revealed a fuzzy blob to be a variable star—a Cepheid! Using Henrietta Leavitt's period-luminosity relationship, Hubble calculated its distance, realizing Andromeda wasn't a nebula *within* our Milky Way, but an entirely separate galaxy millions of light-years away. Boom! The universe instantly became a much grander, more populous place, and our cosmic perspective totally shifted.
