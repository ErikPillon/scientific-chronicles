Imagine getting the absolute first permission slip for your genius idea from the U.S. government! On July 31, 1790, Samuel Hopkins of Pittsford, Vermont, became that lucky guy, receiving the first U.S. patent for a new method of making potash, an essential ingredient for fertilizer and soap. Signed by President George Washington, Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson, and Attorney General Edmund Randolph, this wasn't just a piece of paper; it was a foundational moment for American innovation. It signaled that the young nation valued invention and would protect intellectual property, encouraging a flood of new ideas that would shape its future. From potash to smartphones, it all started with Hopkins's humble innovation.
