Before reinforced concrete became the go-to, engineers like Thomas W. Kennard were doing incredible things with cast iron. Case in point: the Crumlin Viaduct in South Wales, completed in 1857. At 200 feet high and over 600 yards long, it was a breathtaking structure, holding the title of the world's highest railway viaduct for decades. It wasn't just tall; its lattice-girder design and use of prefabricated iron sections were groundbreaking, making it a relatively quick build and a testament to industrial innovation. It basically allowed trains to soar over a deep valley, connecting coalfields and ports, and reminding everyone that Victorian engineers were absolute rockstars, turning heavy metal into elegant, functional art. Sadly, it was dismantled in 1967, but its legend lives on!
