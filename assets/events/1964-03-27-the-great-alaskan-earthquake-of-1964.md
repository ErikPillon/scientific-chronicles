On Good Friday, March 27, 1964, Alaska was hit by the second-strongest earthquake ever recorded – a colossal 9.2 magnitude temblor that shook the ground for over four minutes! This wasn't just a big shake; it was a devastating event that triggered tsunamis across the Pacific, landslides, and widespread destruction, claiming 139 lives. But beyond the tragedy, it was a seismic super-study. Geologist George Plafker's meticulous post-quake research on the ground deformation provided compelling evidence for the theory of plate tectonics, specifically demonstrating how subduction zones cause these massive 'megathrust' earthquakes. The 1964 Alaska earthquake wasn't just a historical disaster; it was a foundational moment in our understanding of Earth's powerful, shifting crust, forever changing how we view seismic hazards and the very mechanics of our planet.
