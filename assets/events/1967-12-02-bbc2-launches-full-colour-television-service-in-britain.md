After years of black-and-white bliss (or blandness, depending on your view), British television got a dazzling upgrade. On December 2, 1967, BBC2 officially launched its full colour television service, making the UK a global pioneer in colour broadcasting. While experimental colour had been around for a bit, this was the real deal – a complete service for viewers with colour sets. Suddenly, snooker balls were green, newsreaders' ties popped, and nature documentaries truly came alive. This wasn't just a visual treat; it was a massive technological leap, involving complex encoding and transmission systems. It quickly set a new standard for quality and entertainment, enticing viewers to upgrade their sets and forever changing the way they experienced their favourite programmes. Colour TV transformed the viewing experience from 'pretty good' to 'oh wow!', ensuring that British screens would never be dull again.
