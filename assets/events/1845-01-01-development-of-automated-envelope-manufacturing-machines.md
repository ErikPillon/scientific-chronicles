While the idea of a machine to fold envelopes might seem quaint now, in the 19th century, it was pure industrial magic! As postal services grew, the demand for envelopes exploded, making manual production a slow, costly bottleneck. Inventors like Warren De La Rue in the 1840s (who secured a notable patent in 1845) and later George Henry Reay, spearheaded the development of machines that could automatically cut, fold, and gum envelopes with remarkable speed and precision. These 'envelope machines' weren't just about folding; they were about creating an entire automated manufacturing process. This innovation drastically reduced the cost and increased the availability of envelopes, quietly fueling the communications boom and shaping the modern postal system. No more licking thousands of envelopes by hand!
