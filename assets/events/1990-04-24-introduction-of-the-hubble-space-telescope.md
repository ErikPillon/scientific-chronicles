After decades of planning and engineering, the Hubble Space Telescope finally launched aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery in April 1990. Named after astronomer Edwin Hubble, this orbiting observatory was designed to provide crystal-clear images of the universe, unhindered by Earth's atmosphere. It was a massive undertaking, with contributions from both NASA and ESA. While its initial blurry vision (due to a mirror flaw) caused some cosmic heartburn, subsequent repairs transformed it into arguably the most iconic and scientifically productive instrument in astronomical history. It has since delivered breathtaking images and profound discoveries, changing our understanding of everything from planetary nebulae to dark energy.
