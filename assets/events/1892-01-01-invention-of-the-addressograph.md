In the era before spreadsheets and email lists, imagine having to handwrite every address on every piece of mail for a large business. Joseph S. Duncan had had enough! In 1892, he invented the Addressograph, a revolutionary device designed to automate the repetitive task of addressing envelopes, statements, and other documents. The machine used embossed metal plates, each containing an address, which could be fed through the device to imprint the information onto paper. It was an enormous time-saver for businesses, banks, and government agencies, drastically improving efficiency in mailrooms and accounting departments. The Addressograph company grew rapidly, becoming synonymous with automated addressing and a crucial predecessor to modern data processing and office automation. It truly streamlined communication for the nascent information age.
