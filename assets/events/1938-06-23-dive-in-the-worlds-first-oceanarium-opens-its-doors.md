Before 'Finding Nemo' or even Jacques Cousteau, how did folks get up close with marine life? In 1938, Marineland of Florida, near St. Augustine, made a huge splash by opening as the world's first oceanarium. Conceived by a visionary group including W. Douglas Burden, Cornelius Vanderbilt Whitney, and Ilia Tolstoy, it wasn't just a glorified fish tank. Marineland was designed as an underwater movie studio (dubbed 'Marine Studios') for filming sea creatures in a natural-ish environment, with public viewing as a secondary, albeit popular, feature. It housed thousands of specimens, from sharks to porpoises, in huge concrete tanks, pioneering marine animal husbandry and inspiring generations to care about the oceans. It was basically a giant, living science lesson, with a few dolphin shows thrown in for good measure!
