Before 1796, if you wanted to print anything in America, you were largely dependent on typefaces imported from Europe. This wasn't ideal for a young nation keen on its independence, especially when it came to spreading ideas. Enter Archibald Binny and James Ronaldson, who, in 1796, founded Binny & Ronaldson in Philadelphia—the first permanent type foundry in the United States. Imagine going from begging for letters to casting your own! This wasn't just a business; it was a crucial step towards intellectual self-sufficiency for the new republic. Their foundry quickly became the leading supplier of printing types, enabling newspapers, books, and pamphlets to be produced domestically with a consistent and high-quality appearance. They literally shaped the words that shaped the nation, proving that sometimes, the smallest details (like individual letters) can have the biggest impact.
