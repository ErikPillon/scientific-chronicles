Imagine you've just helped create the most destructive weapon known to humankind. What do you do next? If you're the brilliant minds behind the Manhattan Project, you start a magazine to warn everyone! Born in the wake of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945, the 'Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists' was initially a vehicle for physicists to convey their profound concern about nuclear warfare and advocate for international control of atomic energy. Co-founded by Eugene Rabinowitch and Hyman Goldsmith, it quickly became a vital, independent voice on science and global security. Most famously, it introduced the iconic 'Doomsday Clock' in 1947, a stark visual metaphor for humanity's proximity to self-destruction. Today, it still ticks away, keeping an eye not just on nukes, but on climate change and other tech-driven threats. It's science with a conscience, folks!
