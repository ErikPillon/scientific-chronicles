Imagine being given a death sentence with no hope, then suddenly, a miracle drug appears. That's precisely what happened on January 11, 1922, when 14-year-old Leonard Thompson, wasting away from Type 1 diabetes, received the first injection of insulin. Developed by Frederick Banting and Charles Best, and refined by James Collip and John Macleod, this pancreatic extract was nothing short of revolutionary. Before insulin, a diabetes diagnosis meant a slow, inevitable decline. But after that first shot, Thompson's condition dramatically improved, literally bringing him back from the brink. This wasn't just a treatment; it was a resurrection, transforming diabetes from a fatal disease into a manageable chronic condition and earning Banting and Macleod a Nobel Prize. A medical triumph that continues to save lives every single day.
