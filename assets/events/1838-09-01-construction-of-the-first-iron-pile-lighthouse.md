Building lighthouses on shifting sands, muddy seabeds, or in deep, exposed waters was a monumental engineering challenge for centuries. Traditional stone lighthouses were heavy, costly, and often impractical for such locations. Enter Alexander Mitchell, an Irish engineer, who patented the 'screw-pile' foundation in 1833. His innovative design involved screwing giant iron piles directly into the seabed, providing a stable, resilient base for the lighthouse structure above. The first screw-pile lighthouse in the U.S. was completed in 1838 at Port Clarence, Virginia. These 'spider-legged' lighthouses quickly became popular, especially in the shallow, soft-bottomed waters of the American East Coast and Gulf of Mexico. They were cheaper, faster to build, and more resistant to storms and ice than earlier designs, dramatically improving navigation and saving countless lives at sea. A truly elegant solution to a very tricky problem!
