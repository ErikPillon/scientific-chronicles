Back in the mid-19th century, getting a top-notch education, especially in technical fields, was largely a privilege of the wealthy. Enter Peter Cooper, an inventor, industrialist, and all-around visionary. In 1859, he made history by securing the Cooper Union Charter, which established the Cooper Union for the Advancement of Science and Art in New York City. The truly revolutionary part? It offered *free* education to everyone – men and women, Black and white, rich and poor. This wasn't just a school; it was a radical social experiment built on the belief that talent and ambition shouldn't be limited by one's bank account or background. From its iconic Great Hall, which hosted Abraham Lincoln's famous "Right Makes Might" speech, to its ongoing legacy of producing brilliant engineers, artists, and architects, Cooper Union embodied the democratic spirit of American innovation. It was a bold step towards accessible education and a testament to Cooper's belief in human potential.
