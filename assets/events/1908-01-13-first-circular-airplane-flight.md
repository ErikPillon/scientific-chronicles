On January 13, 1908, a French-British aviator named Henri Farman took to the skies over Issy-les-Moulineaux, France, for a groundbreaking flight. Piloting his Voisin-Farman I biplane, Farman didn't just fly; he completed a full one-kilometer circle, landing precisely where he started. This wasn't just a fancy maneuver; it was a monumental achievement, demonstrating true aircraft control and stability. It earned him the prestigious Deutsch de la Meurthe-Archdeacon Prize and showed the world that aviation wasn't just a series of hops, but a viable mode of controlled travel, capable of navigating the wild blue yonder with precision.
