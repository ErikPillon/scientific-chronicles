In 1874, the American Midwest wasn't just struggling with settlers; it was battling a biblical-scale invasion of Rocky Mountain locusts. Imagine skies so dark with insects they blocked out the sun, and swarms so dense they literally stopped trains and piled feet deep on the ground! These hungry hordes devoured everything in their path – crops, clothes, even wooden tool handles. This wasn't just a nuisance; it was an ecological disaster that devastated livelihoods and forced many families to abandon their homesteads. The plague was so massive that the locust species itself went extinct by the early 20th century, a mysterious demise for one of history's most destructive pests.
