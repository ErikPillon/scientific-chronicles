Imagine a world where the only fire alarm was someone shouting really, really loudly. Not ideal, right? Enter William F. Channing, a Boston physician, and Moses G. Farmer, an electrical inventor. Together, they revolutionized fire safety by patenting the first municipal electric fire alarm telegraph system in 1852. This groundbreaking invention allowed firefighters to be alerted swiftly and precisely to the location of a blaze, turning a chaotic shout into an organized, rapid response. It was a game-changer for urban planning and public safety, making cities a lot safer by giving fire departments a technological edge against destructive fires. No more 'surprise!' infernos!
