When the Great Fire of London ravaged the city in 1666, it was an absolute catastrophe, but for Robert Hooke, it was also an unexpected opportunity to flex his lesser-known muscles. Appointed as one of the three City Surveyors (alongside his sometimes-rival, Christopher Wren), Hooke played a monumental role in the monumental task of rebuilding London. He wasn't just drawing maps; he was involved in setting out new streets, arbitrating disputes over property lines, and ensuring buildings were constructed according to safer, more efficient designs. This wasn't some sideline; it was a decade-long commitment that consumed much of his time and showcased his immense practical skills in architecture and civil engineering. It’s a testament to his incredibly diverse talents that the man who gave us "cell" and Hooke's Law was also instrumental in literally laying the groundwork for the modern city of London. What a polymath!
