Before touchscreens and trackpads, there was a humble wooden box with wheels, and it changed everything! In 1968, computing visionary Douglas Engelbart debuted his "X-Y Position Indicator for a Display System" – you know it as the computer mouse – during his legendary "Mother of All Demos." This wasn't just a gadget reveal; it was an audacious vision of interactive computing. Engelbart showcased a system that included hypertext, video conferencing, and, yes, a device to point and click on screen. The mouse, initially crafted from wood and featuring two perpendicular wheels, made interacting with a computer intuitive and direct, freeing users from the tyranny of purely command-line interfaces. It took a while to catch on, but once Xerox PARC and then Apple picked it up, the mouse became indispensable. It forever transformed how we navigate digital worlds, proving that sometimes, the simplest tools create the biggest revolutions.
