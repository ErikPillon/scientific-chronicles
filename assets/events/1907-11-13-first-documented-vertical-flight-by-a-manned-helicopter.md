While the Wright Brothers were busy conquering horizontal flight, French engineer Paul Cornu was dreaming vertically. On November 13, 1907, just outside Lisieux, France, Cornu made history by successfully lifting off the ground in his twin-rotor helicopter. It only hovered a foot or so for about 20 seconds, and it wasn't exactly stable, but it proved that vertical takeoff was possible. It was a clumsy, noisy contraption powered by a 24-horsepower engine, but it planted the seed for all future helicopters, showing the world that you didn't always need a runway to get airborne. From then on, the sky was literally the limit in more ways than one!
