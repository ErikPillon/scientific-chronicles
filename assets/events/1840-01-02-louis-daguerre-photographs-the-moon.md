In the early days of photography, capturing anything beyond a still-life or a patient portrait was a monumental task. But Louis Daguerre, not content with merely inventing the daguerreotype, turned his lens to the heavens. On January 2, 1840, he managed to capture an image of the moon. While his initial attempt was unfortunately lost in a fire, and the details weren't exactly high-definition, it was a monumental first step. It proved photography could reach beyond Earth, paving the way for astronomical imaging that would later reveal the universe's most mind-boggling secrets.
