Before Netflix binges and TikTok dances, there was the thrilling dawn of regular television broadcasts in the U.S.! While experimental flickers had graced screens for years, the real show kicked off on April 30, 1939, when NBC, powered by RCA, launched its regularly scheduled programming from atop the Empire State Building. The grand debut? None other than President Franklin D. Roosevelt opening the New York World's Fair, making him the first U.S. President ever to be televised. Imagine the awe (and maybe a bit of confusion) of folks gathered around grainy black-and-white sets, witnessing history being beamed into their living rooms. This wasn't just a technical marvel; it was the birth of a new era in entertainment and information, a tiny window to the world that would eventually grow into the pervasive force we know today. Forget your 4K HDR; these pioneers were just happy to see *anything* move on screen!
