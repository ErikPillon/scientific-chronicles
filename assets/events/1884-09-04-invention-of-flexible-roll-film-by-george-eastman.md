Before George Eastman came along, photography was a cumbersome affair, involving heavy glass plates and tricky chemical processes. Taking pictures was practically a scientific expedition! But in 1884, Eastman changed everything with his patent for roll film – a flexible, light-sensitive material coated onto a paper base (later celluloid). This wasn't just a new type of film; it was a complete game-changer. It allowed for multiple exposures on a single roll, significantly reducing the size and complexity of cameras. Suddenly, photography wasn't just for pros in studios; it was for everyone! It paved the way for his iconic Kodak camera ('You push the button, we do the rest') and democratized picture-taking, turning personal memories into tangible keepsakes. Eastman truly put the 'roll' in rock-and-roll photography for the masses!
