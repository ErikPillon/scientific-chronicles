After years of audacious construction, the mighty Hoover Dam (then Boulder Dam) finally sealed off the Colorado River. On February 1, 1935, the diversion tunnels were closed, and the river's waters began to back up, slowly, majestically, forming what would become Lake Mead – the largest reservoir by volume in the United States. This wasn't just water accumulating; it was the physical manifestation of controlling a wild river, creating a vital resource for irrigation, drinking water, and hydroelectric power for millions in the Southwest. The act of forming Lake Mead symbolized humanity's triumph over nature, transforming a rugged canyon into a serene yet colossal freshwater sea.
