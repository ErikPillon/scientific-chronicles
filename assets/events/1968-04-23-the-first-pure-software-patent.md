Back in 1968, before software was eating the world, it was barely a recognized entity in the patent office. Enter Martin A. Goetz of IBM, who secured U.S. Patent 3,380,029 for a "Data Processing System" – essentially a "Sort Generator." While not "pure software" in the modern abstract sense, it was a pivotal moment. This wasn't about a new circuit board; it was about the logic, the instructions, the *code* that made machines dance. This early patent sparked a long-running, often fiery debate about whether abstract algorithms and code should be treated like physical inventions. It threw a wrench into the legal gears, setting the stage for decades of arguments over who owns the invisible instructions that power our digital lives. It was messy, it was controversial, and it totally set the precedent for every app you use today.
