Picture this: it's 1910, and mild-mannered Dr. Hawley Harvey Crippen has (allegedly!) murdered his wife and tried to make a daring escape across the Atlantic to Canada with his mistress, Ethel Le Neve, disguised as his son. Sneaky, right? Not sneaky enough! The captain of their ship, the SS Montrose, a chap named Henry Kendall, grew suspicious. Why? The couple's affectionate behavior seemed a bit much for a father-son duo. Kendall, a true pioneer, used the ship's brand-new Marconi wireless telegraphy system to send a message back to Scotland Yard. This was an unprecedented move, sparking a transatlantic chase! The police chief, Inspector Walter Dew, then hopped on a faster ship, the SS Laurentic, arriving in Quebec *before* Crippen's vessel. When the Montrose docked on July 31, 1910, Crippen and Le Neve were met by Dew, leading to the world's first capture assisted by wireless communication. It was a massive media sensation, proving that even across oceans, technology was shrinking the world and making it much harder for dastardly deeds to go unpunished.
