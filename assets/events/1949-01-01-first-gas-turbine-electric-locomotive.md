Forget steam, forget even traditional diesel-electrics; in 1949, General Electric introduced a locomotive that sounded like a jet taking off! The first successful gas-turbine electric locomotive in the U.S. promised immense power for pulling heavy freight, especially across long, flat stretches. These behemoths used a gas turbine to generate electricity, which then powered electric traction motors, much like a diesel-electric, but with a different prime mover. They were powerful and fast but came with a notorious appetite for fuel, especially at lower speeds, and were incredibly noisy. Despite their limited commercial success, mostly with Union Pacific in the American West, they represented a fascinating, albeit fleeting, vision of the future of rail travel: raw power on rails, with a sound to match.
