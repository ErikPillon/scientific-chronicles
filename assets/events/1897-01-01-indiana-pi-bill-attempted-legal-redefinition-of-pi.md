You know Pi, right? That magical number around 3.14159... Well, back in 1897, an Indiana physician and amateur mathematician named Edward J. Goodwin thought he'd cracked the code, proving not only how to square the circle but also deriving new values for Pi. His "discoveries" were so wildly incorrect that he convinced a state representative to introduce a bill to the Indiana General Assembly, essentially making his flawed calculations the *legal* value of Pi in Indiana! Yes, a state legislature was seriously considering passing a law to dictate a mathematical constant. Fortunately, Purdue University professor C.A. Waldo happened to be in the legislature that day and stepped in to explain the bill's scientific absurdity, ensuring it was indefinitely postponed. It's a hilarious and slightly terrifying reminder that science isn't decided by popular vote or legislative decree, no matter how confident the "discoverer" or well-intentioned the lawmakers. Thank goodness mathematicians had the last laugh!
