Before LinkedIn and professional networking events, American engineers decided to get together the old-fashioned way. On November 5, 1852, a distinguished group of civil engineers gathered in New York City to establish the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE), the nation's very first professional engineering society. Their primary goal? To foster the advancement of engineering science and practice, exchange ideas, and establish professional standards in a rapidly expanding country. It was about time, too, as bridges, railways, and canals were popping up everywhere, demanding a unified voice for the masterminds behind them, ensuring that the colossal infrastructure projects of the era were built with integrity and innovation.
