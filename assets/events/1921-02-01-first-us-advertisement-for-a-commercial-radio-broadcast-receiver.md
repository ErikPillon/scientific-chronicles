Before streaming and podcasts, there was radio, and before you could listen, you needed a receiver! While early radio was mostly for hobbyists, the launch of commercial broadcasting by KDKA in late 1920 sparked a new market. Westinghouse, quick off the mark, began placing advertisements for its "Aeriola Jr." receiver in magazines like "Scientific American" and others around February 1921. This wasn't just an ad for a gadget; it was an invitation to join a revolution. Suddenly, news, music, and stories could be beamed directly into your living room, transforming leisure and communication. This marked the true birth of consumer radio, turning a technical marvel into an everyday household item.
