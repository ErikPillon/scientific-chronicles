Before you could become a fancy doctor, you needed to know your femur from your fibula. In 1765, the College of Philadelphia (now the University of Pennsylvania) made history by establishing what is considered America's first school of anatomy. Pioneered by figures like Dr. William Shippen Jr., this institution brought systematic anatomical instruction, including cadaver dissection, to the colonies. This was a radical step, as medical training was often haphazard apprenticeships. It transformed American medical education, moving it from rudimentary practice to scientific rigor, ensuring future physicians actually knew how the human body worked before they started poking around inside it.
