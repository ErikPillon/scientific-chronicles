Imagine setting up shop with a mere $28,000 in capital (and a few investors who probably thought you were a bit bonkers). That's exactly what Henry Ford did on June 16, 1903, when he officially founded the Ford Motor Company in Dearborn, Michigan. This wasn't his first attempt at a car company, but it was the one that stuck! Starting with a small factory and a handful of employees, Ford’s vision wasn't just to build cars, but to build *affordable* cars for the masses. He aimed to move beyond the handcrafted, expensive vehicles of the era and create a durable, practical automobile that anyone could own. This ambition would soon lead to the Model T and the revolutionary assembly line. The Ford Motor Company didn't just produce vehicles; it ignited a manufacturing revolution, created an entirely new middle class through its innovative wages, and fundamentally changed how people lived, worked, and traveled. It wasn't just a company; it was a blueprint for modern industry, proving that sometimes, the biggest ideas start with a guy, a garage, and a whole lot of grit.
