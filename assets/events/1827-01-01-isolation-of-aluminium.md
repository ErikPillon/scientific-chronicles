Aluminium! Today, it's everywhere—from your soda can to airplane wings. But back in the day, isolating this shiny, lightweight metal was quite the chemical challenge. Danish physicist Hans Christian Ørsted made an impure form in 1825, but it was German chemist Friedrich Wöhler who truly cracked the code in 1827, producing pure aluminium. For a while, it was actually rarer and more expensive than gold, a true luxury item. Imagine serving dinner on aluminium plates! Thanks to later industrial processes (like the Hall-Héroult process), it became affordable and ubiquitous, revolutionizing industries from transportation to packaging. It's a prime example of how scientific curiosity can lead to a material that completely changes daily life.
