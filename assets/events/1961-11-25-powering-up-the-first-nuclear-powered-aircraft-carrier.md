In an era defined by the Cold War's arms race, the United States decided to go big, literally. On November 25, 1961, the USS Enterprise (CVN-65) was commissioned, becoming the world's first nuclear-powered aircraft carrier. Imagine an aircraft carrier that didn't need to refuel for 20 years! Thanks to its eight nuclear reactors, this behemoth could travel at high speeds indefinitely, giving the U.S. Navy unprecedented global reach and power projection. Often championed by the 'father of the nuclear navy,' Admiral Hyman G. Rickover, the Enterprise was a marvel of engineering, a symbol of American technological prowess, and a clear signal to the world that naval warfare was entering a new, atomic age. It was a seriously big boat, with even bigger implications.
