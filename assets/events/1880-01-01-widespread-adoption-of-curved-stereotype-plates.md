Before the digital age, printing was a heavy, mechanical beast, and getting newspapers out quickly was a constant challenge. Enter the curved stereotype plate! Stereotyping involved making a mold of an entire page of type and then casting a solid metal plate from it. But for high-speed rotary presses (think massive cylinders spinning away), flat plates just wouldn't cut it. The innovation of curving these durable, cast-metal plates to fit the cylinders of the press, especially popularized in the late 19th century, was a game-changer. It meant newspapers could churn out thousands of copies per hour, delivering the news faster than ever and fueling the rise of mass media. It literally bent printing technology to its will!
