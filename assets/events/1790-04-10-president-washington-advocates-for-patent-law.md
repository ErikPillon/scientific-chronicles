Back in 1790, when the United States was just getting its stride, President George Washington had a brilliant idea: let's protect inventors! He firmly believed that encouraging and rewarding ingenuity was crucial for a thriving nation. This wasn't just a casual suggestion; Washington, along with Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson, personally oversaw the early days of America's patent system. On April 10, 1790, the first federal patent act was signed into law, establishing a process for inventors to secure exclusive rights to their creations. This wasn't just bureaucracy; it was a foundational commitment to fostering innovation, making sure that brilliant minds could build, create, and share without fear of immediate theft. It truly paved the way for American ingenuity to flourish, setting the stage for everything from the cotton gin to the internet.
