Following their groundbreaking lunar dress rehearsal, which saw them get tantalizingly close to the moon, the Apollo 10 crew – Tom Stafford, John Young, and Gene Cernan – safely returned to Earth on May 26, 1969. Their Command Module, 'Charlie Brown,' splashed down precisely in the Pacific Ocean, where they were retrieved by the USS Princeton. The mission had achieved all its objectives: testing navigation, communication, and rendezvous procedures in lunar orbit, and meticulously scouting out the Apollo 11 landing site. Their safe return solidified confidence in the Apollo program's readiness and set the stage perfectly for the monumental lunar landing just a few short weeks later. It was the crucial final act before the grand performance, proving that the Moon was indeed within reach.
