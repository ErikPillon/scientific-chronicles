Imagine a world where electricity only traveled short distances, dimming quickly and requiring many localized power stations. That was the challenge until the 'War of the Currents' saw the alternating current (AC) system emerge victorious. In 1886, William Stanley Jr., backed by George Westinghouse, flipped the switch on America's first commercial AC power plant in Great Barrington, Massachusetts. This pioneering plant demonstrated that AC could efficiently transmit electricity over long distances using transformers, effectively lighting up an entire community from a single source. It was a pivotal moment, unleashing the power grid we know today and fundamentally transforming how we light our homes and fuel our industries. A truly electrifying development!
