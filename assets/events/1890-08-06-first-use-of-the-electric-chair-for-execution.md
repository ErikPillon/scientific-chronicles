The invention and first use of the electric chair mark a grim chapter in late 19th-century American history. Driven by the search for a more 'humane' method of execution than hanging (and fueled by the infamous 'War of the Currents' between Edison and Westinghouse), the electric chair was first patented in 1888. The first person to suffer this new form of capital punishment was William Kemmler, executed on August 6, 1890, in Auburn Prison, New York. The event was highly controversial and gruesomely botched, failing spectacularly to be the clean, swift death its proponents envisioned. While initially hailed as a modern advancement, the electric chair's legacy remains deeply divisive, forever linking electricity not just to illumination and progress, but to a chilling and often agonizing form of state-sanctioned death.
