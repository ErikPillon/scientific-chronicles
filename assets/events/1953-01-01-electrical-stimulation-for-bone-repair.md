Imagine healing a broken bone just by zapping it! While it sounds like sci-fi, the idea of using electricity for bone repair gained serious scientific traction in the mid-20th century, with figures like Robert O. Becker showing its potential around 1953. It wasn't about Frankenstein; it was about understanding the body's natural bioelectrical processes. Scientists discovered that bone cells, when exposed to precise electrical fields, could be stimulated to grow and heal faster, especially in difficult-to-treat non-union fractures. This groundbreaking work transformed orthopedic medicine, offering a non-invasive way to mend stubborn breaks. It proved that sometimes, a little electric spark is exactly what the doctor ordered to get those bones knitting back together!
