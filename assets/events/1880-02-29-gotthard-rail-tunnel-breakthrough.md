Imagine digging through the heart of the Alps, facing rockfalls, water inflows, and sheer exhaustion, all to connect northern and southern Europe! That's precisely what happened on February 29, 1880, when the two ends of the original Gotthard Rail Tunnel finally met. This wasn't just a hole in a mountain; it was a game-changer for trade and travel across Switzerland, reducing journey times dramatically and cementing Switzerland's role as a European crossroads. Led by visionary engineer Louis Favre (who tragically died inside the tunnel during construction!), this 15-kilometer engineering marvel was a testament to human grit and technological advancement. It took over a decade to build, but its legacy as a triumph of civil engineering endures, proving that even the most formidable natural barriers can be overcome.
