The early 20th century saw Chicago tackle its notorious downtown congestion by building a series of tunnels beneath the Chicago River. While freight tunnels existed earlier, the construction of major streetcar and eventually pedestrian tunnels, like the one at LaSalle Street in 1906, was a significant urban development. These tunnels allowed streetcars to bypass the increasingly crowded drawbridges, improving the flow of traffic and people across the busy river. They were crucial pieces of infrastructure for a rapidly expanding metropolis, showcasing how engineers sought innovative solutions to the challenges of urban mobility in a bustling industrial age. Because who wants to wait for a drawbridge when you can just go underneath?
