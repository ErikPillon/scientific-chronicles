While Hans Christian Ørsted got a bit of a messy sample in 1825, it was the meticulous German chemist Friedrich Wöhler who truly isolated pure aluminium in 1827. Think of Ørsted as the guy who found a muddy nugget, and Wöhler as the one who polished it into a shining, recognizable gem. Before this, nobody had actually seen pure aluminium in its metallic form, even though its compounds were common. Wöhler's breakthrough was a significant moment in inorganic chemistry, opening the door for future understanding and, eventually, industrial production. It's the moment aluminium stopped being a theoretical element and became a tangible (and eventually, incredibly useful) metal, proving that sometimes, patience and persistence really do pay off in the lab.
