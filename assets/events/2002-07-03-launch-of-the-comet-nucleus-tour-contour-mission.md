In the grand celestial ballet, comets are the dazzling, if sometimes elusive, stars. But what secrets lay at their heart, the nucleus? NASA decided to find out with the Comet Nucleus Tour (CONTOUR) mission, launched on a hot summer's day in July 2002. The idea was brilliant: send a spacecraft zipping past at least two different comets – Encke and Schwassmann–Wachmann 3 – getting up close and personal to snap high-resolution images and analyze their icy guts. Imagine the cosmic gossip we'd uncover about the early solar system! Sadly, sometimes even the best-laid plans go sideways. Just over a month after its launch, CONTOUR tragically lost contact with Earth shortly after its main engine firing. Despite an extensive search, it was never heard from again, leaving scientists with a giant 'what if?' in the comet exploration story. While its mission ended in heartbreak, CONTOUR’s ambitious goals paved the way for future comet-chasing missions, proving that even a fiery demise can inspire new frontiers. It was a bold attempt to truly lift the veil on our solar system's icy time capsules.
