In 1913, Niels Bohr dropped a mic on the world of physics with his atomic model, forever changing how we pictured the atom. Before Bohr, atoms were a bit of a messy puzzle – electrons just sort of zipping around chaotically. Bohr, with a flash of genius (and a huge assist from quantum ideas), proposed that electrons don't just orbit willy-nilly; they stick to specific, quantized energy levels, like planets in discrete, non-negotiable paths. This explained why atoms emit and absorb light in distinct colors and why they're generally stable and don't just implode. It was a game-changer, merging classical physics with the nascent weirdness of quantum mechanics, even if it wasn't the final word. Talk about a glow-up for atomic theory!
