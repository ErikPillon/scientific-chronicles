Just days after the catastrophic explosion at Chernobyl, the invisible, deadly plume of radioactive material began its journey across Europe, reaching the UK by early May 1986. Prevailing winds carried isotopes like Iodine-131 and Cesium-137, primarily affecting sheep-farming regions in Cumbria, North Wales, and Scotland. The fallout led to immediate bans on sheep movement and sales, some of which incredibly lasted for decades, serving as a stark, long-term reminder of a disaster over 1,500 miles away. It truly brought the reality of nuclear disaster right to Britain's doorstep, prompting widespread concern about food safety and environmental contamination.
