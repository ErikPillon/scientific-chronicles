When you think 'supercomputer,' chances are you're picturing a Cray. Seymour Cray, the legendary 'father of supercomputing,' launched Cray Research and, in 1976, delivered the iconic Cray-1. This wasn't just a bigger calculator; it was a sleek, C-shaped marvel that could process data at speeds previously unimaginable, hitting 160 megaflops (a staggering number for the time!). Scientists, engineers, and government agencies suddenly had the muscle to run incredibly complex simulations, from weather prediction to nuclear weapons design. The Cray wasn't just fast; it was beautiful, pushing the boundaries of what machines could do and setting the standard for computing power for decades. It basically told regular computers to 'hold my beer' and then solved problems that were literally out of this world.
