As electrical generators grew larger to meet increasing power demands in the early 20th century, a big problem emerged: heat. Air cooling just wasn't cutting it for these behemoths, leading to inefficiencies and reduced power output. Enter hydrogen! In 1926, General Electric introduced the first commercial hydrogen-cooled generator, a brilliant solution that used the gas's superior thermal conductivity (about seven times that of air) to whisk away excess heat. By enclosing the generator in a hydrogen-filled, sealed casing, engineers could run machines at much higher power densities, improving efficiency by about 1% (which is huge for a power plant!) and significantly extending their lifespan. This cool innovation became the standard for large generators worldwide, ensuring our cities stay lit and our industries humming, all thanks to a bit of clever gas-work.
