On September 4, 1882, Thomas Edison flipped the switch on a revolution. His Pearl Street Station in lower Manhattan wasn't just a building; it was the world's first central electric power plant supplying electricity to paying customers. Initially serving a mere 59 customers (including J.P. Morgan's office and *The New York Times*), this direct current (DC) behemoth demonstrated that electricity could be reliably generated and distributed on a commercial scale. While DC had its limitations, Pearl Street proved the concept, literally lighting up the path for modern electrical grids and setting the stage for the epic "War of the Currents." It was a truly shocking feat of engineering and entrepreneurial vision!
