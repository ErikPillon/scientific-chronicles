Once a purely artisanal craft, bread-making moved into the industrial age with the advent of the automatic bread plant. This wasn't about one single invention but a revolution in the entire production process, starting in the early 20th century and becoming widespread after World War II. From automated mixing and kneading to proofing and baking, machines orchestrated the entire process. This meant more loaves, faster, and cheaper, fundamentally transforming how people bought and ate their daily bread. It's the reason mass-produced, affordable bread became a kitchen staple, making fresh-baked dreams a reality for millions and solidifying the shift from local bakeries to large-scale food manufacturing.
