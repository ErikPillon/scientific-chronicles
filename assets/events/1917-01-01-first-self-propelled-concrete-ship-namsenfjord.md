When steel was a precious commodity during World War I, some clever engineers thought, 'Why not concrete?' Enter the Namsenfjord, launched in 1917, often considered the first self-propelled concrete ship. Built in Norway, this vessel proved that concrete wasn't just for sidewalks and buildings; it could actually carry cargo across the seas. While concrete ships were heavier and less flexible than their steel counterparts, they were a testament to human resourcefulness in a pinch. It just goes to show, sometimes you gotta get a little creative to keep those supply lines moving!
