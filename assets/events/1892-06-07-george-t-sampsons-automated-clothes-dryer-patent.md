Before the modern electric dryer, clothes often dried outside or on inconvenient racks indoors. Enter George T. Sampson, an ingenious inventor who, on June 7, 1892, secured a patent for an automated clothes dryer. His design used a metal rack over a stove with a series of wires to improve air circulation, significantly improving efficiency and convenience for domestic chores. While perhaps not as flashy as some inventions, Sampson's contribution was a quiet game-changer, helping to ease the burden of household labor and bringing us one step closer to the laundry rooms we know and (sometimes) love today. It was a clear step towards modernizing home appliances, showcasing ingenuity in everyday problem-solving for the average household.
