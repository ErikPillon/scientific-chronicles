Remember when getting your car painted took weeks? No? That's because of Duco! In 1923, DuPont dropped this game-changer onto the automotive scene. Before Duco, car bodies were painstakingly painted with multiple coats of enamel, each requiring days to dry. This slow process was a huge bottleneck for Henry Ford's dream of mass production (hence, 'any color as long as it's black' – black dried faster!). Duco, a nitrocellulose lacquer, dried in a flash, allowing carmakers to spray multiple coats in a single day. Suddenly, cars could roll off the assembly line in a kaleidoscope of colors, not just somber black. It wasn't just a paint; it was a speed demon for production lines and a vibrant splash of color on the roaring twenties streets!
