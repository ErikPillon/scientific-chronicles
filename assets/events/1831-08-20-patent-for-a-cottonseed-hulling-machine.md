After the cotton gin sorted the fibers from the seeds, farmers were left with mountains of cottonseed – largely considered a waste product. But clever folks realized those seeds held a treasure: oil! The problem was getting the tough hull off. Enter innovators like Francis L. Smith, who on August 20, 1831, secured a patent for an early cottonseed hulling machine. This invention wasn't just about making things tidy; it was about unlocking the potential of cottonseed. Suddenly, a byproduct could be transformed into valuable oil for cooking, lamps, and later, margarine, while the meal could feed livestock. This shift turned an industrial headache into an economic boon, showcasing how ingenuity can transform waste into wealth and create entirely new industries.
