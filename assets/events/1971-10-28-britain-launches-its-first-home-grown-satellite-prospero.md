On October 28, 1971, Britain achieved a major space milestone, launching its first and (to date) only satellite, Prospero, on a home-grown Black Arrow rocket. Blasting off from Woomera, Australia, Prospero was designed to test communication systems in space, particularly how well solar cells and thin-film circuits held up against the harsh cosmic environment. While the Black Arrow program was unfortunately cancelled just before this successful launch (what a twist!), Prospero continued to transmit data for years, proving Britain's capability in satellite technology. It was a proud, albeit bittersweet, moment for British engineering, showing that even with budget cuts, they could still reach for the stars – and hit them!
