In the early 19th century, blood loss was a death sentence. But British obstetrician Dr. James Blundell had a radical idea: could transferring blood from one human to another save a life? After experimenting with dogs, he performed the first human-to-human transfusion in Great Britain around 1818-1825. His patient, a woman hemorrhaging after childbirth, received about 12-14 ounces of blood from her husband. Blundell even designed his own apparatus for the procedure! While many attempts were still unsuccessful (blood types wouldn't be discovered until 1901), Blundell's courageous, pioneering work laid the groundwork for modern transfusion medicine, proving that this seemingly miraculous procedure was not only possible but a vital, life-saving intervention.
