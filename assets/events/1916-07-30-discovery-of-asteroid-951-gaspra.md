Asteroid 951 Gaspra might not sound like a rockstar, but this space potato earned its stripes twice over! First, it was officially discovered in 1916 by Russian astronomer Grigory Neujmin. However, its real claim to fame rocketed into the spotlight much later. In 1991, NASA's Galileo spacecraft, on its way to Jupiter, made a daring detour to snap the first-ever close-up images of an asteroid. Gaspra, with its craggy, irregular surface and mysterious grooves, suddenly became a celebrity. This flyby wasn't just a pretty picture; it gave scientists invaluable data, proving that asteroids are far from simple chunks of rock and offering a sneak peek into the violent, chaotic history of our solar system. It was a momentous leap in understanding our cosmic neighborhood, all thanks to a small, tumbling space rock.
