Before high-speed bullet trains and daily commutes, there was Richard Trevithick's Pen-y-darren locomotive, a proper iron horse. On February 21, 1804, in South Wales, this engineering marvel proved that steam power could move serious weight. It successfully hauled 10 tons of iron, five wagons, and about 70 very excited passengers over a nine-mile journey. Okay, so it was a little slow (think walking speed, if your walking speed involved a lot of smoke and steam), and it broke the rails a bit, but it worked! This groundbreaking demonstration wasn't just about moving iron; it sparked the imagination for how people and goods could travel, laying the tracks for the railway revolution that would soon connect continents and change the world forever. All aboard for a bit of history!
