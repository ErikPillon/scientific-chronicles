Remember when white bread was just... white bread? Not for long! By the early 20th century, modern milling processes stripped flour of vital nutrients like B vitamins and iron, leading to widespread deficiencies such as pellagra and anemia among populations reliant on white bread. In response, nutritionists and public health advocates pushed hard for 'enriched flour.' The game-changer came in the 1940s, particularly in the US with a 1941 mandate, when iron, niacin, thiamine, and riboflavin were added back. This simple yet revolutionary act, championed by figures like Russell M. Wilder, drastically improved public health, turning everyday staples into nutritional powerhouses and preventing countless cases of deficiency-related illnesses. Talk about baking a better world!
