Before 1912, starting a car was a perilous chore. You had to physically crank an engine, a job that could break bones (or at least your spirit). Enter Charles F. Kettering, a brilliant inventor who, while working at the Dayton Engineering Laboratories Company (DELCO), tackled this very problem. The story goes that Cadillac chief Henry Leland's friend, Byron Carter, died trying to crank-start his car, inspiring Leland to commission a safer solution. Kettering's ingenious electric self-starter, first introduced in Cadillac models in 1912, used a small electric motor powered by the car's battery to get the engine going with a simple press of a button. This wasn't just a convenience; it was a revolution! It made cars infinitely safer, easier to operate, and crucially, opened up driving to a much wider audience, including women. No longer did you need brute strength to start your ride. Kettering's invention transformed the automobile from a cranky beast into a user-friendly machine, forever changing personal transportation and giving us all an easier morning commute.
