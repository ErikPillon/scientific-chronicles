Before the handy caulking gun, sealing cracks and gaps was a messy, laborious, and often uneven affair, typically done by hand with putty knives or fingers. While primitive tools for applying sealants existed for centuries, the modern, cartridge-based caulking gun truly simplified and standardized the process. A significant patent for a 'caulking device' resembling today's push-rod gun was granted to Albert G. Johnson in 1910. This invention allowed for precise, controlled application of sealants from a pre-filled tube, making construction, plumbing, and general repairs far more efficient and professional. From sealing leaky windows to waterproofing boats, the caulking gun became an indispensable tool, saving countless hours and ensuring a better, more consistent finish across countless applications. It's one of those unsung heroes of the toolbox that just quietly gets the job done.
