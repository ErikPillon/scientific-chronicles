Before 1944, babies born with severe congenital heart defects, often called 'blue babies' due to lack of oxygen, had little hope. Then, the incredible team of Dr. Alfred Blalock (surgeon), Dr. Helen Taussig (pediatric cardiologist), and Vivien Thomas (surgical technician extraordinaire) came along. In a groundbreaking surgery, they created a 'shunt' to reroute blood flow to the lungs, allowing oxygen-poor blood to pick up oxygen. The first successful operation in November 1944 was a miracle, dramatically improving the lives of these infants and marking a monumental leap in pediatric cardiac surgery. It wasn't just a procedure; it was a promise of life for thousands.
