On December 20, 1793, a quiet revolution began in Pawtucket, Rhode Island. Samuel Slater, often called the "Father of the American Industrial Revolution," opened his first cotton mill. What made it revolutionary was that it was the first successful mill in the U.S. to produce cotton yarn using machines entirely designed and built in America, based on British industrial models he'd memorized (as it was illegal to export plans). Partnering with Moses Brown, Slater's mill kicked off the factory system in the United States, transforming textile production from a cottage industry to a mechanized powerhouse. It laid the foundation for America's industrial might and changed the economic and social fabric of the young nation.
