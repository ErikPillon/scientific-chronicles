In 1937, most people thought animation was just for short, silly cartoons. Then, Walt Disney, often told he was crazy, poured his studio's entire fortune (and then some) into 'Disney's Folly': *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*. This wasn't just a cartoon; it was an audacious, hand-drawn epic, stretching over an hour. Animators painstakingly drew over a million frames, pushing the boundaries of cel animation and pioneering techniques like the multiplane camera to create breathtaking depth. When it premiered on December 21, 1937, it was a smash hit, critically acclaimed and a box-office sensation. *Snow White* didn't just prove that animated films could be commercially successful and emotionally resonant; it established animation as a legitimate art form, paving the way for countless animated masterpieces and forever changing the landscape of cinematic storytelling. It was a gamble that paid off in fairy tale proportions.
