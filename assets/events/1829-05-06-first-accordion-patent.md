Before it became the soul of polka and an unlikely rock instrument, the accordion was just a new idea. On May 6, 1829, Cyrill Demian and his sons secured a patent in Vienna for their 'Accordeon,' describing an instrument with a small keyboard and bellows that could produce chords. This wasn't the very first free-reed instrument, but Demian's version, designed for easy travel and chordal accompaniment, marked a significant step in popularizing the instrument. It promised a 'great advantage to all kinds of musicians, particularly to those who play brass instruments, and also to vocalists.' Little did they know, it would become a global sensation, loved and occasionally loathed, but always distinctive.
