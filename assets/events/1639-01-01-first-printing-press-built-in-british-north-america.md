Before Amazon delivered books to your door, someone had to actually make the books! In 1639, a locksmith named Stephen Daye, along with his son Matthew, assembled the very first printing press in what would become the United States. This wasn't some imported European marvel; this was a true colonial DIY project, built for the nascent Harvard College Press in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Imagine the clunking, whirring, and inky fingers as they cranked out the first printed materials, starting with the "Oath of a Freeman" and the "Bay Psalm Book." This press was more than just a machine; it was a cornerstone of colonial education and communication, allowing for the widespread dissemination of ideas, laws, and religious texts. It proved that even in the rugged early days, the pursuit of knowledge (and a good sermon) was paramount, making the colonies a little less wild and a lot more literate.
