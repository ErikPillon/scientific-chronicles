Just two years after its close encounter with Gaspra, the intrepid Galileo spacecraft pulled off another asteroid-related first: discovering that asteroids can have their *own* moons! On August 28, 1993, while whizzing past asteroid 243 Ida, Galileo snapped images that clearly showed a tiny, 1.4-kilometer-wide companion orbiting Ida. This little rock, later named Dactyl, totally upended our understanding of asteroids. Before Dactyl, the idea of a binary asteroid system was mostly theoretical; now it was fact. This discovery opened up new avenues for studying asteroid dynamics, formation, and evolution, revealing that even small celestial bodies can host complex systems. It was a cosmic 'aha!' moment that proved space is full of surprises, even among the 'minor' planets.
