While Henry Ford was busy putting America on wheels, he wasn't content to keep his automotive ambitions confined to the U.S. Just a year after the Ford Motor Company's grand opening, on August 17, 1904, the Ford Motor Company of Canada Limited was officially incorporated. This wasn't just a simple expansion; it was a strategic move that allowed Ford vehicles to be manufactured and sold within the British Empire, bypassing tariffs and opening up vast new markets like Australia, New Zealand, and India. The plant, initially in Walkerville (now part of Windsor), Ontario, quickly became a hub for innovation and production. Ford Canada didn't just build cars; it built an industrial powerhouse, employing thousands and becoming a cornerstone of the Canadian economy. It was a testament to Henry Ford's vision that his company's influence extended so rapidly and globally, demonstrating that sometimes, the best way to conquer the world is one efficient, affordable automobile at a time.
