After President Eisenhower signed the Federal-Aid Highway Act in June 1956, the race was on to build a nationwide network of superhighways. While Missouri won the initial contract for construction, it was a 24-mile stretch of what is now I-70 west of Topeka, Kansas, that earned the bragging rights for the first newly paved section of the Interstate Highway System to officially open under the new act. On November 14, 1956, drivers got their first taste of smooth, multi-lane, limited-access travel. This wasn't just a road; it was the beginning of an era, promising faster commutes, easier freight transport, and epic cross-country road trips. Suddenly, America felt a whole lot smaller and a lot more connected.
