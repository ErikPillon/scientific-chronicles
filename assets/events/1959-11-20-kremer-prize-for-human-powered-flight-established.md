For centuries, humans have dreamed of flying like birds. But what if you could do it purely under your own steam? Enter Henry Kremer, a British industrialist, who in November 1959, put up a substantial prize (initially £5,000) for the first truly sustained human-powered flight. It wasn't about a jump and a glide; it was about navigating a figure-eight course, covering over a mile, all powered by a human pedaling inside a lightweight aircraft. This challenge wasn't just about engineering; it was about pushing the limits of human endurance and aerodynamic design. It inspired countless attempts, leading to quirky, massive, yet incredibly delicate flying machines. It took nearly two decades, but in 1977, Paul MacCready's *Gossamer Condor* finally snatched the prize, proving that with enough ingenuity and sheer leg power, even humans can fly without an engine.
