You know how sometimes you try something new, and it doesn't quite go as planned? Well, imagine that, but with a giant rocket and the eyes of the world watching! On December 6, 1957, the U.S. attempted to launch its very first satellite, Vanguard TV3, in a desperate bid to catch up to the Soviet Union's Sputnik. What happened next became a classic (and rather embarrassing) moment in space history. Just seconds after ignition, the rocket lifted a mere four feet off the launchpad, wobbled dramatically, and then spectacularly exploded, collapsing in a fiery heap. The media, ever so kind, dubbed it "Flopnik" and "Kaputnik." While a public relations nightmare, this fiery failure was a crucial (and very public) lesson in rocketry. It fueled a renewed determination that eventually led to success, proving that even epic failures can light the fire for future triumphs.
