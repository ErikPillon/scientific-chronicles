Before Aspirin, pain relief was a bit of a hit-or-miss affair, often involving plant extracts with nasty side effects. Enter Felix Hoffmann, a chemist at Bayer, who, in 1897, successfully synthesized a stable, pure form of acetylsalicylic acid – what we now know as Aspirin. While salicylic acid was known, it was tough on the stomach. Hoffmann's breakthrough created a gentler, yet still effective, version. It wasn't just a new drug; it sparked the pharmaceutical industry's focus on synthetic compounds and became one of the most widely used and studied medications in history, proving a little chemistry can go a long way in relieving a lot of headaches (literally!).
