Before the cracking process, making gasoline was a bit of a low-yield affair, like trying to get a full meal from a single pea. But then along came pioneers like William M. Burton, who figured out how to 'crack' larger, heavier hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, more volatile ones. This wasn't just a clever trick; it dramatically increased the yield of gasoline from crude oil, making it affordable and plentiful enough to power the burgeoning automobile industry. It was a chemical engineering game-changer, literally transforming the way we move, live, and think about energy. Suddenly, a lot more bang for your petroleum buck!
