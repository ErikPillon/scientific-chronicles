Talk about dedication to science! James Lick, the eccentric millionaire whose fortune built the famed Lick Observatory, had a very specific last request: to be buried at the observatory site, right beneath the pier of the Great Lick Refractor telescope. And so it was. On January 9, 1887, his remains were ceremoniously moved and interred within the foundation of the very instrument he funded, a full decade after his death. This isn't just a quirky historical footnote; it profoundly symbolizes the personal investment Lick had in advancing astronomical discovery. His tomb under the telescope is a permanent, silent testament to a philanthropist who literally wanted to be part of the future of cosmic exploration, making him perhaps the most grounded (literally!) patron of the stars.
