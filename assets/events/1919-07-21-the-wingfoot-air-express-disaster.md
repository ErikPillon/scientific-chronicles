On July 21, 1919, a Goodyear airship named the Wingfoot Air Express was cruising over Chicago, seemingly a majestic symbol of modern progress. Carrying a few passengers and taking part in a promotional flight, it was showcasing the future of air travel. However, what started as a serene journey quickly turned into horror. The airship caught fire, likely due to a mechanical issue, and plummeted to the ground, crashing through the skylight of the Illinois Trust and Savings Bank building. Three crew members and one bank employee were killed, making it the first major commercial airship disaster in the United States. It was a shocking moment for the city and for the burgeoning aviation industry, highlighting the inherent risks involved in early air travel, especially with highly flammable hydrogen. The incident served as a somber reminder that even the most innovative technologies come with a steep learning curve and sometimes, devastating consequences.
