For centuries, plows were made of wood, often tipped with iron, making them heavy, prone to breaking, and difficult to repair. Talk about back-breaking work! Then came the game-changer: the cast iron plow. Charles Newbold patented the first all-cast iron plow in America in 1797. While farmers were initially skeptical (fearing the iron would poison the soil – imagine that!), his design, and later Jethro Wood's improved version with interchangeable parts in 1819, revolutionized agriculture. These robust plows cut through tough soil more efficiently, lasted longer, and were easier to fix, vastly increasing farm productivity and helping feed a growing nation. Truly, a literal groundwork for progress!
