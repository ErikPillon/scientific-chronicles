On September 15, 1830, the grand opening of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway was supposed to be a triumph of Victorian engineering. Instead, it became a grim milestone. William Huskisson, a prominent Member of Parliament, exited his carriage during a stop to greet the Duke of Wellington. Distracted, he was struck by Stephenson's "Rocket" locomotive and tragically died later that day. This wasn't just an accident; it was the world's very first recorded railway fatality, and it sent shockwaves through society. It was a stark, brutal reminder that this marvelous new technology, capable of breathtaking speed (for the time!), also carried immense danger. The event sparked immediate debates about railway safety and public conduct around these powerful new machines, forever linking progress with peril in the public consciousness. A truly unfortunate first, indeed.
