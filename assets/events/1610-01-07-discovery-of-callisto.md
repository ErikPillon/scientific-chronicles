In early 1610, Galileo Galilei, with his freshly improved telescope, decided to point it at Jupiter. What he saw blew everyone's mind (and challenged ancient astronomy): not just a bright planet, but four smaller 'stars' dancing around it. These were the Galilean moons, and Callisto was one of them. On January 7, 1610, Galileo first logged its presence, along with Europa and Io. Observing their movement over several nights, he realized they weren't fixed stars but satellites orbiting Jupiter. This wasn't just a cool discovery; it was concrete evidence that not everything revolved around Earth, giving a huge boost to the Copernican model and forever changing our view of the cosmos. Callisto, the furthest of the big four, still patiently orbits, a silent testament to a revolution in understanding.
