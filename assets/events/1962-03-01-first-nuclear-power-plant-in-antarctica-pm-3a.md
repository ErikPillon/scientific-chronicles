Who needs fossil fuels when you've got atoms? In 1962, the U.S. Navy introduced PM-3A (Portable Medium Power Reactor 3A) at McMurdo Station, making Antarctica the unlikely home of its first (and only) nuclear power plant. This compact pressurized water reactor was designed to supply electricity and desalinate water, significantly reducing the colossal logistics of shipping diesel fuel to the isolated research outpost. Imagine the challenge: installing a nuclear reactor in one of the most hostile environments on Earth! PM-3A ran for a decade, proving nuclear power could operate reliably in extreme conditions. Though eventually decommissioned and removed due to operational challenges and concerns, it was a fascinating, ambitious experiment in self-sufficiency for polar science. Talk about going green (or glowing, in this case) in a white wilderness!
