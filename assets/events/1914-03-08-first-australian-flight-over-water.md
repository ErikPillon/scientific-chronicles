While the Wright brothers were still getting their land legs, Australia was looking to the skies over its vast coastlines! The first Australian flight over water wasn't just a splash; it was a significant leap for aviation down under. On March 8, 1914, French aviator Maurice Guillaux (yes, a Frenchman!) took to the skies in a Maurice Farman hydroplane, gracefully lifting off from Sydney Harbour. This wasn't just a joyride; it demonstrated the potential of seaplanes for communication, reconnaissance, and transport in a country surrounded by water. Imagine the cheers as this pioneering aircraft sliced through the air above the iconic harbour, marking a new era where the sky became another highway for travel and progress. It showed that even over water, Australia was ready to embrace the age of flight!
