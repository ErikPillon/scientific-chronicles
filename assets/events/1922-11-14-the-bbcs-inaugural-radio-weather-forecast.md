Before smartphones and instant satellite imagery, how did Britons know if they needed an umbrella? On November 14, 1922, the newly formed British Broadcasting Company (BBC) offered a groundbreaking solution: the radio weather forecast! As part of its very first official broadcast from London's 2LO station, the BBC began transmitting weather reports provided by the Met Office. Imagine the novelty! Farmers could plan their harvest, sailors could check the seas, and picnickers could, well, reassess their plans, all from the comfort of their homes. This wasn't just about predicting rain; it was about connecting a nation, providing vital public information, and demonstrating the incredible power of radio. It marked a new era where technology made essential daily information accessible to the masses, turning mundane atmospheric conditions into must-listen radio. And yes, it probably rained.
