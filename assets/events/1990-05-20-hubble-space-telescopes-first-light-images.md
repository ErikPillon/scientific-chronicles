After its much-anticipated launch, the Hubble Space Telescope quickly began capturing its 'first light' images. While the very first pictures were part of engineering checks, the public got its first glimpse of Hubble's potential (and initial problems) with images like those of the star cluster NGC 3532 in May 1990. Though these early photos hinted at the telescope's incredible capabilities, they also revealed the infamous spherical aberration flaw that caused its images to be disappointingly fuzzy. It was a moment of mixed emotions: excitement for the future, but also a dash of 'oh no, what happened?' that would lead to its heroic repair mission three years later.
