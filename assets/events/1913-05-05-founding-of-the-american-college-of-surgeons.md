Before the American College of Surgeons (ACS), surgical standards could be, well, a bit wild west. But on May 5, 1913, a group of visionary surgeons, led by the dynamic Franklin H. Martin, decided it was time to bring order to the operating room. Officially incorporated in November of that year, the ACS set out to "elevate the standards of surgery" and ensure patient safety. Think of them as the gatekeepers of good surgical practice, ensuring that when you went under the knife, you were in the hands of someone who knew their stuff. By promoting education, ethical conduct, and scientific research, the ACS transformed surgery from an often haphazard affair into a highly respected and meticulously standardized medical discipline. It's all thanks to them that your appendix removal is a lot less dramatic today!
