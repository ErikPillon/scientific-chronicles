Before the cotton gin, separating sticky green seeds from fluffy cotton fibers was a painfully slow, hand-cramping affair, making short-staple cotton a non-starter for mass production. Enter Eli Whitney, who in 1793 cooked up a simple machine with wires and brushes that did the job in a flash. This little invention, the 'cotton gin' (short for engine), was a total game-changer for the textile industry. Suddenly, cotton cultivation became incredibly profitable. The catch? Instead of reducing the need for labor, it skyrocketed the demand for it, tragically cementing and expanding the institution of slavery in the American South. A marvel of engineering with a devastating, and often overlooked, social cost.
