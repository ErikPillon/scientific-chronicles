After Giuseppe Piazzi discovered Ceres on January 1, 1801, he meticulously tracked it for 40 days. But then, disaster struck: he fell ill on February 11th, and Ceres disappeared behind the Sun, making further observations impossible. Panic! How would they find it again? Enter the legendary mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss. Using only Piazzi's limited observations, Gauss developed a revolutionary method for orbit determination. His calculations were so precise that astronomers were able to re-locate Ceres almost a year later, exactly where he predicted. Piazzi's illness inadvertently spurred a major mathematical breakthrough and cemented Gauss's reputation as a wizard of numbers.
