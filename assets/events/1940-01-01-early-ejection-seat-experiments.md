The seemingly simple idea of an ejection seat – getting out of a failing plane, fast – was anything but simple to perfect! Early experiments on ejection seats kicked off in Germany in the early 1940s, driven by the ever-increasing speeds of aircraft and the perilous difficulty of bailing out manually. Engineers tirelessly tested various propulsion systems and seat designs, often using dummies (and occasionally, incredibly brave volunteers). These initial trials, involving controlled launches from stationary rigs and eventually from flying aircraft, were absolutely crucial in developing the life-saving technology that would soon become standard in military aviation. It was all about making sure that 'getting out' didn't involve 'getting splattered.' Safety, first... or second, after 'getting out'.
