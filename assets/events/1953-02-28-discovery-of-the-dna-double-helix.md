Before 1953, the molecule of heredity was a big, curly question mark. Then, Watson and Crick, fueled by coffee and Franklin's crucial X-ray diffraction images (hello, Photo 51!), cracked the code. They proposed the iconic double helix, two strands elegantly twirling around each other, like a molecular staircase. It wasn't just pretty; it explained how genetic information is stored and copied, launching a revolution in biology that still buzzes today. Suddenly, life's blueprint was laid bare, and the scientific world gasped, 'Aha!'
