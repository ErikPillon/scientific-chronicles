Imagine a time before credit cards or even reliable cash registers, when paying for goods meant waiting patiently while a sales clerk painstakingly carried your money to a central cashier and brought back change. Talk about a workflow killer! Enter the cash carrier system. Pioneered by inventors like David Brown, who received a patent for a 'cash railway' in 1879, and later popularized by companies like Lamson, these ingenious devices — be they whizzing wires or whooshing pneumatic tubes — transported cash and receipts across bustling department stores. They weren't just a quirky novelty; they revolutionized retail efficiency, improving security and speeding up transactions, making shopping a slightly less tedious affair for generations.
