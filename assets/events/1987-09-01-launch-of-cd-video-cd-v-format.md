Before DVDs, there was CD Video (CD-V), an interesting but ultimately short-lived media format launched in 1987. Imagine a standard audio CD, but with a 5-minute video clip (plus 20 minutes of audio) on a shiny, golden disc, or even a full-length movie on a larger, LaserDisc-sized version. The idea was to combine the digital audio quality of a CD with the analog video quality of LaserDisc, creating a hybrid format. Philips and Sony spearheaded the effort, hoping to kickstart a new home video experience. However, with incompatible players, limited content, and the impending rise of digital video codecs and later, DVDs, CD-V struggled to gain traction. It's a fascinating footnote in the history of home entertainment, showing how companies tried to bridge the gap between different media technologies before digital truly took over.
