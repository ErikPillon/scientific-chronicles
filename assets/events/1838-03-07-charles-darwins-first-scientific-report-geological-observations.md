After his incredible voyage on the HMS Beagle, Charles Darwin wasn't just chilling out. He was busy! On March 7, 1838, he delivered his first formal scientific report, 'On the connexion of the volcanic phenomena in South America; and on the formation of mountain-chains and volcanos as the effect of the same powers by which continents are elevated,' to the esteemed Geological Society of London. This wasn't about finches yet; it was all about rocks and quakes. He presented his observations from Chile, including the uplift of land he witnessed during the 1835 earthquake. It was a clear demonstration of his prowess as a geologist and his ability to synthesize vast observations, laying the groundwork for his future, even more revolutionary, ideas. Geology was his first love, scientifically speaking!
