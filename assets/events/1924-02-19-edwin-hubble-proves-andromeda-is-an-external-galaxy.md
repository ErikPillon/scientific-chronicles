For years, astronomers debated 'The Great Debate': were spiral nebulae just gas clouds within our Milky Way, or were they 'island universes' — other galaxies entirely? In 1923, Edwin Hubble, using the powerful Hooker telescope, found Cepheid variable stars in the Andromeda Nebula. These 'standard candles' allowed him to calculate its distance. It was *way* too far to be part of the Milky Way! On February 19, 1924, he sent a letter with his findings to Harlow Shapley, a prominent proponent of the 'nebulae are within our galaxy' view. Shapley, upon receiving the evidence, famously said, 'Here is the letter that destroyed my universe.' It was a watershed moment, finally confirming the vastness of the cosmos and our galaxy's place as just one of countless others.
