Before Willis Carrier came along, the dog days of summer were truly dreadful, especially indoors. While early attempts at cooling existed, it was Carrier, an engineer from Buffalo, New York, who developed the first modern electrical air conditioning unit on July 17, 1902. His initial goal wasn't just comfort; it was to control humidity in a Brooklyn printing plant to keep paper from wrinkling. He realized he could control temperature and humidity simultaneously. His "Apparatus for Treating Air" quickly moved beyond industrial uses, eventually making its way into movie theaters, department stores, and homes, changing architecture, enabling migration to hotter climates, and making the modern skyscraper a practical reality. Talk about a breath of fresh, cool air!
