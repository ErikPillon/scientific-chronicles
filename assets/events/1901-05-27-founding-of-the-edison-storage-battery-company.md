Always the tinkerer, Thomas Edison wasn't satisfied with the lead-acid batteries of his day. They were heavy, fragile, and prone to leakage. So, he spent a decade developing a more robust, long-lasting alternative: the nickel-iron battery. To commercialize this innovation, he founded the Edison Storage Battery Company in 1901. These batteries powered everything from early electric vehicles to railway signals and industrial equipment, proving incredibly durable. It was Edison's push for practical, rechargeable power, showing he was just as interested in storing energy as he was in generating it.
