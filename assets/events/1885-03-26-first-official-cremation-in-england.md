For centuries, burial was the only way to go in England. But on March 26, 1885, a pivotal moment in funerary history occurred: the first official cremation took place. After a long campaign by figures like Sir Henry Thompson, who founded the Cremation Society of England, and facing considerable public and religious opposition, the practice finally gained legal and social acceptance. The first cremation was of a Welsh woman, Mrs. Jeanette Pickersgill, at the newly opened Woking Crematorium. This wasn't just a change in how bodies were disposed of; it reflected a growing concern for public health, sanitation, and efficient land use in Victorian society, slowly but surely paving the way for cremation to become a widely accepted alternative to traditional burial.
