Before smartphones could light up your dimly lit selfies, photographers wrestled with low light, often resorting to explosive and dangerous flash powder. Enter the game-changer: the practical flashbulb, patented by Johannes Ostermeier in 1929. This clever little glass globe, packed with magnesium foil and oxygen, created a brilliant, instantaneous burst of light when an electric current passed through it. While it was single-use and sometimes startling, it revolutionized photography after dark or indoors. Suddenly, portraits could be taken anywhere, and photojournalists could capture crucial moments no matter the lighting. This invention literally brought light to dark subjects, democratizing photography and ensuring countless moments wouldn't be lost to the shadows.
