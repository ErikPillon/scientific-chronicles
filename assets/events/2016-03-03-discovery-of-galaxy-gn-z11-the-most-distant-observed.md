Imagine trying to spot a candle flicker in the dark from across the entire continent. That's essentially what astronomers do when they hunt for the furthest galaxies! For decades, scientists have pushed the limits of telescopes, constantly finding new record-holders for the most distant observable galaxy, each discovery giving us a peek into the universe's infancy. One particularly dazzling find was GN-z11, identified in 2016 using the Hubble Space Telescope. This ancient galaxy, observed as it was just 400 million years after the Big Bang, shattered previous distance records. Its discovery wasn't just about bragging rights; it provided invaluable data on the early formation of stars and galaxies, helping us understand how our universe evolved from a cosmic soup into the complex structure we see today. It's like finding a baby picture of the cosmos!
