In 1902, Andrew Carnegie, who had more money than he knew what to do with (after building a steel empire, of course), decided to invest in something truly grand: scientific discovery. He chartered the Carnegie Institute of Washington, endowing it with a whopping $10 million – an astronomical sum back then – to support 'discovery in the broadest sense.' His vision was to create a haven for researchers, free from the usual academic constraints, allowing them to chase big, audacious scientific questions. From astronomy to genetics, the Institute quickly became a premier center for cutting-edge research, proving that sometimes, even titans of industry can leave a lasting legacy of pure intellectual pursuit, making the world a whole lot smarter.
