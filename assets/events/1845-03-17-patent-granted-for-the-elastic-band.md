Who knew a simple rubber band had a specific birthdate? On March 17, 1845, Stephen Perry of Messrs. Perry & Co., prominent rubber manufacturers in London, was granted the patent for the elastic band. While rubber itself had been known for centuries, Perry found a clever way to 'vulcanize' the rubber (a process developed by Charles Goodyear) and form it into continuous rings. This made them flexible, durable, and incredibly useful for holding papers or other unruly items together. This seemingly mundane invention revolutionized organization, making it easier to keep documents in order and offering a cheap, reliable solution to countless bundling problems. It's one of those brilliant, unassuming inventions we now can't imagine life without, much like sliced bread, but stretchier and far less crumbly.
