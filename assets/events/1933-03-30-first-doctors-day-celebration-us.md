Back in 1933, in Winder, Georgia, a brilliant idea sparked: why not dedicate a day to appreciate our doctors? Eudora Brown Almond, wife of Dr. Charles B. Almond, spearheaded the very first Doctors' Day celebration. It was a heartfelt affair, marked by sending cards and placing red carnations on the graves of deceased physicians. This simple, thoughtful gesture grew into a national observance, acknowledging the dedication and sacrifices of medical professionals. It was a recognition that doctors, often working tirelessly behind the scenes, deserve a moment in the spotlight for their life-saving work and commitment to our health. Who knew a small town in Georgia would kick off a tradition that now spans the nation?
