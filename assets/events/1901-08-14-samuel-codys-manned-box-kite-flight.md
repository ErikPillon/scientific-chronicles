Long before powered flight became common, inventors were tinkering with all sorts of aerial contraptions. One of the most audacious was the manned box kite! On August 14, 1901, the flamboyant American-born aviation pioneer Samuel Franklin Cody made history near Aldershot, England. He strapped himself into a basket beneath a massive train of his signature Man-Lifting War Kites (an evolution of Lawrence Hargrave's box kite design) and ascended to a dizzying height of 2,600 feet, staying aloft for over an hour. This wasn't just a joyride; Cody was demonstrating the military potential of his kites for observation. His daring feat proved the stability and lifting power of the box kite design and showed that with a bit of ingenuity (and a lot of wind), humans could become truly airborne, pushing the boundaries of early aviation.
