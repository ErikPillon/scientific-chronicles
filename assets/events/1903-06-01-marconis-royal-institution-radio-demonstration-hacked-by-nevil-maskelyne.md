Imagine the scene: Guglielmo Marconi, the rockstar of wireless, is about to dazzle an audience at London's Royal Institution in June 1903 with a supposedly secure, transatlantic radio transmission. He's expecting to receive a message from Cornwall. Instead, the audience gets an unexpected prank message and some rather colorful Morse code poetry from 'a touting Italian.' The culprit? Nevil Maskelyne, a magician and wireless enthusiast who had set up a more powerful transmitter nearby. Maskelyne's 'hack' wasn't malicious in the modern sense; he wanted to expose the lack of security in early radio. It was a spectacular and embarrassing moment for Marconi, but it kickstarted crucial discussions about encryption and signal integrity in the burgeoning field of wireless communication. Who knew a magician could be a cybersecurity pioneer?
