Ever grumble about changing your clocks? You can trace it back, in part, to Germany in 1916! While the idea had been floated by folks like Benjamin Franklin and William Willett (who reportedly got annoyed by drawn curtains on sunny mornings), it was Germany during World War I that first widely implemented "Sommerzeit" (summer time) on April 30, 1916. The logic was simple: shift an hour of daylight from the morning to the evening to save coal for the war effort. Other nations quickly followed suit, hoping to conserve energy and boost productivity. It's a testament to how global events can trigger widespread, seemingly simple changes that still echo in our calendars today – proving that even our clocks can be conscripted for national needs!
