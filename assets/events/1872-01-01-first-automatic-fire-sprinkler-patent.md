Before automatic sprinklers, a fire in a factory or large building often meant total devastation and a very bad day. Enter Henry S. Parmelee, a piano manufacturer from New Haven, Connecticut. Frustrated by the frequent fires in his own factory, he invented and patented the first automatic fire sprinkler head in 1872. This ingenious device used a heat-sensitive element to trigger water release directly over a fire, allowing for immediate, localized suppression without human intervention. Parmelee's innovation was a monumental leap in fire safety, transforming industrial and commercial building protection and saving countless properties and lives from fiery ruin. Talk about a cool invention!
