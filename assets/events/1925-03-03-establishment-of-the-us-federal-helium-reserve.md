Long before helium was a party trick for squeaky voices, it was a critical national security asset! During World War I, the U.S. realized helium's non-flammable properties made it perfect for military airships, unlike flammable hydrogen. With a near-monopoly on natural helium deposits, the U.S. moved to secure its supply. The Helium Act of 1925 officially established the U.S. Federal Helium Reserve near Amarillo, Texas, to stockpile and manage this precious gas. This was a strategic move to ensure American dominance in airship technology. While the blimp era faded, the reserve continued to supply helium for everything from scientific research and MRI machines to, yes, those ever-important birthday balloons. It's a fascinating tale of gas, war, and national foresight!
