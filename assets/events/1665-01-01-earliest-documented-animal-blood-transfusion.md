Before blood transfusions were a routine life-saving procedure, they were the stuff of daring, sometimes gruesome, scientific experiments. One of the earliest documented successful transfusions was carried out by English physician Richard Lower in 1665, who successfully transferred blood from one dog to another. This wasn't just a random act; it was a crucial step in understanding the circulatory system and exploring the potential of blood as a therapeutic agent. While early attempts at human-to-human or animal-to-human transfusions often met with tragic results (due to unknown blood incompatibilities and infection), these animal experiments laid foundational knowledge. They proved that blood could indeed be transferred and maintain life, setting the stage for centuries of research that eventually led to safe and effective blood banking for humans. Talk about a bloody good start to a crucial medical advancement!
