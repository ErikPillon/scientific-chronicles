The medical world and public alike watched with a mix of hope and trepidation as Baby Fae battled for her life with a baboon heart. Tragically, after 21 days, on November 16, 1984, she succumbed to organ rejection and passed away. Her death was not just the end of a single, brave life; it marked a pivotal moment in the history of xenotransplantation and medical ethics. While the surgery itself was a stunning display of surgical skill, Baby Fae's passing brought the ethical dilemmas surrounding such extreme experimental procedures into sharp focus. Critics questioned the rationale, the animal sacrifice, and the emotional toll on all involved. Her case spurred significant changes in the regulations for experimental animal-to-human transplants and cemented the necessity for robust ethical review boards. Baby Fae's brief life and ultimate death left an indelible mark, reminding us of the profound responsibilities that come with pushing the frontiers of science.
