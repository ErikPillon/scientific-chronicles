Long before 'going green' was a thing, inventor Charles F. Brush was already ahead of the curve. In December 1887, in his backyard in Cleveland, Ohio, he completed what's recognized as the first automatic wind turbine for electricity generation. This colossal machine, standing 60 feet tall with a 56-foot rotor and 144 blades, powered his mansion for 20 years, running 350 incandescent lights, 2 arc lamps, and multiple motors. While not practical for mass production, Brush's behemoth was a groundbreaking proof-of-concept, demonstrating that clean, renewable energy wasn't just a dream, but a tangible reality, even in the age of coal and steam.
