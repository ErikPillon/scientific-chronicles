Before fibre optics, international communication across the English Channel relied on slower, less capacious copper cables. But in the mid-1980s, the future arrived! The first commercial fibre-optic cable connecting the UK and continental Europe (for example, a major link between the UK and Belgium or France) was deployed. This wasn't just an upgrade; it was a revolution. Fibre optics, using light pulses to transmit data through thin glass strands, offered vastly greater bandwidth and speed, drastically improving international telephone calls, data transfer, and eventually, the nascent internet. It laid crucial digital groundwork, shrinking the Channel into a mere whisper of data and forever changing how Europe communicated.
