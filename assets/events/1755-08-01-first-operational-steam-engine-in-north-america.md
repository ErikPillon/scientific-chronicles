You might think of steam engines as a purely 19th-century marvel, but the first one to chug to life in North America was actually much earlier! Back in 1755, a fellow named Josiah Hornblower, fresh from England, brought his expertise (and a Newcomen atmospheric engine) to the Pompeston Mine in what's now North Arlington, New Jersey. This wasn't for trains or boats yet, but to pump water out of a copper mine, a Herculean task by hand. It was a massive, clunky beast, but it worked! This contraption laid the groundwork for future American industrial power, demonstrating that the future wasn't just about muscle and sweat, but about harnessing the raw, tireless force of steam. It was the original 'Made in America' power source, even if it sailed over from Britain first!
