On June 15, 1667, French physician Jean-Baptiste Denys transfused a small amount of lamb's blood into a gravely ill 15-year-old boy, in one of the earliest well-documented blood transfusions in medical history. Remarkably, the boy survived and reportedly improved, and Denys went on to perform several more transfusions, mostly using animal blood, based on the era's medical theories about temperament and blood. The practice proved dangerously unpredictable, however; later transfusion attempts caused severe reactions and at least one patient death, leading to legal action against Denys and an eventual ban on the practice across France and much of Europe for over a century. It would take until the early 20th century and Karl Landsteiner's discovery of blood groups for transfusion to become the safe, routine medical procedure it is today. A genuinely brave (if reckless) first step, followed by a very long, necessary pause. 🩸⚕️
