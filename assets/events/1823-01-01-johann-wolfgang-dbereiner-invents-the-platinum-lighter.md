Imagine a world without matches or modern lighters – getting a flame was a whole ordeal! But in 1823, German chemist Johann Wolfgang Döbereiner brought a spark of genius to the problem. He invented what became known as the 'Döbereiner lighter,' an incredibly clever (and rather dramatic) device. It used zinc to react with sulfuric acid to produce hydrogen gas. When a jet of this hydrogen hit a sponge of platinum, the platinum acted as a catalyst, igniting the hydrogen into a flame! It was basically a chemistry experiment that fit on your desk, providing instant fire long before safety matches became a thing. While a bit cumbersome and prone to occasional chemical mishaps, it was a groundbreaking demonstration of catalysis and a very flashy way to light your pipe, or just show off your scientific prowess!
