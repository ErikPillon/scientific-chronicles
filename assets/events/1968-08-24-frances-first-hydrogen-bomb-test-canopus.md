In a blaze of radioactive glory on August 24, 1968, France joined the exclusive club of nations possessing the hydrogen bomb. Codenamed 'Canopus,' this thermonuclear device was detonated over Fangataufa Atoll in French Polynesia, unleashing an estimated yield of 2.6 megatons. This test was a huge political and scientific statement for France, particularly under President Charles de Gaulle's assertive foreign policy. De Gaulle was determined that France should maintain its 'force de frappe' (strike force) independently of the US and UK, ensuring its own security and influence on the world stage. Achieving H-bomb capability, just like the US, USSR, UK, and China before it, was a massive technical undertaking and a clear declaration of French national sovereignty and scientific prowess. It solidified France's position as a major player in the Cold War nuclear standoff, but also underscored the perilous global arms race.
