In the early days of aviation, even short flights were perilous, let alone attempting to cross a significant body of water. But on July 25, 1909, French aviator Louis Blériot etched his name into history by making the first airplane flight across the English Channel. Piloting his monoplane, the Blériot XI, he took off from Calais, France, and after a tense, 37-minute flight battling strong winds and uncertain navigation, crash-landed (relatively safely) near Dover Castle in England. This audacious feat not only won him a £1,000 prize from the Daily Mail but also definitively proved the airplane's potential for intercontinental travel and forever changed perceptions of national borders and geographic barriers.
