Forget vacuum tubes and room-sized behemoths for a moment. In the midst of World War II, a brilliant German engineer named Konrad Zuse was quietly building the future. On December 1, 1941, his Z3 computer clicked into action, becoming the world's first fully-functional, programmable, digital computer. Imagine that! While others were still dreaming of mechanical calculators, Zuse crafted a machine that could perform calculations automatically from a program, using relays and binary floating-point numbers. It wasn't flashy or well-known outside wartime Germany, but the Z3 laid the groundwork for everything we now take for granted in computing. This wasn't just a calculator; it was the genesis of software, algorithms, and the digital age itself, proving that genius can bloom even in the toughest times.
