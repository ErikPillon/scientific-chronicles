Before 'AI' became a household buzzword, it was an ambitious idea looking for a name. In the summer of 1956, a group of brilliant minds, led by John McCarthy, converged at Dartmouth College for a two-month workshop. McCarthy proposed the term 'Artificial Intelligence' for the field, effectively giving birth to modern AI research. This wasn't about building Skynet immediately; it was about laying the theoretical groundwork, exploring how machines could simulate human intelligence, solve problems, and even learn. It was a foundational moment, sparking decades of innovation, debate, and the occasional existential crisis about robots taking over. Good thing they didn't foresee ChatGPT, or they might have extended the workshop!
