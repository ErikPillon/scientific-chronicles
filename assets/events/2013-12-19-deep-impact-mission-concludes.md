Every good story has an end, and for NASA's plucky Deep Impact spacecraft, that day came in late 2013. After launching in 2005 and famously smashing an impactor into Comet Tempel 1, the mission continued its journey, performing flybys of other comets like Hartley 2 and even EPOXI, observing exoplanets. But after nearly a decade of incredible service, contact was lost in August 2013, likely due to a software glitch causing its thrusters to fire constantly, depleting its fuel. NASA officially declared the mission over in December. It was a bittersweet goodbye to a probe that truly left its mark – both literally and figuratively – on our understanding of comets and the universe.
