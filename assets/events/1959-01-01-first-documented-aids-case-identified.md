The AIDS epidemic burst into public consciousness in the early 1980s, but its origins stretch back much further. Through meticulous scientific detective work, researchers retrospectively identified what is believed to be the earliest documented case of AIDS in a man from Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of Congo, in 1959. This discovery, made through genetic analysis of preserved tissue samples, provided crucial insights into the timeline and geographic spread of HIV, the virus that causes AIDS. While the individual remains anonymous, this finding was vital for understanding the silent, decades-long journey of a virus that would eventually change global health forever. A historical puzzle solved, revealing a somber truth.
