For years, David Vetter, the 'bubble boy,' lived completely isolated due to severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID). By 1984, after numerous attempts to find a compatible donor, a groundbreaking decision was made: a bone marrow transplant using bone marrow from his older sister, Katherine. This procedure was a monumental effort, offering a glimmer of hope for a boy who had only known a sterile enclosure. While the transplant was a medical marvel, it tragically introduced a dormant virus (Epstein-Barr) that led to his death a few months later. Despite the devastating outcome, David's case and this pioneering transplant provided invaluable lessons for the medical community, paving the way for safer, more effective bone marrow transplants and accelerating research into gene therapy for SCID, ultimately saving countless lives.
