While many tinkered with steam-powered boats before, it was Robert Fulton's Clermont that truly made waves, literally. On August 17, 1807, this majestic (for its time) vessel embarked on its maiden commercial voyage from New York City to Albany on the Hudson River. Skeptics gathered, some even calling it 'Fulton's Folly,' but as the Clermont chugged along, defying wind and current at a respectable 5 miles per hour, minds were changed. It wasn't the fastest, prettiest, or even the absolute *first* steamboat, but it was the *first commercially successful* one, proving that steam power could be a reliable and profitable way to transport people and goods. It opened up inland waterways, shrinking distances and kicking off a new era of American commerce and travel.
