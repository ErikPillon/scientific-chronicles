On May 24, 1844, a simple phrase, "What hath God wrought!", tapped out by Samuel Morse himself from the U.S. Supreme Court chamber in Washington D.C. to his assistant Alfred Vail in Baltimore, signaled not just the successful operation of his telegraph, but the birth of instant news. This wasn't just a party trick; it was a revolution! Imagine waiting days or weeks for election results, then suddenly, BAM! News from hundreds of miles away arrives in minutes. The specific "news" transmitted shortly after this inaugural message was about the Whig National Convention in Baltimore, reporting the nomination of Henry Clay for president. Suddenly, information could travel faster than a speeding horse, shrinking the vast American continent and laying the groundwork for everything from breaking news alerts to text messages. It truly felt like magic, and it fundamentally reshaped how society received and reacted to information.
