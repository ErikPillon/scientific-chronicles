Talk about making a grand entrance! The discovery of Eris in 2005 was a cosmic bombshell. This icy body, initially spotted in 2003 by Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo, and David Rabinowitz at Palomar Observatory, turned out to be more massive than Pluto. Oops! Suddenly, the solar system had another big player lurking way out in the Kuiper Belt. The official announcement on January 5, 2005, threw the astronomical community into a frenzy, sparking heated debates and ultimately leading to the International Astronomical Union's decision in 2006 to redefine what it means to be a planet. Poor Pluto got demoted, all thanks to Eris crashing the party and reshaping our understanding of the outer solar system and how we classify its residents.
