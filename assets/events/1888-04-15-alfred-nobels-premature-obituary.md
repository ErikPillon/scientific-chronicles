Talk about a wake-up call! In 1888, a French newspaper, getting its Nobels mixed up, prematurely published an obituary for Alfred Nobel, thinking his brother Ludvig had died. The headline? 'The merchant of death is dead.' Ouch. The article didn't pull any punches, condemning Alfred for inventing dynamite and making his fortune from instruments of war. Imagine reading your own scathing review of your life's work! This accidental, and rather brutal, exposé reportedly deeply affected Nobel. He was already a hugely successful inventor and businessman, but this glimpse into his perceived legacy—being remembered only for destructive power—was apparently the catalyst. He decided he wanted to be remembered differently. And thus, nine years later, in his will, he established the Nobel Prizes, an enduring legacy celebrating peace, literature, and scientific advancement. It just goes to show, sometimes the biggest blunders can inspire the most profound good.
