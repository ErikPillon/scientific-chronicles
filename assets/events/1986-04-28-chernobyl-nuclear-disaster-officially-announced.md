On April 28, 1986, after days of alarming radiation spikes detected across Europe, the Soviet Union's TASS news agency begrudgingly acknowledged a 'minor accident' at the Chernobyl nuclear power plant. This terse, understated announcement, following earlier denials and a cloak of secrecy, confirmed the worst fears of an international community already on high alert. It wasn't just news; it was the chilling official start of one of history's most devastating technological disasters, forcing the world to confront the terrifying reality of nuclear catastrophe and the perils of government secrecy.
