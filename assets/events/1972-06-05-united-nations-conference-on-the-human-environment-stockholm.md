In 1972, the world suddenly realized maybe trashing the planet wasn't such a great idea. From June 5-16, 1972, representatives from 113 nations convened in Stockholm, Sweden, for the United Nations Conference on the Human Environment. Chaired by the dynamic Canadian diplomat Maurice Strong, this landmark event wasn't just a talk-shop; it marked the formal birth of the international environmental movement. It led to the creation of the UN Environment Programme (UNEP) and the adoption of the Stockholm Declaration, a set of principles for preserving and enhancing the human environment. Basically, it was humanity's first big 'uh-oh, maybe we should clean up our act' moment on a global scale.
