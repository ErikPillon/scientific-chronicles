On July 5, 1996, a little lamb named Dolly entered the world, but it wasn't until February 1997 that the world collectively gasped. Dolly was no ordinary sheep; she was the first mammal successfully cloned from an adult somatic cell. Her creation at Scotland's Roslin Institute by Ian Wilmut and Keith Campbell was a monumental breakthrough, proving that cells from an adult animal could be reprogrammed to create a whole new identical organism. This wasn't just a fluffy story; it ignited a firestorm of ethical, philosophical, and scientific debates, while also opening new avenues for research into regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and understanding genetic development. Dolly lived a full life for a cloned sheep, and her legacy continues to echo through bioethics and science journals.
