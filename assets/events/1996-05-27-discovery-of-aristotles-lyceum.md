For centuries, the exact location of Aristotle's Lyceum, the philosophical school where the great man taught his students while strolling (giving rise to the term 'Peripatetic school'), was lost to time and urban development in Athens. Then, in 1996, during excavations for a new museum, archaeologists hit the jackpot! They unearthed ruins matching ancient descriptions of the Lyceum, including a gymnasium and palaestra. It was like finding the intellectual birthplace of Western philosophy, confirming a key historical site and giving us a tangible, almost touchable connection to one of history's most influential thinkers. Suddenly, Aristotle's walks with his students felt a little more real.
