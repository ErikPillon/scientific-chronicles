In the midst of the American Revolution, while cannons roared and independence was fought for, a different kind of revolution was brewing in Boston. On May 4, 1780, with a charter signed by John Hancock, the American Academy of Arts and Sciences was born. Brainchild of luminaries like John Adams, this isn't just an old club; it's one of the nation's oldest and most prestigious learned societies, created to "cultivate every art and science which may tend to advance the interest, honor, dignity, and happiness of a free, independent, and virtuous people." Essentially, they wanted to gather the brightest minds to make America smart and strong. From its revolutionary roots, it continues to bring together leaders from across disciplines to tackle big ideas, ensuring America's intellectual firepower keeps burning bright.
