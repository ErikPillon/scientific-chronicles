Picture this: it's 1885, and horses are still the dominant mode of personal transport. Then, Gottlieb Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach — two visionary German engineers — file a patent that would change everything. On August 29, 1885, they secured the rights for their 'Reitwagen' (riding car), a wooden-framed contraption with a single-cylinder internal combustion engine. Though it looked more like a glorified velocipede with a motor than today's sleek machines, it officially holds the title for the world's first true motorcycle patent. It was clunky, probably terrifying to ride, and definitely loud, but it kicked off a revolution that led to every roaring hog and zippy scooter we see today. Talk about starting a trend!
