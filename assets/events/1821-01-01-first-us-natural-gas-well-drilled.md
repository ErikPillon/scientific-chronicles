Before electricity was even a twinkle in Edison's eye, folks were looking for better ways to light things up. Enter Fredonia, New York, in 1821, where William Aaron Hart decided to try something a little different. Instead of just digging for water, he decided to drill a well specifically to tap into the natural gas seepages that had been observed in the area. And guess what? He hit gas! This wasn't some accidental stumble; it was a deliberate attempt to harness a new energy source. Imagine the excitement: free fuel bubbling up from the ground! This pioneering well led to the first commercial use of natural gas in the U.S., lighting homes and a local hotel with a cleaner, brighter flame than whale oil or candles could provide. It was a modest beginning, but it kickstarted an industry that would eventually power a nation, proving that sometimes, the best ideas are right beneath your feet.
