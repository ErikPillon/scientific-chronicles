In the mid-19th century, while conventional medicine was still grappling with bleeding and purges, a new approach called homeopathy was gaining traction, much to the chagrin of many traditional doctors. This alternative system, based on the principle of 'like cures like' and highly diluted remedies, found its institutional footing in the U.S. with the founding of the American Medical College of Pennsylvania in 1848. Later known as Hahnemann Medical College (after the founder of homeopathy, Samuel Hahnemann), it was one of the first dedicated institutions to formally teach homeopathic principles and practices. This event wasn't just about opening a school; it signaled the rise of a parallel medical system that challenged orthodox beliefs, creating a lively (and sometimes heated) debate within the medical community that continues, in various forms, to this day. It truly marked a fork in the road for medical education and practice in America.
