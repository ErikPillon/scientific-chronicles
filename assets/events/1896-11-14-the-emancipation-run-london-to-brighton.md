Imagine a world where cars were slower than a person walking and had to be preceded by a flag-waving pedestrian. That was Britain before November 14, 1896! The "Emancipation Run" was a joyous, slightly chaotic procession from London to Brighton, celebrating the repeal of the infamous Red Flag Act. This antiquated law had mandated a 4 mph speed limit in rural areas (2 mph in towns) and required a man with a red flag to walk ahead of any self-propelled vehicle. The new Locomotives on Highways Act raised the speed limit to a dizzying 14 mph (though later set at 12 mph) and ditched the flag-bearer. About 33 brave drivers, including Walter Arnold in his new Benz, embarked on the 52-mile journey, a real adventure for early motorists and their sputtering contraptions. It wasn't just a drive; it was a defiant roar of modernity, marking the true beginning of the motorcar era in Britain and signaling a shift from a horse-drawn past to a petrol-powered future.
