While artificial hearts had been around, the AbioCor was a game-changer: the first fully self-contained total artificial heart approved for human use. Meaning, no external wires or bulky power sources were sticking out of the patient! Developed by the company Abiomed, the AbioCor was designed to replace both ventricles of a failing heart. On July 3, 2001, it made history when it was successfully implanted into Robert Tools, a 59-year-old man, at Jewish Hospital in Louisville, Kentucky. While it wasn't a permanent solution (patients typically lived for months, not years), it represented a monumental leap in bioengineering and critical care medicine, demonstrating the possibility of extending life for those with no other options. It showed that with enough ingenuity, humans could build a machine that kept the most vital organ ticking.
