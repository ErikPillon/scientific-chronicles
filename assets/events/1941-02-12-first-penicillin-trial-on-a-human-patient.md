While Alexander Fleming famously discovered penicillin's antibacterial properties in 1928, it was the persistent work of Howard Florey and Ernst Chain's team at Oxford that brought it to human patients. On February 12, 1941, Police Constable Albert Alexander, gravely ill with a life-threatening bacterial infection, became the first person to receive the 'miracle drug.' The initial results were dramatic and hopeful; Alexander began to recover! Unfortunately, due to limited supplies, the treatment couldn't be sustained, and he later died. Despite this tragic outcome, the trial unequivocally proved penicillin's incredible therapeutic potential in humans. This pivotal moment kicked off the frantic race to mass-produce the antibiotic, forever changing medicine and saving countless lives during World War II and beyond. Talk about a true game-changer!
