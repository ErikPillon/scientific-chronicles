Before anyone truly mastered the helicopter, Emile Berliner, already a sound recording pioneer, decided he wasn't done with innovation. Along with his son Henry, he dived into the dizzying world of vertical flight. Their big moment arrived on July 16, 1907. Picture this: a clunky, yet revolutionary, helicopter design, featuring two 16-foot diameter rotors powered by a 36 hp engine. The goal? To lift a human off the ground. And boom! It did just that, carrying its pilot three feet into the air. While it wasn't exactly soaring through the clouds, this flight was a monumental leap. It demonstrated that a heavier-than-air machine could indeed lift itself and a passenger vertically, laying crucial groundwork for all the sky-high acrobatics helicopters perform today. Berliner proved that sometimes, you just need a little lift to change the world.
