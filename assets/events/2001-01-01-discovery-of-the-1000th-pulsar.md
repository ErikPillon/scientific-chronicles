Imagine finding a celestial lighthouse, furiously spinning and sweeping beams of radiation across the cosmos. That's a pulsar for you! These super-dense, rapidly rotating neutron stars are remnants of massive stellar explosions, and their discovery in 1967 was a huge deal. Finding just one was mind-blowing, but imagine finding a thousand! While pinpointing the *exact* 1,000th discovery is like trying to catch starlight, astronomers hit this major milestone around the early 2000s, showcasing the incredible progress in radio astronomy. Each new pulsar adds to our understanding of extreme physics, gravitational waves, and the bizarre end-states of stars. It's a testament to global scientific collaboration and endless hours spent scanning the radio skies for those tell-tale cosmic "beeps."
