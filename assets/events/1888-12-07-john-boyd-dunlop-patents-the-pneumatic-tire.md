Before Dunlop, cycling was quite literally a pain in the backside. Bicycles rattled along on solid rubber tires, making every bump a memorable (and uncomfortable) experience. Enter John Boyd Dunlop, a Scottish veterinarian who, in 1887, watched his son struggling on his tricycle. His eureka moment? Inflating rubber tubes with air! He wrapped his son's tricycle wheels with canvas-backed rubber tubes filled with air, and presto – a smoother ride. He patented his 'pneumatic tire' in 1888. This ingenious invention didn't just make cycling more enjoyable; it dramatically improved grip and speed, eventually becoming essential for automobiles and aircraft. Dunlop’s invention proved that sometimes, the best way to tackle a bumpy road is with a little bit of air and a lot of ingenuity!
