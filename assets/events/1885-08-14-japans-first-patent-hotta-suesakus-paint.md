In the wake of the Meiji Restoration, Japan was rapidly modernizing, eager to catch up with Western industrial powers. A crucial step was establishing a modern patent system. On August 14, 1885, the first Japanese patent was officially granted to Hotta Suesaku for "Hotta Paint." This wasn't some flashy gadget, but a durable, rust-preventing paint, perfect for ships and industrial machinery, reflecting Japan's focus on foundational industrial development. This seemingly mundane invention marked a monumental moment: the beginning of a formal system to protect intellectual property in Japan, directly fueling its industrial growth and setting the stage for Japan's future as a global innovation powerhouse.
