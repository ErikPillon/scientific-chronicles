Ever wondered what the first piece of officially recognized Canadian genius looked like? Well, on August 16, 1869, the newly formed Dominion of Canada issued its very first patent, Patent No. 1, to a rather clever fellow named Thomas C. Keefer. His stroke of brilliance? A novel "mode of raising and removing obstructions from rivers, canals, etc." — essentially, a super-efficient way to dredge and clear waterways. In an era when rivers and canals were the highways of commerce, Keefer's invention was a big deal for keeping trade flowing smoothly. This patent marked the official beginning of Canada's intellectual property system, a cornerstone for innovation and protecting the brilliant (and sometimes quirky) ideas that would shape the nation. It showed that even from its nascent stages, Canada was ready to foster its own homegrown innovators.
