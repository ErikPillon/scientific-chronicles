In 1984, the world held its breath for Baby Fae, an infant girl who became the unlikely focus of a monumental medical experiment and ethical firestorm. Born with a fatal heart defect, she underwent a groundbreaking, but highly controversial, xenotransplantation procedure on October 26, 1984. Surgeons at Loma Linda University Medical Center, led by Dr. Leonard Bailey, transplanted the heart of a baboon into the tiny baby. The goal was to extend her life, but the procedure pushed the boundaries of medical ethics, raising questions about animal rights, informed consent, and the very definition of 'experimental.' Baby Fae lived for 21 days with the baboon heart, a remarkable feat but ultimately a tragic outcome when her body succumbed to rejection. Her case, while a failure in terms of survival, undeniably ignited crucial discussions that shaped future regulations and public understanding of xenotransplantation and medical ethics, cementing her place in medical history.
