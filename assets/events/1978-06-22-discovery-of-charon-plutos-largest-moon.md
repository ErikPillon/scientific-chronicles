Back in 1978, astronomer James Christy was having a bit of a headache at the US Naval Observatory. He was meticulously examining highly magnified photographic plates of Pluto, trying to refine its orbital data, when he noticed something odd: a slight, recurrent 'bulge' on some of the images. This wasn't just photographic blur, oh no. After some clever deduction, Christy realized that Pluto wasn't alone; it had a rather substantial companion – a moon so big, it makes Pluto wobble! He named it Charon, after the mythical ferryman who carried souls across the river Styx (and, conveniently, after his wife, Charlene). This discovery completely changed our understanding of Pluto, turning it from a solitary icy rock into a binary system, long before it was reclassified as a dwarf planet. Charon's sheer size relative to Pluto (it's about half Pluto's diameter!) is still a jaw-dropper in the solar system.
