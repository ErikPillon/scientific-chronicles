In 1957, a groundbreaking feat of engineering saw the completion of the Consolidation Coal pipeline in Ohio. This wasn't just any pipe; it was the first long-distance coal slurry pipeline in the United States, stretching a hefty 108 miles. The idea was simple but brilliant: grind coal into a fine powder, mix it with water to create a 'slurry,' and then pump it like a liquid. This innovative method promised to drastically cut the cost and effort of transporting massive quantities of coal, offering a sleek, efficient alternative to trains and barges. It was a pioneering moment in energy infrastructure, proving that coal could flow!
