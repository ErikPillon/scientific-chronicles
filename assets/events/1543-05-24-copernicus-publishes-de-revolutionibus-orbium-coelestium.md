Talk about a mic drop! In 1543, just before his death, Polish astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus published his monumental work, *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium* (On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres). This wasn't just another star chart; it was a radical proposal that turned the prevailing geocentric view of the universe (Earth at the center, everything else orbiting it) completely on its head. Copernicus dared to suggest that the Sun was actually the center, and Earth, along with other planets, revolved around it. This heliocentric model, initially met with skepticism and later condemnation, was a slow-burning fuse that eventually ignited the Scientific Revolution. While his model wasn't perfect (he still used circles for orbits!), it provided a much simpler explanation for planetary motions and laid the essential groundwork for Kepler, Galileo, and Newton. It took courage to challenge centuries of dogma, and Copernicus certainly delivered!
