Before the ambulance, wounded soldiers were often left on the battlefield for hours, or even days, until fighting stopped. That’s a rather grim waiting game, isn't it? Enter Dominique Jean Larrey, Napoleon's chief surgeon, who revolutionized military medicine in the late 18th century by inventing the 'flying ambulance' – a swift, horse-drawn cart designed to evacuate wounded soldiers *during* battle, not just after. This concept, literally taking the hospital to the field, drastically improved survival rates. The idea eventually spread to civilian life, with the first hospital-based ambulance service appearing in Cincinnati in 1865. From its battlefield origins to the flashing lights and sirens of today, the ambulance has continuously evolved, ensuring that critical medical care can reach people swiftly, proving that sometimes, simply getting there fast makes all the difference.
