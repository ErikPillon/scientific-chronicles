Before DSLRs and Instagram, capturing the cosmos on film was an unimaginable feat. Yet, on March 2, 1840, American scientist John William Draper achieved just that, taking the very first daguerreotype of a star – Vega – from his rooftop observatory in New York City. This wasn't a pristine, Hubble-esque image; it was a tiny, fuzzy speck of light after a 20-minute exposure. But it was revolutionary! It proved that celestial objects beyond our solar system could be permanently recorded, opening up the entire universe to photographic study. This humble image launched the field of astrophotography, forever changing how we observe, analyze, and marvel at the stars.
