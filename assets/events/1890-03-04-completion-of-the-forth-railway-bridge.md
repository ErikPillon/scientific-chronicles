In a display of Victorian engineering prowess that still wows today, the Forth Railway Bridge officially opened in 1890. Designed by Sir John Fowler and Sir Benjamin Baker, this colossal cantilever bridge wasn't just a railway crossing; it was a symbol of ingenuity and the industrial age. Spanning the Firth of Forth in Scotland, its distinctive red steel structure was a game-changer, demonstrating the strength and potential of steel in bridge construction. Before this, large-scale bridges were often iron or suspension designs. The Forth Bridge's innovative cantilever design and sheer scale (it was the longest single cantilever bridge span in the world for years!) proved that complex problems could be solved with bold, scientific engineering, linking Scotland's east coast like never before. It even has its own verb: 'to paint the Forth Bridge' – a metaphor for an endless task!
