Before patents became a global industry, there was Venice. This bustling hub of trade and innovation, ever keen to protect its clever citizens, passed a revolutionary law on March 19, 1474. The Venetian Patent Statute wasn't just a one-off grant; it was the first general patent law in history, offering a ten-year monopoly to inventors who introduced new and useful devices to the Republic. Imagine, an entire city saying, 'Hey, your brilliant idea? We'll protect it so you can make some ducats!' This forward-thinking legislation created a framework that encouraged invention by ensuring creators could profit from their hard work, laying the essential groundwork for every patent system that followed. It proved that good ideas aren't just for show; they're worth protecting, especially when they boost the economy!
