When AIDS emerged, there were no effective treatments, leading to devastating outcomes. The search for a cure was desperate. Zidovudine, better known as AZT, was originally synthesized in 1964 as a potential anti-cancer drug, but it was shelved. Decades later, during the frantic search for AIDS treatments, researchers at the National Cancer Institute and Burroughs Wellcome (now GlaxoSmithKline) discovered AZT's potent antiviral activity against HIV. Its rapid development and subsequent FDA approval on March 19, 1987, was a monumental breakthrough. While not a cure and coming with significant side effects, AZT was the *first* drug to offer hope, extending and improving the lives of people with AIDS. It proved that HIV was treatable and kickstarted the development of many more effective antiretrovirals, fundamentally changing the course of the epidemic. It was a true game-changer!
