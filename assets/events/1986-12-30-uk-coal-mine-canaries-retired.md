For over 70 years, canaries were the chirping, feathered frontline of coal mine safety in the UK, detecting deadly carbon monoxide before it could harm miners. But in a bittersweet moment on December 30, 1986, the last of these avian heroes were officially retired. The reason? Progress! More reliable, humane, and frankly, less prone to avian dramatics, electronic gas detectors had taken over. It marked the end of an era, a quaint but effective piece of industrial history giving way to modern technology, ensuring safety without needing a feathered fatality to sound the alarm.
