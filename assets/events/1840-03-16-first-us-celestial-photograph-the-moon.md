In the early days of photography, everyone wanted to snap a selfie with the cosmos. Enter John William Draper, a New York University professor, who, on March 16, 1840, pointed his daguerreotype camera and telescope at the Moon. After a patient 20-minute exposure, he captured what's widely considered the first successful photograph of a celestial object in the United States, and potentially the world's first clear astronomical photograph. While it wasn't a crystal-clear HD image by today's standards, it was a giant leap for astro-photography, proving we could document the universe beyond what our eyes could see. Take that, starry night painters!
