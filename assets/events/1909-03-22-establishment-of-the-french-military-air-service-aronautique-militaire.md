Before fighter jets and strategic bombers, there was the nascent 'Aéronautique Militaire,' France's pioneering military air service. While the formal, independent *Armée de l'Air* came later in 1934, the foundations were laid much earlier, with military aviation officially becoming a branch of the army on March 22, 1909. Initially a motley collection of balloons and early biplanes, this service quickly recognized the strategic potential of flight. Imagine a handful of daring aviators, with little more than canvas and wood, soaring above battlefields, charting enemy movements! This was a pivotal moment, signaling the transformation of warfare from two-dimensional ground combat to a dizzying, three-dimensional aerial ballet. France, a leader in early aviation, was among the first to grasp the eagle's eye view.
