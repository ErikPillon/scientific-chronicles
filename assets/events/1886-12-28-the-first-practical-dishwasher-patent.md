Tired of her valuable china getting chipped by clumsy servants, Josephine Cochrane, a well-to-do Illinois socialite, decided enough was enough. Instead of just complaining, she took matters into her own hands (literally!) and designed a machine to wash dishes more safely and efficiently. On December 28, 1886, she secured a patent for her hand-powered contraption, featuring wire compartments to hold dishes and a motor that sprayed soapy water. While it was initially marketed to hotels and restaurants, and only later became common in homes, Cochrane's ingenuity laid the groundwork for the modern dishwasher, sparing countless hands from prune-like fingers and countless plates from untimely demise. She proved that necessity truly is the mother of invention, especially when that necessity involves protecting your fancy dinnerware!
