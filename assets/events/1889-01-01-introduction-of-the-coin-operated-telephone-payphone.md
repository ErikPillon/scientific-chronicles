Before mobile phones, needing to make a call on the go was a genuine challenge. Enter the public payphone, courtesy of William Gray! Frustrated by his inability to make an emergency call, Gray invented the coin-operated telephone, receiving a patent in 1889. The first public payphone was installed in a bank in Hartford, Connecticut, that very year. This ingenious device allowed anyone to make a call for a small fee, typically by dropping coins into a slot. It was a democratizing moment for communication, making telephony accessible to the general public, not just those with private lines. The iconic payphone quickly became a fixture of urban life, connecting people one coin at a time.
