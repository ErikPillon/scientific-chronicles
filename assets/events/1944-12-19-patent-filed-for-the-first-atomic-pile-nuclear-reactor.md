Before we had nuclear power plants generating electricity, we had the atomic pile. On December 19, 1944, brilliant physicists Enrico Fermi and Leó Szilárd, key figures in the Manhattan Project, filed the patent for their groundbreaking invention: the nuclear reactor. This wasn't just a theoretical concept; they had already built and successfully operated the world's first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction in Chicago in 1942. Their patent was for the apparatus that would control this powerful reaction, laying the blueprint for all future nuclear reactors. It was a critical step not just for the atomic bomb, but also for unlocking the peaceful potential of nuclear energy, promising a future powered by the atom.
