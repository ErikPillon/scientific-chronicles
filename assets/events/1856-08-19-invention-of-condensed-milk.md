Imagine a world without fresh milk for weeks on end—that was the reality for sailors and pioneers in the 19th century. Enter Gail Borden Jr., a truly innovative thinker who, after seeing children die from contaminated milk during a sea voyage, dedicated himself to finding a solution. On August 19, 1856, he secured a patent for his process of making condensed milk, essentially removing most of the water and adding sugar to preserve it. This wasn't just a sweet treat; it was a lifesaver, providing safe, nutritious milk that could be stored for extended periods. Thanks to Borden, milk could go where it had never gone before!
