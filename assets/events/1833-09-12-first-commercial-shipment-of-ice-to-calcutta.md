Imagine trying to get ice to India in the 1830s. Sounds impossible, right? Not for Frederic Tudor, the audacious 'Ice King' from Boston. After years of perfecting insulation and logistics (and losing a fortune in the process), his ship, the *Tuscan*, finally arrived in Calcutta on September 12, 1833, laden with 180 tons of pristine New England ice. This wasn't just about cold drinks; it was a groundbreaking feat of global commerce, demonstrating that perishable goods could be transported across vast distances. Tudor’s enterprise connected continents, brought luxury to the tropics, and literally cooled down an entire continent. Now that's what you call a cool business model!
