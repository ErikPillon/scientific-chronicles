While Sigmund Freud was busy extolling cocaine's supposed therapeutic virtues (and perhaps having a little too much fun with it), it was his colleague, Karl Koller, who stumbled upon its true medical marvel. In 1884, Koller, then an ophthalmologist, recognized cocaine's remarkable ability to numb tissues. On September 15, 1884, he famously demonstrated its use as a local anaesthetic for eye surgery, a groundbreaking moment that spared countless patients from agonizing pain. This discovery swiftly transformed surgical practices, particularly in ophthalmology, making delicate procedures far more humane. It was a sharp, if controversial, turning point in the history of pain management.
