In 1976, two simultaneous outbreaks of a mysterious, deadly hemorrhagic fever erupted in Sudan and Zaire. Doctors were baffled until a team, including Dr. Karl Johnson and Dr. Peter Piot, identified a new, filament-shaped virus. Named after the Ebola River in Congo, near one of the outbreak sites, this discovery marked the arrival of a pathogen that would become synonymous with rapid, devastating epidemics. It sent shivers down spines and kicked off decades of intense research into one of humanity's most formidable viral foes. Talk about a dramatic entrance!
