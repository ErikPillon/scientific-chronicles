In the late 19th century, electricity was the dazzling new kid on the block, sparking innovation but also a fair bit of chaos. To bring some order and intellectual horsepower to this electrifying field, the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (AIEE) was founded on May 13, 1884. This wasn't just any club; it brought together the heavy hitters of the time, including early members like Thomas Edison and Alexander Graham Bell. Their mission? To advance the theory and practice of electrical engineering and its allied arts and sciences. Essentially, they were the original electrical gurus, laying down the standards and fostering the discussions that would literally power the modern world. In 1963, the AIEE merged with the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) to form the mighty IEEE, continuing its legacy of shaping the technological future.
