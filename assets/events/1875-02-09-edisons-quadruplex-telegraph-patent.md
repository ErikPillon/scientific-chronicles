Before the internet, there was the telegraph, and Thomas Edison was absolutely instrumental in making it sing – or rather, send four messages simultaneously! In 1875, he was granted a patent for his groundbreaking quadruplex telegraph. This wasn't just a tweak; it was a major leap forward, allowing two messages to be sent in each direction over a single telegraph wire. Think of it as the original broadband! This invention dramatically increased the capacity and efficiency of telegraph lines, reducing costs and speeding up communication across vast distances. It was a massive commercial success, demonstrating Edison's early genius in electrical engineering and his knack for solving practical problems with brilliant, scalable solutions.
