In August 1888, the automobile was a cool invention, but most people thought Karl Benz's 'Motorwagen' was just a novelty, suitable only for short jaunts. Enter Bertha Benz, Karl's intrepid wife, who decided to change that perception – dramatically! Without telling Karl, she hopped into the Benz Patent-Motorwagen No. 3 with her two teenage sons and set off from Mannheim to Pforzheim, a journey of about 66 miles (106 km). This wasn't a joyride; it was a daring proof-of-concept. She had to find fuel at pharmacies (ligroin, a petroleum solvent), use her hatpin to clean a blocked fuel line, and even improvise brake lining repairs with her garter strap! When a steep incline proved too much for the engine, her sons pushed. Upon arrival, she telegraphed Karl, informing him of her success and the car's few design flaws. This 'Benz car tour' wasn't just a road trip; it was a marketing masterclass and a pivotal moment for the automobile industry. Bertha's courageous journey demonstrated the car's reliability and potential, silencing critics and sparking public interest. She literally drove the invention into the future, cementing her place as a true automotive pioneer.
