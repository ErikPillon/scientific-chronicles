Before electric cars and hybrid wonders, there was a simpler, more explosive dream: the gasoline engine. While invented in Germany by Nikolaus Otto, its commercial journey in the United States truly began around 1876 when the Philadelphia-based company Schleicher, Schumm & Co. started manufacturing Otto's four-stroke internal combustion engine under license. This wasn't just any engine; it was the first widely available and successful gasoline-powered engine for commercial use in the U.S. No longer confined to steam power or the limited reach of electric motors, industries could now harness portable, powerful energy. This innovation was a game-changer, providing reliable power for factories, workshops, and eventually, the nascent automobile industry. It literally sparked a revolution, laying the groundwork for everything from personal cars to power tools, forever changing the face of American industry and transportation. Talk about an engine of change!
