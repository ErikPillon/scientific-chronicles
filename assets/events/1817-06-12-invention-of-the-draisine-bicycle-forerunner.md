Imagine a world without bicycles – unthinkable, right? But before the two-wheeled wonders became ubiquitous, personal transport was mostly horses or your own two feet. Then came Karl Drais, a German baron and inventor, who, in 1817, unveiled his 'Laufmaschine' (running machine) or 'Draisine.' This ingenious contraption was essentially a steerable wooden frame with two wheels, but crucially, no pedals. Riders propelled themselves forward by pushing off the ground with their feet, much like scootering. Drais gave its first public demonstration on June 12, 1817, riding it for several miles near Mannheim. The Draisine wasn't just a toy; it offered a much faster way to get around than walking, especially after volcanic eruptions in 1815 led to widespread crop failures and a shortage of oats for horses, making personal transport a serious problem. While a far cry from the modern bicycle, Drais's invention was the foundational ancestor, sparking a flurry of innovations that eventually led to the pedal-driven velocipede and the 'safety bicycle' we recognize today. It truly set the wheels in motion for a personal transport revolution!
