Building tunnels under a massive body of water like the Hudson River was an engineering nightmare in the late 19th century. The first attempt, starting in 1874 under DeWitt Clinton Haskin, faced immense difficulties, including deadly blowouts and financial woes, earning it the nickname 'the graveyard of engineers.' Work stopped and started multiple times over decades! Eventually, with new technology like the shield method and a push from figures like William Gibbs McAdoo, the first passenger tubes, now part of the PATH system, were finally completed in 1908. It was a Herculean effort that redefined urban connectivity and pushed the boundaries of civil engineering, making it possible for millions to commute between New Jersey and New York City without getting wet.
