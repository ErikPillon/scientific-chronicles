Bovine growth hormone (bGH), or bovine somatotropin (bST), is a protein hormone naturally produced by cows that regulates milk production and growth. Its growth-promoting effects were first identified in the 1930s, with purification efforts solidifying its understanding by mid-century. However, the real "event" in scientific history was the development of recombinant bGH (rBGH) in the 1980s, which allowed for its mass production using genetic engineering. Approved by the FDA in 1993, rBGH was then used commercially to increase milk yield in dairy cows. This biotechnological leap sparked massive debate about food safety, animal welfare, and agricultural economics, forever changing the discussion around genetic engineering in food production.
