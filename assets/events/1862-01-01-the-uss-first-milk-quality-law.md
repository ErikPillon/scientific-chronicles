Before pasteurization and refrigeration were common, milk quality was a wild, often dangerous, affair. Enter the 'swill milk' scandal of 19th-century New York City, where cows were fed distillery waste, leading to sickly animals and contaminated milk. Dr. Stephen Smith, a public health pioneer, was instrumental in sounding the alarm. To combat this public menace, New York City passed an ordinance in 1862, marking the United States' very first attempt to regulate milk quality. It was a crucial (and overdue!) step towards ensuring that what ended up in your glass wasn't a potential health hazard, setting a precedent for food safety regulations nationwide.
