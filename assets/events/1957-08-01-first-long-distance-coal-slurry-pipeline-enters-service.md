Just months after its completion, the Consolidation Coal pipeline in Ohio roared to life in August 1957. This was the moment the dream of pumping coal hundreds of miles became a reality. The pipeline, stretching 108 miles, started moving a continuous stream of coal slurry from Cadiz to a power plant near Cleveland. It was a bold step, demonstrating the viability and efficiency of this novel transport method. Engineers had to overcome challenges like maintaining slurry consistency and preventing pipe clogging, but they pulled it off. This operational debut proved that pipelines weren't just for oil and gas; coal was ready for its liquid journey, too!
