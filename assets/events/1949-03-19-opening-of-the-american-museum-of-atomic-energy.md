Remember when atoms were all the rage (and a little terrifying)? Well, in 1949, someone decided it was time to show the public what all the fuss was about, sans the mushroom clouds. The American Museum of Atomic Energy opened its doors in Oak Ridge, Tennessee – the secret city where the bomb was largely concocted. This wasn't just dusty exhibits; it was a way to educate people about nuclear physics, the atom bomb's development, and the promise of atomic energy. From Geiger counters to models of reactors, it was the ultimate atomic show-and-tell, aiming to demystify the atom and, perhaps, make it a little less scary for the average Joe. It was a bold move to bring the 'secret city's' science to the masses, later becoming the American Museum of Science and Energy.
