Before he gave us light bulbs and phonographs, a young Thomas Edison made his official entry into the world of invention by filing his very first patent application on June 1, 1869. The invention? An electric vote recorder designed to speed up legislative processes. Ironically, politicians hated it, preferring the slower, more deliberate manual system. Though it wasn't a commercial success, this moment marked the beginning of Edison's prolific patenting career, a journey that would see him register over a thousand patents and become one of history's most celebrated inventors. Every genius has to start somewhere!
