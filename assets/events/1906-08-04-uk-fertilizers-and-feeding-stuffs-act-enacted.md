Before the early 20th century, farmers often bought fertilizers and animal feeds without much guarantee of their quality or composition, leading to potential fraud and poor agricultural outcomes. The UK's Fertilizers and Feeding Stuffs Act of 1906 aimed to bring some order to this wild west of agriculture. This landmark legislation required sellers to provide purchasers with a written statement guaranteeing the analysis of certain fertilizers and feeding stuffs. It was a crucial step in consumer protection for farmers and helped standardize the agricultural industry, ensuring that a 'bag of fertilizer' actually contained what it promised. No more secret ingredients, folks – just good old-fashioned chemistry, regulated by law!
