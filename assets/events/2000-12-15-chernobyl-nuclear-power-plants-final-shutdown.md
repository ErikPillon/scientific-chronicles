On December 15, 2000, Chernobyl's Reactor No. 3, the last operational unit at the infamous plant, was officially shut down in a ceremony attended by then-President Leonid Kuchma of Ukraine. This closure, almost 15 years after the catastrophic explosion of Reactor No. 4, was a symbolic and practical milestone, fulfilling an agreement with international bodies like the G7. It marked the end of an agonizing chapter, transitioning the site from power generation to a long, complex process of decommissioning and containment. The plant that had once symbolized Soviet ambition and later global nuclear fear was finally silent, though its legacy continues to resonate.
