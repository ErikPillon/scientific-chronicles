For decades, AT&T, often called 'Ma Bell,' held a near-monopoly on telephone services in the United States, controlling everything from long-distance calls to local lines and even telephone manufacturing. But by the 1970s, the U.S. government argued this dominance stifled innovation and competition. After a lengthy antitrust lawsuit, a monumental agreement was reached. On January 1, 1984, AT&T was officially broken up into a much smaller long-distance company and seven independent 'Baby Bells' (local operating companies). This wasn't just corporate restructuring; it was a seismic shift that revolutionized the telecommunications industry, opening the door for new competitors, technologies, and eventually, the internet era we know today. Customers suddenly had choices, and the phone company wasn't just one giant behemoth anymore!
