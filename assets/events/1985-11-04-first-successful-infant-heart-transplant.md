Imagine being a tiny baby, just days old, with a heart so broken it can't sustain life. In the mid-1980s, this was a death sentence. Then came Dr. Leonard Bailey at Loma Linda University Medical Center, a pioneer who dared to challenge the impossible. While controversial early attempts (like the 'Baby Fae' baboon heart transplant) garnered headlines, Bailey achieved a landmark in 1985: the first successful human-to-human heart transplant in an infant. This wasn't just a surgical feat; it opened up a whole new world of possibilities for neonatal cardiac care, giving countless tiny patients a fighting chance at life. It was a huge leap for medicine, showing that even the smallest hearts could get a second chance.
