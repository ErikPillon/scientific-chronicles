Imagine trying to string power lines across vast distances, only to find copper too heavy and expensive. Enter aluminium conductors! Once the Hall-Héroult process made aluminium cheap and plentiful (around the 1890s), engineers quickly realized its potential. It's lighter than copper for the same conductivity and more cost-effective. The first significant use for electrical transmission began in the mid-1890s, particularly for long-distance power grids. This was a game-changer for electrification, allowing electricity to reach further and more economically. Without these lighter, more affordable wires, our expansive power grids might look very different—and certainly more expensive! It's a testament to practical innovation meeting material science, making modern power distribution possible.
