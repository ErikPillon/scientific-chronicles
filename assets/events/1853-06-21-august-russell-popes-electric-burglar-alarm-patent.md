Before smartphones could alert you to a rogue squirrel, protecting your valuables meant a barking dog or a hefty lock. But in 1853, August Russell Pope had a brighter idea: let electricity do the talking! He secured a patent for an electric burglar alarm that used magnets and a battery to detect when a door or window was opened, triggering a bell. While earlier, cruder mechanical alarms existed, Pope's invention was a leap forward, bringing more reliability and the potential for networked systems. Edwin Holmes later commercialized this idea, making the electric alarm a staple for banks and eventually homes. This clever piece of engineering wasn't just about protecting your stuff; it was about inventing peace of mind, proving that even a tiny electrical current could be a formidable deterrent against unwelcome visitors.
