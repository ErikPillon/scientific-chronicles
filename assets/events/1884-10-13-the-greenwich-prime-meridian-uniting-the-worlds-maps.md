Ever wondered why your map starts at a specific longitude? Thank the International Meridian Conference of 1884, which officially adopted the meridian passing through the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London, as the world's Prime Meridian. This wasn't some arbitrary choice; for centuries, astronomers and navigators had been making meticulous observations from Greenwich. The decision meant that East and West could finally agree on a universal zero point for longitude, revolutionizing cartography and making global navigation infinitely more precise. It was a massive international agreement that quite literally put Greenwich on the map as the center of the world's geographic understanding!
