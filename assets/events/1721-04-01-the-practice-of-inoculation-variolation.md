Before vaccines, there was inoculation, sometimes called variolation! This wasn't a discovery in the traditional sense, but a practice observed for centuries in places like China, India, and Africa before making its way to Europe. The idea was simple but daring: deliberately expose someone to a mild form of a disease (like smallpox, using material from a less severe case) to trigger immunity, hoping they'd get a less dangerous illness than the full-blown epidemic. It was brought to the Western world's attention in the early 18th century, notably by Lady Mary Wortley Montagu and Cotton Mather. While risky, it drastically reduced smallpox mortality and was a crucial, albeit crude, precursor to Edward Jenner's safer vaccination methods. Pretty gutsy move for public health!
