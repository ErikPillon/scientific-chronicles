Back in the early days of aviation, flying was already pretty daring. Doing it at night? That was next-level stuff. On July 2, 1910, pilot Charles K. Hamilton strapped himself into his Curtiss biplane at Mineola, New York, and, with a bit of improvisation (like hanging a lantern on the plane!), ascended into the evening sky. Imagine navigating without modern instruments, just a flickering light and the stars! This wasn't just a stunt; it was a testament to human ingenuity and the relentless push to conquer the skies, day or night. Before Hamilton, pilots generally stuck to daylight hours for obvious safety reasons. His pioneering flight proved that with courage and a bit of daring, the night was no longer a barrier to air travel, opening up new possibilities for everything from mail delivery to military reconnaissance. He literally saw the future in the dark!
