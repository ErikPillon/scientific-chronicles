For a long time, figuring out how dinosaurs grew up was pure guesswork. Then, in the late 1970s, paleontologists Robert Makela and Jack Horner hit the jackpot in Montana's "Egg Mountain." They unearthed nests brimming with fossilized eggs and, astonishingly, some contained exquisitely preserved embryos of a small dinosaur named *Orodromeus makelai* (and later, *Maiasaura*). Published in 1979, this wasn't just finding a few bones; it was like getting a prenatal ultrasound from 75 million years ago! These tiny, curled-up bundles of joy offered unprecedented insights into dinosaur reproduction, growth, and even social behavior, fundamentally changing our understanding of these ancient giants from their very first days.
