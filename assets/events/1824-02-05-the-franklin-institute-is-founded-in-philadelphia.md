On February 5, 1824, a group of Philadelphia citizens founded the Franklin Institute, named in honor of Benjamin Franklin, with the goal of promoting mechanical and technical education and practical science for ordinary tradespeople and craftsmen, not just elite academics. It quickly became a hub for American invention and industry, running technical courses, publishing an influential scientific journal, and famously conducting one of the first rigorous engineering investigations into steam boiler explosions, helping establish early safety standards for the technology powering the industrial revolution. The Institute later expanded into public science education, building a major science museum and planetarium that still draws visitors today, and it continues to award the prestigious Franklin Medals recognizing outstanding achievement in science and engineering. Two centuries on, and it's still doing exactly what it set out to do. 🏛️⚙️
