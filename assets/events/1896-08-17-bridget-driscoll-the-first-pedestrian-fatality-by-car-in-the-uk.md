The arrival of the motor car brought promises of speed and progress, but also unforeseen dangers. On August 17, 1896, Bridget Driscoll, a 44-year-old mother, became the UK's first pedestrian fatality involving a motor car. As she crossed the grounds of Crystal Palace in London, she was struck by an Anglo-French Motor Carriage driven by Arthur Edsell, an employee of the company. The jury's verdict was 'accidental death,' with the coroner famously declaring he hoped such an event would 'never happen again.' Sadly, it was a grim foreshadowing of the millions of lives that would be impacted by the rise of automotive traffic, marking a somber milestone in the history of transport.
