Picture this: it's the 1960s, and speed is king. Enter Donald Campbell, a man with gasoline in his veins and a thirst for breaking barriers inherited from his equally legendary father, Sir Malcolm Campbell. On December 31, 1964, on the glassy surface of Lake Dumbleyung in Australia, Campbell strapped himself into his jet-powered hydroplane, the iconic Bluebird K7. With a roar and a spray, he shattered the world water speed record, pushing the craft to an astonishing 276.33 mph. This wasn't just fast; it was historic! Why? Because earlier that year, he'd also set the land speed record. This made him the only person ever to hold both land and water speed records in the same year. Talk about a New Year's Eve fireworks display! Campbell was a true daredevil, constantly pushing the limits of engineering and human endurance, often with a cheeky grin and a twinkle in his eye, right up until his tragic final attempt in 1967. A true legend of speed!
