Imagine a wave taller than the Empire State Building. Sounds like a sci-fi movie, right? But on July 9, 1958, this terrifying reality unfolded in Lituya Bay, Alaska. A colossal magnitude 7.8 earthquake shook the region, causing an enormous rockslide – an estimated 90 million tons of rock and ice – to plummet into the narrow bay. This sudden displacement of water generated a gargantuan wave that surged up the opposite mountainside, reaching an incredible run-up height of 1,720 feet (524 meters). While the actual wave height in the open water was smaller, its sheer power devastated everything in its path, stripping forests clean from the slopes. It was a stark and terrifying reminder of the Earth's raw, unbridled power, giving scientists invaluable data on the mechanics of megatsunamis and how geological events can unleash such destructive natural phenomena. Thankfully, only a few lives were lost due to the remote location, but the landscape was irrevocably scarred.
