On March 11, 1931, the shovels officially hit the desert floor (or rather, the blasts started going off) for the Hoover Dam. While preliminary work like road and railway construction was already underway, this date marked the formal commencement of the massive undertaking to build what would become one of the world's most iconic dams. This wasn't just another construction project; it was an ambitious response to an untamed river and a struggling economy. Thousands of workers, seeking employment during the Great Depression, flocked to the desolate Black Canyon, ready to take on unimaginable challenges. This start date wasn't just a bureaucratic formality; it signaled the beginning of a relentless, five-year battle against nature, heat, and gravity, laying the groundwork for a structure that would redefine the American Southwest. The desert was about to get a serious makeover!
