After a revolutionary run, the French Republican Calendar, with its decimal days and poetic nature-themed months, finally met its maker on January 1, 1806. Who delivered the coup de grâce? None other than Napoleon Bonaparte. While it had been a powerful symbol of revolutionary fervor, its practical disadvantages—especially in a Europe largely operating on the Gregorian calendar—were becoming insurmountable. Imagine trying to coordinate international diplomacy or trade when your days, weeks, and even years are completely out of sync! Napoleon, ever the pragmatist, saw the calendar as an obstacle to stability and integration. So, with a stroke of his pen, France returned to the good old Gregorian, putting an end to a fascinating, if short-lived, experiment in timekeeping.
