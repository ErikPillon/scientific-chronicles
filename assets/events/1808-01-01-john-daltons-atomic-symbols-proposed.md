Before John Dalton came along, chemistry was a bit of a free-for-all when it came to notation. In 1808, as part of his groundbreaking atomic theory, Dalton introduced a set of distinctive circular symbols for each known element – a dot for hydrogen, a shaded circle for nitrogen, and so on. While these symbols eventually gave way to the simpler, letter-based system we use today (thanks, Berzelius!), they were revolutionary for their time. They provided a visual, intuitive way to represent atoms and how they combined, helping to standardize chemical communication and bringing much-needed clarity to the nascent field of atomic theory.
