The Galileo spacecraft, a true Jupiter enthusiast, had an epic run from 1989 to 2003. After orbiting the gas giant for eight years, meticulously studying Jupiter and its icy moons, its fuel was running low. To prevent any chance of it accidentally crashing into and contaminating potentially life-harboring moons like Europa – a big no-no for planetary protection – NASA decided on a dramatic finale. On September 21, 2003, Galileo was intentionally commanded to plunge into Jupiter's crushing atmosphere, burning up like a glorious meteor. It was a bittersweet farewell to a mission that reshaped our understanding of the Jovian system, providing unprecedented data on its atmosphere and moons, proving that even robots can go out with a bang!
