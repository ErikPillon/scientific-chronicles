Having detonated its first atomic bomb in 1952, Britain quickly realised that the nuclear arms race was rapidly moving towards something far more powerful: the hydrogen bomb. Not wanting to be left behind by the US and USSR, and still keen to maintain an independent deterrent, Britain embarked on its own thermonuclear weapon programme. This culminated in Operation Grapple X, on May 15, 1957, when a British H-bomb was detonated high above Malden Island in the Pacific Ocean. The blast was enormous, significantly more powerful than the atomic bombs dropped on Japan, and confirmed Britain's status as a formidable nuclear power. Led once again by the indomitable William Penney, this test was a colossal scientific and engineering feat, though not without its ethical complexities. It solidified Britain's position in the global power structure, proving that even a smaller nation could build a very big bang, forever altering the landscape of international relations.
