Before "test-tube babies" and advanced fertility treatments, there was Walter Heape and his groundbreaking rabbit experiment in 1890. Heape, a British reproductive physiologist, successfully transferred an embryo from one Angora rabbit to a Belgian Hare, resulting in the birth of healthy Angora offspring from the Belgian Hare mother. This wasn't just a neat parlor trick; it was the very first successful embryo transfer in mammals, a true "Eureka!" moment that proved the concept of moving an early-stage embryo from one uterus to another. This pivotal discovery cracked open the door for entirely new avenues in reproductive biology, paving the way for improved livestock breeding and, much later, human in vitro fertilization (IVF). Heape's humble rabbit experiment was a giant leap for reproductive science, demonstrating that gestation could be separated from genetic parentage and laying the essential biological groundwork for the fertility revolution that would follow decades later.
