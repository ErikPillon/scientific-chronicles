While the Wright brothers were pioneering flight in America, across the pond, a flamboyant Texan-turned-Brit, Samuel Franklin Cody, was making his own aerial waves. On October 5, 1908, after numerous attempts and a bit of a dramatic flair, Cody successfully piloted his British Army Aeroplane No. 1 for a flight of 424 yards at Farnborough. It was the first sustained, controlled, powered flight on English soil, and it caused quite a stir! Cody, known for his Wild West shows before turning to aviation, was a self-taught genius who built his aircraft largely from bamboo and canvas. This daring flight kickstarted British military and civilian aviation, proving that the skies above England were also ripe for conquest. Take that, Channel!
