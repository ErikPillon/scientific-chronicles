Forget Mars; in 2005, all eyes were on Titan, Saturn's largest and most mysterious moon, as the Huygens probe plunged through its thick, orange atmosphere. This was no ordinary landing: Huygens, part of the Cassini-Huygens mission, was the first probe ever to land on a moon other than Earth's. After a seven-year journey hitching a ride with Cassini, Huygens detached and parachuted down, transmitting data for hours from a world eerily similar to early Earth, complete with liquid methane lakes and rivers. Its images revealed a landscape carved by exotic liquids, hinting at complex chemistry and the tantalizing possibility of life. This audacious feat, a triumph of international collaboration, gave us our first real peek at a truly alien world, proving that sometimes, you just have to go land on a giant, frozen orange to understand the universe a little better.
