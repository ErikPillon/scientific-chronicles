Before weather satellites and radar, curious minds looked up and thought, "How do we get up there?" The answer? Balloons! While hot-air balloons got the party started, it was Jacques Charles and his hydrogen balloon on December 1, 1783, that truly opened the doors to "free air meteorological observations." Sure, it was a thrill to fly, but scientists quickly realized these floating marvels could carry instruments high above the ground, measuring temperature, pressure, and humidity in previously inaccessible atmospheric layers. It was less about gazing at clouds and more about sticking a thermometer into them, fundamentally changing how we understood the complex dynamics of our planet's atmosphere. It marked the moment meteorology literally took flight.
