Steam engines were great, but Werner von Siemens had a brighter, cleaner vision for the future of rail travel. At the Berlin Industrial Exhibition, opening on May 29, 1879, he unveiled his groundbreaking electric locomotive. It wasn't just a static display; it was a fully functional electric train, pulling three small passenger cars along a 300-meter circular track. While experimental electric vehicles existed earlier, Siemens' creation was the first to demonstrate practical, reliable passenger transport, drawing power from a third rail. It was a truly electrifying preview of the silent, swift, and smoke-free future of railways, sparking a global shift away from steam.
