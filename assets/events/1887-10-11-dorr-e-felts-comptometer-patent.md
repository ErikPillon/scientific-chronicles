While the idea of mechanical calculation goes back centuries (think Pascal's Pascaline in 1642), the 'Calculator patent' often brings to mind the commercially successful and impactful inventions of the late 19th century. A significant one was Dorr E. Felt's Comptometer, patented on October 11, 1887 (U.S. Patent No. 371,368). This wasn't just another adding machine; it was the first successful key-driven mechanical calculator, meaning you didn't have to pull levers or turn cranks to input numbers. You simply pressed keys, and the answer appeared. It revolutionized office work, speeding up accounting and bookkeeping immensely. Felt's genius lay in creating a machine that was both fast and relatively easy to use, making complex calculations accessible to a broader range of people and laying groundwork for future advancements in office automation. It was a true leap forward in making numbers behave!
