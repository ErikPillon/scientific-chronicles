Before the mRNA revolution, there was another groundbreaking moment in vaccine history: the debut of the first genetically engineered vaccine. On July 23, 1986, the FDA gave its nod to Recombivax HB, a vaccine for Hepatitis B. This wasn't just another shot; it was a marvel of biotechnology. Instead of using weakened or dead viruses, scientists engineered yeast cells to produce a specific protein from the Hepatitis B virus. This protein, when injected, safely triggered an immune response without exposing anyone to the actual virus. It was a massive leap forward, proving that genetic engineering could create safer, more effective vaccines, and paving the way for countless future innovations in fighting infectious diseases. A true game-changer, proving science could outsmart pathogens in entirely new ways.
