While much of the Western world had slowly, and sometimes grudgingly, adopted the Gregorian calendar over centuries, Japan stuck to its traditional lunisolar calendar, often aligning with the Chinese calendar. That all changed on January 1, 1873. As part of the sweeping Meiji Restoration reforms, which aimed to modernize and Westernize Japan, the government officially switched to the Gregorian calendar. This wasn't just a simple date change; it was a profound symbolic move, aligning Japan with the international standards of trade and diplomacy. It brought an end to centuries of unique timekeeping and streamlined Japan's integration into the global system, making business and international relations much smoother. From then on, Japanese New Year fell on January 1st, just like in Europe and America, marking a significant step in Japan's rapid transformation into a global power.
