Back in the early 20th century, drinking milk was a bit like playing Russian roulette; it could be contaminated with all sorts of nasty bacteria, leading to diseases like tuberculosis and typhoid. But in 1908, Chicago decided enough was enough. It became the first major U.S. city to pass a mandatory milk pasteurization law, ensuring that all milk sold within its limits was heated to kill off those pesky germs. This wasn't a small thing; it was a monumental stride in public health, drastically reducing milk-borne illnesses and making one of the most basic food staples safe for consumption. It laid the groundwork for food safety regulations across the nation and changed how we viewed and consumed dairy forever, saving countless lives.
