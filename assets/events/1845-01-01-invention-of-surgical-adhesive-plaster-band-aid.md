The idea of an 'adhesive plaster' for wounds has a history longer than you might think. While various forms of medicated plasters existed, the concept of a self-adhesive bandage really took off in the mid-19th century. One key figure was Horace Day, who received a patent in 1845 for an adhesive plaster suitable for surgical dressings. However, the true household hero, the pre-packaged 'Band-Aid,' was invented much later. In 1921, Earle Dickson, a cotton buyer for Johnson & Johnson, created the Band-Aid for his accident-prone wife. He combined a strip of surgical tape with a piece of gauze, covering it with crinoline to keep it sterile. His boss loved the idea, and Johnson & Johnson began mass-producing them. This simple, brilliant innovation transformed first aid, making it easy for anyone to quickly cover and protect minor cuts and scrapes, practically inventing the concept of DIY wound care.
