Who knew a super-heavy, man-made element could be so practical? Californium-252, a particularly sprightly isotope, became the unsung hero of the coal industry, starting in the 1970s. Its superpower? Emitting neutrons. These neutrons could be used in devices called Prompt Gamma Neutron Activation Analysis (PGNAA) systems to continuously monitor the quality of coal as it moved on conveyor belts. Instead of taking samples and waiting for lab results, miners could instantly check for sulfur, ash, and moisture content, optimizing their operations and making sure they weren't sending out dirty coal. It’s like having an X-ray vision scanner for rocks – truly a triumph of applied nuclear physics!
