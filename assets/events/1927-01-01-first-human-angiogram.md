Ever wonder how doctors peek inside your arteries and veins without a scalpel? Thank the ingenious invention of the angiogram! While early experiments with contrast agents happened around the turn of the 20th century (often in animals), the first successful diagnostic angiogram in humans is widely credited to Portuguese neurologist Egas Moniz in 1927. He used a contrast medium to visualize the cerebral arteries, opening up a whole new world for diagnosing brain conditions. Later, daring self-experiments by Werner Forssmann in 1929, who threaded a catheter into his own heart (yes, really!), pushed the boundaries further. This technique revolutionized cardiology and radiology, allowing doctors to spot blockages, aneurysms, and other vascular issues, transforming treatment and saving countless lives. It's essentially an internal roadmap for our circulation!
