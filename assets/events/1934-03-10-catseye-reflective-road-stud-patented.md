Picture this: a foggy night, no streetlights, and a desperate need for better road markings. Enter Percy Shaw, a Yorkshire inventor who, according to legend, was inspired by seeing how cat's eyes reflected his car's headlights. In 1934, he patented his brilliant solution: the 'catseye' reflective road stud. It's a remarkably simple device, essentially a rubber dome with two pairs of glass spheres that act like retroreflectors, embedded in a cast iron casing. When a car passes over it, the rubber dome depresses, cleaning the reflectors. This ingenious invention was a game-changer for road safety, especially during blackout conditions in World War II and for poorly lit roads everywhere. It quite literally lit up the way for drivers, preventing countless accidents and becoming a ubiquitous symbol of safer travel.
