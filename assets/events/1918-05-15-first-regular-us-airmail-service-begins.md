Imagine needing a letter to get across the country *fast* in the early 20th century. Before May 15, 1918, you were out of luck if trains weren't quick enough. That day, the U.S. Post Office Department launched its first regular airmail service, connecting Washington D.C., Philadelphia, and New York City. Army pilots, like the famous Max Miller who flew the inaugural leg from Washington to Philadelphia, initially piloted the biplanes. Despite some early hiccups (including Miller initially flying in the wrong direction!), airmail quickly proved its value, cutting delivery times dramatically. This groundbreaking service showcased aviation's commercial potential and laid crucial groundwork for the passenger airline industry, making the world feel a little smaller and much faster.
