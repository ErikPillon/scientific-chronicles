Before the Argand diagram, complex numbers were a bit, well, complex and abstract for many. Swiss mathematician Jean-Robert Argand came along and, in 1806, published a brilliant idea: why not just *draw* them? By representing complex numbers as points or vectors on a 2D plane (now famously called the Argand plane), suddenly their addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division made intuitive geometric sense. It was like giving numbers a physical address, making them much easier to visualize and manipulate. While others had similar ideas, Argand's clear exposition made his name stick to this indispensable tool for mathematicians and engineers alike. It truly brought complex numbers down to earth, literally!
