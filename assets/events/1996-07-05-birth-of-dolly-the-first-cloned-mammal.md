On July 5, 1996, the world quietly welcomed a sheep that would change everything: Dolly. Born at Scotland's Roslin Institute, Dolly wasn't just any lamb; she was the first mammal successfully cloned from an adult somatic cell. Led by Ian Wilmut and Keith Campbell, the team's groundbreaking work used a technique called somatic cell nuclear transfer, essentially taking a cell from an adult ewe and fusing it with an unfertilized egg. When her birth was announced to the public in February 1997, it sent shockwaves through the scientific community and society alike, igniting fierce debates about ethics, the nature of life, and the very limits of genetic engineering. Dolly wasn't just a scientific marvel; she was a symbol of humanity's ever-expanding power to reshape life itself, proving that sometimes, even a sheep can be a biological rockstar.
