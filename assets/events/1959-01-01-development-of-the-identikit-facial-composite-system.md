Before CSI and fancy computer imaging, if you needed to catch a crook based on an eyewitness description, you often relied on an artist's sketch. Enter the Identikit in 1959, conceived by American police captain Hugh C. MacDonald. This ingenious system provided law enforcement with a kit of standardized transparent overlays – think different hairstyles, noses, eyes, mouths, and chins – that could be combined to build a composite facial image. It was like a pre-digital Mr. Potato Head for crime solvers! While perhaps a bit clunky by today's standards, the Identikit was a revolutionary step forward, allowing police departments worldwide to create more consistent and accurate suspect profiles, giving witnesses a more structured way to describe features, and putting countless bad guys behind bars.
