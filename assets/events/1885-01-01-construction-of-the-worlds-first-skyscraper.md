Before 1885, buildings were limited by load-bearing walls, meaning they got thicker and heavier the taller they got – not ideal for prime city real estate! But in Chicago, a city rebuilding after a devastating fire, a revolutionary idea literally took shape. William Le Baron Jenney designed the Home Insurance Building, completed in 1885, standing a modest (by today's standards) 10 stories tall. Its secret? An internal steel frame that supported the building's weight, rather than the exterior walls. This wasn't just a tall building; it was the world's first true "skyscraper," a term coined to describe these ambitious structures reaching for the heavens. It completely transformed urban architecture, allowing cities to grow vertically and ushering in the era of the modern metropolis. Suddenly, the sky was literally the limit for urban development.
