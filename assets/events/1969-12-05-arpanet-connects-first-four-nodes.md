Back in the late 1960s, a futuristic concept emerged from the U.S. Department of Defense's Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA): a robust, decentralized computer network. On December 5, 1969, ARPANET achieved a monumental feat: it successfully linked four separate computer 'nodes.' These were located at UCLA, Stanford Research Institute (SRI), UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah. While the very first message ('LOGIN') famously crashed after the 'G', this four-node connection proved the network's foundational design. It wasn't just a bunch of wires; it was the birth of packet switching, distributed computing, and the very architecture that would evolve into the global internet. The world was about to get a whole lot more connected, and it all started with these four nodes saying 'hello' to each other (or at least, trying to)!
