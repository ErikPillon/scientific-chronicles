In August 1960, NASA launched Echo 1, a colossal Mylar balloon satellite designed purely to reflect radio signals. Just three days later, scientists successfully bounced a phone call from Holmdel, New Jersey, to Goldstone, California, via the satellite. Then, for the real show-stopper, they used it to beam signals across the Atlantic. While not a practical communication method (it was literally a passive reflector!), this high-altitude party balloon proved that satellite communication was no longer sci-fi fantasy. It was the first "hello world!" from space, kickstarting the era of global satellite connectivity that lets you FaceTime your aunt in Australia today.
