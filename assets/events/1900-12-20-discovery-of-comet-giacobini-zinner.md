This icy wanderer isn't just any old comet; it's a cosmic celebrity! Michel Giacobini first spotted it in 1900, with Ernst Zinner independently rediscovering it in 1913, confirming its periodic nature. Comet Giacobini-Zinner is perhaps most famous for two things: the annual Draconid (or Giacobinid) meteor shower, which occasionally puts on a spectacular show when Earth passes through its dusty tail, and its historic rendezvous in 1985. That year, the International Cometary Explorer (ICE) spacecraft zoomed past it, becoming the first probe to directly fly through a comet's tail. Talk about a close encounter of the cosmic kind! It showed scientists exactly what makes a comet tick, long before we started landing on them. A true pioneer in space exploration and a regular skywatcher's delight.
