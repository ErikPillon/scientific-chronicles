After scientists like Henry Faulds and Francis Galton had established that fingerprints were unique and permanent, the challenge was to turn this biological marvel into a practical tool for catching crooks. The moment that truly sealed the deal for 'Fingerprint ID' in the justice system happened in Argentina in 1892. Police investigator Juan Vucetich successfully used bloody fingerprints left at a crime scene to identify Francisca Rojas, who had murdered her two children. This wasn't just a theory; it was a real-world, irrefutable piece of evidence that led to a conviction. This pioneering case demonstrated the undeniable power of dactyloscopy (the fancy word for fingerprint analysis), quickly leading to its adoption by police forces worldwide, making it much harder for criminals to literally leave no trace. Suddenly, everyone had a permanent, unique calling card they couldn't ditch!
