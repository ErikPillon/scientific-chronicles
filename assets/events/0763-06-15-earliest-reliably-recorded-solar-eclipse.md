Picture this: it's the 15th of June, 763 BC, and the sky over Assyria goes dark. Instead of panicking, some sharp-witted scribes, likely at the ancient city of Assur, wrote it down. This isn't just a cool anecdote; this Assyrian solar eclipse record is one of the earliest and most reliably dated astronomical events in human history. It became a Rosetta Stone for ancient chronology, allowing historians to pin down the dates of kings and events in Mesopotamia. Who knew a cosmic shadow play could be so useful for untangling the past?
