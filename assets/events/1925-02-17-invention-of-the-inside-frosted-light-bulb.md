Remember those old clear light bulbs? They were bright, sure, but often blindingly glary, casting harsh shadows. General Electric engineer Marvin Pipkin had a brilliant idea in the 1920s: what if we frosted the *inside* of the bulb instead of the outside? The problem with outside frosting was that it weakened the glass and collected dust. Pipkin developed an acid etching process that created a smooth, diffused surface on the *inner* side of the glass. This innovation significantly reduced glare, made the light more uniform, and was much easier to clean, all while keeping the bulb strong. It was a simple, yet elegant solution that made artificial lighting much more pleasant and widely adopted. A truly illuminating invention!
