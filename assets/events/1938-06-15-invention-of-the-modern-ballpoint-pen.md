For centuries, fountain pens caused inky messes and frustrated writers. But in 1938, Hungarian journalist László Bíró, tired of smudged newspapers and constantly refilling, teamed up with his chemist brother György to create a pen using quick-drying ink and a tiny rotating ball that dispensed it. While American inventor John J. Loud patented a similar concept in 1888 for writing on rough surfaces like wood, the Bíró brothers perfected the design for paper, making it commercially viable. Their invention, patented on June 15, 1938, was a game-changer, eventually replacing messy inkwells and becoming the ubiquitous, ever-reliable writing tool we all know and love today.
