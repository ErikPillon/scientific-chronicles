On July 25, 1909, amidst choppy waters and uncertain winds, French aviator Louis Blériot climbed into his monoplane, the Blériot XI, and pointed it across the English Channel. After a nail-biting 37-minute flight, navigating by compass and the roar of his engine (he famously got lost for a bit!), he crash-landed near Dover Castle, becoming the first person to fly across the Channel. This wasn't just a stunt; it proved the practical potential of aircraft for international travel and defense, shrinking the world and inspiring countless future aviators. Plus, he won £1,000 for his troubles – talk about a high-flying reward!
