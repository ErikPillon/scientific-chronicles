On November 13, 1577, the prodigious Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe observed a spectacular comet. But this wasn't just any pretty celestial light show; it was a game-changer for astronomy. By carefully measuring its parallax (or lack thereof, compared to the Moon), Brahe definitively proved that the comet was *beyond* the Moon's orbit and therefore traversing the supposedly immutable, crystalline "celestial spheres" of the Aristotelian cosmos. This observation directly challenged the ancient Greek model of the universe, where everything beyond the Moon was perfect and unchanging. Brahe's groundbreaking work, though not fully heliocentric, paved the way for future astronomers like Kepler to dismantle the old order and build a new understanding of our dynamic solar system.
