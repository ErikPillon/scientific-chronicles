Before fancy digital displays and apps, getting real-time stock quotes was a challenge, often relying on runners and chalkboards. The automatic electric stock quotation board, building on Thomas Edison's improved stock ticker of 1871, brought a new level of efficiency to financial markets. These early 20th-century boards, often large and clunky, became the nerve centers in brokerage houses. They flashed ever-changing numbers almost instantly, providing crucial, up-to-the-minute information. It was a vital step in making financial markets more dynamic and accessible (relatively speaking), letting traders react to news faster than ever, and likely causing a lot of frantic shouting in the process. This automation fundamentally changed the pace of trading.
