Before San Francisco's iconic hills were conquered by their charming, clanging cable cars, getting around was a real chore for horses and people alike. Enter Andrew Smith Hallidie, a Scottish-American engineer and wire rope manufacturer. Inspired by seeing horses struggle and slip on wet cobblestones, he conceived of a system where streetcars would be pulled by a continuously moving cable beneath the street. The first successful commercial cable car line, Hallidie's Clay Street Hill Railroad, began operation on August 1, 1873. It was an immediate sensation, a marvel of Victorian engineering that transformed urban transportation, making previously inaccessible hilly areas viable for development. The cable car became a symbol of ingenuity and a beloved, enduring fixture in cities like San Francisco.
