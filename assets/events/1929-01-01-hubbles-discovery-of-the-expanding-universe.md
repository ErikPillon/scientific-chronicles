Picture this: for centuries, folks thought the universe was pretty much a static, unchanging place. Then along came Edwin Hubble in the late 1920s, armed with his telescope and an insatiable curiosity. Building on earlier observations by Vesto Slipher and theoretical work by Georges Lemaître, Hubble meticulously measured the distances to galaxies and their 'redshifts' (how fast they seemed to be moving away from us). His stunning conclusion? The farther a galaxy was, the faster it zipped away. This wasn't just a quirky observation; it was undeniable proof that the universe is expanding, like a giant cosmic balloon inflating. This groundbreaking discovery reshaped cosmology forever, giving birth to the Big Bang theory and fundamentally changing our place in the cosmos. Suddenly, the universe had a history, a beginning, and a whole lot of room to grow.
