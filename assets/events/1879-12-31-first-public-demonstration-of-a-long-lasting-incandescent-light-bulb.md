Before electric lights, nights were truly dark, lit by flickering gas lamps or dangerous candles. Then came Thomas Edison, the wizard of Menlo Park, and his quest for a practical, long-lasting light bulb. After countless experiments, he finally hit on a carbonized cotton thread filament, which burned for an astonishing 13.5 hours. On New Year's Eve, December 31, 1879, he threw open his laboratory doors for a public demonstration that was nothing short of magical. Thousands flocked to witness the glow of his bulbs, envisioning a future bathed in steady, safe light. This wasn't just an invention; it was a revolution, paving the way for electrification of homes and cities, extending working hours, and fundamentally reshaping modern life. Edison didn't just invent a bulb; he lit up the world.
