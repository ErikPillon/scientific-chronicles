Think hybrids are a new thing? Think again! The "gas-electric automobile" wasn't a futuristic concept, but an early solution to motoring's challenges, pioneered over a century ago. One of the earliest, and arguably most famous, was Ferdinand Porsche's Lohner-Porsche "Mixte" system, first demonstrated around 1900. This clever design used a gasoline engine to power a generator, which then supplied electricity to motors in the wheel hubs, or to batteries. Belgian engineer Henri Pieper also developed a notable hybrid system around the same time. It was a sophisticated bit of kit for its era, addressing issues like range anxiety and the limitations of early battery technology. While fully gasoline cars eventually dominated due to cost and complexity, these early hybrids proved that combining electric and internal combustion power was a brilliant idea – one that savvy engineers would revisit much later to solve modern environmental and efficiency challenges.
