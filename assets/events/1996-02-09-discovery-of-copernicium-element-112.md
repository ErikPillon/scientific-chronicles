The GSI team in Darmstadt just couldn't stop! On February 9, 1996, they added another feather to their cap: Element 112. Led by Sigurd Hofmann, researchers bombarded a lead target with zinc ions to synthesize a single atom of this new, incredibly heavy element. Its official name, Copernicium, was chosen to honor the revolutionary astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus. It’s a fantastic example of science linking seemingly disparate fields—from the subatomic realm of element creation to the grand cosmic scale of planetary motion. Who knew chemistry could be so celestial?
