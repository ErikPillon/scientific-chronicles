Before Henry Ford made cars for the masses, one man held a patent that nearly bottlenecked the entire American automotive industry. George Baldwin Selden, a lawyer and inventor, had the foresight to file a patent in 1879 for a 'road engine' that combined a light gasoline-fueled internal combustion engine with a vehicle chassis. The patent, which covered the *concept* of the automobile, was finally granted on November 5, 1895. Selden fiercely defended his patent, forcing early automakers to pay royalties, sparking massive legal battles (most notably with Ford) until the patent was ultimately limited in scope in 1911. It was a fascinating legal twist in the dawn of the automotive age, showing that sometimes, an idea can be as powerful as a machine.
