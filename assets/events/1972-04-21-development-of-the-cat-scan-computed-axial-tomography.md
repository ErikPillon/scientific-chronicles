Before the CAT scan came along, peering inside the human body without surgery was mostly guesswork with blurry X-rays. Then, in the early 1970s, Sir Godfrey Hounsfield, working at EMI (yes, the music label!), cooked up a genius idea: combine X-rays with computer processing to create detailed cross-sectional images. Independently, Allan Cormack laid the mathematical groundwork years earlier. The first clinical CAT scan was performed in 1972, and suddenly, doctors could see tumors, clots, and injuries with unprecedented clarity, transforming diagnosis and treatment forever. It was like giving doctors X-ray vision, but with way more data – a true medical marvel!
