Horses and steam engines were the laborious workhorses of American farms until John Froelich, a clever inventor from Iowa, changed the game forever. In 1892, after successfully building a gasoline-powered traction engine that could move both forward and backward, he sold his innovative machine to a group of farmers in South Dakota. This wasn't just any sale; it was the birth of the modern agricultural tractor! Froelich's invention was far more practical and efficient than its steam-powered predecessors, making farming less labor-intensive and dramatically increasing productivity. It set the stage for the widespread mechanization of agriculture, transforming vast swathes of American land into super-efficient food factories and freeing farmers from endless toil.
