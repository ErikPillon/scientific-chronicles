Before the invention of mechanical mowers, harvesting crops was back-breaking work, primarily done by hand with scythes. Then, in the mid-19th century, inventors started dreaming of easier ways to cut hay. E. Ball stepped up to the plate, patenting his 'Ohio Mower' in 1856. This machine was a significant improvement over earlier designs, known for its robust construction and effectiveness in the field. It wasn't just another piece of farm equipment; it played a crucial role in the mechanization of agriculture, drastically reducing the labor required for harvesting and increasing efficiency. Ball's Ohio Mower helped farmers tackle vast fields with unprecedented speed, feeding a growing nation and transforming the landscape of American agriculture one cut at a time.
