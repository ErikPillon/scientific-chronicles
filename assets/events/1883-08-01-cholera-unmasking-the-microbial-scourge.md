Cholera, the infamous gut-wrencher, was once a terrifying, swift killer, sweeping through populations like a phantom. For ages, folks blamed 'bad air' or divine wrath. Then came medical detective John Snow in 1854, who, with a map and some serious shoe leather, linked an outbreak in London to a contaminated water pump – a brilliant, early epidemiological feat. But it was Robert Koch in 1883 who finally put a face to the enemy, isolating *Vibrio cholerae*, the comma-shaped bacterium responsible. His discovery wasn't just a win for microbiology; it was a game-changer for public health, showing that dirty water, not bad vibes, was the real culprit. No more 'miasma' theories, just good old hand-washing and sanitation!
