Before YouTube and 24/7 news channels, newsreels were the way the world got its visual updates in movie theaters. For years, these were strictly black and white. But thanks to pioneering color processes like Technicolor, which was making strides in the 1920s, that was about to change. While finding a single "first" color newsreel is tricky due to early experimental films, the late 1920s saw Technicolor become commercially viable, leading to the production of newsreels that could finally show events in something closer to their natural hues. These early color snippets, though often using limited two-strip Technicolor, were a revelation, bringing a new dimension of realism and excitement to major events. It was a clear sign that the future of cinematic storytelling, even for news, was going to be anything but monochromatic.
