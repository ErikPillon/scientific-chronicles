Before this 1901 patent, machining metal was a slow, messy business. Tools would get red hot and instantly lose their edge, forcing workers to slow down or constantly swap out parts. Enter Frederick Winslow Taylor and Maunsel White, who, after years of experimenting at Bethlehem Steel, cracked the code: heat-treating specific steel alloys to withstand scorching temperatures. Their high-speed tool steel (HSS) meant machines could cut faster, run hotter, and work harder without batting an eyelid. This wasn't just a fancy new material; it supercharged manufacturing, making everything from car parts to turbine blades quicker and cheaper to produce. It's safe to say these guys sped up the entire industrial world, one tough bit of steel at a time.
