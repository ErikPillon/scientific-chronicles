While earlier electric railways existed, it was Frank J. Sprague's audacious system in Richmond, Virginia, that truly put electric streetcars on the map. Opened on February 2, 1888, the Richmond Union Passenger Railway wasn't a short demonstration; it was a challenging, multi-line system with steep grades, proving the viability of electric traction on a large, urban scale. Sprague engineered a comprehensive system, including an efficient overhead trolley and powerful motors, making it reliable and economically competitive. His success was a lightning bolt for urban transit, leading to the rapid adoption of electric streetcars across the globe and forever changing the face of cities.
