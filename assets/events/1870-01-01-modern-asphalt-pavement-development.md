Before asphalt pavement, roads were a bumpy, dusty, or muddy mess – think horse-drawn carriages getting stuck! While natural asphalt has been used for millennia, the development of modern 'sheet asphalt' paving came to prominence in the late 19th century. Belgian chemist Edward De Smedt is often credited with laying the first successful sheet asphalt pavement in Newark, New Jersey, in 1870, and later in Washington D.C. This innovation provided a smoother, more durable surface than cobblestones or dirt, dramatically improving transportation and setting the stage for the automobile age. It literally paved the way for modern infrastructure!
