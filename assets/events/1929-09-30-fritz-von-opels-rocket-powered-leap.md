Before Elon Musk and SpaceX, there was Fritz von Opel, heir to the German automobile empire, who loved speed and drama! On September 30, 1929, at the Rehlin airfield near Berlin, he strapped himself into the Opel RAK.1, a sleek glider fitted with 16 solid-fuel rockets. With a massive bang and a trail of smoke, the plane launched, covering about a mile and a half in just over a minute at speeds up to 100 mph before landing safely. This wasn't just a stunt; it was the world's first public manned rocket-powered flight, a dazzling demonstration of the potential of rocket propulsion for aircraft. Opel, collaborating with rocketry pioneers like Max Valier, helped propel rocketry from theoretical dream to a spectacular, albeit smoky, reality, paving the way for future space travel.
