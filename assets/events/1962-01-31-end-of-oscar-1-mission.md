In the race to space, governments dominated, but in 1961, a plucky group of amateur radio operators decided to get in on the action. Thus, OSCAR 1 (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio) was born, becoming the world's first non-governmental satellite and proving that you didn't need a superpower's budget to reach orbit. Launched on December 12, 1961, as a piggyback payload on a Discoverer mission, OSCAR 1 spent 22 days transmitting its simple 'HI' message in Morse code to thousands of amateur radio enthusiasts worldwide. It demonstrated the feasibility of orbital tracking for amateurs and paved the way for future 'ham radio in space' missions. When its batteries finally gave out on January 31, 1962, and it re-entered Earth's atmosphere a few weeks later, it left behind a legacy of citizen science, proving that space wasn't just for the pros.
