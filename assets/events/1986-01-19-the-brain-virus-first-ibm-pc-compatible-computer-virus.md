In 1986, two Pakistani brothers, Basit and Amjad Farooq Alvi, created what is widely considered the first computer virus for IBM PC compatible machines. Dubbed "Brain," it wasn't malicious in the modern sense; their intention was reportedly to protect their medical software from piracy. The virus infected the boot sector of floppy disks, displaying a copyright message and slowing down the disk drive. While not destructive, Brain was a nuisance, showing the world the potential for software to spread unwanted code. It marked a pivotal moment, shifting the focus of computing from pure creation to also include defense, forever changing the landscape of digital security and inspiring generations of antivirus software.
