Before 1877, being a baseball catcher was a fast track to a dental bill or worse. Catchers often played far behind the batter to avoid foul tips, making accurate throws a nightmare. Enter Fred Thayer, a Harvard University student who, inspired by a fencing mask, designed a wire contraption to protect the catcher's face. His teammate, James Tyng, was the brave soul to first don this revolutionary gear in an official game on April 12, 1877. Initially met with skepticism (and probably a few laughs), the catcher's mask quickly proved its worth, allowing catchers to move closer to the plate, improving gameplay, and saving countless noses and teeth. Baseball was never the same, much to the relief of catchers everywhere.
