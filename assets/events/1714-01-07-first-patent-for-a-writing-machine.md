Think typewriters are a 19th-century invention? Think again! While the practical, commercially successful typewriter arrived much later, the very first patent for a 'machine for impressing or transcribing letters singly or progressively one after another' was granted to an English engineer named Henry Mill on January 7, 1714. Yes, 1714! While no working model of Mill's device is known to exist, and his design was likely far from practical for mass production, it laid the conceptual groundwork. It proved that the idea of mechanized writing was percolating long before its technological reality could catch up. So, next time you see a keyboard, give a nod to Henry Mill, the unsung intellectual ancestor of every stroke you make. He might not have given us QWERTY, but he definitely typed the first legal 'i' in 'invention.'
