Before the electric trolley car, city streets were a messy mix of horse-drawn vehicles and clunky steam trams. Enter Frank J. Sprague, a former Edison protégé and all-around electrical wizard. On February 2, 1888, his 'Union Passenger Railway' in Richmond, Virginia, launched America's first large-scale, commercially successful electric street railway. Sprague's innovations—like the overhead trolley pole and regenerative braking—meant these zippy electric cars could climb hills and run reliably. This wasn't just a fancy new ride; it totally revolutionized urban life, making cities more accessible and expanding their boundaries faster than you can say 'all aboard!' It electrified urban transport, literally.
