In 1961, with the Space Race heating up faster than a rocket engine, America needed a hero to go where no American had gone before: space! On May 5, 1961, that hero was Alan Shepard, strapped into his Freedom 7 capsule atop a Mercury-Redstone rocket. While his flight was a suborbital hop lasting a mere 15 minutes, it was an exhilarating, nail-biting, and utterly triumphant moment for the nation. The whole world watched as Shepard ascended, performed some quick maneuvers, and splashed down safely in the Atlantic. His famous quote, "Don't f*** up, Shepard," (or the more polite version, "Let's light this candle!") perfectly captured the immense pressure and daring spirit of the mission. This wasn't just a ride; it was a defiant declaration that America was in the space game, proving that with enough courage and thrust, the sky was definitely *not* the limit.
