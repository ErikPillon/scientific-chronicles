As air travel became more prevalent in the mid-20th century, so too did the alarming rise of aircraft hijackings, prompting urgent calls for enhanced security measures. Among the many proposed solutions, inventors began to patent systems designed to prevent or mitigate these aerial threats. One such early example is US Patent 3,753,549, granted to Charles D. Miller on August 21, 1973. This patent described an "Aircraft Anti-Hijacking System" which included features for isolating the cockpit, monitoring cabin activity, and even non-lethal incapacitation methods. While the specifics of such early patents might not all have been implemented, they represent the crucial initial conceptual steps in a continuous, evolving effort to make air travel safer for everyone.
