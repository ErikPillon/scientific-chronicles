Back in May 1997, things got a bit squawky in Hong Kong when a particularly nasty strain of avian influenza, H5N1 (the 'bird flu'), decided it wasn't just for the birds anymore. This was the first time this high-pathogenicity bug was known to infect humans, causing a severe respiratory illness and, tragically, some fatalities. Cue the global health alarm bells! Authorities scrambled, leading to a massive cull of poultry in Hong Kong to prevent further spread. It was a stark wake-up call, showing just how easily diseases can hop species and highlighting the urgent need for international surveillance and rapid response systems. In short, H5N1 taught us a tough lesson about viral mischief and gave public health officials a few more grey hairs.
