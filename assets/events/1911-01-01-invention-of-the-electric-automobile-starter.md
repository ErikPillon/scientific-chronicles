In the early days, starting a car meant a risky, bone-jarring, and often arm-breaking process of hand-cranking. It was a real barrier, especially for women or those not keen on fractured limbs. This dangerous ritual tragically led to the death of a friend of Henry Leland, the head of Cadillac, spurring a demand for a safer alternative. Enter Charles F. Kettering, who developed the electric self-starter in 1911. First introduced in the 1912 Cadillac, this ingenious device eliminated the need for manual cranking, making driving safer, easier, and much more appealing to a wider public. It was a game-changer for automotive convenience and safety, transforming driving from a strenuous chore into a truly effortless experience.
