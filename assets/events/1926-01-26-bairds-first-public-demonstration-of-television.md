Imagine a world without screens! That was the reality before John Logie Baird, a Scottish engineer with a flair for invention, showcased the world's first true mechanical television system to members of the Royal Institution and a reporter from The Times. On January 26, 1926, in his London laboratory, Baird demonstrated actual moving images – a dummy's head, and later, an office boy named William Taynton, becoming the first person to be televised. It wasn't exactly HD, more like a flickering, orange-red silhouette, but it was undeniably television. This groundbreaking event wasn't just a clever trick; it laid the foundation for the electronic screens that now dominate our lives, proving that moving pictures could indeed be transmitted across distances. Talk about a glow-up!
