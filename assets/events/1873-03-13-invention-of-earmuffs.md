Picture this: it's a bone-chilling winter in Farmington, Maine, 1873. A plucky 15-year-old named Chester Greenwood is ice skating, and his ears are absolutely *freezing*. Scarves just aren't cutting it, and frankly, they cramp his style. So, like any good future inventor, Chester decided enough was enough. He fashioned two ear-shaped loops of wire, asked his ingenious grandmother to sew some beaver fur onto them, and just like that, the world's ears got a whole lot warmer. He patented his 'ear-protectors' that very year, and while he probably didn't foresee the fashion statements they'd become, Chester’s simple, ingenious solution saved countless ears from frostbite, one cozy pair at a time. It’s a classic tale of necessity birthing innovation, proving sometimes the best ideas are born from sheer discomfort (and a bit of grandma’s sewing prowess!).
