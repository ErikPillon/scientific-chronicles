Before smartphones and social media, there was smallpox, a terrifying disease that killed millions and disfigured many more. Then, along came Edward Jenner, a British physician who noticed that milkmaids who contracted the mild cowpox rarely caught smallpox. Hmm, interesting! On May 14, 1796, Jenner put his theory to the test: he took pus from a cowpox lesion on a milkmaid's hand and inoculated a young boy, James Phipps. A few weeks later, he exposed James to smallpox – and nothing happened! James was immune. This wasn't just a clever trick; it was the birth of 'vaccination' (from the Latin 'vacca' for cow), a revolutionary concept that provided safe, effective protection against a deadly disease. Jenner's brilliant, if slightly daring, experiment changed medicine forever, laying the groundwork for eradicating smallpox and inspiring countless other vaccines that protect us today.
