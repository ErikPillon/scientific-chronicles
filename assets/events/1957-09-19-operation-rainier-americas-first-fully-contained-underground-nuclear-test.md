The early days of nuclear testing involved huge, dramatic mushroom clouds polluting the atmosphere – not exactly eco-friendly. On September 19, 1957, the U.S. took its atomic fireworks underground with Operation Rainier, the first fully contained underground nuclear test. Conducted at the Nevada Test Site, this 1.7-kiloton device was detonated deep inside Rainier Mesa. The idea was to keep the radioactive fallout from reaching the surface, preventing atmospheric contamination. This wasn't just a new trick; it was a significant pivot in nuclear testing strategy, aiming for 'cleaner' tests and influencing future treaties, even if the ground did shake a fair bit!
