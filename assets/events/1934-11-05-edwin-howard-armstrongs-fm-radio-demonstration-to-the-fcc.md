Imagine tuning into the radio and all you hear is static, crackle, and interference. That was AM radio in the early 20th century. Enter Edwin Howard Armstrong, a radio genius who, in 1934, brought his incredible Frequency Modulation (FM) system to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC). He literally set up a competing AM transmitter right next to his FM one, both broadcasting the same program. The FM signal? Crystal clear, pure sound. The AM? A muddy, noisy mess. It was a jaw-dropping demonstration of FM's superior sound quality and immunity to noise, proving it was the undeniable future of radio, even if it took a while for the world (and certain established radio corporations) to fully embrace it.
