Before flatscreens and streaming, there were clunky contraptions promising 'pictures over radio.' In 1928, General Electric, under the guidance of engineer Ernst Alexanderson, made history by installing the first experimental television receivers in a few lucky homes in Schenectady, New York. These weren't exactly HD experiences; viewers tuned into transmissions from GE's experimental station WGY, watching images roughly the size of a postage stamp, complete with blurry lines and the occasional flickering phantom. Yet, this was monumental! It was the very first step in taking television out of the laboratory and into the public's imagination, hinting at a future where living rooms would be transformed into personal entertainment hubs. It might have been primitive, but those early installations were the pixelated dawn of a global phenomenon.
