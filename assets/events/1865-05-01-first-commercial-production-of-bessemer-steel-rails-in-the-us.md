Forget fragile iron! America's railways got a massive upgrade thanks to the Bessemer process, which transformed pig iron into strong, durable steel. While Henry Bessemer perfected the process in England, it was Alexander Lyman Holley who championed its adoption in the U.S. In May 1865, his Troy, New York plant, using the Bessemer converter, successfully rolled the first commercial Bessemer steel rails in America. This wasn't just about train tracks; it was a game-changer for infrastructure, manufacturing, and industry. Stronger steel meant bigger bridges, taller buildings, and a true industrial boom, literally laying the groundwork for modern America.
