On January 13, 1942, the world witnessed the very first successful operational use of an ejection seat. German test pilot Helmut Schenk was flying a Heinkel He 280 jet prototype when its controls unexpectedly froze. Rather than attempting a perilous manual bailout into the icy air, Schenk coolly deployed the aircraft's experimental compressed-air powered ejection seat, shooting clear of the crippled plane and landing safely. This dramatic escape wasn't just a minor technical triumph; it proved the technology wasn't just a quirky invention but a genuine life-saver, heralding a new era of aviation safety and giving pilots a fighting chance when things went sideways. Suddenly, a failing plane didn't necessarily mean a failing pilot.
