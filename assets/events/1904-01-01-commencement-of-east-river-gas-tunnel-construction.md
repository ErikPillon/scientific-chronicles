In the early 1900s, New York City was booming, and its energy demands were skyrocketing. But how do you get gas from an enormous production plant in Queens to the hungry consumers in Manhattan? You dig, of course – deep under the East River! The ambitious East River Gas Tunnel project kicked off in 1904, a monumental undertaking that would see engineers battling incredibly challenging subterranean conditions. This wasn't just about digging a hole; it was about creating two massive, concrete-lined tunnels, each almost a mile long, hundreds of feet below the riverbed, using then-cutting-edge shield tunneling techniques. The start of this project marked a critical step in providing reliable, large-scale energy infrastructure for one of the world's fastest-growing cities. It was the beginning of a decade-long saga of engineering ingenuity against nature, all to keep New Yorkers lit and warm.
