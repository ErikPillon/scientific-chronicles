Before there were widely prescribed sleeping pills, there was barbituric acid, the granddaddy of a whole class of sedatives. Synthesized by the brilliant German chemist Adolf von Baeyer in 1864, this compound itself wasn't directly a drug, but its discovery opened the floodgates for derivatives that *were*. Baeyer reportedly named it after a woman he knew named Barbara, or perhaps because he discovered it on St. Barbara's Day – the true story is shrouded in charming chemical folklore! While today we're more familiar with its descendants like phenobarbital, the initial creation of barbituric acid was a pivotal moment in pharmacology. It laid the foundation for effective sleep aids and anti-epileptic drugs, revolutionizing how we treat insomnia and seizures, albeit with a complex legacy of dependency.
