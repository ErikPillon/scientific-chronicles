Before 1882, cities were lit by gas lamps and the occasional flickering arc light. Then, on September 4th, Thomas Edison, ever the showman and innovator, opened his Pearl Street Station in Lower Manhattan. This wasn't just another power plant; it was the world's first commercial central power station designed to provide electricity to a wide network of customers using direct current (DC). Suddenly, 59 customers, including J.P. Morgan's office, could enjoy the steady glow of Edison's incandescent bulbs. It kicked off an electrical arms race and transformed urban life forever, making modern cities possible. Thanks, Tom, for getting us off the gas!
