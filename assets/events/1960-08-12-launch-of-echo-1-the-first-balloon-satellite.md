In 1960, amidst the Space Race, NASA decided to get reflective – literally! On August 12, they launched Echo 1, a colossal 100-foot diameter Mylar balloon satellite. It wasn't designed to send signals; it just reflected them back down to Earth. This shiny orb, visible from the ground, became the world's first passive communications satellite, proving the concept of using space for global communication. While its mission was simple, Echo 1 was a dazzling success, paving the way for the active communication satellites that now power our interconnected world. Plus, it was quite the spectacle, becoming the brightest artificial object in the night sky!
