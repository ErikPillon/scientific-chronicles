Back in 1616, Galileo was already making waves with his telescope and observations supporting Copernicus's idea that the Earth orbits the Sun. This, however, went against the Church's long-standing geocentric view. On February 24, 1616, the Holy Office formally condemned Copernicanism as 'foolish and absurd in philosophy, and formally heretical.' Cardinal Bellarmine then personally delivered a warning to Galileo, telling him not to 'hold or defend' the sun-centered model. While he wasn't forbidden from discussing it hypothetically, this stern warning set the stage for his later, more infamous trial, showing the growing friction between scientific evidence and religious doctrine. It was the calm before the storm for the rebellious astronomer.
