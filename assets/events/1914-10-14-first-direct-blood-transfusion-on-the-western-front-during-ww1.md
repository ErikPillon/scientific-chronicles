The trenches of World War I were hell on Earth, and battlefields were littered with critically wounded soldiers bleeding out. Amidst this carnage, Canadian doctor Lawrence Bruce Robertson, on October 14, 1914, performed a groundbreaking direct blood transfusion on the Western Front. Instead of relying on saline, he directly transferred blood from one soldier to another, saving a life that would otherwise have been lost. This daring procedure, though still experimental, marked a critical turning point in military medicine, laying the groundwork for the systematic blood storage and transfusion techniques that would save countless lives throughout the rest of the war and beyond. Talk about a true battlefield hero!
