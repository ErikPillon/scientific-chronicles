While the concept of cloning had long been fodder for science fiction, July 5, 1996, marked the day it definitively became scientific fact with the birth of Dolly the sheep. This event, later announced in February 1997 by the Roslin Institute team led by Ian Wilmut and Keith Campbell, demonstrated the unprecedented ability to create a genetically identical copy of an adult mammal from a somatic cell. It wasn't just about making duplicates; it was a profound proof-of-concept for nuclear reprogramming and cellular plasticity. "Cloning" became a household word, sparking intense global discussions about its ethical implications, potential benefits in medicine and agriculture, and the very definition of life. It showed us that sometimes, reality can be stranger (and woollier) than fiction.
