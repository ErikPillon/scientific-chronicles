Before the familiar sight of external iron staircases, escaping a burning multistory building often meant leaping or, well, not escaping. While rudimentary ladders existed, the concept of a permanently fixed 'fire escape' structure on a building really took off in the mid-19th century. George M. Baldridge secured a US patent in 1867 for a more robust system of balconies and ladders, aiming to provide a safer, more reliable exit path. This invention was crucial for urban safety, helping to save countless lives by offering a clear escape route when disaster struck, forever changing how buildings were designed and occupied. A truly elevated idea for public safety!
