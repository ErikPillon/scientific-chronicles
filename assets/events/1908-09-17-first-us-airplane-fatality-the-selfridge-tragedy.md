On September 17, 1908, humanity's soaring ambitions hit a snag—literally. During a demonstration flight at Fort Myer, Virginia, with Orville Wright at the controls of his Flyer, a propeller blade decided to part ways with the aircraft. The ensuing crash resulted in the tragic death of Lieutenant Thomas Selfridge, an official observer from the U.S. Army Signal Corps. This wasn't just any crash; it marked the very first fatality in a powered aircraft. Orville Wright himself was seriously injured, fracturing a leg and several ribs. Selfridge, a young military officer keenly interested in aviation, became an unwitting pioneer in the grim statistics of flight. It was a stark reminder that while the sky beckoned, it also demanded respect, and early aviation was a dangerous, often heartbreaking, endeavor. The world mourned, but the drive to conquer the skies only intensified, learning painful lessons from such a terrible day.
