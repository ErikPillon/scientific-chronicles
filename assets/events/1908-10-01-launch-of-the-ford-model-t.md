Before the Model T, cars were a luxury item, toys for the rich that often required a mechanic on standby. Then, on October 1, 1908, Henry Ford rolled out his 'Tin Lizzie,' and the world was never the same. The Ford Model T wasn't the first car, nor was it the most technologically advanced, but it was *the* car that democratized driving. Ford's genius lay not just in its sturdy, simple design, but in how he manufactured it. Its affordability, thanks to groundbreaking mass production techniques (hello, assembly line!), meant that for the first time, ordinary families could own a car. It famously came in 'any color so long as it is black' (a nod to efficiency!), and was so robust and easy to fix that nearly anyone could keep it running. The Model T didn't just transform transportation; it reshaped society, creating suburbs, driving economic growth, and giving millions unprecedented freedom. It wasn't just a car; it was a societal earthquake on wheels, proving that sometimes, revolution comes in a very practical, black package.
