In 1893, Chicago hosted the World's Columbian Exposition, and it needed a showstopper to rival the Eiffel Tower. Enter George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., a brilliant bridge builder, who delivered something truly revolutionary: the Ferris Wheel! This colossal rotating structure, standing 264 feet tall, featured 36 cars, each holding up to 60 people. It wasn't just a ride; it was a marvel of engineering, a symbol of American ingenuity, and an instant sensation, giving fairgoers unparalleled panoramic views of the exposition. It spun a new chapter in amusement park history, proving that sometimes, the best way to see the world is to go around in a giant circle, high in the sky. Quite the spin doctor, that Ferris!
