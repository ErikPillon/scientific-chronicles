Forget NASCAR – the original U.S. auto race was a far chillier affair! On a snowy Thanksgiving Day, November 28, 1895, six brave (or foolhardy) entrants gathered in Chicago for the Chicago Times-Herald Race. The challenge? Drive 54 miles from Chicago to Evanston and back, battling not just each other, but also blizzard conditions and roads that were definitely not built for speed demons. Only two vehicles actually finished the grueling course. The winner was Frank Duryea, driving a car built by his brother Charles and him, taking home a hefty prize of $2,000. This wasn't just a race; it was a grand public demonstration of the automobile's potential, capturing the public imagination and sparking widespread interest in the horseless carriage. Despite the slow speeds and arduous conditions, it cemented the car's place as more than a novelty, revving up the engine of America's automotive future.
