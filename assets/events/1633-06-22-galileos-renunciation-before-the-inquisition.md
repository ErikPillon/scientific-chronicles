On June 22, 1633, Galileo stood before the Roman Inquisition, a broken man. After months of interrogations and the subtle (or not-so-subtle) threat of torture, the 69-year-old scientist was compelled to kneel and publicly renounce his belief in the Copernican system, declaring that the Earth 'certainly does not move.' Legend has it he muttered under his breath, 'Eppur si muove' ('And yet it moves'), though there's no solid proof. This dramatic recantation, forced to avoid a harsher sentence, saw him condemned to house arrest for the rest of his life. It remains one of history's most poignant moments, highlighting the struggle between established authority and revolutionary scientific truth. A tough day to be a visionary.
