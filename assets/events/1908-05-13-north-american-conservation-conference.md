In 1908, President Theodore Roosevelt, a true nature enthusiast, gathered governors from across the United States for the "Conference on the Conservation of Natural Resources." Held at the White House, this wasn't just a fancy dinner party; it was a serious pow-wow to kickstart a national conversation about protecting America's forests, waters, lands, and mineral wealth. Teddy, always ahead of the curve, saw the writing on the wall: unchecked resource exploitation was a fast track to disaster. This shindig marked a pivotal moment, shifting conservation from a fringe idea to a national priority, paving the way for future environmental policies and reminding us that even presidents can be tree-huggers.
