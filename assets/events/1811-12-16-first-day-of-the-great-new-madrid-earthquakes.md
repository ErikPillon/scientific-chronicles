Imagine waking up to an earthquake so powerful it rings church bells in Boston, creates waterfalls in the Mississippi River, and even makes the river flow backward for a time. That's precisely what happened on December 16, 1811, marking the first of the legendary New Madrid Earthquakes. This series of massive tremors, centered in what is now the New Madrid Seismic Zone in the central United States, included some of the most powerful quakes in recorded North American history. While the area was sparsely populated at the time, the quakes dramatically altered the landscape, forming new lakes and islands. They serve as a chilling reminder that even the supposedly stable heartland of a continent can experience seismic fury on a grand scale.
