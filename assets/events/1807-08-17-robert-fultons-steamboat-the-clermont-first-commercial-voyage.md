Before Robert Fulton came along, river travel against the current was a grueling, slow affair, reliant on sails or muscle power. But on August 17, 1807, something truly revolutionary chugged its way up the Hudson River from New York City to Albany. It was Fulton's steamboat, often mistakenly called the *Clermont*, though its original name was just 'North River Steamboat.' Spectators lined the banks, some calling it 'Fulton's Folly,' expecting it to fail. Instead, it completed the journey in a record 32 hours, proving that steam power could reliably conquer rivers and revolutionize water transport. This wasn't just a boat ride; it was the dawn of a new era. Fulton's successful demonstration sparked a steamboat craze, transforming commerce and passenger travel across America's waterways and firmly establishing steam as the future of shipping.
