In 1845, Michael Faraday, never one to shy away from a good magnetic mystery, stumbled upon something bizarre: diamagnetism. While most materials are attracted to magnets (paramagnetic or ferromagnetic), he found that some, like bismuth and even water, were actually *repelled* by strong magnetic fields. It was a subtle, almost counter-intuitive effect, but it proved that magnetism wasn't just a property of iron, but a fundamental force interacting with *all* matter. This discovery expanded our understanding of material properties and laid another brick in the foundation of electromagnetism. Who knew a frog could float in a magnetic field? (Eventually, someone did, thanks to diamagnetism!)
