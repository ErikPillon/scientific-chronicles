Before the age of instant messages, sending mail was a big deal, and that meant lots of envelopes! But imagine folding every single one by hand. What a nightmare! Thankfully, clever inventors stepped up. While the exact 'first' is debated, pioneers like Edwin Hill and Warren De La Rue (around 1840-1845) in England, and later George Henry Reay in the US, developed some of the earliest successful envelope folding machines. These mechanical marvels took the tedious, time-consuming process of hand-folding and sealing envelopes and automated it, vastly speeding up mail production. This wasn't just a convenience; it made postal services more efficient and affordable, playing a quiet but crucial role in global communication. Your junk mail owes a lot to these guys!
