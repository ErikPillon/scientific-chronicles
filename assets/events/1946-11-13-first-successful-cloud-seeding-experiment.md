Ever wished you could just *make* it rain? In 1946, two brilliant General Electric scientists, Vincent J. Schaefer and Irving Langmuir, did exactly that. On November 13, 1946, Schaefer, flying in a small plane over Massachusetts, dropped three pounds of dry ice into a supercooled cloud. What happened next was pure magic (or rather, pure science): the cloud instantly transformed into snow, effectively demonstrating the first successful cloud seeding. This pioneering experiment proved that weather modification was possible, opening up possibilities for increasing rainfall in drought-stricken areas or clearing fog from airports. While its effectiveness is still debated and refined today, that chilly November day forever changed how we look at the clouds – and our audacious attempts to control them!
