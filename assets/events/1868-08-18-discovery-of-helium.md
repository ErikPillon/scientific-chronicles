Imagine finding a new element not in a lab, but in the fiery corona of the Sun! That's precisely what happened on August 18, 1868, during a total solar eclipse. French astronomer Pierre Janssen, observing the Sun's chromosphere in India, noticed a mysterious yellow spectral line (587.49 nm). Independently, British astronomer Norman Lockyer and chemist Edward Frankland made similar observations and, realizing it didn't match any known element on Earth, named it 'helium' after 'Helios,' the Greek god of the Sun. It wasn't until 1895 that William Ramsay finally isolated helium on Earth by treating cleveite, a uranium mineral. Talk about a cosmic scavenger hunt—finding an element light years away before it was under your nose!
