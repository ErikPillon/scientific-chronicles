While chairs have been around since ancient Egypt, the idea of *patenting* improvements to them really took off with the Industrial Revolution. One notable early example is Joseph B. Fenby's 1855 U.S. patent (No. 13,382) for an 'improvement in chairs' – a reclining invalid chair, aiming to provide greater comfort and adaptability. This wasn't just about protecting an invention; it marked a shift towards commercializing design and functionality in everyday objects. Suddenly, a chair wasn't just a place to sit; it was a product with features, innovations, and intellectual property. It's a tiny peek into how industrialization transformed even the most mundane items into subject matter for scientific and engineering improvement.
