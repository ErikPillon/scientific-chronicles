Imagine a world lit by gas and oil lamps – smoky, dim, and a constant fire hazard. Then came Thomas Edison's Pearl Street Station, which opened on September 4, 1882, ushering in the age of central electricity. Among its very first customers, eager to embrace this brilliant new technology, was The New York Times. Their building at 41 Park Row became one of the inaugural recipients of Edison's revolutionary power. This wasn't just about brighter newsrooms; it symbolized a major leap in urban infrastructure and industry. Suddenly, printing presses could run more reliably, offices were safer, and the future of information looked a whole lot brighter. Talk about a power upgrade!
