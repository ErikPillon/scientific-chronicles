Picture this: it's August 1831, young Charles Darwin, fresh out of Cambridge and not quite sure what to do with his life, gets a letter from his botany professor, John Stevens Henslow. The gist? 'Hey, mate, there's a spot open for a naturalist on the HMS Beagle, captained by Robert FitzRoy, for a round-the-world survey trip. Fancy it?' Darwin, originally considered too young and possibly too much of a 'gentleman' for the unpaid, arduous role, jumped at the chance. This invitation wasn't just a job offer; it was the genesis of a scientific revolution, setting Darwin on a path that would transform our understanding of life on Earth. Who knew a letter could change the world?
