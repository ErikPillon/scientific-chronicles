In an era when mail was still largely king, what better way to celebrate progress than by putting it on a stamp? On May 1, 1901, the U.S. Post Office released a series of Pan-American Exposition stamps, and among them, the 10-cent issue featured a magnificent image of the 'Best Friend of Charleston.' This wasn't just any train; it was the first steam locomotive built entirely for service in the United States and the first to carry passengers regularly scheduled service in the U.S. The stamp honored this early titan of transportation, symbolizing America's rapid industrialization and the power of the 'iron horse.' It was a tiny tribute to a monumental machine, showing how technology was moving the nation forward, one clickety-clack at a time!
