Before World War I, orthopaedics was a rather niche field, often lumped in with general surgery. But the brutal injuries sustained by soldiers on the front lines – shattered bones, amputations, debilitating deformities – highlighted the desperate need for specialized care in bone and joint surgery. Visionaries like Sir Robert Jones, a pioneering orthopaedic surgeon, and Henry Gauvain, who established one of the first hospitals dedicated to orthopaedics, spearheaded the movement to professionalize the discipline. Their efforts culminated in the founding of the British Orthopaedic Association (BOA) on January 18, 1918. Born out of necessity during wartime, the BOA aimed to advance the science and practice of orthopaedic surgery, improve patient outcomes, and establish proper training. It quickly became the leading voice for orthopaedics in the UK, transforming how musculoskeletal injuries and diseases were treated, and ensuring that future generations wouldn't have to 'grin and bear' their broken bits quite so much.
