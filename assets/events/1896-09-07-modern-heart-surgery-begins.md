For centuries, the heart was considered untouchable by surgeons, its very manipulation thought to be instantly fatal. But then came the late 19th century, and with it, groundbreaking bravery. While early attempts were often grim, the first truly successful repair of a human heart wound by German surgeon Ludwig Rehn in 1896 kicked off a revolution. This wasn't just a medical feat; it was a psychological breakthrough, proving that the heart wasn't sacredly inaccessible. It paved the way for open-heart surgery, transplants, and the incredible cardiac interventions we see today, transforming a once-forbidden realm into a bustling medical frontier where lives are routinely saved.
