Karl Benz may have invented the first automobile, but it was his gutsy wife, Bertha, who truly put it on the map. In August 1888, without telling Karl, she 'borrowed' his new Benz Patent-Motorwagen No. 3, piled her two sons in, and embarked on a daring 66-mile journey from Mannheim to Pforzheim. This wasn't a joyride; it was the world's first long-distance road trip! Along the way, Bertha had to fix mechanical issues, refuel (with ligroin from pharmacies, no less), and even use her garter to insulate a wire. Her courage wasn't just about breaking down gender barriers; it was a masterclass in marketing and engineering feedback. She demonstrated the car's practicality, highlighted its flaws, and ultimately convinced the public (and her husband) that the automobile was here to stay. Talk about taking the wheel!
