While ENIAC often gets the glory, the real unsung hero of early computing is the Atanasoff-Berry Computer (ABC). Developed between 1937 and 1942 by physics professor John Vincent Atanasoff and his graduate student Clifford Berry at Iowa State College, the ABC was the first electronic digital computing device to use binary arithmetic and regenerative memory. It didn't have a stored program like later machines, but it was purely electronic and designed to solve systems of linear equations. A 1973 court ruling officially recognized ABC as the first electronic digital computer, cementing its place as the true grand-daddy of our digital world. Sorry, ENIAC, you're more like the cool older sibling!
