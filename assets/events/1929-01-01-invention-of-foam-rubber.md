Before foam rubber, sitting down was often a firmer affair. Couches had springs and horsehair, and car seats… well, let's just say they weren't known for their plushness. All that changed around 1929 when the brilliant minds at Dunlop Rubber Co. in the UK, primarily Eric Owen and John Boyd Dunlop Jr. (yes, the son of the tire legend!), whipped up a new kind of material. Their big idea? To beat liquid latex into a frothy foam, then vulcanize it to create a lightweight, resilient, and incredibly bouncy material – foam rubber! Imagine a giant mixer making the world's bounciest meringue, but out of rubber. This innovation was a game-changer, bringing unprecedented comfort and cushioning to countless products. From mattresses that actually conformed to your body (a novel concept then!) to car seats that made long drives bearable, foam rubber made the world a softer, more comfortable place. It wasn't just an invention; it was a revolution in squishiness, proving that sometimes, the greatest leaps in comfort come from a bit of scientific fluff.
