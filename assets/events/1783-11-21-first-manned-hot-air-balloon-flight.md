Long before airplanes, humans dreamed of taking to the skies. On November 21, 1783, that dream became a dazzling reality over Paris. Piloted by the brave Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and François Laurent, Marquis d'Arlandes, a hot air balloon designed by the Montgolfier brothers gracefully lifted off from the Château de la Muette. For 25 thrilling minutes, they floated above the city, reaching an altitude of about 3,000 feet, covering roughly 5.5 miles. This wasn't just a show; it was humanity's first taste of free, untethered flight, proving that atmospheric travel was possible. It ignited a global obsession with flight and set the stage for all future aerial adventures, proving that with enough hot air (and clever engineering!), anything is possible.
