When you think "dam," you probably picture a massive concrete wall. But the Coolidge Dam, completed in 1928 in Arizona, is a bit more stylish. This isn't just any old dam; it's a pioneering example of a "dome-multiple arch" design, one of the first of its kind in the world! Its unique construction features two massive domes leaning against each other, giving it a distinctive appearance (sometimes jokingly called "Calvin Coolidge's breasts" by critics, much to the president's presumed chagrin). Built to provide irrigation water and hydroelectric power for the San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation, it was a major engineering feat of its time. Although it suffered from water scarcity issues later on, it showcased innovative structural design and was a testament to early 20th-century civil engineering, bringing much-needed resources to arid lands. It even garnered attention from architect Frank Lloyd Wright, who proposed a rather dramatic (and ultimately rejected) alternative design.
