At the turn of the 20th century, a vast, hidden network of narrow-gauge freight tunnels began snaking beneath the streets of Chicago. Starting with the first segment opening around 1901, this innovative system, eventually spanning over 60 miles, used electric trains to transport coal, ashes, packages, and even mail between downtown buildings, railway stations, and the Chicago River docks. It was a brilliant, if largely unseen, solution to decongest surface streets and improve urban logistics in a booming metropolis. Though largely abandoned by the 1950s, these subterranean passages were a marvel of early 20th-century engineering, proving that sometimes the best way to move a city is to move it underground. Out of sight, out of traffic!
