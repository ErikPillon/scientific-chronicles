Before streaming and podcasts, there was the radio, and in the UK, the biggest name in the airwaves became the BBC. It all started on October 18, 1922, as the British Broadcasting Company. Initially, it was a consortium of radio manufacturers, but it quickly evolved into something much more. Under the stern but visionary leadership of its first General Manager, John Reith, the BBC aimed not just to entertain, but to 'inform, educate, and entertain' – a motto that still guides it today. It quickly became a national institution, bringing news, music, drama, and educational programs into homes across the country. By 1927, it transitioned into the publicly-owned British Broadcasting Corporation, setting a global standard for public service broadcasting. From its humble beginnings, the BBC grew into a media titan, shaping public discourse and influencing countless lives, proving that a wireless signal could connect an entire nation.
