On September 14, 1990, a four-year-old girl named Ashanti DeSilva made history as the first human recipient of gene therapy. Suffering from severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID-ADA), a condition that cripples the immune system, Ashanti received her own white blood cells that had been genetically altered to correct the defect. This wasn't a cure-all overnight, but it was a monumental proof-of-concept, demonstrating that it was indeed possible to introduce functional genes into a patient's cells to combat disease. The scientific world held its breath, and the success of this pioneering trial, led by W. French Anderson and his team, opened the floodgates for research into using genetic alteration to mend what nature had broken, offering hope for countless future therapies.
