In the bustling era of the Industrial Revolution, the steam engine was the undisputed king of power. But even kings needed an upgrade! While James Watt was making waves with his separate condenser, Cornish engineer Jonathan Hornblower was cooking up another brilliant idea: the compound steam engine. On July 13, 1781, he secured a patent for his innovative design, which featured two cylinders. The steam would first expand in a high-pressure cylinder, then move to a second, larger, low-pressure cylinder to expand further. This 'compounding' allowed for more energy to be extracted from the steam before it was condensed, significantly improving efficiency and fuel economy. Think of it as getting a second (and third!) wind from your steam. Unfortunately for Hornblower, Watt's patent on the separate condenser was still active and broad, leading to legal battles that hampered Hornblower's commercial success. However, his ingenious concept was a fundamental advancement in thermodynamics and engine design, proving that there's always a more efficient way to harness power. Decades later, with Watt's patent expired, compound engines became standard, powering everything from factories to ships and making Hornblower an unsung hero of industrial efficiency. He certainly knew how to get the most out of a good puff!
