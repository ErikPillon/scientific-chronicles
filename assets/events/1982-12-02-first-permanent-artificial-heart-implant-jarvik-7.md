Imagine your heart failing, with no hope for a transplant. That was the grim reality for many until 1982, when Dr. William DeVries implanted the Jarvik-7 artificial heart into patient Barney Clark. Developed by Robert Jarvik, this device was a mechanical marvel designed to completely replace a failing human heart. While Clark only lived for 112 days and faced significant challenges, the Jarvik-7 proved that a permanent artificial heart was possible. It was a bold, controversial, and ultimately groundbreaking step in biomedical engineering, pushing the boundaries of what was medically achievable and paving the way for future cardiac assist devices and transplant alternatives.
