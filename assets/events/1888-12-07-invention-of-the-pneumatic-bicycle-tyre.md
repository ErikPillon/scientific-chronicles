Before the pneumatic tyre, cycling was largely a painful affair. Solid rubber tyres meant every pebble and crack on the road sent a jolt straight up your spine – hence the nickname 'boneshaker' for early bicycles. Enter John Boyd Dunlop, a Scottish veterinary surgeon living in Belfast. In 1887, he was trying to give his son a smoother ride on his tricycle. His solution? He wrapped the wheels in canvas, inflated them with air, and presto! A much more comfortable ride. He patented his invention in December 1888. However, an earlier patent for a pneumatic tyre had been filed in 1845 by another Scot, Robert William Thomson, but it never gained commercial traction for bicycles. Dunlop's timing, combined with the booming bicycle market, made his invention a sensation. The pneumatic tyre drastically improved comfort, speed, and grip, making cycling enjoyable for the masses and sparking the 'golden age' of cycling. It wasn't just about comfort; it fundamentally changed bicycle design and performance, proving that sometimes, a little air can make a huge difference in how we move.
