In the wild, early days of automotive mayhem, when horseless carriages started sharing the roads with actual horses and pedestrians, things got a bit dicey. So, in 1899, New York City, always ahead of the curve (or trying to avoid total chaos), decided enough was enough and slapped the nation's first speed limit on these newfangled motor cars. Drivers were told they could go a blistering 8 mph on straightaways, but had to slow down to a leisurely 4 mph around turns. Can you imagine getting a ticket for going 9 mph? This wasn't just about slowing people down; it was a recognition that a new technology required new rules to ensure public safety. It marked the very beginning of traffic laws as we know them, turning our bustling streets from an unregulated free-for-all into, well, a slightly more regulated free-for-all. Progress!
