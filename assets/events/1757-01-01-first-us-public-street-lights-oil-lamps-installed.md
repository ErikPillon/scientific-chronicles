Before street lights, walking around at night was a spooky, often dangerous affair. Imagine trying to navigate cobblestone streets in pitch darkness, with only a lantern (if you were lucky) to guide you! But in 1757, Philadelphia, thanks in part to the advocacy of visionary polymath Benjamin Franklin, began installing the first public street lights in America. These weren't electric marvels, but simple oil lamps, manually lit each evening by "lamplighters." It might seem quaint now, but it was a revolutionary step in urban living. Suddenly, cities became a little safer, a little more accessible after dusk, and crime rates saw a dip. It wasn't just about illumination; it was about extending the day, fostering social interaction, and making the urban environment more livable. Franklin even designed an improved street lamp, proving that even in simple innovations, his genius shone bright.
