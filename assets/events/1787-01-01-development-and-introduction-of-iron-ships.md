For millennia, ships were made of wood. Period. Then came the Industrial Revolution, and with it, the idea of building vessels out of iron. The first known iron boat, a barge called *Vulcan*, was launched by John Wilkinson in 1787. Initially, iron ships were met with skepticism – 'Iron won't float!' scoffed many. But the advantages were undeniable: iron ships could be built larger, stronger, and were less prone to rot, barnacles, and fire than their wooden counterparts. Pioneers like Isambard Kingdom Brunel truly pushed the envelope with colossal iron ships like the SS *Great Britain* (1843) and SS *Great Eastern* (1858), proving the viability of massive, ocean-going iron vessels. This shift from wood to iron (and later steel) transformed global trade, naval power, and passenger travel, allowing for faster, safer, and far more capacious journeys across the world's oceans. It truly was a titanic shift in naval architecture!
