Forget selfies, this was the ultimate celestial snapshot! On July 28, 1851, during a total solar eclipse visible from Königsberg (now Kaliningrad), a talented daguerreotypist named Julius Berkowski set up his equipment. Using a small refracting telescope fitted with a daguerreotype camera, he managed to capture an image of the solar corona – the sun's ghostly outer atmosphere. This wasn't just a pretty picture; it was a monumental leap for both astronomy and photography, allowing scientists to study the sun's mysteries long after the sky returned to normal. Before this, you basically had to sketch it in the moment. Talk about a glow-up for scientific documentation!
