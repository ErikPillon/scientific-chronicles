In the year 1640, something truly groundbreaking happened in colonial Cambridge, Massachusetts: the printing of the "Bay Psalm Book." This wasn't just any book; it was the very first book published in what would become the United States. Before this, colonists relied on imports from England, which was a slow, expensive, and often unreliable process. Imagine needing a textbook, but it has to sail across the Atlantic first! Stephen Daye, an indentured printer, was behind the press that churned out these Psalms. The book itself was a new translation of the Psalms into English, aimed at making them more accurate and suitable for congregational singing. It wasn't exactly a bestseller in the modern sense, but it represented a huge leap towards intellectual independence and cultural self-sufficiency for the fledgling colonies. Plus, surviving copies are now ridiculously valuable – a testament to its enduring historical significance!
