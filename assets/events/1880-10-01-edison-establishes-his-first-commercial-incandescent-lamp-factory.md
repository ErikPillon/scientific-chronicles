Having cracked the code for a practical, long-lasting incandescent light bulb, Thomas Edison knew the next step wasn't just invention, but production! In October 1880, he opened his very first commercial lamp factory, the Edison Lamp Works, right there in Menlo Park, New Jersey. This wasn't just a shed; it was a pioneering facility designed for mass production, churning out thousands of his carbon-filament bulbs. This move was crucial. It transformed the light bulb from a laboratory curiosity into a marketable product ready for widespread adoption, making electric light available to the masses and laying the foundation for modern industrial manufacturing. Edison wasn't just an inventor; he was an industrialist, scaling his genius for global impact.
