Before 1882, getting wrinkles out of clothes was a literal hot mess. It involved heating heavy metal irons on a stove or an open fire, a process that was not only cumbersome and time-consuming but also prone to scorching fabric or worse, burning fingers! Enter Henry Seely White, who decided there had to be a more elegant solution. On June 6, 1882, he patented the electric flatiron, a device that promised consistent heat without the smoke and soot. Imagine the relief! While early models might have been a bit temperamental, relying on the nascent and often inconsistent electricity supply, White's invention was a simple yet profound game-changer for household chores. It literally ironed out a major wrinkle in domestic life, bringing a touch of modern convenience to the laundry room and freeing up precious time.
