Before we had nuclear power stations or submarines, someone had to figure out how to build an "atomic reactor." That someone was primarily Enrico Fermi, leading a team under the stands of Stagg Field in Chicago. On December 2, 1942, their creation, dubbed Chicago Pile-1 (CP-1), became the first artificial device to achieve a self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction. This wasn't some futuristic, glowing machine; it was a carefully stacked pile of graphite blocks and uranium, with cadmium rods acting as brakes. This humble "pile" was the birth of the nuclear reactor, proving that controlled fission was possible. It was the scientific and engineering breakthrough that unlocked the atomic age, making everything from energy production to nuclear medicine possible. Pretty cool for a pile of bricks, huh?
