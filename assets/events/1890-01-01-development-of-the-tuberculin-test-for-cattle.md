Tuberculosis isn't just a human problem; it plagued cattle too, posing a serious threat to livestock and, through contaminated milk, to people. Enter the legendary bacteriologist Robert Koch, who in 1890 announced his discovery of tuberculin – a glycerol extract of the tubercle bacillus culture. While initially hoped to be a cure for human TB, it quickly proved more useful as a diagnostic tool. The 'tuberculin test' involved injecting a small amount of tuberculin into the skin of an animal. If the animal had been exposed to TB, an inflammatory reaction would occur. This simple yet effective test became a cornerstone of veterinary medicine, allowing farmers and public health officials to identify and cull infected animals, dramatically reducing the spread of bovine TB and making milk a much safer beverage for everyone. It was a huge win for both animal welfare and human public health.
