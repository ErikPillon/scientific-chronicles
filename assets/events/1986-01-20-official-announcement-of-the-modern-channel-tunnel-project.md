The idea of a fixed link between Britain and France has been floated since Napoleon's time, but it wasn't until January 20, 1986, that it officially got the green light! British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher and French President François Mitterrand signed the Treaty of Canterbury, committing their nations to building the Channel Tunnel, affectionately known as the 'Chunnel.' This wasn't just a massive civil engineering project; it was a huge geopolitical statement, symbolizing closer ties between the two historical rivals. After countless false starts, political squabbles, and engineering challenges, the announcement finally signaled that the dream of a land link across the Channel was becoming a very concrete reality. A monumental decision, indeed!
