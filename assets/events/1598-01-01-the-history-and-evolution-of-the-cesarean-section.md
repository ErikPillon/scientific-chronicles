The Cesarean section, or C-section, is an ancient surgical procedure for delivering a baby by making an incision in the mother's abdomen and uterus. Legend has it, Julius Caesar himself was born this way, though that's historically shaky as his mother survived and early C-sections were usually post-mortem to save the baby. For centuries, it was a last-resort procedure with high maternal mortality. However, thanks to advancements in anesthesia, antisepsis, and surgical techniques from the late 19th century onwards, it transformed into a relatively safe and common life-saving operation. The first reliably documented case of a mother surviving a C-section and her child being saved dates to 1598. It's a prime example of medical science evolving to tackle the most fundamental human event: birth.
