Before smartphones and instant forecasts, knowing whether to grab an umbrella was a guessing game. That changed dramatically on October 10, 1877, when the New York Herald, under the innovative eye of owner James Gordon Bennett Jr., published the world's first daily weather map. This groundbreaking graphic integrated data from telegraph reports across the country, visually depicting temperature, pressure, and precipitation. It wasn't just a novelty; it made complex meteorological information accessible to the average reader, helping them plan their day (or their picnics!). This simple map was a huge step in popularizing weather forecasting and demonstrating the power of visual journalism. Take that, unpredictable skies!
