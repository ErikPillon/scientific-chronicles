For a long time, helping people with hearing loss meant big, clunky ear trumpets – functional, but not exactly discreet or powerful. Enter the early 20th century, and with the advent of electronics, everything changed! Around 1920, inventors like Earl C. Hanson started putting vacuum tubes to work, creating the first truly electronic hearing aids. These early devices were still pretty bulky, but they offered a whole new level of sound amplification and clarity that purely mechanical devices couldn't match. It was a giant leap from simply funneling sound to actively boosting it, setting the stage for the tiny, powerful devices we have today and giving millions the gift of clearer sound.
