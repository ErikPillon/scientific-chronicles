Before smartphones and instant translations, accessing the Bible in your own language was a revolutionary act, often punishable by death! But in 1535, brave reformer Myles Coverdale changed the game. He published the first complete Bible printed in English, likely overseas (Antwerp or Cologne are suspected locales). This wasn't just a book; it was a cultural bombshell. For centuries, Latin had been the language of scripture, accessible only to the clergy. Coverdale's work opened up religious texts to the common person, sparking a thirst for literacy and individual interpretation that reshaped English society, language, and the very course of the Reformation. It showed that knowledge, once controlled by a few, could now be within reach of the many. Talk about a divine intervention in publishing!
