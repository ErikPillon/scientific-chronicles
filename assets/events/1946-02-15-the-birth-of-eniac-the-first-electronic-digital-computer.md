On February 15, 1946, the world got its first proper glimpse of ENIAC (Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer), officially dedicated at the University of Pennsylvania. This monstrous machine, bristling with 17,468 vacuum tubes and weighing 30 tons, wasn't just big; it was a game-changer. Conceived by John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert, it was the first general-purpose electronic digital computer, originally designed to calculate artillery firing tables for the U.S. Army. With its incredible speed (for the time!) and programmability, ENIAC didn't just crunch numbers; it essentially laid the foundation for every computer that followed, making it the great-great-grandparent of your smartphone. A truly electrifying moment in history!
