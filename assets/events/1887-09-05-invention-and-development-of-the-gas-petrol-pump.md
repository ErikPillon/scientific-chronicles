Before the gas pump, you'd typically buy fuel in barrels or cans from general stores, which was, let's say, less than convenient and a bit messy. Enter Sylvanus F. Bowser, an American inventor from Fort Wayne, Indiana, who, in 1887, patented the first commercial gasoline pump. His original design, a wooden box with a simple hand pump, was initially used for kerosene. But as automobiles rapidly became more popular, Bowser quickly adapted his invention for gasoline. This contraption wasn't just a fancy dispenser; it marked a pivotal moment in the rise of the automobile, making fueling cars accessible, safer, and faster. It went from a clunky device to the sleek, self-serve stations we know today, all thanks to that initial ingenious spark from Bowser that solved a critical bottleneck in early motoring.
