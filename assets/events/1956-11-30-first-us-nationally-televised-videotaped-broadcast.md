Before 1956, "live" television usually meant *live*. Replays? Forget about it! But that all changed on November 30, 1956, when CBS used the revolutionary Ampex VR-1000 videotape recorder for a deferred broadcast of "Douglas Edwards and the News." This wasn't just a technical novelty; it was a broadcasting game-changer. Suddenly, shows could be recorded, edited, and replayed across different time zones without blurry, delayed film. This innovation meant better quality, greater scheduling flexibility, and ultimately, paved the way for all the binge-watching and instant replays we now take for granted. Say goodbye to one-shot performances and hello to television as we know it!
