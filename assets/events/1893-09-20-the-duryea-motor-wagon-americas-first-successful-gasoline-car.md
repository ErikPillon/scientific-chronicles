Move over, Henry Ford – before the Model T, there was the Duryea Motor Wagon! In 1893, brothers Charles and Frank Duryea of Springfield, Massachusetts, successfully tested their single-cylinder, gasoline-powered 'horseless carriage.' While European pioneers like Benz and Daimler were already making strides, the Duryeas were the first to get a functional gasoline car on American roads, ushering in the age of personal transportation across the pond. Two years later, Frank Duryea even won America's first automobile race, proving their contraption wasn't just a novelty but a viable machine. Their innovative spirit not only gave America its first car but also ignited the entrepreneurial spark that would soon make the U.S. a global automotive powerhouse. Talk about a joyride into history!
