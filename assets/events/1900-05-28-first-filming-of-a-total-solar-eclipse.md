Before YouTube and smartphone cameras, capturing a fleeting total solar eclipse was a Herculean task. But in 1900, during an eclipse visible across the southern United States, teams from the British Astronomical Association and individual enthusiasts like Nevil Maskelyne (a magician and amateur astronomer!) set up their clunky film cameras. Their efforts captured the corona's ethereal glow, turning a rare astronomical phenomenon into a moving picture for the very first time. It was a dazzling fusion of science and nascent cinema, reminding us that even back then, people loved to document epic cosmic events.
