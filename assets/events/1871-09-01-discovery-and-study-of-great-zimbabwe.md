Nestled in southeastern Africa, the monumental ruins of Great Zimbabwe were an awe-inspiring sight for European explorers like Carl Mauch, who first reported on them in 1871. This vast stone city, constructed between the 11th and 15th centuries by the Shona people, defied colonial narratives that such sophisticated architecture couldn't be indigenous to Africa. Its towering walls and intricate dry-stone masonry were a testament to a powerful and complex civilization. For years, racist theories tried to credit its creation to foreign powers, but dedicated archaeological work, particularly by Gertrude Caton-Thompson, definitively proved its African origins. Great Zimbabwe stands as a proud symbol of African ingenuity and a crucial piece of pre-colonial history.
