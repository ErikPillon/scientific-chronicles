Imagine looking at Venus, that dazzling evening star, and seeing a faint, eerie glow on its *unlit* side. That's the Ashen Light of Venus, a phenomenon first definitively recorded by Giovanni Riccioli in 1643, though hints go back further. It's like Venus is secretly illuminating itself! For centuries, astronomers have been scratching their heads: is it lightning storms, airglow, or even light reflected from Earth? While no definitive answer has emerged, it remains one of Venus's most persistent and intriguing atmospheric mysteries, a testament to the planet's enigmatic nature and our continuous quest to understand our cosmic neighbors.
