While World War II raged, a quiet revolution was taking place in British aviation. On May 15, 1941, the Gloster E.28/39, powered by an engine designed by Frank Whittle, roared into the sky from RAF Cranwell. This wasn't just another plane; it was Britain's first successful jet aircraft! Test pilot P.E.G. Sayer took it on a maiden flight that lasted only 17 minutes, but those minutes changed aviation forever. Whittle had been toiling on jet propulsion for years, facing skepticism and funding challenges. His innovative turbojet engine eliminated the need for a propeller, promising incredible speed and efficiency. The Gloster E.28/39 was a testbed, proving the concept's viability and paving the way for the jet age. It transformed military aviation, led to commercial jetliners, and made the world a much smaller place. Whittle's perseverance literally gave wings to a new era of flight, making propeller planes look positively antique overnight.
