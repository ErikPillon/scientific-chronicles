On June 5, 1981, the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention published a brief, clinically dry report in its Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report describing five previously healthy young gay men in Los Angeles who had developed a rare pneumonia usually only seen in patients with severely compromised immune systems. Nobody reading it at the time could have known they were looking at the opening notice of what would become the global HIV/AIDS pandemic, which has since caused tens of millions of deaths worldwide. The unglamorous surveillance report set off a scramble to identify the underlying cause, eventually isolated as HIV in 1983, and kicked off decades of research, activism, and public health effort that fundamentally reshaped virology, immunology, and medicine itself. Sometimes the biggest turning points in medical history read, at first, like the least dramatic paragraph imaginable. 🩺🎗️
