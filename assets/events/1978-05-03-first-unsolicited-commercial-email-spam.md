Ah, spam. The bane of every email inbox, the digital equivalent of that annoying flyer tucked under your windshield wiper. But where did this unsolicited digital detritus begin? Believe it or not, it started innocently (or perhaps, misguidedly) on May 3, 1978. A marketing manager named Gary Thuerk, working for DEC (Digital Equipment Corporation), sent out the first mass unsolicited commercial email over ARPANET (the internet's ancestor) to about 400 recipients, advertising a new product. He thought it was a brilliant marketing hack; recipients thought it was... well, spam. The term 'spam' itself wouldn't be widely adopted until the mid-1990s, inspired by a Monty Python sketch. This humble, annoying email wasn't just a marketing ploy; it marked the beginning of a digital deluge, proving that even in the nascent internet age, the human desire to sell stuff (and the annoyance of being sold to) was alive and well. Your inbox has never been the same since!
