Driverless trains aren't a newfangled idea from the 21st century; their journey toward autonomy has been chugging along for decades! While early applications focused on specific industrial or shuttle systems, the real push for public transit began in the 1960s. Pioneering systems like those on London's Victoria Line (operational from 1967) and the Port Authority Transit Corporation (PATCO) Speedline in New Jersey (from 1969) showcased the potential. These systems introduced Automatic Train Operation (ATO), where computers controlled acceleration, braking, and stopping at stations, with human attendants often still on board for supervision. The goal was to increase efficiency, reduce human error, and allow for higher service frequencies. It was a complex dance between electrical, mechanical, and later, digital engineering, with systems relying on trackside beacons, sophisticated signaling, and on-board logic. These early innovations laid the crucial tracks for the fully automated metros and light rails we see in cities worldwide today, demonstrating that the future of transport might just be hands-free.
