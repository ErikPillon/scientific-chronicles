October 11, 1968, was a monumental day: Apollo 7 launched, marking the triumphant return of American manned spaceflight after the devastating Apollo 1 fire. Astronauts Wally Schirra, Donn Eisele, and Walt Cunningham spent 11 days orbiting Earth, putting the Command and Service Module through its paces with meticulous precision. They tested crucial systems, performed the first live TV broadcast from an American spacecraft (hello, space celebrities!), and generally proved the Apollo spacecraft was ready for prime time – and eventually, the moon. Their mission was a huge confidence booster for NASA and the nation, proving that tragedy could be overcome with ingenuity, sheer grit, and a whole lot of courage. It was the crucial dress rehearsal before the main event: the race to the moon.
